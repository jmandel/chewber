## ADI (Acceptable Daily Intake)

- **Group ADI: 40 mg phosphorus per kg body weight per day**
- This is a GROUP ADI covering all phosphates: E 338–341, E 343, E 450–452
- Expressed as phosphorus (P), not as the phosphate salt itself
- For a 70 kg adult, this equals 2,800 mg phosphorus/person/day
- This was the FIRST TIME EFSA established an ADI for phosphates (previously no ADI was set)
- The 2,800 mg/person/day is within the safety level of exposure of 3,000 mg/person/day set by the EFSA NDA Panel (2005)

## Approval Status

- **APPROVED** as a food additive in the EU under Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 (Annex II and III)
- **GRAS** (Generally Recognized as Safe) in the U.S. by FDA
- Permitted across the European Union
- Functions: acidity regulator, emulsifier, sequestering agent, humectant, stabiliser, leavening agent, anti-caking agent, mineral fortifier

## E341 Sub-types

- **E 341(i)** – Monocalcium phosphate (Ca(H₂PO₄)₂) – leavening acid in baking
- **E 341(ii)** – Dicalcium phosphate (CaHPO₄) – mineral supplement in cereals/infant foods
- **E 341(iii)** – Tricalcium phosphate (Ca₃(PO₄)₂) – anti-caking agent in powdered foods

## Key Findings from the 2019 Re-evaluation

1. **Low acute oral toxicity** – phosphates considered to be of low acute oral toxicity
2. **No genotoxicity concern** – no concern with respect to genotoxicity and carcinogenicity
3. **No developmental toxicity** – no effects reported in developmental toxicity studies
4. **Primary adverse effect:** Nephrocalcinosis and tubulointerstitial nephropathy (kidney effects) were the only significant adverse effects found in animal studies
5. **Rapid onset** of kidney effects – onset and progression appear to be fairly rapid
6. **Bone effects uncertain** – although animal studies report bone resorption/decreased bone formation with high phosphorus intake, the Panel felt the effects were not sufficiently well characterized
7. **Phosphate absorption:** 80-90% of phosphate is absorbed as free orthophosphate excreted via the kidney
8. **Inorganic vs organic:** Inorganic phosphate (from additives) is absorbed at 90-100% vs ~50% for organic phosphate from natural food sources

## Safety Concerns

### Exceedance of ADI in Vulnerable Groups
- **Infants, toddlers, and other children** EXCEED the proposed ADI at **mean** exposure levels
- **Infants, toddlers, children, and adolescents** EXCEED the proposed ADI at the **95th percentile**
- **Food supplements** exposure alone exceeds the proposed ADI
- Exposure estimates ranged from 251 mg P/person/day (infants) to 1,625 mg P/person/day (adults) at mean levels
- High exposure (95th percentile): 331 mg P/person/day (infants) to 2,728 mg P/person/day (adults)

### Cardiovascular Concerns
- Elevated phosphate intake associated with vascular calcification, endothelial dysfunction, and altered calcium-phosphate metabolism
- Particular concern for individuals with impaired kidney function or higher cardiovascular risk
- The 2013 EFSA evaluation noted scientific reviews suggesting association between high phosphate intake and increased cardiovascular risk, but could not make causal inferences

### Aluminium Contamination
- Panel suggested the EC revise the current limit for aluminium in the specifications for calcium phosphate (E 341)

### Nanoparticles
- Specifications for E 341(i), E 341(ii), E 341(iii) should include characterisation of particle size distribution and percentage of particles in the nanoscale

## Panel Recommendations

1. **Set Maximum Permitted Levels (MPL)** for phosphates in food supplements (currently allowed quantum satis)
2. **Revise limits for toxic elements** (Pb, Cd, As, Hg) in EU specifications for phosphates
3. **Revise aluminium limit** in specifications for E 341
4. **Include particle size distribution** characterisation in specifications
5. **Develop analytical methods** to determine phosphate additive levels in foods/beverages
6. **Review health-based guidance** approach for regulated substances that are also nutrients
