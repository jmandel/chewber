# E216 (Propyl p-hydroxybenzoate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E216 (INS 216)
- **Primary name:** Propyl p-hydroxybenzoate
- **CAS number:** 94-13-3
- **Chemical class:** Paraben (ester of p-hydroxybenzoic acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Propylparaben; n-propyl ester of p-hydroxybenzoic acid; propyl p-oxybenzoate
- **Origin:** **Synthetic** for food use (FDA CFR describes manufacture by esterification of p-hydroxybenzoic acid with n-propanol)

**Evidence:** WHO/JECFA chemical entry (name/synonyms/CAS/INS), FDA 21 CFR 184.1670 production description. 

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative / antimicrobial agent.
- **Mechanism of action (functional):** Inhibits growth of spoilage microorganisms (especially fungi/yeasts; generally antimicrobial preservative class action).
- **Regulatory use framing:**
  - US CFR: antimicrobial agent in food, GMP-limited, with a stated current GMP maximum level of **0.1%** in food.
  - EU historical context: use in some liquid food supplements before withdrawal.

**Evidence:** WHO/JECFA functional class = preservative; 21 CFR 184.1670(c),(d); EU Directive 2006/52/EC recitals.

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA / EU
- **Latest EFSA scientific opinion identified:** 2004 AFC Panel opinion on p-hydroxybenzoates (E214-E219) (EFSA Journal 2004;83).
- **ADI outcome:** EFSA opinion maintained a group ADI for methyl/ethyl parabens (and sodium salts) but concluded **propyl p-hydroxybenzoic acid should not be included** in that group ADI due reproductive-endpoint concerns.
- **EU legal outcome after EFSA/JECFA concerns:** Directive 2006/52/EC removed E216/E217 authorizations from Directive 95/2/EC (withdrawal in EU food additive framework).
- **Approval status (EU practical):** **Withdrawn**.

### FDA / CFR (Title 21)
- **Status:** Listed in **21 CFR 184.1670** under Part 184 (substances affirmed as GRAS).
- **GRAS basis in CFR structure:** 21 CFR 184.1(a) states substances listed in Part 184 are affirmed GRAS under prescribed conditions.
- **Conditions of use:** 21 CFR 184.1670(c)-(d): antimicrobial agent; use at levels not exceeding GMP, with current GMP yielding max **0.1% in food**.

### JECFA / WHO
- **WHO/JECFA database entry for propyl p-hydroxybenzoate:** Evaluation year **2006**, ADI listed as **WITHDRAWN (2006)**.
- **Key JECFA rationale shown on entry:** exclusion from paraben group ADI due adverse effects in male rats, noting effects down to 10 mg/kg bw/day and no identified NOEL for that endpoint.
- **Previous ADI history shown:** prior group ADI context included 0-10 mg/kg bw/day (historical entries).

### IARC
- Checked IARC classifications resources; no listing found for propylparaben / propyl p-hydroxybenzoate in the current IARC classified-agents dataset.
- **Classification:** no IARC group classification identified (null/not listed).

### Notable bans/restrictions
- **European Union:** E216 (and E217) withdrawn from the food additive authorization framework via Directive 2006/52/EC.

## Key Safety Evidence

### 1) Regulatory toxicology signal (highest-weight evidence)
- JECFA and EFSA both flagged propyl paraben-specific concern around male reproductive endpoints, resulting in exclusion from prior group ADI and eventual ADI withdrawal (JECFA) and EU withdrawal from authorized additives.
- This is the strongest evidence block because it drove regulatory action.

### 2) Animal/mechanistic studies (recent)
- **Mouse prenatal exposure model (2026):** reported reduced ovarian reserve/fertility and mechanistic changes (oxidative stress, inflammatory/fibrotic signaling, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, DNA methylation changes).
- **Zebrafish models (2025/2026):** reported immunotoxicity and developmental toxicity with oxidative stress and microbiome-linked pathways.

### 3) Cancer/mechanistic in vitro evidence (recent)
- **ER-positive breast cancer cell mechanistic study (2026):** suggested procarcinogenic pathway activation under experimental conditions.
- **Bladder cancer cell/in vivo model study (2025):** reported increased invasive behavior under tested conditions.
- These are hypothesis-generating and mechanistic; they do not establish population-level cancer risk on their own.

### 4) Human epidemiological signal (limited/mixed)
- Recent pooled mother-child cohort analysis reported phenotype associations for several phenols/parabens with sex-specific differences; propylparaben association direction was not uniformly adverse in all strata.
- Human causal evidence specific to E216 as a food additive remains limited.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EU historical pre-withdrawal concern:** Directive 2006/52/EC recitals state children (high consumers) could exceed temporary ADI in some scenarios (up to 2-fold via food supplements and 3-fold via medicinal products; one estimate cited 16% above temporary ADI for children 3-6 years consuming supplements at max legal concentration).
- **Current EU food-additive exposure expectation:** likely much lower after withdrawal (non-authorized status).
- **US exposure context:** still permitted under GRAS conditions in CFR with GMP and a cited current GMP maximum of 0.1% in food.
- **Vulnerable populations:** children (historical exceedance risk), potentially prenatal/early-life windows (based on experimental and cohort studies).

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:** US CFR still allows use under GRAS framework and GMP limits.
- **Evidence against risk_free:** JECFA ADI withdrawn; EFSA/JECFA identified reproductive-endpoint concerns specific to propyl paraben; EU withdrew authorization.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:** Continued FDA GRAS status with explicit GMP limits and max current use level in CFR.
- **Evidence against limited:** Multiple high-authority international evaluations did not retain a conventional acceptable intake basis for propyl paraben itself; regulatory withdrawal in EU indicates concern beyond a minor caveat profile.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:** Mixed human epidemiology and substantial reliance on animal/mechanistic evidence rather than definitive human causality could argue for a cautious but not maximal tier.
- **Evidence against moderate:** Combination of (a) EU withdrawal, (b) JECFA ADI withdrawal, and (c) regulatory reproductive toxicity concern in juvenile/male rat endpoints is stronger than a typical “approved with caveats” profile.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Banned/withdrawn in a major jurisdiction (EU food additive regime).
  - JECFA ADI withdrawn (2006) with reproductive-endpoint rationale.
  - EFSA opinion did not support inclusion of propyl in the paraben group ADI.
  - Historical child exposure exceedance concerns explicitly documented in EU legal recitals.
- **Evidence against high:**
  - FDA GRAS listing remains in force under use limits.
  - Human epidemiology remains limited and not uniformly adverse.

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factors are regulatory toxicology outcomes from EFSA/JECFA and the resulting EU withdrawal, supported by ongoing mechanistic evidence (endocrine/reproductive and other toxicity signals). Although US GRAS status remains, the international weight of evidence for propyl paraben is more cautionary than for methyl/ethyl parabens.

### 3. Recommended tier
**high**
