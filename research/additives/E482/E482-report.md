# E482 (Calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E482 (commonly E482(i) for the calcium salt)
- **Name:** Calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate
- **CAS number(s):** 5793-94-2
- **Chemical class:** Lactylated fatty-acid salt; emulsifier (calcium salt of stearoyl-lactylic acids)
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium stearoyl lactylate; calcium stearoyl lactate; calcium stearyl-2-lactylate; INS 482(i)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (industrial reaction of stearic acid and lactic acid, then conversion to calcium salts)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic emulsifier that lowers oil-water interfacial tension and helps stabilize gas/fat/protein structures in doughs and whipped systems.
- **Common food categories:**
  - Yeast-leavened bakery products and bakery mixes (dough conditioning)
  - Liquid/frozen/dried egg white (whipping aid)
  - Whipped vegetable oil toppings
  - Dehydrated potatoes

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Status:** Authorized in the EU as E481/E482 and re-evaluated by EFSA.
  - **ADI:** EFSA set a **temporary group ADI of 20 mg/kg bw/day (as lactic acid)** for calcium, sodium, and stearyl lactylates.
  - **Evaluation year:** **2013** re-evaluation (EFSA Journal 11(3):3144).
  - **Key conclusions:**
    - Mean exposure estimates were generally below the temporary ADI.
    - High-level exposure estimates exceeded the temporary ADI in all age groups except the elderly.
    - EFSA requested tighter limits for toxic elements in specifications and minimization of alpha-chlorinated fatty acids from propylene-glycol route impurities.
  - **Access note:** Direct EFSA journal URL was visited but returned `403 Forbidden` in this environment; conclusion details were taken from an accessible indexed abstract mirror of the same EFSA opinion.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Status:** **Approved direct food additive** (not codified as a Part 184 GRAS listing in the queried eCFR title index).
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 172.844**.
  - **Conditions of use (examples):**
    - Up to 0.5 part per 100 parts flour in yeast-leavened bakery products/mixes.
    - Up to 0.05% in liquid/frozen egg white; up to 0.5% in dried egg white.
    - Up to 0.3% in whipped vegetable oil topping.
    - Up to 0.5% in dehydrated potatoes.
    - Labeling/directions requirements for additive and premixes.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **0-20 mg/kg bw** (as listed on the calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate JECFA record).
  - **Evaluation year shown on record:** **1973**; the same page lists later specification-related update years through **1996** in “Previous Years”.
- **IARC:**
  - No IARC classification entry was found for calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate / stearoyl lactylates in the current IARC classification dataset checked.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis (EFSA/JECFA):**
  - EFSA 2013 and JECFA listings support continued authorized use with ADI-based management.
- **Animal data:**
  - A one-year oral rat study on sodium stearoyl lactylate (related stearoyl-lactylate) reported no treatment-related adverse effects at tested doses, with NOAEL at highest dietary level tested.
- **Epidemiological / population exposure data:**
  - Belgian dietary exposure study (E481/E482) found potential ADI exceedance under conservative MPL assumptions (tier 2), especially in children; exceedance dropped markedly with measured concentration-based estimates (tier 3), but a small proportion of children still exceeded ADI.
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - In vitro microbiome work reported that sodium stearoyl lactylate at 0.025% altered fecal microbial composition and reduced butyrate production, indicating a plausible gut-microbiome concern signal that is not yet a direct human harm demonstration.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure context (2013):**
  - Mean exposure estimates generally did not exceed the temporary group ADI.
  - High-level exposure exceeded ADI for all age groups except elderly; EFSA also noted potential exceedance in all populations under a use-extension scenario.
- **Belgian assessment (2018):**
  - Tier 2 (MPL-based) modeled exceedance: 92% of children, 53% adolescents, 15% adults, 26% elderly above ADI.
  - Tier 3 (measured concentrations): intakes mostly below ADI; ~1.9% of children still above ADI.
  - Main contributors: bread/rolls/fine bakery products.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children and high consumers of processed bakery/emulsifier-containing foods.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence for: Long-standing approvals and no IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - Evidence against: ADI-managed additive with documented high-percentile exceedance scenarios and some mechanistic gut-microbiome concern signals; does not meet a "no credible evidence of harm" threshold.
- **limited**
  - Evidence for: Approved in EU/US, established ADIs (EFSA temporary group ADI; JECFA group ADI), and no strong confirmed severe hazard signal at typical use levels.
  - Evidence against: Exposure exceedance can occur in high consumers (especially children), which is stronger than a purely minor/high-dose-only caveat.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence for: Approved but with meaningful caveats: EFSA temporary ADI framework, high-percentile ADI exceedance in some populations, and plausible mechanistic microbiome signals.
  - Evidence against: No major ban pattern, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing, and no decisive human causal harm evidence specific to E482 at normal intake.
- **high**
  - Evidence for: None of the high-tier triggers (major bans, IARC 2A/2B plus regulatory action, inability to establish safety) were met.
  - Evidence against: Core global regulators still permit use with ADI constraints.

2. **Rationale**
- The additive remains broadly authorized, but risk is not best characterized as minimal because children/high consumers can exceed ADI in realistic scenarios and mechanistic microbiome concerns exist for related stearoyl-lactylates. Evidence supports ADI-based risk management rather than outright high-hazard classification.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
