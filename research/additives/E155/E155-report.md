# E155 (Brown HT) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E155
- **Name:** Brown HT
- **CAS number:** 4553-89-3
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic azo food colour (bis-azo sulfonated dye; disodium naphthalenesulfonate derivative)
- **Common synonyms:** Chocolate Brown HT; C.I. Food Brown 3; INS 155
- **Origin:** Synthetic

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Colour additive (brown colour standardization).
- **Mechanism:** The azo chromophore absorbs visible light and imparts brown coloration.
- **Common food categories:** EFSA (2010 communication) lists uses in fermented milk products, fish/crustacean paste and smoked fish, cheeses, pickled vegetables, sauces and seasonings, processed potato/cereal snacks, soups, beverages and liqueurs, candied fruit, fine bakery wares, and confectionery.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - Re-evaluation concluded an **ADI of 1.5 mg/kg bw/day** (maintained from previous SCF basis), with no need to revise in the 2010 re-evaluation.
  - **Evaluation year:** 2010 (EFSA Journal publication for Brown HT re-evaluation).
  - **Refined exposure update (2014):** Mean exposure was generally below ADI in major scenarios, but **95th percentile exposure exceeded ADI** in at least some population groups (all age groups except elderly) in two scenarios; reported-use scenario was below ADI.
  - **Current interpretation from retrieved sources:** approved in EU with an ADI and exposure caveats.
- **FDA / eCFR (U.S.):**
  - eCFR API search for `Brown HT` and `Chocolate Brown HT` returned **no Title 21 entries**.
  - Therefore, this specific additive is **not listed as an approved U.S. food colour additive in retrieved Title 21 search results**, and no GRAS entry for this name was identified.
  - A different substance exists: **21 CFR 74.2151 (D&C Brown No. 1)** with specific restrictions (externally applied cosmetic colouring), indicating name/identity distinction from E155.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - JECFA chemical entry for BROWN HT (INS 155) reports **ADI: 0-1.5 mg/kg bw**.
  - **Last evaluation year shown:** 1984.
- **IARC:**
  - Not found as Brown HT/Chocolate Brown HT/C.I. Food Brown 3/CAS 4553-89-3 in the current IARC list-of-classifications dataset file (`loc.app.js`), so no direct IARC group classification was identified from retrieved data.
- **Notable bans/restrictions from retrieved sources:**
  - No explicit EFSA/JECFA withdrawal found.
  - U.S. status from retrieved eCFR data is effectively non-listing for Brown HT by that name.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology (historical core):**
  - Long-term rat and mouse studies indexed in PubMed (1978) did not report treatment-related carcinogenic signals in study summaries at tested dietary levels.
  - Three-generation rat study (1987) summary indicates no treatment-related reproductive effects in tested dose range.
  - Metabolism study (1987) reports substantial excretion within 72 h, mostly fecal.
- **Recent literature signals:**
  - A 2024 rat study reports an association between Brown HT exposure and mammary tumour outcomes in its model.
  - A 2024 review on synthetic food colours highlights continuing uncertainty and need for updated assessment.
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - As an azo dye, Brown HT is commonly discussed in relation to reductive metabolism/biotransformation concerns; current regulatory conclusions from retrieved EFSA/JECFA sources still rely on established ADIs.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2010 re-evaluation context:** estimated dietary exposure scenarios included ranges where upper-end/high-percentile estimates exceeded ADI in some groups.
- **EFSA refined exposure (2014):**
  - Mean exposure generally below ADI in two main scenarios.
  - 95th percentile exposure above ADI for some populations in two scenarios (not elderly).
  - Reported-use scenario below ADI at both mean and 95th percentile.
- **Vulnerable populations:** children and other high consumers of coloured foods are the main at-risk groups for ADI exceedance scenarios.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports:** No direct IARC classification identified; historical toxicology database contains several negative/low-signal long-term findings.
  - **Against:** Numerical ADI exists (not “ADI not specified”), and EFSA exposure assessments identified potential exceedance in high consumers; newer animal concern signal exists.
- **limited**
  - **Supports:** EFSA/JECFA approvals with defined ADI; JECFA and historical studies provide substantial legacy safety data.
  - **Against:** EFSA high-percentile exceedance scenarios and emerging recent concern study make “minor concerns only at high doses” too optimistic.
- **moderate**
  - **Supports:** Approved but with caveats: ADI exceedance risk in some populations (especially high consumers/children under some scenarios), plus renewed animal-literature concern.
  - **Against:** No strong current carcinogenic classification by IARC and no EFSA/JECFA withdrawal in retrieved sources.
- **high**
  - **Supports:** Non-listing in U.S. Title 21 searches for Brown HT name and one recent tumour-focused animal paper could be interpreted as stronger concern signals.
  - **Against:** Criteria for high tier are not met by retrieved evidence: no confirmed IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for Brown HT, no EFSA inability-to-confirm-safety conclusion, and no major-jurisdiction ban identified in retrieved regulatory documents.

### 2. Rationale
E155 has established ADIs from EFSA and JECFA and remains available in jurisdictions that use E-number authorization, but exposure modelling caveats (notably high-percentile exceedance in some groups) and newer animal-signal literature prevent a low-concern classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
