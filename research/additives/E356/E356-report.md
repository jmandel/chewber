# E356 (Sodium adipate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E356
- **Name:** Sodium adipate
- **CAS number(s):** 7486-38-6 (disodium adipate); 18996-34-4 (sodium hydrogen adipate form listed in chemical databases)
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salt(s) of adipic acid (hexanedioic acid), i.e., dicarboxylate acidulant/buffer salts
- **Common synonyms:** Disodium adipate; sodium hydrogen adipate; disodium hexanedioate; sodium hexanedioate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic/commercially manufactured** (adipic acid is industrially produced and then neutralized to sodium salt forms)

Evidence:
- WHO JECFA chemical page for sodium adipate (INS 356) lists CAS 7486-38-6 and functional class. [S7]
- PubChem records identify disodium adipate and sodium hydrogen adipate forms and CAS numbers. [S15][S16][S17][S18]
- 21 CFR 184.1009 describes adipic acid production route (nitric-acid oxidation of cyclohexanol/cyclohexanone), supporting synthetic origin of upstream acid precursor. [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Acidity regulator / buffering agent (acid-base control).
- **Mechanism of action:** Adipate salts contribute adipate anion buffering capacity and pH adjustment in formulated foods; in acidic systems, they function as part of acidulant systems that influence sourness and processing stability.
- **Common food categories:** Category-level usage evidence in U.S. CFR is for adipic acid (the corresponding acid) under GMP, including baked goods, beverages, condiments/relishes, dairy analogs, fats/oils, gelatin/puddings, gravies, meat products, and snack foods.

Evidence:
- WHO JECFA page lists sodium adipate as food additive functional class `ACIDITY_REGULATOR`. [S7]
- 21 CFR 184.1009 (adipic acid) provides pH-control/leavening/flavoring functional use and category maxima under GMP. [S4][S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence (primarily JECFA monograph basis)
- Metabolic handling studies indicate substantial metabolism and excretion of adipic acid/adipate-related carbon, with evidence of beta-oxidation pathway handling in rats.
- Subchronic/high-dose dietary studies in rats reported effects such as diarrhea and reduced growth at high intakes, while lower levels showed fewer adverse findings.
- Teratology studies summarized in JECFA monograph did not identify compound-related embryotoxic/teratogenic effects in tested species/dose ranges.
- Genotoxicity assays summarized in JECFA monograph did not show strong positive signals in the tested systems reported.

Evidence:
- JECFA toxicological monograph (FAS 12) and JECFA evaluation summary. [S10][S9]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting:
  - No IARC listing for sodium adipate/adipic acid found.
  - JECFA established a bounded group ADI and historical toxicology did not show a strong severe-harm pattern at typical-use framing.
- Evidence against:
  - ADI is numeric and bounded (0-5), not “ADI not specified.”
  - Contemporary EFSA retrievable dataset/opinion for E356 could not be confirmed in this run.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting:
  - JECFA group ADI exists for adipic acid and sodium/potassium/ammonium salts.
  - U.S. CFR has GRAS framework entries for adipic acid (closest direct anchor), with GMP-based use conditions.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification signal for sodium adipate/adipic acid identified.
- Evidence against:
  - Exact `sodium adipate` standalone CFR listing was not found in the retrieved Title 21 text.
  - Modern exposure datasets specific to E356 were not identified in this run.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting:
  - High-dose animal findings (e.g., diarrhea/reduced growth in older studies) indicate adverse effects are possible above typical use ranges.
  - Data recency and direct modern epidemiology for E356 are limited.
- Evidence against:
  - Available regulatory anchors (JECFA ADI, FDA adipic-acid GRAS framework) do not indicate current major hazard escalation.
  - No IARC classification for sodium adipate/adipic acid.

**high**
- Evidence supporting:
  - None identified from reviewed EFSA-access attempts, FDA/eCFR, JECFA, IARC, and PubMed evidence in this run.
- Evidence against:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC 2A/2B/1 classification for sodium adipate/adipic acid found.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest directly retrievable regulatory evidence is JECFA’s historical group ADI and FDA’s adipic-acid GRAS framework anchors. Hazard signals are mainly high-dose/older-study effects rather than strong modern low-dose evidence. The largest uncertainty is incomplete direct EFSA extraction for E356 in this environment.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
