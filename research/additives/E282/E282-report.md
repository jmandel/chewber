# E282 (Calcium propionate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E282. [S1][S8]
- **Primary name:** Calcium propionate. [S8][S10]
- **CAS number:** 4075-81-4. [S5][S8]
- **Chemical class:** Calcium salt of propionic acid (short-chain fatty acid salt preservative). [S5][S8][S10]
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium propanoate; propionic acid calcium salt. [S8][S10]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercial food additive is generally manufactured by neutralizing propionic acid with calcium hydroxide; propionate itself is also a natural metabolic intermediate and occurs naturally in some fermented foods. [S5][S10][S1]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Antimicrobial preservative, especially anti-mould in bakery and related foods. [S5][S8]
- **Mechanism of action:** Propionate salts inhibit mould/yeast growth by weak-acid antimicrobial action (intracellular acidification and metabolic inhibition), especially effective in lower-pH foods. [S10][S5]
- **Common food categories:** In U.S. regulation for direct human food use, calcium propionate is used (GMP) in baked goods, cheeses, confections/frostings, gelatins/puddings/fillings, and jams/jellies. [S5]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Re-evaluated in **2014** (EFSA Journal 2014;12(7):3779). EFSA concluded there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for propionic acid and propionates (including E282), and no safety concern at reported uses/levels. This supports continued authorization status in EU uses reviewed in that opinion. [S1][S2]
- **FDA / CFR (U.S.):** **GRAS** status in Title 21 via **21 CFR 184.1221** (direct human food ingredient). Conditions: used as antimicrobial, with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice, in specified food categories. Also listed for animal feed as GRAS at **21 CFR 582.3221** (good manufacturing or feeding practice). [S5][S6][S7]
- **JECFA/WHO:** WHO JECFA database entry for calcium propionate reports ADI as **"NOT LIMITED"** (group ADI for propionic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium salts), originally set in **1973** and maintained at meeting 49 (1997). [S8][S10]
- **IARC:** No listing for calcium propionate/propionic acid was found in IARC’s current “Agents classified by the IARC Monographs” dataset (last update shown in dataset: 2026-02-25). [S11][S12]
- **Notable bans:** No major-jurisdiction ban identified from the reviewed regulatory sources. [S1][S5][S8][S11]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** EFSA (2014) and JECFA conclude low concern at authorized uses, with no numeric ADI required (EFSA) / ADI not limited (JECFA group basis). [S1][S8][S10]
- **Human mechanistic signal:** A randomized crossover trial in healthy adults (n=28) found acute ingestion of **1500 mg calcium propionate** increased glucagon/catecholamine responses and endogenous glucose production vs placebo, interpreted by authors as potential metabolic disruption signal. [S14]
- **Human observational signal (recent):** A large prospective cohort analysis (NutriNet-Santé, 2026 publication) reported higher type 2 diabetes incidence associated with several preservative additives, including calcium propionate exposure. Observational design means residual confounding and co-exposure remain possible. [S13]
- **In vitro signal:** A 2024 in-vitro lymphocyte study reported cytotoxic/genotoxic effects at tested concentrations; external validity for normal dietary exposure is uncertain. [S15]
- **Mixed-preservative animal data:** Rat study with long-term exposure to multiple preservatives (including calcium propionate) reported immune/hematologic changes, but design does not isolate calcium propionate as a single-cause effect. [S16]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimates in re-evaluation:** Mean estimated exposure range was **0.01 to 7.31 mg/kg bw/day**, and high (95th percentile) range **0.2 to 41.17 mg/kg bw/day**; highest reported estimates were in adults (non-alcoholic flavoured drinks) and toddlers (bread/rolls). [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed (group conclusion).
  - JECFA: ADI “not limited” (group basis).
  - Therefore, classical numeric ADI exceedance calculations are not directly applicable; practical concern shifts to emerging non-traditional endpoints (e.g., metabolic signals) and high-consumption groups. [S1][S8][S10][S13][S14]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of preservative-rich bakery/processed foods (including children/toddlers in EFSA exposure scenarios), and people with pre-existing metabolic risk where emerging data suggest caution. [S1][S13][S14]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA found no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses; JECFA group ADI is not limited; propionate is a normal metabolic intermediate. [S1][S8][S10]
  - Evidence against: Newer human and mechanistic studies report potential metabolic associations/effects not central to older regulatory endpoints. [S13][S14]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Additive remains authorized/GRAS in major jurisdictions; CFR conditions are GMP-based; no IARC carcinogenic classification identified; no major bans identified. [S5][S6][S7][S11][S12]
  - Evidence against: Recent observational and short-term human mechanistic signals introduce uncertainty that may justify future re-review. [S13][S14]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Emerging evidence includes association with type 2 diabetes incidence (observational) and acute counterregulatory hormonal activation in humans; some in-vitro genotoxic findings exist. [S13][S14][S15]
  - Evidence against: Evidence is not yet a consistent, additive-specific causal package at realistic dietary intakes; regulatory authorities have not issued downgrades/withdrawals based on current evidence. [S1][S5][S8]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Limited support; mostly theoretical if future data confirm robust harm at common intake levels.
  - Evidence against: No IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for calcium propionate, no major-jurisdiction bans, and no EFSA/JECFA determination of inability to confirm safety at authorized uses. [S1][S8][S11][S12]

### 2. Rationale
E282 has strong legacy regulatory support (EFSA/JECFA/FDA framework) and no current major-regulator carcinogenic or ban signal. However, newer studies suggest possible metabolic concerns (acute hormonal effects and prospective associations) that warrant monitoring and potential targeted re-assessment as evidence matures.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
