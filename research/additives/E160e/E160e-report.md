# E160e (Beta-apo-8'-carotenal) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E160e. [S1][S2]
- **Primary name:** beta-apo-8'-carotenal (also written beta-apo-8′-carotenal; 8'-apo-beta-carotenal). [S4][S5]
- **CAS number(s):** 1107-26-2 (primary), plus 12676-20-9 listed among synonyms/registry entries. [S4]
- **Chemical class:** apocarotenoid (carotenoid aldehyde / apo-carotenal). This is inferred from the chemical name used in JECFA and PubChem records. [S4][S5]
- **Synonyms (common):** Apocarotenal, C.I. Food Orange 6, Food Orange 6, all-trans-beta-apo-8'-carotenal. [S4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Regulatory toxicology language in JECFA explicitly discusses beta-apo-8'-carotenal within a group of **synthetic carotenoids**; in food additive use it is treated as a synthetic color additive. [S5]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** food color additive (orange-red hue), authorized in the U.S. for coloring foods generally with quantitative limits. [S3]
- **Mechanism of action (coloring):** as a carotenoid/apocarotenoid with an extended conjugated chain, it absorbs visible light and imparts orange coloration (chemical-structure inference from identity/class). [S4][S5]
- **Common food categories:**
  - U.S. rule: foods generally (except where standards of identity disallow added color). [S3]
  - EFSA refined exposure indicates notable contribution in children from non-alcoholic flavored drinks and flavored fermented milk products. [S2]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** approved (re-evaluated; ADI established). [S2]
- **Latest EFSA evaluation year found:** **2014** (reconsideration of ADI and refined exposure). [S2]
- **ADI:** **0.3 mg/kg bw/day** (updated from earlier temporary ADI). [S2]
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - 2012 re-evaluation set a **temporary ADI 0.05 mg/kg bw/day** and indicated possible exceedance in children under conservative assumptions. [S1]
  - 2014 reconsideration updated the ADI to **0.3 mg/kg bw/day** and concluded current use at reported levels was **not a safety concern**. [S2]

### FDA / U.S. CFR
- **Status in CFR framework:** **approved color additive** under **21 CFR 73.90**, exempt from batch certification; not handled as a GRAS listing in this section. [S3][S10]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 73.90 (beta-apo-8'-carotenal)**. [S3]
- **Conditions of use:**
  - Not more than **15 mg/lb** of solid or semisolid food.
  - Not more than **15 mg/pint** of liquid food.
  - Not allowed in foods with standards of identity unless color is authorized by that standard. [S3]

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA entry:** beta-apo-8'-carotenal (INS 160e), chemical page ID 941. [S5][S6]
- **Latest evaluation year found:** **2019** (meeting 87). [S5]
- **ADI:** **0-0.3 mg/kg bw**. [S5]
- **Basis note (JECFA page text):** ADI based on NOAEL 30 mg/kg bw/day in a 13-week rat study, UF 100. [S5]

### IARC
- **IARC classification found for beta-apo-8'-carotenal:** none identified in the IARC classification dataset checked (up to last update shown in the app script). [S7][S8]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed here (EU/FDA/JECFA evidence shows continued permitted use with limits/ADI). [S2][S3][S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- JECFA states the current ADI derives from a 13-week rat study (NOAEL 30 mg/kg bw/day, UF 100). [S5]
- Historical toxicology study exists in dogs (1962), but the PubMed record provides citation metadata only in the retrieved view. [S13]

### Epidemiological / human outcome data
- No robust, additive-specific epidemiologic dataset was identified in the searched regulatory records and PubMed query outputs; available recent PubMed items are mostly mechanistic, analytical, or metabolism-focused rather than population risk studies. [S9][S11]

### Mechanistic concerns
- In vitro work reports beta-apo-8'-carotenal-associated DNA strand break signals and CYP1A2 induction under specific cell conditions (A549; ARPE-19 contexts). [S14][S15]
- These findings are mechanistic and not, by themselves, equivalent to demonstrated adverse effects at permitted dietary exposure levels. [S2][S5][S14][S15]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2014 refined exposure estimates:**
  - General population: up to **0.06 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **0.15 mg/kg bw/day (high percentile)**.
  - Children: up to **0.10 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **0.20 mg/kg bw/day (high percentile)**.
  - These values were below the revised ADI (0.3 mg/kg bw/day). [S2]
- **Historical context:** EFSA 2012 temporary ADI scenario indicated potential exceedance in children at conservative assumptions. [S1]
- **Vulnerable groups:** children are the highest modeled exposure group in EFSA’s refined assessment. [S2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - Multiple regulators permit use with an ADI and explicit limits; no IARC carcinogen classification identified. [S2][S3][S5][S7][S8]
- Evidence against placement:
  - Finite ADI exists (not “ADI not specified”), and mechanistic in vitro genotoxic signals are reported. [S2][S14][S15]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - EFSA and JECFA both use ADI **0.3 mg/kg bw/day** and did not conclude current authorized use is unsafe. [S2][S5]
  - U.S. FDA/CFR permits use with explicit concentration limits and exemption from certification requirements in this listing. [S3]
  - No IARC classification hit found for this additive in the checked classification resources. [S7][S8]
- Evidence against placement:
  - In vitro mechanistic studies suggest DNA-damage-related signals at experimental conditions, so “no concern at any dose/context” is not supportable. [S14][S15]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - Earlier EFSA temporary ADI assessment noted possible exceedance in children under conservative assumptions; mechanistic studies raise biologic plausibility for oxidative/genotoxic pathways. [S1][S14][S15]
- Evidence against placement:
  - Updated EFSA/JECFA assessments set higher ADI (0.3 mg/kg bw/day) and refined exposures stayed below ADI in assessed groups. [S2][S5]

**high**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - No strong support found (no major-jurisdiction ban, no IARC Group 1/2 classification found, no regulator conclusion of inability to confirm safety at permitted uses). [S2][S3][S5][S7][S8]
- Evidence against placement:
  - Regulatory consensus currently supports controlled use rather than prohibition. [S2][S3][S5]

### 2. Rationale
The dominant weight of evidence is regulatory convergence (EFSA, JECFA, U.S. CFR) around permitted use with ADI and use limits, plus refined exposure estimates below ADI in key populations. Mechanistic in vitro findings justify continued caution and monitoring but do not currently overturn the approved-use safety conclusions. [S2][S3][S5][S14][S15]

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
