# E331 (Sodium citrates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E331 (covers E331(i), E331(ii), E331(iii)). [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3]
- **Name:** Sodium citrates (sodium salts of citric acid). [EFSA-1][FDA-2]
- **CAS numbers (reported across forms):**
  - **68-04-2** (trisodium citrate / sodium citrate). [JECFA-1][PUBCHEM-3]
  - **144-33-2** (disodium citrate). [PUBCHEM-2]
  - **18996-35-5** (monosodium citrate / sodium citrate monobasic in PubChem/JECFA synonym context). [PUBCHEM-1][JECFA-4]
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salts of citric acid (organic citrate salts; buffering/chelating salts).
- **Common synonyms:** monosodium citrate, disodium citrate, trisodium citrate, sodium citrate, sodium citrate monobasic. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3][PUBCHEM-1][PUBCHEM-3]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Industrial food additive form is manufactured by neutralizing citric acid with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate; this is a synthetic manufacturing step. [FDA-2]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** acidity regulator / pH buffer.
- **Other functions:** sequestrant, stabilizer (JECFA functional classes). [JECFA-4]
- **Mechanism of action:** citrate anion buffers pH and complexes metal ions; sodium citrate systems help stabilize formulations and control acidity.
- **Common food categories:** broad use under GMP/quantum satis-style conditions in processed foods where pH control/stabilization is needed; U.S. GRAS rule states no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice. [FDA-1][FDA-2]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- A 2024 mouse study (postweaning exposure in drinking water) reported long-lasting, sex-dependent social behavior effects and associated biological changes, indicating a potential signal at developmental exposure levels in that model. [PUBMED-1]

### Human data
- A 2026 human supplementation study reported gastrointestinal symptom tracking after sodium citrate supplementation and blood bicarbonate response assessment; this is high-dose supplementation context rather than normal food-additive exposure. [PUBMED-2]

### Regulatory toxicology conclusions
- JECFA comments for sodium citrate entries state that citric acid and calcium/potassium/sodium salts were not considered a significant toxicological hazard in evaluated uses, with ADI not specified/not limited. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- FDA GRAS affirmation and GMP-limited use condition are consistent with low concern under intended food-use conditions. [FDA-1][FDA-2]

### Epidemiological data
- No strong human epidemiological dataset specifically isolating typical dietary E331 exposure and chronic adverse outcomes was identified in this search session.

### Mechanistic concerns
- At higher intakes (especially supplementation settings), GI tolerance can be a practical adverse-effect domain; extrapolation to ordinary additive exposure remains uncertain. [PUBMED-2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:** JECFA ADI is “not specified/not limited” and historical comments indicate no significant toxicological hazard for citric acid and its sodium/potassium/calcium salts; sodium citrate is broadly authorized and metabolically related to citrate pathways. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- **Evidence against:** EFSA’s current re-evaluation is still ongoing (no finalized modern EFSA output posted in reviewed records), and at least some recent experimental literature reports potential effects at non-typical exposure contexts. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3][PUBMED-1]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:** FDA GRAS status with GMP condition, JECFA ADI not limited, no IARC classification found, and lack of strong epidemiologic harm signal at normal food-use context in sources reviewed. [FDA-1][FDA-2][JECFA-1][IARC-2]
- **Evidence against:** Ongoing EFSA re-evaluation without final posted output, plus newer animal/high-dose supplementation findings that justify continued monitoring. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3][PUBMED-1][PUBMED-2]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:** Could be argued because EFSA re-evaluation is unfinished and there are newer adverse-effect signals in specific models/contexts.
- **Evidence against:** No regulatory restriction trend found (FDA GRAS intact; JECFA ADI not limited; no IARC listing), and evidence quality for typical dietary additive exposure harm remains limited.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:** None substantial in reviewed regulatory evidence.
- **Evidence against:** No major-jurisdiction ban identified, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for sodium citrate itself, and long-standing permissive regulatory conclusions. [FDA-1][JECFA-1][IARC-2]

### 2. Rationale
Regulatory consensus from FDA and JECFA remains permissive, with JECFA assigning a non-numeric “not specified/not limited” ADI and FDA affirming GRAS use under GMP. The main uncertainty is that EFSA’s current re-evaluation is ongoing and has no posted final output yet. Recent studies suggest possible effects at high or non-standard exposure contexts, but these do not currently overturn the core regulatory picture for normal food additive use.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
