# E471 (Mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E471 (INS 471) [S5].
- **CAS number(s)**: **68953-43-5** (JECFA entry for mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids) [S5].
- **Chemical class**: Mixture of glycerol mono- and di-esters (with minor triesters) of long-chain saturated/unsaturated fatty acids from edible fats/oils [S5, S6].
- **Common synonyms**: Glyceryl monostearate, glyceryl monopalmitate, glyceryl monooleate, monostearin, monopalmitin, monoolein, mixed mono- and diglycerides [S5, S8].
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** in food additive practice (manufactured by glycerolysis/esterification using edible fats/oils and glycerin) [S6].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Amphiphilic glycerides reduce interfacial tension and stabilize oil-water systems (emulsification), with additional texturizing/surface effects [S6, S8].
- **Regulatory functional uses (US CFR context)**: Dough strengthener; emulsifier/emulsifier salt; flavoring agent/adjuvant; formulation aid; lubricant/release agent; solvent/vehicle; stabilizer/thickener; surface-active/surface-finishing agent; texturizer [S6].
- **Common food categories**: Broadly used across processed foods; EFSA assessed many categories for the general population and specifically infant formula and FSMP formula uses [S1, S2].

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - 2017 re-evaluation concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** and **no safety concern at reported uses/use levels** [S1].
  - 2021 follow-up for infants below 16 weeks concluded **no reason for a safety concern** for authorized infant uses, while recommending tighter impurity limits (e.g., arsenic, lead, cadmium, mercury, glycidyl esters, 3-MCPD, erucic acid) [S2].
  - **Latest EFSA scientific opinion year identified**: **2021** [S2, S4].
- **FDA / CFR (US)**:
  - **21 CFR §184.1505** places mono- and diglycerides in direct food substances affirmed as **GRAS**; use is limited by current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) [S6].
  - FDA inventory page maps mono-/diglycerides to CFR citations including **172.812, 175.105, 184.1505** [S8].
  - Direct eCFR page access from this VM was blocked by anti-bot challenge; citation content was retrieved from the eCFR mirror text at Cornell and FDA inventory [S6, S7, S8].
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **ADI**: **Not limited** (equivalent to ADI not specified numerically) [S5].
  - **Evaluation year**: **1973** [S5].
- **IARC**:
  - No E471/mono- and diglycerides listing was found in IARC's current machine-readable classification list (Volumes 1-140 dataset) [S9, S10].
- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources [S1, S2, S5, S6, S8, S9].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology package (EFSA 2017)**:
  - Hydrolysis in GI tract to glycerol and fatty acids is expected.
  - No evidence of adverse effects in reviewed short-term, subchronic, chronic, reproductive/developmental studies.
  - No genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern identified.
  - No numerical ADI needed per EFSA conclusion [S1].
- **EFSA 2021 follow-up and impurity risk management**:
  - No indication of adverse effects in available animal/post-marketing data for the infant-focused assessment.
  - EFSA identified that impurity/toxic-element specifications needed tightening (to reduce exposure risk from contaminants rather than core glyceride chemistry itself) [S2].
- **Recent PubMed evidence (mechanistic/animal-heavy, mixed direction)**:
  - A 2024 mouse study reported metabolic and microbiota disturbances with common dietary emulsifiers, including MDG-related signals [S11].
  - A 2018 mouse study on glycerol monolaurate (GML; a monoacylglycerol, not the full E471 mixture) reported metabolic syndrome and dysbiosis at tested doses [S12].
  - A 2022 mouse study on glycerol monodecanoate (another monoacylglycerol) reported improved insulin sensitivity/inflammation markers at tested doses, showing inconsistent monoacylglycerol findings across models [S13].
  - Relevance caveat: these monoacylglycerol animal studies are informative for mechanism hypotheses but are not direct replacements for E471 regulatory dossier evidence [S11, S12, S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population (EFSA 2017 perspective)**: Mean contribution of E471 to recommended daily fat intake was reported at about **0.8-3.5%** in refined estimates [S1].
- **US intake estimate (published FDA-affiliated exposure assessment)**: Mean exposure for mono-/diglycerides was about **80 mg/kg bw/day** in analyzed datasets; authors did not identify safety concerns at then-current use levels [S14].
- **Infants (<16 weeks, EFSA 2021)**:
  - Refined scenario (mean reported use levels): about **418 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **543 mg/kg bw/day (high)**.
  - Regulatory max-level scenarios were higher (up to roughly 800-1300 mg/kg bw/day depending on category).
  - EFSA noted infant-formula exposure was of the same order as mono-/di-acylglycerol exposure from breast milk [S2].
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI; JECFA: ADI not limited, so classical numerical ADI exceedance is not directly applicable [S1, S5].
  - Main quantitative concern in EFSA follow-up was potential exposure to **impurities/contaminants** if specs are not sufficiently strict [S2].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - Formula-fed infants/high consumers and populations with cumulative contaminant exposure concern (lead/arsenic/cadmium/mercury, 3-MCPD, glycidyl esters, erucic acid) [S2].

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA and JECFA both did not set numerical ADIs ("no need for numerical ADI" / "ADI not limited"); EFSA did not identify core toxicological hazards for authorized uses [S1, S5].
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA 2021 highlighted non-trivial impurity specification issues and recent mechanistic animal literature raises microbiome/metabolic hypotheses, so "no credible evidence of harm at all" is too strong [S2, S11, S12].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Consistent continued approval (EU/FDA/JECFA), GRAS/cGMP framework, no major toxicology red flags at authorized uses, and no IARC listing identified [S1, S2, S5, S6, S8, S9].
  - Evidence against this tier: Residual uncertainty from impurity-driven risk and heterogeneous animal mechanistic findings [S2, S11, S12, S13].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA called for lowering impurity limits and showed that impurity assumptions could materially affect margins/exceedance concerns in some scenarios [S2].
  - Evidence against this tier: Regulatory bodies did not withdraw approval or set restrictive low ADI for E471 itself; overall conclusions remained "no safety concern" at reported/authorized use conditions [S1, S2, S5, S6].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Limited; mainly hypothetical if impurity controls failed broadly [S2].
  - Evidence against this tier: No major bans identified, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for E471 itself found, and no EFSA/JECFA conclusion of inability to confirm safety for authorized uses [S1, S2, S5, S9].

2. **Rationale**
- Weight-of-evidence supports continued approval with low concern for the core additive at current authorized uses.
- The main non-zero risk signal is **specification/contaminant control**, plus limited translational relevance of newer animal microbiome studies.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**.
