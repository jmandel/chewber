# E902 (Candelilla wax) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E902 (INS 902)
- **Name:** Candelilla wax
- **CAS number(s):** 8006-44-8
- **Chemical class:** Natural plant wax; mixture mainly of long-chain hydrocarbons, with smaller fractions of esters and free acids.
- **Common synonyms:** INS 902, Candelilla wax
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Natural** (obtained from the candelilla plant).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Forms a hydrophobic surface film and reduces tackiness/friction; used as a glazing/surface-finishing wax and lubricant.
- **Common food categories (documented):** chewing gum and hard candy (US CFR listing), plus broader glazing/texturizing roles in international additive classification.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest identified EFSA scientific opinion:** *Scientific Opinion on the re-evaluation of candelilla wax (E 902) as a food additive* (2012), EFSA Journal article ID 2946.
  - **ADI:** EFSA conclusion states there is **no need for a numerical ADI** (equivalent to ADI not specified in practice).
  - **Key conclusion:** EFSA concluded there was **no safety concern at reported uses and use levels**.
  - **Approval status:** **Approved/authorized** (re-evaluated as an existing permitted additive).
  - **Access note:** The direct EFSA Journal page redirects to Wiley and returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) from this environment; data above were taken from EFSA-hosted search snippets and the EFSA Journal entry metadata.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Status:** **GRAS** as a direct human food ingredient.
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1976**.
  - **Conditions of use (key points):**
    - No limitation other than current good manufacturing practice.
    - Functional use as lubricant and surface-finishing agent.
    - Food uses explicitly include chewing gum and hard candy under GMP.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **Record:** WHO JECFA chemical record for candelilla wax (INS 902).
  - **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED**.
  - **Last evaluation year listed:** **2005**.
  - **Comment:** “No safety concern at the predicted dietary exposure (less than 650 mg/person per day).”
- **IARC:**
  - No candelilla wax listing found in the current IARC list-of-classifications data file checked.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in the sources checked.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology conclusions:**
  - EFSA (2012) concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels and no need for a numerical ADI.
  - JECFA (2005 listing) set ADI as not specified and reported no safety concern at predicted exposure (<650 mg/person/day).
- **Animal studies / mechanistic toxicology:**
  - In the sources accessible in this run, detailed individual animal-study tables from EFSA/JECFA monographs were not fully retrievable from primary full text due access limitations (EFSA page challenge). Regulatory conclusions are available and consistent.
- **Recent PubMed evidence (mostly formulation/performance, not classical hazard testing):**
  - 2025 (J Food Sci): candelilla-wax oleogel showed improved oxidative stability during frying/storage; in vitro digestion showed altered lipolysis kinetics.
  - 2025 (J Sci Food Agric): among multiple wax oleogels, candelilla-wax-containing systems reduced formation of oxidation products during repeated frying.
  - 2021 (Food Res Int): in vitro digestion of nanostructured lipid carriers indicated candelilla wax itself was not digested by lipase in that model; study focus was delivery behavior, not chronic toxicity.
- **Epidemiological data:**
  - No robust candelilla-wax-specific epidemiological safety signal identified in the PubMed queries performed.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Reference intake statement:** JECFA comment indicates predicted exposure below 650 mg/person/day in its assessment context.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA and JECFA both reported “no safety concern” conclusions with no numerical ADI required/specified.
  - Based on available evidence, exceedance concern appears **low** at currently authorized use patterns.
- **Potentially higher-exposure groups:**
  - Frequent consumers of confectionery/coated products and chewing gum users, especially children by body weight.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Natural-origin wax; EFSA and JECFA both report no safety concern and no numerical ADI requirement.
  - Evidence against: Evidence base is heavily regulatory and older; limited modern human/epidemiological safety datasets specific to E902.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Clear EU/US authorization, GRAS status with GMP conditions, and concordant EFSA/JECFA “no safety concern” outcomes.
  - Evidence against: Some uncertainty from limited contemporary toxicology publications specifically designed as hazard studies for E902.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Could be argued only on data-modernity grounds (older core evaluations).
  - Evidence against: No regulatory downgrades, no ADI tightening, no credible signal of harm at typical use levels.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None found (no ban pattern, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 signal for this substance).
  - Evidence against: Regulatory consensus is permissive; no major corroborating high-risk indicators.

2. **Rationale**
- The weight of evidence is regulatory and consistent across EFSA, FDA/eCFR, and JECFA: candelilla wax remains permitted, with no numerical ADI required/specified and no identified safety concern at intended uses. The main residual uncertainty is the relative scarcity of recent dedicated toxicology/epidemiology studies specific to E902.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
