# E280 (Propionic acid) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E280 (INS 280) [S6]
- **Name:** Propionic acid (IUPAC: propanoic acid) [S4][S16]
- **CAS number:** 79-09-4 [S4][S6][S16]
- **Chemical class:** short-chain aliphatic monocarboxylic acid [S16]
- **Common synonyms:** propanoic acid, methylacetic acid, ethylformic acid [S16]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** occurs naturally (microbial fermentation products), and as a food additive is produced by chemical synthesis or bacterial fermentation [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological function:** preservative/antimicrobial. US CFR explicitly lists propionic acid as an antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent under GMP use. [S4]
- **Mechanism of action:** weak-acid preservative behavior (inference): undissociated acid can inhibit microbial growth (especially molds) by acid stress and intracellular pH effects.
- **Common food categories:** generally permitted foods at GMP in US for propionic acid itself; in practice, propionate preservatives are commonly used in bakery and some cheese/processed-cheese systems (regulatory and study context around sodium/calcium propionate). [S4][S10][S13]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest core re-evaluation for E280/E281/E282/E283:** EFSA Journal opinion with DOI `10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3779` (2014). [S1][S2]
- **ADI conclusion:** EFSA concluded a numerical ADI was not considered necessary for propionic acid and its salts, while noting local gastric effects at high concentrations and concluding no safety concern at reported uses/use levels. [S1]
- **Follow-up:** 2016 EFSA opinion on extension of sodium propionate (E281) use did not change the main 2014 safety position for the group. [S3]
- **Approval status:** approved/authorized in EU food additive framework (with EFSA no-safety-concern conclusion at reported uses). [S1][S2]

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** affirmed GRAS as a direct human food ingredient. [S4]
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1081 (Propionic acid)**. [S4]
- **Conditions of use:** no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice; listed functional uses include antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent. [S4]

### JECFA / WHO
- **Database entry:** PROPIONIC ACID (INS 280; CAS 79-09-4). [S6]
- **ADI:** **NOT LIMITED (1973)** (group ADI for propionic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium salts), maintained at 49th meeting. [S6][S7]
- **Evaluation year shown:** 1997 (meeting 49 entry, with maintenance statement). [S6]

### IARC
- **Classification:** no propionic acid entry found in the current IARC Monographs classification dataset (last update shown: 2026-02-25). Therefore no IARC Group 1/2A/2B/3 assignment identified for propionic acid in that list. [S8][S9]

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified in this research pass (EU authorized; US GRAS; JECFA not-limited group ADI context). [S1][S4][S6]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology baseline
- EFSA (2014) and JECFA (group ADI not limited, maintained) support low concern at approved use conditions. [S1][S6]

### Human data
- A randomized crossover human study (PMID: 34312159) reported that 1500 mg calcium propionate acutely increased counterregulatory hormones and endogenous glucose production markers, with authors proposing potential metabolic-disruptor relevance. [S11]

### Experimental mechanistic signals
- Drosophila model study (PMID: 36253933) reported concentration-dependent oxidative stress/gut damage/DNA damage endpoints at higher tested concentrations. [S12]
- Human lymphocyte in vitro study (PMID: 39262268) reported cytotoxic/genotoxic and oxidative-stress signals at tested concentrations. [S13]
- Zebrafish embryo model (PMID: 35753272) reported circadian/neurodevelopmental and glucose-metabolism related perturbations. [S14]

### Epidemiology
- NutriNet-Sante prospective cohort paper (PMID: 41501013) found association between several preservatives (including calcium propionate among listed substances) and incident type 2 diabetes; this is observational and not by itself causal proof. [S15]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory ADI context:** EFSA did not set a numerical ADI for this group; JECFA lists group ADI as not limited. This makes a classic ADI exceedance ratio unavailable. [S1][S6]
- **Typical intake quantification:** this source set did not provide a single harmonized current population mg/kg bw/day estimate for E280 alone.
- **Signal-oriented exposure evidence:** a controlled human challenge used 1500 mg calcium propionate acutely (experimental, not necessarily representative habitual intake). [S11]
- **Potentially vulnerable groups (uncertainty-based):** high consumers of preserved/ultra-processed foods and people with metabolic vulnerability may warrant attention in future risk characterization, given emerging metabolic association signals. [S11][S15]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: endogenous/naturally occurring short-chain fatty acid context; long-standing regulatory acceptance; EFSA no safety concern at reported uses; JECFA group ADI not limited. [S1][S6]
  - Evidence against this tier: newer human/mechanistic studies report metabolic and genotoxicity-related signals under some conditions. [S11][S12][S13][S14][S15]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: US GRAS with GMP conditions; no major regulatory withdrawal; established historical safety assessments. [S4][S1][S6]
  - Evidence against this tier: accumulating non-regulatory studies suggest potentially meaningful metabolic and genotoxic concerns, not just trivial high-dose artifacts in all cases. [S11][S12][S13][S15]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: approved status but with caveat-generating evidence (human acute metabolic effects, observational association with diabetes, and multiple model-system toxicity signals). [S11][S12][S13][S14][S15]
  - Evidence against this tier: authoritative risk assessors (EFSA/JECFA) have not moved to restriction based on available regulatory dossiers. [S1][S6]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: none strong in this dataset (no major-jurisdiction ban; no IARC Group 2A/2B assignment identified for propionic acid). [S8][S9]
  - Evidence against this tier: continued authorization in EU/US and JECFA not-limited framework are inconsistent with high-risk classification at current approved uses. [S1][S4][S6]

### 2. Rationale
Regulatory agencies still treat propionic acid as low concern at approved use levels, but post-regulatory literature includes enough metabolic and mechanistic red flags to move beyond a purely low-concern framing. The main uncertainty is external validity of experimental doses/models versus real-world intake distributions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
