# E953 (Isomalt) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E953 / INS 953.
- **Name:** Isomalt.
- **CAS number(s):** 64519-82-0.
- **Chemical class:** Sugar alcohol (polyol); hydrogenated isomaltulose mixture (1,1-GPM + 1,6-GPS).
- **Common synonyms:** Hydrogenated isomaltulose, Palatinit, Isomaltitol, E953.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (industrial hydrogenation of isomaltulose derived from sucrose).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Low-digestible polyol sweetener and bulking agent; provides sweetness with reduced glycemic impact and lower caloric value than sucrose.
- **Common food functions/categories:** Sweetener, bulking agent, glazing agent, anticaking aid (WHO JECFA functional classes).
- **Typical products:** Sugar-free confectionery, chewing gums, reduced-sugar baked goods, and tabletop/processed low-sugar foods.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (JECFA):**
  - No carcinogenicity was demonstrated in lifetime rodent studies at tested dietary levels in the JECFA monograph.
  - Chronic dog/rat studies reported tolerance at tested levels, with gastrointestinal effects (loose/liquid stools) as the principal high-dose finding.
  - JECFA notes human laxative effects at high intakes.
- **Human tolerance studies (PubMed):**
  - Children consuming 25 g/day isomalt sweets had more mild GI symptoms and laxative-type effects versus sucrose, but most effects were mild (PMID: 12064338; 12122552).
  - In healthy adults, 30 g/day over 4 weeks increased stool frequency modestly without major metabolic harm signals (PMID: 16197583).
  - 30 g/day data also suggested microbiota shifts (notably more bifidobacteria) without clear harmful colonic biomarker shifts (PMID: 16441915).
- **Recent literature context (PubMed):**
  - More recent indexed literature is mainly reviews or formulation/technology papers; no strong new systemic toxicity signal specific to Isomalt was identified in the recent records screened.

## Exposure Assessment
- **ADI-based assessment:** Not numerical, because JECFA ADI is “not specified” and no finalized EFSA ADI was identified in the ongoing EFSA re-evaluation record.
- **Dose-response tolerability signal:** Human data consistently indicate GI/laxative effects as the main limiting effect at higher bolus intakes (e.g., around 20-30 g/day in JECFA monograph context; mild dose-related symptoms in clinical studies).
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not quantifiable in standard mg/kg bw/day terms from current EFSA/JECFA ADI outputs for this additive.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Children and high consumers of sugar-free confectionery/polyol-rich products; people with IBS/FODMAP sensitivity may be more likely to experience GI symptoms.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA ADI “not specified”; no carcinogenic signal in JECFA animal data; no IARC listing.
  - Evidence arguing against: Reproducible GI/laxative effects occur at higher intakes, including in children; EFSA re-evaluation is still ongoing.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Authorized in EU, recognized in US CFR contexts, JECFA ADI not specified, and no serious systemic toxicity signal in reviewed evidence.
  - Evidence arguing against: GI intolerance can occur at practical consumer intake levels in some groups.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Ongoing EFSA re-evaluation without final output adds some uncertainty; GI symptoms are clearly dose-related and relevant for frequent high consumers.
  - Evidence arguing against: No major carcinogenic/genotoxic/regulatory red-flag signal and no major bans identified.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: No strong evidence supporting high-risk classification from reviewed data.
  - Evidence arguing against: No IARC classification, no major bans, and regulatory systems continue to allow use.

### 2. Rationale
The dominant risk pattern is **dose-dependent gastrointestinal intolerance**, not severe systemic toxicity. Regulatory evidence (especially JECFA ADI not specified and absence of carcinogenic signal) supports low overall hazard at normal use levels, but “risk_free” is too strong because laxative/GI effects are consistent and clinically relevant at higher intakes.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
