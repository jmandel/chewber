# E220 (Sulfur dioxide) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E220
- **Name:** Sulfur dioxide
- **CAS number:** 7446-09-5
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfur oxide; sulfiting/preserving agent
- **Common synonyms:** sulfur dioxide, sulphur dioxide, sulfurous anhydride, SO2, INS 220, E220
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercial food-additive use is primarily **synthetic/industrial**; sulfur dioxide/sulfite can also occur naturally in some fermentation contexts.

**Primary identity evidence:**
- WHO/JECFA chemical entry for sulfur dioxide lists **INS 220** and **CAS 7446-09-5**.
- PubChem compound CID 1119 confirms name/synonyms/CAS linkage.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative (antimicrobial) and antioxidant/reducing agent.
- **Other functions:** Flour treatment agent.
- **Mechanism:** In food matrices, sulfur dioxide/sulfite species can react with aldehydes/ketones and other reactive compounds, helping limit oxidative browning and microbial spoilage.
- **Common food categories:** wine and cider, dried fruits, some processed potato/grape products, crustaceans, and other processed foods where sulfite preservation is permitted by regulation.

**Function evidence:**
- WHO/JECFA entry functional classes: **ANTIOXIDANT, FLOUR_TREATMENT_AGENT, PRESERVATIVE**.
- FDA "Substances Added to Food" search result lists technical effects including antimicrobial/antioxidant/reducing uses.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest follow-up evaluation year:** **2022** (follow-up to 2016 re-evaluation).
- **2016 status:** EFSA had set a **temporary group ADI of 0.7 mg SO2 equivalents/kg bw/day** and already noted exceedance in high consumers (notably children, and some adults).
- **2022 follow-up:** EFSA concluded the toxicity database remained inadequate to maintain an ADI, so it **withdrew the temporary ADI** and used an MOE approach.
- **Key technical outputs in 2022 abstract:** reference point **BMDL = 38 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day**, assessment factor 80, with MOE results indicating concern in high-end exposure scenarios.
- **Interpretation for current status:** additive remains authorized in the EU framework but with strong caveats/uncertainty, so practical status is **restricted/caveated approval**.

### FDA/CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** `21 CFR 182.3862` places sulfur dioxide under **Substances Generally Recognized as Safe**.
- **Conditions/restrictions:** GRAS when used per GMP, **except** not used:
  - in meats,
  - in foods recognized as a source of vitamin B1,
  - on fruits/vegetables intended to be served or sold raw/fresh.
- **Additional label context:** `21 CFR 101.100(a)(4)` defines detectable sulfiting agent as **>=10 ppm (mg/kg)** for ingredient declaration purposes.

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **0-0.7 mg/kg bw** (group context for sulfur dioxide/sulfites).
- **Last evaluation year on sulfur dioxide entry:** **1998** (Meeting 49).

### IARC
- **Classification:** **Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity in humans) for sulfur dioxide; sulfites also listed Group 3 in the same historical volume context.

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No blanket ban in EU/US for E220.
- Material targeted prohibitions/restrictions exist (US CFR restrictions above; labeling thresholds for sulfiting agents).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and experimental data
- **Neurotoxicity signal (animal):** rat study (PMID: 21511899) reported hippocampal neuron loss after chronic sulfite exposure.
- **Genotoxicity signal at high dose (animal):** mouse comet/micronucleus study (PMID: 21185391) reported increased damage at very high oral doses.
- **Gastric irritation uncertainty:** 2016 rat work (PMID: 27401986) did **not** replicate older severe gastric-lesion findings, highlighting endpoint uncertainty.
- **Cell toxicity/mechanistic signal:** in vitro human fibroblast study (PMID: 33957602) reported apoptosis/ROS/autophagy effects with sodium metabisulfite exposure.

### Human/epidemiological evidence
- **Prospective cohort signal:** NutriNet-Sante (PMID: 41500678, 2026) reported positive associations between higher intake of several preservatives (including total sulfites/potassium metabisulfite) and incident cancer outcomes. This is observational and not proof of causality.
- **Hypersensitivity/airway concern:** controlled airway challenge literature indicates sulfite/metabisulfite can provoke bronchoconstriction in susceptible asthmatics (e.g., PMID: 1318295).

### Mechanistic concerns
- Oxidative stress/cell death pathways in vitro and airway reactivity in susceptible individuals remain the most consistent biologically plausible concern domains.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2016 context:** average consumers generally below the temporary ADI, but high consumers (especially children, plus some adults) could exceed it.
- **EFSA 2022 follow-up context:** after ADI withdrawal and MOE-based assessment, high-percentile exposure scenarios yielded MOEs below EFSA's target margin for multiple groups.
- **At-risk/vulnerable populations:**
  - high consumers of sulfite-containing foods (especially children),
  - sulfite-sensitive individuals, particularly some people with asthma,
  - potentially persons with impaired sulfite-oxidation capacity (rare metabolic susceptibility context).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting placement:** long history of food use; still legally allowed in major jurisdictions; IARC Group 3 (no human carcinogen classification).
- **Evidence against placement:** EFSA withdrew temporary ADI in 2022 and identified concern in high-end exposure scenarios; reproducible sensitivity issues exist for a subset of individuals.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting placement:** US CFR GRAS status with explicit GMP conditions; JECFA numeric ADI exists (0-0.7 mg/kg bw); IARC Group 3.
- **Evidence against placement:** EFSA follow-up judged data gaps unresolved and moved away from ADI to MOE; exposure concerns persist for high consumers.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting placement:** additive remains approved but with important caveats; prior ADI exceedance concerns in children/high consumers; mechanistic and animal studies show plausible adverse effects at higher doses; clinical sensitivity exists in susceptible populations.
- **Evidence against placement:** strongest hazard findings are often from high-dose or non-dietary-equivalent settings; no IARC Group 2/1 carcinogenic classification; no broad international ban.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting placement:** EFSA inability to maintain an ADI plus MOE concern under high-exposure scenarios is a substantial regulatory warning signal.
- **Evidence against placement:** no major-jurisdiction blanket ban, and core global frameworks (FDA/JECFA) still permit use with controls.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence indicates a **regulated additive with non-trivial caveats**, not a clearly low-concern ingredient. Key drivers are: EFSA's ADI withdrawal/data-gap persistence, high-end exposure concern, and known susceptible subgroups (notably sulfite-sensitive asthmatics). Counterbalancing factors are continued legal authorization, JECFA/FDA permissive positions under conditions, and lack of IARC carcinogenic upgrade.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
