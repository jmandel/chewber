# E472f (Mixed acetic and tartaric acid esters) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E472f (INS 472f) [S1, S2, S5].
- **Chemical class**: Mixed acetic and tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids (glycerol ester emulsifier mixture) [S1, S2, S5].
- **Common synonyms**: Mixed acetic and tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids; tartaric, acetic and fatty acid esters of glycerol, mixed; INS 472f [S1, S2, S5].
- **CAS number(s)**: No definitive CAS number was provided in the primary EFSA/JECFA/eCFR records reviewed; JECFA entries used here show `CAS_NO: null` [S5, S7].
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured ester mixture from edible glycerides/fatty acids reacted with tartaric/acetic acid derivatives) [S1, S2, S8].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Amphiphilic glycerol-fatty-acid esters lower oil-water interfacial tension and stabilize emulsions.
- **Primary function class**: Emulsifier [S1, S2].
- **Common food categories**: Processed foods requiring fat-water stabilization (including bakery-type systems for related E472 emulsifiers) [S3].

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Latest EFSA scientific opinion located**: 2025 specifications opinion for E472a,b,d,e,f (PubMed index of EFSA doi `10.2903/j.efsa.2025.9602`) [S2].
  - **Status**: Approved/authorized in the EU additive framework; 2025 work addressed specification amendments rather than withdrawal [S2].
  - **ADI**:
    - 2020 EFSA re-evaluation (E472a-f): group ADI for E472d/e/f of **240 mg/kg bw/day expressed as tartaric acid** [S1].
    - 2025 EFSA specifications opinion: exposure for E472f assessed against **600 mg/kg bw/day** (additive basis) and found below ADI across assessed populations/scenarios [S2].
  - **Key conclusions**:
    - 2020: no adverse effects relevant for humans identified from the E472a-f toxicology database; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels [S1].
    - 2025: for E472f, the Panel said technical data were insufficient to support the proposed spec amendment, but dietary exposure estimates remained below ADI [S2].

- **FDA / eCFR (US)**:
  - **Exact additive name search in current Title 21 XML**: no explicit listing found for "mixed acetic and tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides" / E472f [S3].
  - **Closest related CFR entries**:
    - **21 CFR 184.1101**: diacetyl tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides (DATEM) affirmed GRAS as direct food ingredient, with GMP conditions and specified food categories [S3].
    - **21 CFR 582.4101**: DATEM in animal feed context as GRAS under good manufacturing or feeding practice [S3].
  - **Conditions of use (related DATEM entry)**: GMP-limited use as emulsifier/emulsifier salt and flavoring adjuvant in categories including baked goods and mixes, nonalcoholic beverages, confections/frostings, dairy analogs, and fats/oils [S3].

- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **Additive-specific entry found**: `TARTARIC, ACETIC and FATTY ACID ESTERS of GLYCEROL, mixed` (Chemical ID 3714) [S5].
  - **ADI**: **WITHDRAWN** [S5].
  - **Last evaluation year**: **2001** [S5].
  - **JECFA comment on withdrawal**: prior "not limited" ADI was withdrawn after specifications were merged with the DATEM entry [S5].
  - **Related merged entry**: DATEM (`DIACETYLTARTARIC and FATTY ACID ESTERS of GLYCEROL`, ID 1126) shows ADI **0-50 mg/kg bw**, evaluation year 2003 [S6].

- **IARC**:
  - No IARC Monographs classification for E472f or its specific chemical name was identified in the current IARC classifications dataset/app bundle [S8, S9].

- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified in consulted EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Direct regulatory toxicology**:
  - EFSA 2020 re-evaluation for E472a-f: no adverse effects relevant for humans; ADI framework established for E472d/e/f; no safety concern at reported use levels [S1].
  - EFSA 2025 specifications opinion: E472f exposure below ADI in assessed scenarios; specification-amendment support considered insufficient for E472f due missing technical data [S2].

- **Human epidemiology (indirect; emulsifier-class signals)**:
  - Prospective NutriNet-Sante cohort (2023) reported associations between higher intake of emulsifier groups including E471/E472 and cardiovascular outcomes [S10].
  - NutriNet-Sante cancer analysis (2024) reported associations between some emulsifier intakes (notably E471 and several subclasses) and cancer outcomes [S11].

- **Animal/mechanistic evidence (indirect)**:
  - 2024 mouse study reported metabolic and gut-microbiome disruption with several common emulsifiers, including mono-/diglycerides as a class, but not E472f-isolated testing [S12].

- **Evidence gap**:
  - Direct recent toxicology/clinical studies isolating E472f specifically were not identified in targeted PubMed searches [S4, S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure conclusion (2025)**: for E472f, estimated dietary exposure across assessed population groups/scenarios was below the ADI of 600 mg/kg bw/day [S2].
- **EFSA risk interpretation (2020 + 2025)**: no safety concern at reported uses/use levels in the 2020 re-evaluation; 2025 update did not indicate ADI exceedance for E472f [S1, S2].
- **ADI exceedance risk**: low under assessed regulatory-use scenarios; residual uncertainty remains from limited additive-specific human data.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**: high-consumption children are generally the most relevant subgroup for emulsifier exposure assessment, though no E472f-specific exceedance signal was reported in the consulted EFSA abstracts [S1, S2].

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA did not identify safety concern at reported uses; 2025 exposure assessment remained below ADI [S1, S2].
  - Evidence arguing against placement: numerical ADI exists (not "ADI not specified"), JECFA additive-specific ADI is withdrawn, and direct E472f-specific modern toxicology is limited [S5, S13].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA approval context with clear ADI framework and below-ADI exposure estimates; no IARC classification; no major-jurisdiction ban identified [S1, S2, S8, S9].
  - Evidence arguing against placement: U.S. Title 21 lacks an explicit E472f listing by name; JECFA additive-specific entry is withdrawn; concern signals exist in broader emulsifier literature [S3, S5, S10, S11, S12].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting placement: credible cohort and animal studies raise class-level concern signals for some emulsifiers (cardiometabolic/cancer/microbiome endpoints), and JECFA's additive-specific status is withdrawn [S5, S10, S11, S12].
  - Evidence arguing against placement: these studies are not E472f-specific causal demonstrations; EFSA's additive-specific regulatory conclusions remain reassuring at reported use levels [S1, S2].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting placement: limited; no direct major-jurisdiction ban and no IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification located for E472f.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: EFSA currently retains an ADI-based safety conclusion for reported uses, and available regulatory actions do not indicate high hazard status [S1, S2].

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest additive-specific evidence is EFSA's 2020 re-evaluation plus the 2025 follow-up on specifications/exposure, both of which are broadly reassuring for current uses.
- Uncertainty remains because JECFA's additive-specific entry (mixed tartaric/acetic esters) is withdrawn and recent human data are mostly emulsifier-class associations rather than E472f-specific evidence.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**.
