# E501 (Potassium carbonates) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E501 (E501(i), E501(ii))
- **Name:** Potassium carbonates
- **Primary components:**
  - **E501(i):** Potassium carbonate (K2CO3)
  - **E501(ii):** Potassium hydrogen carbonate / potassium bicarbonate (KHCO3)
- **CAS numbers:**
  - **584-08-7** (potassium carbonate)
  - **298-14-6** (potassium hydrogen carbonate)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic alkaline potassium salts of carbonic acid
- **Common synonyms:** Dipotassium carbonate, potassium bicarbonate, acid potassium carbonate, potassium acid carbonate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic/manufactured** for food use (CFR manufacturing routes list chemical production processes) [7][8].

## Function in Food
- **Main technological roles:** acidity regulator / pH control, leavening/raising support (for bicarbonate), processing aid, and nutrient supplement functions in U.S. GMP conditions [7][8][9][10].
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Carbonate/bicarbonate neutralize acids and buffer pH.
  - Bicarbonate can release CO2 in acid/heat systems, supporting leavening.
- **Common food categories (functional-use based):** bakery/leavened products, foods requiring pH adjustment, and processed foods where formulation aids are used [7][8][9][10].

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal studies
1. **Bone (2025; PMID 39674389)** - acidotic mice model comparing bicarbonate salts:
   - Potassium bicarbonate did not reduce bone dissolution under those acidotic experimental conditions.
   - This is a disease-model animal study, not a direct food-additive exposure study at typical dietary additive levels [14].

### Human data
2. **J Bone Miner Res (2015; PMID 25990255)** - randomized dose-finding trial in older adults using potassium bicarbonate for 3 months:
   - Reported favorable bone-turnover marker changes and lower urinary calcium versus placebo.
   - Abstract does not report a major acute safety signal at tested doses [16].

3. **Clin Nutr (2019; PMID 29739680)** - controlled human bed-rest alkalinization study with potassium bicarbonate:
   - Reported metabolic/biochemical effects (including glutathione/protein-balance changes).
   - Not an E501 food-additive exposure design, but relevant to ingestion tolerance context [15].

### Mechanistic and clinical concern context
4. **NIH ODS Potassium Fact Sheet (Health Professional):**
   - No UL set for potassium in healthy people; kidneys generally excrete excess.
   - In people with impaired urinary potassium excretion (e.g., chronic kidney disease) or on specific drugs (ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics), hyperkalemia can occur even below AI intake levels [17].

### Evidence gap
- Recent PubMed literature contains little direct modern toxicology specifically on **E501 as a food additive**; much newer literature concerns potassium bicarbonate as a supplement/intervention or non-food technical use [13][14][15][16].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Additive-specific intake datasets:** No robust, recent E501-specific intake quantification was identified in the sources reviewed.
- **Potassium background exposure context (U.S.):** NIH ODS reports average daily potassium intake from foods in adults is ~3,016 mg/day (men) and ~2,320 mg/day (women) in NHANES 2013-2014 [17].
- **ADI exceedance risk for E501:**
  - **JECFA ADI is "NOT LIMITED"**, so conventional ADI exceedance framing is less applicable for the additive itself [9][10].
  - Risk is more likely tied to **total potassium handling capacity** in vulnerable populations than to a low numeric additive ADI threshold [17].
- **Vulnerable populations:** people with CKD or reduced renal potassium excretion, and people on medications that reduce potassium excretion [17].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:** JECFA ADI is "NOT LIMITED" for both E501 components; long-standing U.S. GRAS status under GMP [7][8][9][10].
- **Evidence against:** vulnerable subgroups can develop hyperkalemia from potassium load (contextual risk from total intake/handling), and no modern dedicated EFSA E501 re-evaluation opinion was identified in accessible sources [2][4][17].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:** Approved/GRAS with GMP controls; no major carcinogenic classification found; no major bans identified; concerns are mainly at high systemic potassium burden or susceptible physiology [7][8][11][12][17].
- **Evidence against:** direct contemporary E501 food-additive toxicology/exposure datasets are limited.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:** absence of a readily accessible modern EFSA E501 re-evaluation plus biologically plausible potassium-related risk in renal-vulnerable groups could justify caution.
- **Evidence against:** current major regulators still permit use; JECFA ADI is not limited; strong direct harm signal at normal food-additive exposure was not identified.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:** none substantial in reviewed sources.
- **Evidence against:** no major bans; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification identified; no regulator conclusion of general unsafe status in reviewed material [7][8][9][10][11][12].

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence supports a **low overall risk in the general population under current authorized/GMP use**, but not a fully "risk_free" classification because clinically meaningful potassium-handling vulnerability exists in specific populations and direct modern E501-specific exposure/safety datasets are limited.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
