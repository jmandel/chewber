# E343 (Magnesium phosphates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E343
- **Name:** Magnesium phosphates (includes mono-, di-, and tribasic salts)
- **CAS number(s):**
  - Monomagnesium phosphate (magnesium dihydrogen phosphate): **13092-66-5**
  - Magnesium phosphate, dibasic (magnesium hydrogen phosphate): **7782-75-4** (CFR identity text; some chemistry databases also list **7757-86-0**)
  - Magnesium phosphate, tribasic (trimagnesium phosphate): **7757-87-1**
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic phosphate salts of magnesium
- **Common synonyms:** Monomagnesium phosphate, magnesium hydrogen phosphate, trimagnesium phosphate, magnesium phosphate (di- and tribasic)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Occurs naturally in some mineral forms, but food-grade material is typically manufactured (precipitation/neutralization processes).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Acts as a **buffer/pH control agent** by phosphate equilibrium chemistry.
  - Acts as a **stabilizer/sequestrant** via cation binding and ionic-strength effects.
  - Phosphate salts in general are used as **raising agents, emulsifying salts, anti-caking agents, and humectants** depending on matrix.
- **Common food categories:**
  - Processed cheese and dairy preparations
  - Bakery and cereal-based products (including leavened systems)
  - Meat products and some processed composite foods
  - Specialized nutrition products, including infant formula contexts under applicable law

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Status:** Approved in the EU as part of authorized phosphate additives; re-evaluated.
  - **Latest re-evaluation/opinion:** 2019 (EFSA Journal, phosphates E338–341, E343, E450–452).
  - **ADI:** **40 mg/kg bw/day**, expressed as **phosphorus** (group ADI).
  - **Key conclusions:** EFSA reduced the previous group value (from 70 mg/kg bw/day as phosphorus), noted uncertainty/data gaps, and concluded that exposure can exceed ADI in high consumers, especially younger populations.
- **FDA / CFR (US):**
  - **GRAS status:** Affirmed GRAS.
  - **Citations:**
    - **21 CFR 184.1434** (direct human food ingredient): no limitation other than current GMP; intended functions include nutrient supplement and pH control agent; may be used in infant formula under section 412 requirements.
    - **21 CFR 582.5434** (animal food): generally recognized as safe when used according to good manufacturing or feeding practice.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - WHO/JECFA monograph summary lists magnesium phosphate mono-/di-/tribasic under phosphate evaluations.
  - **Group MTDI:** **70 mg/kg bw/day as phosphorus** for phosphoric acid and phosphate salts (JECFA 1982 listing in WHO INCHEM summary).
  - Historical note in same source: monomagnesium phosphate had earlier “not limited” entry (1970), with later group framework applied.
- **IARC:**
  - No specific IARC monograph classification located for **magnesium phosphate** in IARC’s current classified-agents dataset.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified for E343 specifically in the reviewed sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Human intervention evidence:**
  - A randomized crossover meal study (healthy adults) comparing additive-phosphorus-rich vs low-phosphorus breakfast reported significantly higher postprandial serum phosphate, PTH, and FGF23, and higher urinary phosphorus excretion with additive phosphorus.
- **Mechanistic/clinical concern pattern (reviews):**
  - Recent reviews emphasize that inorganic phosphate additives are highly bioavailable and may increase phosphorus burden, with concern for CKD progression, vascular effects, and cardiovascular risk particularly in susceptible groups.
- **Regulatory hazard basis:**
  - EFSA’s 2019 group ADI derivation referenced nephrocalcinosis-related evidence and emphasized uncertainty due limited suitable toxicological studies for some phosphate salts.
- **Carcinogenicity evidence:**
  - No IARC classification specific to magnesium phosphates was identified.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reported that dietary exposure to phosphate additives can approach/exceed the 2019 group ADI in younger/high-intake groups.
- In EFSA summary data, toddlers showed among the highest estimates (mean and 95th percentile exposure levels), with exceedance at upper intake levels.
- Exposure contributors in young populations include phosphate-containing processed categories (e.g., processed cheeses and some cereal/bakery categories).
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants, toddlers, children with high processed-food intake; people with renal impairment (reduced phosphate handling) are a biologically plausible risk group.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: longstanding authorization in major jurisdictions; endogenous relevance of phosphate in human biology.
  - Evidence against: EFSA set a finite group ADI (not “ADI not specified”), reduced prior group value, and reported possible exceedance in children/high consumers.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: FDA GRAS under GMP; EFSA/JECFA continue to allow use within intake constraints; no IARC carcinogen classification found.
  - Evidence against: exposure exceedance risk in subgroups and renal/cardiovascular concern signals at elevated phosphorus burden weaken a purely “limited” classification.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: matches the prompt criteria directly (approved but caveats: reduced EFSA ADI and potential ADI exceedance in children/high consumers; credible human biomarker shifts with additive phosphorus).
  - Evidence against: no major regulatory ban and no direct carcinogenic classification; many concerns are dose/context dependent.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: none strong from reviewed regulatory sources.
  - Evidence against: no major-jurisdiction ban for E343, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B assignment specific to magnesium phosphate, and continued regulated authorization.

2. **Rationale**
- The dominant factors are: (a) continued regulatory approval, but (b) a stricter EFSA group ADI and documented potential exceedance in young/high-intake groups, plus (c) mechanistic and clinical concern around highly bioavailable inorganic phosphate load.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
