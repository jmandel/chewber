# E508 (Potassium chloride) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E508
- **Name:** Potassium chloride
- **CAS number:** 7447-40-7 [4]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic chloride salt (alkali metal chloride)
- **Common synonyms:** potassium chloride, sylvine, sylvite [5]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily mineral-derived (prepared from source minerals by fractional crystallization/flotation), then purified for food use [4]

## Function in Food
Potassium chloride is used mainly as a sodium-replacement salt and multi-function processing aid.

- **Mechanistic roles:** provides salty taste with less sodium, ionic strength control, nutrient potassium source, pH-control support, stabilizing/thickening support in some formulations [4][5]
- **Common food categories:** sodium-reduced table salts/seasonings, processed foods needing sodium reduction, and permitted use including infant formula under specific legal framework [4]
- **Regulatory functional classes:** flavour enhancer, gelling agent, yeast food (JECFA); flavor enhancer, flavoring agent, nutrient supplement, pH control agent, stabilizer/thickener (FDA CFR) [4][5]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved; EFSA re-evaluated potassium chloride (E508) in 2019 and concluded no safety concern at reported uses and use levels [2].
- **ADI / reference point:** EFSA noted prior ADI "not specified" by SCF/JECFA and used a **reference value of 40 mg chloride/kg bw/day** for risk characterization (not a newly established formal ADI) [2].
- **Evaluation year:** 2019 (opinion adopted 6 June 2019; publication July 2019) [1][2].
- **Key conclusions:** chloride exposure from additives can be high in children/adolescents at upper percentiles, but EFSA considered exceedance likely conservative and did not identify safety concern at reported uses [2].
- **Access note:** direct EFSA opinion URL was inaccessible in this environment (HTTP 403); data were verified via the indexed PubMed abstract of the EFSA opinion [1][2].

## Key Safety Evidence

1. **EFSA scientific re-evaluation (2019):** No safety concern at reported uses/levels; EFSA used a chloride reference value and highlighted higher upper-percentile exposures in younger groups [2].
2. **Randomized clinical trial in stroke patients (JAMA Cardiology, 2025; PMID 39908026):** a 75% NaCl/25% KCl salt substitute reduced recurrent stroke and death; hyperkalemia was not significantly different (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.74-1.38) [8].
3. **Randomized pilot trial in resistant hypertension (2026; PMID 41571229):** potassium-enriched salt substitute showed modest ambulatory BP reduction and reported no clinically relevant hyperkalemia in this small trial [9].
4. **Systematic review/network meta-analysis (2026; PMID 41578335):** moderate-potassium salt substitutes lowered BP and likely improved some outcomes; authors advised screening for kidney disease/hyperkalemia risk before recommendation [10].
5. **Targeted safety concern publication (Circulation, 2025; PMID 41183166):** specifically evaluates hyperkalemia risk among patients with concomitant RAAS blockade in SSaSS context, reinforcing caution in susceptible patients [11].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA additive-related chloride intake estimates:** mean **2-42 mg/kg bw/day**; 95th percentile **5-71 mg/kg bw/day** [2].
- **EFSA total dietary chloride estimates (all sources):** mean **32-45 mg/kg bw/day**; 95th percentile **54-79 mg/kg bw/day** [2].
- **Reference value context:** EFSA used **40 mg chloride/kg bw/day** as a reference value; upper-percentile intakes can exceed this in toddlers/children/adolescents [2].
- **Vulnerable populations:** people with impaired renal potassium handling (e.g., CKD) and those using medications that increase hyperkalemia risk (e.g., RAAS blockade) need additional caution [10][11].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:** longstanding approvals across EFSA/FDA/JECFA; JECFA ADI "not limited" [2][4][5].
- **Evidence against risk_free:** EFSA identified upper-percentile exposure scenarios above its reference value in younger groups; hyperkalemia vulnerability exists in specific clinical populations [2][10][11].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:** approved/GRAS status; EFSA found no safety concern at current use; recent RCTs and meta-analyses generally show favorable cardiovascular outcomes without population-level hyperkalemia signal [2][4][8][9][10].
- **Evidence against limited:** risk management caveats remain for renal impairment and concurrent RAAS blockade [10][11].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:** EFSA reference value exceedance at high-percentile exposure estimates in younger populations; emerging literature emphasizes subgroup hyperkalemia risk management [2][10][11].
- **Evidence against moderate:** no major regulator has restricted/withdrawn E508 at approved use; large trial data did not show overall hyperkalemia increase [2][8].

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:** clinically plausible severe hyperkalemia risk in vulnerable patients.
- **Evidence against high:** no major bans, no IARC carcinogenic classification, and current regulatory consensus remains permissive under GMP [4][6][7][8].

### 2. Rationale
The evidence supports broad safety for general use conditions, with clear regulatory acceptance and favorable large-trial outcomes. The principal safety caveat is not general-population toxicity but **susceptible-subgroup potassium handling risk** (renal impairment/RAAS-blocked patients).

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
