# E904 (Shellac) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E904.
- **Name**: Shellac (bleached shellac / wax-free bleached shellac in specifications).
- **CAS number(s)**: 9000-59-3 (JECFA CAS listing in EFSA comparison table).
- **Chemical class**: Low-molecular-weight natural resin; complex mixture of mono- and polyesters of hydroxyaliphatic acids and sesquiterpenoid acids.
- **Common synonyms**: Bleached shellac; white shellac; confectioner's glaze; resinous glaze; pure food glaze; natural glaze.
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Natural (purified/bleached lac resinous secretion from *Laccifer (Tachardia) lacca*).

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role**: Glazing/coating/surface-finishing agent.
- **Mechanism of action**: Applied as a thin coating (often sprayed from ethanol/alkaline solutions), then solvent evaporates and shellac remains as a protective film; further esterification/polymerization can occur after application.
- **Common food categories/contributors**: Cocoa/chocolate products and other confectionery are major contributors in EFSA exposure modeling; also used on fruits/vegetables/nuts and supplements in some authorized categories.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Approval status**: Approved food additive in the EU (E904; quantum satis in multiple categories), re-evaluated in 2024.
  - **ADI**: 4 mg/kg bw/day.
  - **Evaluation year**: 2024 (EFSA Journal e8897).
  - **Key conclusions**:
    - ADI 4 mg/kg bw/day derived from NOAEL 400 mg/kg bw/day (UF 100) for wax-free shellac produced by physical decolouring.
    - ADI is **temporary** for wax-free chemically bleached shellac pending identity/level data for organochlorine impurities.
    - ADI is not applicable to wax-containing shellac as food additive.
    - P95 exposure exceedances were observed in several age groups, but EFSA concluded low exceedance with conservative assumptions did not indicate a safety concern.
- **FDA / CFR (US)**:
  - **GRAS status**: No dedicated GRAS listing for shellac in Title 21 GRAS parts was identified in the eCFR shellac search results.
  - **CFR citations and conditions**:
    - **21 CFR 175.300**: Shellac listed as a permitted "natural fossil resin" component of resinous/polymeric food-contact coatings.
    - **21 CFR 175.105**: Adhesives may be used with functional barrier / trace-contact seam limitations and GMP constraints.
    - **21 CFR 175.380**: Food-contact coatings may include substances identified in 175.300(b)(3), with extractives limits.
    - **21 CFR 175.390**: Coatings may include substances with safe conditions in 175.300.
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **ADI**: NOT SPECIFIED.
  - **Last evaluation year**: 1992.
  - **JECFA comment**: Present uses (coating, glazing, surface-finishing externally applied to food) not of toxicological concern.
- **IARC**:
  - Shellac was **not found** in the IARC "Agents classified by the IARC Monographs" dataset (last_update string in dataset: 2026-02-25 CET).
- **Notable bans**:
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/eCFR/JECFA materials.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology (EFSA 2024)**:
  - Low acute oral toxicity.
  - No toxicologically relevant effects in subchronic rat studies up to 1600 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Genotoxicity database and in silico review did not indicate genotoxic concern for shellac components.
  - Chronic studies identified NOAELs at 452-569 mg/kg bw/day (chemical bleaching material) and 500 mg/kg bw/day (physical decolouring material).
  - Two-generation reproductive study identified a developmental NOAEL of 400 mg/kg bw/day (basis for ADI derivation).
- **Mechanistic/specification concerns (EFSA 2024)**:
  - Chemically bleached shellac can contain chloroform, inorganic chloride, and organochlorine impurities.
  - EFSA noted structural identity/levels of some organochlorine impurities were insufficiently characterized, preventing full confirmation of no concern for those impurities.
- **Human evidence**:
  - EFSA references contact dermatitis reports with shellac-containing cosmetics.
  - A 2023 retrospective patch-test study (REIDAC) reported 37/980 positives (3.77%) in patients tested for contact dermatitis suspicion; relevance was mainly cosmetic and some occupational/food-handling contexts.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA dietary exposure (from food additive uses)**:
  - Maximum-level scenario: mean up to 3.7 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers), P95 up to 11.5 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Refined non-brand-loyal scenario: mean up to 2.0 mg/kg bw/day, P95 up to 8.7 mg/kg bw/day (highest in children).
  - EFSA considered these scenarios generally conservative/overestimating (e.g., 100% market presence assumptions).
- **ADI exceedance pattern**:
  - P95 exceedances reported for toddlers/children/adolescents (and adults in some scenarios), but EFSA judged the exceedance low and not indicative of safety concern under conservative assumptions.
- **Proposed FSMP tablet extension (EFSA assessed)**:
  - Additional estimated exposure ~1.5 mg/kg bw/day for adults (~1.9 mg/kg bw/day for adolescents if consumed), increasing exceedance likelihood in high-percentile scenarios.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - High-percentile consumers among toddlers/children/adolescents.
  - Users of shellac-coated FSMP tablets/supplements in high-intake scenarios.
  - Individuals with contact-allergy susceptibility (relevant mainly to dermal/occupational/cosmetic exposure rather than typical dietary toxicity endpoints).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA ADI "not specified" with uses deemed not toxicologically concerning; long food-use history.
  - Evidence against: EFSA set a **numerical ADI** (4 mg/kg bw/day), identified high-percentile exceedances in some groups, and flagged unresolved impurity characterization for chemically bleached material.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved in EU/US frameworks; no major genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in EFSA re-evaluation; EFSA concluded calculated exceedances did not indicate safety concern.
  - Evidence against: EFSA's ADI is temporary for chemically bleached wax-free shellac and not applicable to wax-containing shellac; impurity uncertainties remain.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Matches tier triggers: approved with caveats, ADI exceedance at P95 in some populations, and unresolved impurity characterization; additional contact-allergy signal exists in human dermatology literature.
  - Evidence against: Regulators did not conclude current uses pose active toxicological concern when considering conservative exposure assumptions.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Presence of chlorinated impurity concerns in chemically bleached shellac could suggest need for strict controls.
  - Evidence against: No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for shellac, no major ban identified, and EFSA did not conclude current dietary exposure indicates safety concern.

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are: (1) continued authorization and generally reassuring toxicology, (2) EFSA's 2024 caveats on impurity characterization and temporary ADI status for chemically bleached wax-free shellac, and (3) observed high-percentile ADI exceedances in some modeled groups under conservative assumptions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**.
