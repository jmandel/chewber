# E631 (Disodium inosinate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E631 (INS 631)
- **Name:** Disodium inosinate (disodium inosine-5'-monophosphate)
- **CAS number(s):** 4691-65-0
- **Chemical class:** Purine nucleotide salt (disodium salt of inosinic acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium 5'-inosinate; Sodium inosinate; IMP; Disodium 5'-inosinate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** The molecule is naturally occurring/endogenous in purine metabolism, but commercial food-additive material is industrially produced (commonly by fermentation) for use as an additive.

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Flavour enhancer / flavouring adjuvant in savory foods.
- **Mechanism:** Provides umami and acts synergistically with glutamate systems, which can enhance perceived saltiness at equivalent sodium levels.
- **Common food categories:** Savory seasonings and soup stocks, instant/savory formulations, and reduced-sodium savory products where umami enhancement is used.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - Latest re-evaluation found: **EFSA Journal 2025;23(9):9615** (“Safety of inosinic acid and guanylic acid and their salts used as food additives”).
  - EFSA established group ADIs including **2.7 mg inosinic acid/kg bw/day for E630 and E631** (and 2.6 for E632/E633; 3.4 for E626/E627; 3.2 for E628/E629), derived from 5 mg inosinic + guanylic acids/kg bw/day with molecular-weight conversion.
  - EFSA conclusion: for reported use levels, **exposure estimates did not exceed group ADIs in any population group under assessed scenarios**.
  - **Approval status:** approved (re-evaluated with no exceedance of established group ADIs at assessed uses).
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **21 CFR 172.535**: disodium inosinate is permitted as a direct food additive under conditions of use.
  - Conditions include: identity/specification (including max soluble barium in specified hydrate form) and use as a **flavoring adjuvant in food**.
  - GRAS status in Title 21 text checked: no explicit disodium inosinate entry found in parts **182** or **184** from retrieved eCFR API text; regulatory placement is under direct food-additive regulation.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - JECFA database entry for **DISODIUM 5'-INOSINATE (Chemical ID 2512)** lists **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED**, with **evaluation year: 1993** and previous-year note indicating a group ADI basis for inosinic acid and calcium/disodium/dipotassium salts.
- **IARC:**
  - No specific IARC Monographs classification entry found for disodium inosinate / inosinate / CAS 4691-65-0 in the IARC classifications dataset checked.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory core evidence:**
  - EFSA 2025 group ADIs established with exposure estimates below ADIs in assessed populations/scenarios.
  - JECFA maintained ADI as “not specified” for the inosinic-acid salts group context.
- **Recent PubMed-indexed safety assessments (mostly feed context):**
  - EFSA FEEDAP 2020 (PMID: 37649505): IMP (disodium 5'-inosinate) produced by fermentation considered safe for target species/consumer/environment in assessed use context (with reservation on use in drinking water due hygienic considerations).
  - EFSA FEEDAP 2022 (PMID: 35281652): temporary inability to conclude safety due unresolved genetic/recombinant-DNA uncertainties.
  - EFSA FEEDAP 2023 (PMID: 37064054): supplementary data resolved those uncertainties; panel concluded safety for target species/consumers/users/environment (again noting water-hygiene reservation).
- **Mechanistic/clinical signal relevant to purine metabolism:**
  - IMPROVE-MSA RCT (PMID: 33064299) used inosine monophosphate therapeutically and showed significant serum uric-acid increase versus placebo, while generally tolerated in that selected clinical population. This supports a biologically plausible high-dose purine/uric-acid pathway concern rather than a confirmed risk at normal food-additive exposures.
- **Other literature context:**
  - Purine analysis in umami soup stocks (PMID: 29652211) showed IMP/GMP-rich matrices and reported purine-metabolism effects in HepG2 experimental setting.
- **Epidemiology:**
  - No strong additive-specific human epidemiology signal for E631 adverse outcomes was identified in the searched recent PubMed set.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Population-level regulatory exposure:**
  - EFSA 2025 states exposure estimates for the evaluated additive group did not exceed group ADIs across population groups and scenarios assessed.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Based on EFSA 2025 assessment scenarios, exceedance risk appears low for general populations at reported use levels.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Individuals with gout/hyperuricemia susceptibility may warrant conservative intake behavior at high purine exposures (clinical IMP data demonstrate uric-acid-raising potential at therapeutic dosing), though this is not equivalent to confirmed exceedance at normal additive exposure.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Supporting evidence: endogenous/naturally occurring purine intermediate; EFSA found no ADI exceedance in assessed scenarios; no IARC classification; no major bans.
  - Evidence against: EFSA still set numerical group ADIs (not “ADI not specified” at EFSA level), and purine biology supports potential uric-acid effects at high doses.
- **limited**
  - Supporting evidence: approved in EU/US; explicit CFR use conditions; EFSA group ADIs with no exceedance in assessed populations; no strong serious safety signal in reviewed human-food context.
  - Evidence against: mechanistic plausibility for uric-acid-related effects in high-exposure/susceptible settings.
- **moderate**
  - Supporting evidence: credible biological pathway (purine to uric acid) and clinical IMP evidence of uric-acid elevation at therapeutic dose.
  - Evidence against: regulatory exposure assessments for food-additive use did not indicate ADI exceedance; no major regulatory withdrawal.
- **high**
  - Supporting evidence: none substantial from reviewed core regulatory sources.
  - Evidence against: not banned in major jurisdictions reviewed; no IARC carcinogenic classification; EFSA/JECFA positions do not indicate high-risk classification.

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest current evidence base is regulatory and supports continued approved use with controlled conditions and no ADI exceedance in EFSA-assessed scenarios. Potential concerns are mostly theoretical/high-dose or susceptible-population purine metabolism considerations rather than demonstrated population-level harm at reported use levels.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
