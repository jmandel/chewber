# E553a (Magnesium silicate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E553a (includes E553a(i) magnesium silicate and E553a(ii) magnesium trisilicate in EFSA nomenclature)
- **Name:** Magnesium silicate (food additive form is typically synthetic; JECFA entry name: "MAGNESIUM SILICATE (synthetic)")
- **CAS number(s):** 1343-90-4 (magnesium silicate hydrate; JECFA record), 1343-88-0 (magnesium silicate, PubChem synonym set), 14987-04-3 (magnesium trisilicate, E553a(ii), PubChem)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic silicate salts (magnesium silicates)
- **Common synonyms:** Magnesium silicate, magnesium silicate hydrate, magnesium trisilicate, INS 553i, E553a
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** (JECFA listing for INS 553i is "MAGNESIUM SILICATE (synthetic)")

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Anti-caking and filtering functions via adsorption/flow-aid behavior of silicate particles.
- **Common food categories:** Primarily dry powdered foods where anti-caking is needed; in U.S. CFR text, magnesium silicate is specifically listed for use in **table salt**.
- **Regulatory functional class terms:** JECFA classifies magnesium silicate as **ANTICAKING_AGENT** and **FILTERING_AID**.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest scientific re-evaluation identified:** 2018 EFSA Journal opinion on E552/E553a/E553b.
  - **Conclusion:** EFSA stated safety of E553a(i)/E553a(ii) (within the silicates/talc opinion scope) **could not be assessed** due to important toxicology data gaps (notably subchronic/chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity).
  - **ADI position:** EFSA stated the old SCF group ADI "not specified" for silicates/silicon dioxide is **obsolete** and that there is no mechanistic rationale for a group ADI.
  - **Approval context:** The EFSA opinion text (as archived in PMC) notes these additives are authorized in the EU under Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 (risk management authorization), but with the above risk-assessment uncertainty.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **GRAS status:** Listed as GRAS in **21 CFR 182.2437**.
  - **CFR conditions of use:** Tolerance **2%**; limitation says GRAS when used **in table salt** according to good manufacturing practice.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - WHO JECFA database entry for **MAGNESIUM SILICATE (synthetic), INS 553i** shows **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED** (1982 entry on the detail page).
  - The same page shows a later **2011** entry that was specification-focused ("Only specifications evaluated and revised").
- **IARC:**
  - No separate explicit IARC listing was identified for "magnesium silicate (E553a)" itself in the IARC classification list data.
  - Related agent note: IARC list data currently shows **Talc** as **Group 2A** (probably carcinogenic), but talc is E553b, not E553a.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban specifically identified for E553a in the checked EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA hazard/risk assessment signal (2018):**
  - EFSA reported very low absorption and no clear indication of genotoxicity/developmental toxicity for the evaluated silicates/talc set.
  - However, EFSA also reported major data gaps (insufficient reliable chronic/carcinogenicity/reproductive datasets) and therefore concluded safety for E553a could not be assessed.
- **Human evidence (PubMed):**
  - Direct modern dietary E553a human safety studies are scarce.
  - Available newer clinical literature is largely case-based on **silicate urinary stones**, often discussing magnesium trisilicate exposure context, including:
    - 2017 pediatric case series (silicate calculi are rare; commonly related to excessive magnesium trisilicate intake in food/drugs, though cases without identified intake source occurred).
    - 2022 case report/review describing silicate stone pathology and noting historical linkage to magnesium trisilicate ingestion.
- **Mechanistic/animal evidence profile:**
  - EFSA reported low systemic absorption but highlighted uncertainty from missing robust long-term datasets needed for definitive risk characterization.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake framing:**
  - EFSA noted that, in the food-supplement scenario used for risk characterization, estimated silicate exposure was below the historical magnesium trisilicate antacid benchmark dose of **4 g/person/day**.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Classical ADI exceedance assessment is limited because EFSA considered the historical group ADI obsolete, while JECFA still reports "ADI not specified" for synthetic magnesium silicate.
  - Practical uncertainty therefore depends on product use patterns, data gaps, and susceptible exposure scenarios rather than a single numeric ADI threshold.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - People with prolonged/high cumulative silicate exposure (e.g., frequent users of silicate-containing products) and those with renal stone susceptibility are the main populations of concern in the case-report literature.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: JECFA ADI "not specified"; low absorption signal in EFSA; long legacy of use.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA could not assess safety for E553a because key long-term toxicology datasets were insufficient; old group ADI judged obsolete by EFSA.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: U.S. GRAS status with specific use conditions (table salt, 2% tolerance); no clear strong modern signal of widespread severe toxicity from dietary exposure.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA's inability to complete safety assessment is a stronger concern than a minor caveat.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Approved in major jurisdictions but with substantial scientific uncertainty; human evidence suggests renal stone concern is rare and mostly case-report level.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA's formal conclusion was not merely "caution" but "safety cannot be assessed," which can justify an even higher concern tier under this framework.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA re-evaluation explicitly concluded safety for E553a(i)/(ii) cannot be assessed due to missing critical toxicology data; framework criterion for "high" includes this condition.
  - Evidence against this tier: No broad regulatory ban, FDA GRAS listing remains, and JECFA still lists ADI not specified; direct modern evidence of severe dietary harm remains limited.

2. **Rationale**
- The deciding factor is EFSA's explicit inability to confirm safety for E553a in the latest re-evaluation, driven by major data gaps in long-term toxicology. This outweighs the reassurance implied by legacy approvals, while still leaving uncertainty because direct modern human dietary harm data are sparse.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **high**
