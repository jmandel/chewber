# E140 (Chlorophylls) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E140 (INS 140) [S1, S3]
- **Name:** Chlorophylls [S3]
- **CAS number(s):** 479-61-8 (chlorophyll a); 519-62-0 (chlorophyll b) [S3]
- **Chemical class:** Natural porphyrin/chlorin pigments (magnesium-containing tetrapyrrolic plant pigments) [S3]
- **Common synonyms:** Magnesium chlorophyll; Magnesium phaeophytin; C.I. Natural Green 3 [S3]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily natural origin (plant-derived pigments). This is an inference consistent with the JECFA naming and chlorophyll chemistry; note that some regulatory texts in the U.S. focus on semi-synthetic chlorophyll derivatives rather than E140 itself [S3, S4].

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Food colour (green pigment) [S3]
- **Mechanism of action:** The conjugated chlorin/porphyrin ring system absorbs visible light and imparts green colour in food matrices (mechanistic chemistry inference from chlorophyll structure) [S3].
- **Common food categories:**
  - In the EU, EFSA notes chlorophylls/chlorophyllins (E140) are authorised in food categories listed in Annex II/III (Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008) [S1].
  - In the U.S., the closest chlorophyll-derived food color entry is **sodium copper chlorophyllin** under 21 CFR 73.125, limited to citrus-based dry beverage mixes up to 0.2% in dry mix (this is not identical to EU E140 chlorophylls) [S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Authorised food colour in the EU framework; EFSA re-evaluated chlorophylls (E140(i)) in 2015 [S1].
- **ADI:** EFSA reported it was **not able to derive an ADI** from available data [S1].
- **Evaluation year:** 2015 [S1]
- **Key conclusions from EFSA re-evaluation:**
  - Technical data were insufficient to confirm that the additive on the EU market corresponded primarily to chlorophylls/chlorophyllins as specified [S1].
  - No newly submitted toxicology package was available; ADI could not be derived [S1].
  - EFSA reported dietary exposure estimates up to 29.0 mg/kg bw/day (mean; toddlers) and 51.9 mg/kg bw/day (high level; children) [S1].

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **GRAS status (for E140 chlorophylls):** No direct chlorophyll/chlorophylls GRAS listing identified in 21 CFR Part 182 or Part 184 via eCFR API search [S5, S6].
- **Specific CFR citation (chlorophyll-derived color additive in food):** **21 CFR 73.125** (Sodium copper chlorophyllin) [S4].
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 73.125):**
  - May be safely used to color citrus-based dry beverage mixes at not more than 0.2% in dry mix [S4].
  - Exempt from batch certification [S4].
- **Important scope note:** U.S. CFR entries retrieved are for chlorophyll derivatives (especially copper chlorophyllins), not a direct one-to-one listing of EU E140 chlorophylls [S4, S7].

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT LIMITED** (effectively ADI not specified) [S3]
- **Last evaluation year:** 1969 (with specs code reference R (1987) shown on the database page) [S3]
- **INS:** 140 [S3]

### IARC
- **Classification result:** No chlorophyll/chlorophyllin entry was found in the current IARC classified-agents dataset used by the Monographs list interface (dataset last-update string: 2026-02-25 16:34 CET) [S8, S9, S10].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction blanket ban identified in the EFSA, eCFR, JECFA, and IARC sources reviewed for this task [S1, S3, S4, S8].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA 2015 (core regulatory evidence):** EFSA could not derive an ADI due to data limitations and noted sizable estimated exposures in some groups [S1].
- **Recent PubMed evidence (mostly chlorophyllin derivatives, not pure E140 chlorophylls):**
  - **2025 preclinical study (PMID: 40274623):** In rodents, acute oral doses up to 5000 mg/kg bw were reportedly tolerated; sub-acute study reported no-observed-adverse-effect indication above 1000 mg/kg bw in that study design [S11].
  - **2021 comparative toxicity study (PMID: 34486328):** Tri-sodium copper chlorophyllins in rats showed higher LD50 than comparator insecticide and lower oxidative/genotoxic effects than deltamethrin, but some high-dose adverse findings were still described [S12].
  - **2021 mouse study (PMID: 33380297):** Chlorophyllin was studied as a protective intervention against another food-additive toxicity model (alloxan), which is indirect evidence for E140 safety [S13].
- **Interpretation:** Recent studies are heterogeneous and often evaluate chlorophyllin/copper-complex materials in pharmacology or pesticide contexts; they do not fully close EFSA’s data-gap concerns for E140(i) food-color safety characterization [S1, S11, S12, S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA reported ranges:**
  - Mean exposure: 2.6 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) to 29.0 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers) [S1].
  - High-level exposure: 6.5 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) to 51.9 mg/kg bw/day (children) [S1].
- **ADI exceedance framing:**
  - EFSA did not establish an ADI in the re-evaluation, so standard ADI exceedance testing is constrained [S1].
  - Children/toddlers are the most exposure-relevant groups in EFSA estimates [S1].
- **Vulnerable populations:** toddlers and children with higher color-additive intake patterns [S1].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting:** JECFA ADI is "NOT LIMITED," and chlorophylls are natural pigments [S3].
- **Evidence arguing against:** EFSA could not derive an ADI in its 2015 re-evaluation due to data gaps; this is not consistent with a fully risk-free profile [S1].

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting:** Additive remains in regulatory frameworks (EU authorisation context; JECFA listing; no IARC listing found) [S1, S3, S8].
- **Evidence arguing against:** EFSA data limitations and relatively high modeled exposure estimates in children/toddlers push beyond a simple low-concern profile [S1].

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting:** Approved/listed status exists, but with major caveats: EFSA unable to set ADI, and high estimated exposures in younger populations; evidence base is mixed and partly indirect [S1, S11, S12, S13].
- **Evidence arguing against:** JECFA historical ADI "NOT LIMITED" and absence of major regulatory bans argue against escalating to highest concern [S3, S8].

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting:** One high-tier trigger is that EFSA could not confirm a standard ADI-based safety conclusion in 2015 [S1].
- **Evidence arguing against:** No major-jurisdiction ban and no IARC carcinogenic group listing identified; available newer studies are mixed rather than uniformly harmful [S8, S11, S12].

### 2. Rationale
The evidence pattern is not risk-free: EFSA flagged unresolved data limitations and could not derive an ADI in 2015, while exposure estimates were highest in children/toddlers. However, the additive remains authorised/listed in major regulatory systems and no strong ban/carcinogenic-classification signal was identified in the requested sources.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
