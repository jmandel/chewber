# E1413 Research Report — Phosphated distarch phosphate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1413. [S2][S3]
- **Name:** Phosphated distarch phosphate (also written as phosphated di-starch phosphate). [S2][S3][S4]
- **INS number:** 1413. [S3][S8]
- **CAS numbers:** 11120-02-8 (modified starch) and 63055-37-8 (modified amylopectin). [S8]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (phosphate-esterified and cross-linked starch derivative). [S8]
- **Common synonyms:** E 1413, INS 1413, phosphated distarch phosphate, phosphated di-starch phosphate. [S2][S3][S4]
- **Origin:** Semi-synthetic (native food starch is chemically modified with phosphorus-containing reagents). [S8]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Stabilizer and thickener. [S3]
- **Mechanism of action:** JECFA monograph describes phosphorylation and cross-linking of starch chains (Starch-O-R-O-Starch), with substitution at hydroxyl positions on glucose units; this chemistry explains improved process stability and thickening behavior in foods. [S8]
- **Common food categories:**
  - Broadly authorized in the U.S. as "food starch-modified" for foods under GMP/technical-need limits, with specified phosphate residual limits for relevant treatments. [S5]
  - Used in starch-thickened processed foods (recent literature examples include sauce-thickener and texture-modification applications, though these are functionality papers rather than regulatory use lists). [S12][S13]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (strongest evidence):**
  - EFSA 2017 group re-evaluation reports no treatment-related effects relevant to human risk assessment in rats at high doses (up to 31,000 mg/kg bw/day for modified starches in read-across), no genotoxic concern from in silico analyses, and no need for a numerical ADI at reported uses. [S4]
- **Older animal evidence (supporting but indirect for E1413-specificity):**
  - 1986 chronic feeding study in mice including hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate reported no carcinogenicity evidence; some GI/renal-mineral findings were described. [S11]
  - 1982 rat study with acetylated distarch phosphate/adipate reported mineral-metabolism and nephrocalcinosis-related findings, with modified starch effects lower than lactose comparator in that setup. [S12]
- **Recent PubMed landscape:**
  - Targeted PubMed safety/toxicity query for phosphated distarch phosphate in title/abstract returned zero direct hits, indicating limited recent additive-specific toxicology literature. [S14]
  - Recent exact-phrase hits are predominantly food technology/functionality papers (e.g., dough/thickener behavior), not hazard studies. [S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate context:**
  - EFSA reports combined refined 95th percentile exposure for E1404-E1451 up to 3,053 mg/kg bw/day (general population scenario) and E1452 supplement-consumer exposure up to 22.1 mg/kg bw/day. This is a group-level estimate, not E1413-only intake. [S4]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA/JECFA position is "not specified" / no numerical ADI for this modified starch group, so classical ADI exceedance calculation is not directly applicable. [S3][S4][S8]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - High consumers (especially children/high-intake groups) remain the most relevant monitoring population in general exposure frameworks, but EFSA did not identify a safety concern at reported uses. [S4]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA and JECFA do not set a numerical ADI and report no safety concern at reported uses for the modified-starch group. [S3][S4][S8]
  - Evidence against: E1413 is not an endogenous/native unmodified molecule; it is chemically modified starch. Older animal studies on related modified starches reported renal/mineral effects under high-dose conditions. [S8][S11][S12]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: EU re-evaluation published with favorable conclusion; U.S. authorization under 21 CFR 172.892; JECFA ADI "not specified"; no IARC carcinogen listing identified. [S2][S3][S5][S9]
  - Evidence against: Current direct E1413-specific modern toxicology literature is sparse; much evidence is group-level/read-across. [S4][S14]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Some older rodent findings on related modified starches and nontrivial uncertainty from grouped exposure/safety assessment rather than highly E1413-specific modern datasets. [S11][S12][S4]
  - Evidence against: Regulatory consensus remains favorable (no numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at reported uses). [S3][S4]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: No strong supporting evidence found.
  - Evidence against: No major bans found; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B assignment identified; regulatory evaluations are generally permissive. [S2][S3][S5][S9]

### 2. Rationale
The dominant weight of evidence is regulatory consensus from EFSA/JECFA plus U.S. authorization, with no clear carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in the reviewed sources. Remaining uncertainty is mostly about evidence granularity (group read-across and limited recent E1413-specific toxicology) rather than a strong hazard signal.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`**
