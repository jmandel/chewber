# E217 Research Report — Sodium propyl p-hydroxybenzoate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E217
- **Primary name:** Sodium propyl p-hydroxybenzoate (sodium propylparaben)
- **CAS number(s):**
  - **35285-69-9** (sodium propyl p-hydroxybenzoate / E217)
  - **94-13-3** (propyl p-hydroxybenzoate, parent ester / propylparaben, related compound in EFSA/JECFA/FDA records)
- **Chemical class:** Paraben preservative (p-hydroxybenzoic acid ester and corresponding sodium salt)
- **Common synonyms:** sodium propylparaben, sodium propyl p-oxybenzoate, sodium propyl para-hydroxybenzoate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic (food additive use)**. Regulatory/manufacturing records describe synthetic production routes for the paraben family; E217 is the sodium salt form used as a preservative.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antimicrobial preservative.
- **Mechanism of action:** Parabens inhibit growth of spoilage microbes (especially molds/yeasts; some bacteria), extending shelf life.
- **Common food categories / uses identified in regulatory sources:**
  - U.S. CFR records for related propylparaben: direct food use as antimicrobial at GMP (max 0.1%), inclusion in synthetic flavoring substances list, and prior-sanction packaging antimycotic context.
  - EFSA 2004 opinion context included requested uses in several foods (including supplements/snack-related categories).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation located:** 2013 EFSA opinion on parabens (E214–E219).
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA set a **temporary ADI of 0–10 mg/kg bw/day only for methyl- and ethyl-parabens plus sodium salts**, and stated this group ADI **should not include propylparaben (E216) or sodium propylparaben (E217)** due to insufficient basis for a clear NOAEL and concern regarding developmental/reproductive effects (male reproductive system).
- **Evaluation year:** 2013.
- **Approval status (EU market reality):** E217 was subsequently removed from EU authorized additive use via Commission Regulation (EU) No 358/2014 (effective **17 April 2014**), as reported by FSAI regulatory summary.

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- Direct scraping of human-facing `ecfr.gov` pages was blocked; data were retrieved from the official **eCFR API** snapshot (Title 21 up to date as of 2026-02-25).
- **21 CFR §184.1670 (Propylparaben):** affirmed in Part 184 context; use as antimicrobial agent at GMP, with **current GMP maximum 0.1% in food**.
- **21 CFR §172.515:** includes **propyl p-hydroxybenzoate (propylparaben)** among synthetic flavoring substances.
- **Specific to E217 name:** no explicit standalone string match for “sodium propyl p-hydroxybenzoate” was found in the Title 21 XML snapshot used.

### JECFA / WHO
- JECFA database entry for propyl p-hydroxybenzoate reports:
  - **ADI:** **WITHDRAWN (2006)**
  - **Last evaluation year:** **2006**
  - **Key rationale:** adverse effects in male rats; propyl paraben excluded from former group ADI.
- Historical note in JECFA entry indicates earlier **group ADI 0–10 mg/kg bw** (older evaluation history) before withdrawal.

### IARC
- Checked IARC Monographs classification system (list page + current classification dataset script).
- No propylparaben/sodium propylparaben entry was found in current IARC classification list data.
- **IARC classification for E217:** no specific listing identified.

### Notable bans/restrictions
- **European Union:** E216/E217 removed from authorized food additive list (Regulation (EU) No 358/2014; effective 2014-04-17 per FSAI summary page).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/regulatory evidence
- **JECFA (2006)**: ADI withdrawn after male-rat reproductive-organ effects and lack of NOEL in relevant range.
- **EFSA (2013)**: did not include E216/E217 in temporary ADI group due unresolved reproductive/developmental concerns.

### Recent PubMed-indexed evidence
- **Zebrafish developmental/immunotoxicity studies (2025–2026 publications):** report oxidative stress, immune perturbation, microbiota-axis effects, and developmental toxicity under experimental exposures.
- **In vitro mechanistic study (ER+ breast cancer cells):** reports pro-proliferative/procarcinogenic pathway activation signals for propylparaben in cell models.
- **Population exposure/risk study (Taiwan):** urinary parabens detected; reported hazard indices for parabens were below 1 in that dataset.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Endocrine activity (weak estrogenic signaling relative to endogenous hormones) remains a recurring concern in both regulatory and mechanistic literature.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EU context:** following removal from authorized additive list, lawful direct food additive exposure to E217 in EU should be minimal.
- **Non-EU context:** related propylparaben uses remain present in U.S. CFR contexts, so exposure to propylparaben-class preservatives can still occur depending on food/product type.
- **Biomonitoring reality:** contemporary population studies continue to detect paraben exposure.
- **ADI exceedance risk for E217:** difficult to calculate conventionally because current EFSA and JECFA positions for propyl/sodium propyl parabens do not provide an active positive ADI for E217 itself.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** children (higher intake per kg body weight) and male developmental windows (based on regulatory concern from animal reproductive findings).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** older EFSA-era context (2004 opinion) did not identify safety concern at then-proposed uses and relied on historical group ADI framework.
- **Evidence against this tier:** later EFSA/JECFA conclusions moved away from that framework for propyl/sodium propyl parabens; EU removed E217 authorization.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** U.S. CFR contains approved/affirmed use contexts for related propylparaben with GMP limits; some recent human exposure studies report paraben hazard indices below 1.
- **Evidence against this tier:** E217 itself lost EU authorization; JECFA ADI was withdrawn; EFSA did not retain E217 in temporary ADI grouping.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** persistent mechanistic and animal concerns with uncertainty in translating dose/exposure to humans; mixed regulatory landscape across jurisdictions.
- **Evidence against this tier:** explicit high-concern signals in major regulatory actions (EU removal; JECFA withdrawal) are stronger than a typical “approved with caveats” profile.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Major-jurisdiction removal (EU) of E217 authorization.
  - EFSA inability to support inclusion of E217 in temporary ADI for parabens.
  - JECFA withdrawal of ADI with male-rat reproductive toxicity concern and no identified NOEL in critical range.
- **Evidence against this tier:** no specific IARC carcinogen classification entry; some recent human exposure-risk datasets do not show immediate hazard-index exceedance for total parabens.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest drivers are regulatory: EFSA and JECFA both identified unresolved reproductive/developmental toxicology concerns for propyl/sodium propyl parabens, and EU authorization was removed. These outweigh older approvals and non-carcinogenicity classification gaps.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`high`**
