# E472c (Citric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E472c / INS 472c. [S1][S3]
- **Name:** Citric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids. [S1][S2]
- **CAS number(s):** No single CAS was reported in the current WHO/JECFA database entry (`CAS_NO: null`), consistent with E472c being a variable mixture of esters rather than one discrete compound. [S8][S9]
- **Chemical class:** Mixed glycerol-fatty acid-citric acid esters (emulsifier class; "CITREM"). [S2][S4]
- **Common synonyms:** CITREM; citroglycerides; citric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides; mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids esterified with citric acid. [S4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured by esterification of glycerol/citric acid/fatty acid-derived materials). [S2][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Emulsifier. [S10]
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic ester mixture that stabilizes fat-water interfaces; in some systems also acts as a structuring agent for fat networks. [S13]
- **Common food categories:**
  - Fine bakery wares and fat/oil emulsions were major contributors in EFSA exposure modeling for E472c users.
  - Infant formula and FSMP formula are specific high-relevance categories in the EFSA infant follow-up (FC 13.1.1 and 13.1.5.1). [S2][S4]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest scientific opinion:** 2025 follow-up for infants below 16 weeks (EFSA Journal e9202).
  - **ADI:** EFSA 2020 concluded no need for a numerical ADI for E472a,b,c; 2025 follow-up did not introduce a numeric ADI for E472c and concluded no safety concern at reported use levels/MPLs for infant categories assessed.
  - **Key conclusions:**
    - 2020: no need for numerical ADI for E472a,b,c; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
    - 2025: no safety concern for E472c use in infants below 16 weeks in FC 13.1.1 and 13.1.5.1 at reported uses and MPLs, with recommendations to update specifications. [S1][S2][S3][S4]
- **FDA / US CFR (Title 21):**
  - eCFR API text search for the exact phrase "citric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides" returned **0 results** in current indexed changes.
  - No direct Title 21 section explicitly naming E472c/CITREM was found in the retrieved current text.
  - Related sections:
    - **21 CFR 172.832 (Monoglyceride citrate):** approved direct food additive; limited to antioxidant formulations for oils/fats, max 200 ppm in final oil/fat system.
    - **21 CFR 184.1505 (Mono- and diglycerides):** GRAS, GMP-limited uses for broad food functions.
  - **Interpretation:** direct US CFR match for E472c name not identified; closest relevant entries are a related approved citrate ester (172.832) and GRAS parent glyceride class (184.1505). [S5][S6][S7]
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - JECFA chemical record for CITRIC ACID ESTERS OF MONO- AND DIGLYCERIDES (ID 2701) lists ADI as **NOT SPECIFIED**, with evaluation entries through 2019.
  - Historical toxicology monograph (WHO Food Additives Series 5, 1974) reports ADI **"Not limited"** and describes hydrolysis/digestibility rationale. [S8][S9][S10]
- **IARC:**
  - No IARC Monographs classification entry was identified for E472c/CITREM in the current classification dataset used by IARC's "List of Classifications" page. [S11][S12]
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in the above EFSA/JECFA/FDA/IARC checks. [S1][S2][S8][S12]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology core:**
  - EFSA 2020: no relevant adverse effects for E472a-f; no need for numerical ADI for E472a,b,c.
  - EFSA 2025 infant follow-up: no safety concern for infants below 16 weeks at reported use levels/MPLs in FC 13.1.1 and 13.1.5.1. [S1][S2][S3][S4]
- **Mechanistic/digestion evidence:**
  - In vitro digestion work (2014) showed substantial lipase-mediated release of fatty acids from CITREM and no major change in fat digestion in tested models, though some water-soluble glycerol-citric structures were not fully hydrolyzed. [S13]
- **Older animal data (JECFA historical monograph):**
  - High-dose short-term rat data reported no effects at lower high dietary levels and nephron calcification at the highest levels in one short study context; JECFA conclusion remained "not limited" based on metabolic fate and constituent toxicology. [S10]
- **Specification/contaminant concerns (not endpoint toxicity of pure E472c itself):**
  - EFSA highlighted impurity control issues (toxic elements, 3-MCPD/glycidyl esters, fatty-acid profile constraints) and recommended specification revisions. [S2][S4]

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population (EFSA 2020 non-brand-loyal refined scenario):**
  - Highest rounded mean and P95 used in EFSA 2025 follow-up context were **66** and **135 mg/kg bw/day** (toddlers).
  - Table data indicate toddlers as one of the highest exposed groups in multiple scenarios. [S2][S4]
- **Infants in dedicated 2025 follow-up scenarios:**
  - Brand-loyal high-level estimate reached about **2314 mg/kg bw/day** for liquid infant formula scenario.
  - EFSA still concluded no safety concern at reported levels/MPLs in assessed infant categories. [S2]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Classical numeric ADI exceedance is not directly applicable because EFSA did not set a numerical ADI for E472c and JECFA lists ADI as not specified/not limited. [S3][S8][S10]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Formula-fed infants (high per-kg intake), toddlers, and subgroups with high intake of emulsifier-containing foods. [S2][S4]

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports:** EFSA and JECFA conclusions are broadly reassuring; no IARC listing identified.
  - **Against:** E472c is a manufactured mixture; EFSA still requested specification tightening for contaminants and quality parameters, and infant exposure can be very high in mg/kg terms. [S2][S4][S8][S12]
- **limited**
  - **Supports:** Approved/allowed in EU and JECFA frameworks with non-numeric ADI outcomes; EFSA 2025 infant follow-up still concluded no safety concern at reported uses/MPLs; direct mechanistic digestion evidence does not show a clear hazard signal.
  - **Against:** Some residual uncertainty from limited direct modern long-term human endpoint studies specific to E472c as a standalone exposure variable. [S1][S2][S3][S8][S13]
- **moderate**
  - **Supports:** Very high modeled infant exposure in some scenarios and need for ongoing impurity/specification controls could justify precautionary concern.
  - **Against:** Regulators did not identify safety concern at authorized uses; no numerical ADI reduction event, no ban trend, and no carcinogenic classification signal identified. [S2][S4][S8][S12]
- **high**
  - **Supports:** No strong supporting evidence found.
  - **Against:** No major bans found; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B assignment for E472c itself; EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain permissive with conditions/spec updates rather than withdrawal. [S1][S2][S8][S12]

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest and most recent evidence is still regulatory (EFSA 2020 + EFSA 2025 infant follow-up + JECFA record), and those sources do not indicate a safety concern at current reported uses/MPLs. The main non-zero uncertainty is specification quality (impurities/constituents) and sparse independent long-term human endpoint studies specifically isolating E472c.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
