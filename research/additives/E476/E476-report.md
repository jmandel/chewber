# E476 (Polyglycerol polyricinoleate, PGPR) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E476 (INS 476). [S6]
- **Primary name:** Polyglycerol esters of polycondensed fatty acids from castor oil / polyglycerol polyricinoleate (PGPR). [S2][S6]
- **CAS number(s):** 68936-89-0 (appears in EFSA 2017 PubMed keyword metadata for E476). [S2]
- **Chemical class:** Non-ionic, oil-soluble emulsifier composed of mixed esters of polyglycerol and condensed castor-oil fatty acids (mixture, not a single discrete molecule). [S2][S6][S12]
- **Common synonyms:** PGPR; glycerin esters of condensed castor oil fatty acids; polyglycerol esters of polycondensed fatty acids from castor oil. [S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Manufactured additive (chemically produced from glycerol and castor-oil-derived fatty acids; effectively semi-synthetic in origin). [S6][S12]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Functions as a water-in-oil emulsifier that stabilizes dispersed water droplets in fat phases and modifies emulsion behavior. [S6][S12]
- **Common food categories:** Chocolate/couverture and baking tin-greasing emulsions are documented historical uses; EFSA follow-up also evaluated proposed extension to edible ices and higher MPL in emulsified sauces. [S9][S3]
- **Functional class (JECFA):** Food additive, emulsifier. [S6]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Approval status:** Evaluated and permitted as E476; latest follow-up opinion (2022) did not identify a safety concern for the proposed extension of use. [S3]
  - **ADI:** 25 mg/kg bw/day (EFSA 2017 re-evaluation revised prior SCF ADI 7.5 mg/kg bw/day upward to 25 mg/kg bw/day). [S2]
  - **Evaluation year(s):** 2017 re-evaluation; 2022 follow-up on data gaps/specifications and proposed use changes. [S2][S3]
  - **Key EFSA conclusions:** No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity in the evaluated dataset; no significant adverse indication in limited human data; exposure estimates did not exceed ADI; 2022 opinion recommended tighter impurity/specification controls (toxic elements, glycidyl esters, 3-MCPD-related controls). [S2][S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA 2017):** PGPR showed low acute oral toxicity; short-term/subchronic studies tolerated high doses; no concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity; NOAEL reference point 2,500 mg/kg bw/day in the chronic study used for ADI derivation. [S2]
- **EFSA follow-up (2022):** No safety concern for proposed use extension, but recommended stricter impurity limits/specification updates. [S3]
- **Older targeted animal/human studies (PubMed-indexed):**
  - 2-year rat and 80-week mouse carcinogenicity study reported no carcinogenic effect; increased liver/kidney weights were noted without treatment-related histopathology. [S10]
  - Three-generation Wistar rat reproduction study reported no cumulative adverse effect on breeding performance at 1.5% dietary PGPR. [S11]
  - Human volunteer study (n=19; up to 10 g/day for 2 weeks) reported no consistent adverse biochemical/liver/kidney effects. [S9]
- **Recent mechanistic concern signal:** A 2023 mouse study (PGPR and polysorbate-80 in normal and high-fat diet settings) reported increased epididymal fat, blood lipids, and inflammatory markers, with little effect on body weight; this is suggestive but not by itself a regulatory-grade hazard reversal. [S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EU regulatory exposure conclusion:** EFSA 2017 concluded exposure estimates did not exceed the ADI (25 mg/kg bw/day), including proposed extension scenario considered in that opinion. [S2]
- **US context (published exposure modeling):** FDA-authored 2017 US emulsifier exposure paper (including PGPR among seven emulsifiers) concluded updated exposure estimates did not raise safety concerns at specified use levels. [S14]
- **Older human intake context:** Controlled short-term intake up to 10 g/day in volunteers did not show adverse effects in the 1960s study summarized in 1998 publication. [S9]
- **ADI exceedance risk (current evidence):** Low for general population under assessed uses; uncertainty remains for high-processed-food consumption patterns and for impurity-management quality at manufacturing/specification level. [S2][S3][S14]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of emulsifier-containing processed foods; children/toddlers due higher intake per kg body weight generally in additive exposure assessments. [S2][S14]

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA did not identify genotoxic/carcinogenic concern and found exposures below ADI at assessed uses. [S2]
  - Evidence against this tier: Numeric ADI is required (not "ADI not specified"); EFSA follow-up identified non-trivial impurity/specification controls; recent animal mechanistic signals exist. [S2][S3][S13]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Regulatory acceptance in EU with clear ADI; JECFA ADI exists; no IARC listing; core chronic/reproductive/carcinogenicity evidence did not show major hazard at evaluated doses. [S2][S6][S8][S9][S10][S11]
  - Evidence against this tier: FDA/eCFR does not show a direct PGPR-by-name listing in current Title 21 text (regulatory mapping uncertainty in US CFR text). [S5]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Impurity-control concerns in EFSA 2022 and one recent mouse study suggesting inflammatory/metabolic perturbations justify caution. [S3][S13]
  - Evidence against this tier: No major regulator concluded current authorized uses are unsafe; EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain generally permissive within use/specification bounds. [S2][S3][S6]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: None from the reviewed regulatory corpus (no IARC Group 1/2A/2B entry; no major bans identified). [S8]
  - Evidence against this tier: Existing approvals/ADIs and lack of strong carcinogenic/genotoxic evidence at regulatory review level. [S2][S3][S6]

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest evidence remains regulatory: EFSA re-evaluation and follow-up support continued use with ADI/specification controls, and JECFA retains an ADI. The main unresolved issues are impurity limits/specification quality and some newer mechanistic animal findings that are not yet reflected as regulatory withdrawal or major restriction. [S2][S3][S6][S13]

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
