# E1440 Research Report: Hydroxypropyl starch

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1440 (INS 1440) [2][3]
- **Name:** Hydroxypropyl starch (also shown as "Hydroxy propyl starch" in EFSA metadata) [1][2]
- **CAS number(s):** 9049-76-7 [1][2]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (starch ether; hydroxypropylated starch) [2][7]
- **Common synonyms:** Hydroxypropyl starch; E 1440; INS 1440; hydroxy propyl starch [1][2][3]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** **Semi-synthetic** (produced by chemical modification of native starch with hydroxypropyl groups; not a naturally occurring food constituent in this form) [7][8]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydroxypropyl substitution changes starch physicochemical behavior (e.g., process stability/texture behavior) and is used as a functional modified starch in foods [7][8].
- **Regulatory functional role (JECFA):** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [3].
- **Common food categories:** The retrieved US and JECFA regulatory pages classify it by function (thickener/stabilizer/emulsifier) and broad food use as food starch-modified rather than listing a narrow E1440-only category list [3][7].

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorized in the EU within the modified starch group re-evaluated by EFSA [1][4].
- **ADI:** EFSA states previous SCF/JECFA ADI was "not specified" and concluded there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for the re-evaluated modified starch group that includes E1440 [4].
- **Evaluation year:** 2017 (EFSA Journal DOI `10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4911`; publication date 2017-10-05 in EFSA question metadata) [1][4].
- **Key conclusion:** No safety concern for use of modified starches at reported uses/use levels for the general population [4].
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA search/landing pages were visited but blocked/challenged from this VM; EFSA metadata and the corresponding PubMed EFSA abstract were used to extract findings [1][4][9].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** No explicit "hydroxypropyl starch" listing was found in searched 21 CFR Part 182/184 XML endpoints; in the retrieved Title 21 text this additive is handled under food additive regulation for modified starches [7][10].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR § 172.892 (Food starch-modified)** [7].
- **Conditions of use (key excerpts relevant to hydroxypropylation pathways):**
  - "Food starch-modified ... may be safely used in food" with limits for treatment reagents [7].
  - Etherification entries include `propylene oxide, not to exceed 25 percent` and combinations with epichlorohydrin; residual propylene chlorohydrin limit is `not more than 5 ppm` in food starch-modified [7].

- **Last evaluation year shown:** 2018 (commented as "Considered for specification only"); toxicological ADI entry shown for 2016 is NOT SPECIFIED [3].
- **Functional class:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [3].

### IARC
- **Classification for E1440/hydroxypropyl starch:** No listing found in the IARC list data searched for hydroxypropyl starch/CAS 9049-76-7 [5][6].
- **Context:** The same IARC dataset contains entries for process reagents (e.g., propylene oxide Group 2B; epichlorohydrin Group 2A), but this is not an IARC classification of E1440 itself [6].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban was identified in the EFSA/JECFA/eCFR sources reviewed in this run [1][3][7].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory synthesis (EFSA 2017 group re-evaluation including E1440):**
  - SCF/JECFA prior ADI framing "not specified";
  - no genotoxic concern based on in silico analyses;
  - no treatment-related effects relevant for human risk assessment in rats up to 31,000 mg/kg bw/day;
  - no safety concern at reported uses/use levels;
  - no need for a numerical ADI [4].
- **Animal evidence (mostly related hydroxypropyl-modified starches, not always E1440-only):**
  - Rat study (1998): hydroxypropyl-modified potato starch increased fecal bile acid excretion; effects depended on degree of substitution/modification [11].
  - Rat study (2001): hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate reduced Zn/Fe absorption in rats at tested conditions [12].
  - Chronic mouse feeding study (1986, hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate comparator set): high dietary inclusion produced GI/renal-adaptive findings; authors reported no evidence of carcinogenicity for products tested [13].
- **Human data:** Retrieved literature is limited for direct E1440-specific long-term clinical safety outcomes; some small human studies involve related hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate and metabolic endpoints rather than adverse-outcome safety endpoints [14].
- **Epidemiology:** No robust population epidemiology signal specific to E1440 was identified in the retrieved records [4][15].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA quantitative context available from accessible abstract:** Combined exposure to E1404-E1451 at 95th percentile (refined brand-loyal scenario) reported up to 3,053 mg/kg bw/day for the general population; a separate value for E1452 supplement consumers was up to 22.1 mg/kg bw/day [4].
- **E1440-specific intake estimates:** Not separately extracted from accessible records in this run (full EFSA/Wiley page access was blocked from this VM) [4][9].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA/JECFA use non-numerical ADI framing (not specified / no numerical ADI), so exceedance versus a numeric ADI is not directly applicable [3][4].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of processed foods may have higher modified-starch exposure, but E1440-specific vulnerable-group exceedance values were not available in accessible records [4].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA and JECFA both use non-numerical ADI framing ("not specified" / no numerical ADI) and EFSA found no safety concern at reported uses [3][4].
  - Evidence against: E1440 is a chemically modified starch (semi-synthetic) rather than an endogenous/native molecule; some related high-dose animal studies report physiological effects (e.g., mineral absorption shifts/GI-renal adaptive findings), even if not interpreted as strong hazard at normal exposure [11][12][13].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: EU and JECFA evaluations are permissive; US regulation allows food starch-modified use under explicit conditions; no direct IARC classification for E1440 found [3][4][6][7].
  - Evidence against: Sparse modern, additive-specific long-term human safety evidence; much recent literature is materials/technology oriented rather than direct chronic toxicology for E1440 [15].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Related hydroxypropyl-modified starch animal findings at high doses (e.g., altered mineral absorption, GI/renal changes) justify some caution when extrapolating across modified-starch types [11][12][13].
  - Evidence against: Regulatory assessments integrating broader evidence concluded no safety concern at reported uses and did not set a numerical ADI for this group [3][4].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: No direct evidence found for high-tier triggers (no major-jurisdiction ban found; no E1440-specific IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found; no EFSA inability-to-confirm-safety conclusion) [1][3][5][6][7].
  - Evidence against: Existing authoritative regulatory conclusions remain permissive under stated use conditions [3][4][7].

### 2. Rationale
Regulatory evidence is consistently permissive (EFSA/JECFA non-numerical ADI framing and no safety concern conclusions; FDA/eCFR listing with conditions). Counterweights are mostly uncertainty-related: limited E1440-specific modern epidemiology and reliance on read-across/related-modified-starch data for some toxicology context.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`.**
