# E472a (Acetic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E472a (INS 472a) [S6].
- **Chemical name/class**: Acetic and fatty acid esters of glycerol; mixture of acetylated mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids [S6, S4].
- **Common synonyms**: Acetic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides; acetoglycerides; acetylated mono- and diglycerides [S6].
- **CAS number(s)**: No single CAS number is shown in the consulted EFSA/eCFR/JECFA primary records for the E472a mixture; this is treated as a compositionally variable mixture in those sources [S1, S4, S6].
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (produced from edible fats/monoglycerides via acetylation or interesterification) [S4].

## Function in Food
- **Primary function**: Emulsifier (JECFA functional class: food additive, emulsifier) [S6].
- **Mechanism of action**: Amphiphilic glyceride esters reduce oil-water interfacial tension and help stabilize dispersions.
- **Common use context**: In the U.S. regulation, E472a (acetylated monoglycerides) is permitted broadly "in or on food" and in food-processing/packing/storage equipment at levels reasonably required for technical effect [S4].

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Re-evaluation (2020)**: EFSA concluded no adverse effects relevant for humans were identified for E472a-f; for **E472a/b/c, no numerical ADI was needed** [S1, S3].
  - **Latest EFSA follow-up (2025)**: EFSA assessed updated specifications for E472a,b,d,e,f and concluded no safety concern for the proposed impurity limits (including toxic elements and sum of 3-MCPD + glycidyl esters) at reported uses/exposure assumptions [S2, S14].
  - **Approval status**: Remains authorized in the EU; recent work is specification refinement rather than withdrawal [S1, S2].
- **FDA / eCFR (U.S.)**:
  - **CFR citation**: **21 CFR 172.828 (Acetylated monoglycerides)** [S4].
  - **Conditions of use**: Must meet composition/specification limits (including Reichert-Meissl value and acid value), defined manufacturing pathways, and be used only at amounts reasonably required for intended effect [S4].
  - **GRAS status**: Inference from eCFR structure indicates this substance is regulated as an **approved direct food additive** under Part 172, not as a GRAS-affirmed substance under Part 184 by this name [S4].
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **ADI**: **Not limited** (non-numeric), with comments on total tartaric acid intake in the broader grouped context [S6, S7].
  - **Last evaluation year shown**: **1973** (with earlier historical evaluations listed) [S6].
- **IARC**:
  - No matching IARC Monographs agent entry/classification found for E472a or its primary synonyms in the IARC classification pages/dataset checked [S8, S9, S10].
- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC materials [S1, S2, S4, S6].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (EFSA/JECFA)**:
  - EFSA (2020) considered E472a-f to be extensively hydrolyzed to normal dietary constituents and did not identify adverse effects relevant for humans in the available database [S3].
  - JECFA toxicology monograph describes acetoglycerides as readily hydrolyzed and handled similarly to other glycerides; ADI conclusion was not limited [S7].
- **Animal data**:
  - Legacy animal data in JECFA monograph include acute, short-term and long-term studies, with interpretation emphasizing metabolic breakdown to common constituents [S7].
- **Epidemiology / newer human-context evidence (mostly indirect, not E472a-specific)**:
  - 2024 French cohort: higher intake of **E471** (mono- and diglycerides, not E472a) associated with higher risk of some cancers; observational and not causal [S12].
  - 2024 IBD cohort study: higher total emulsifier exposure in IBD vs controls; exposure data include MDGs/E471 and other emulsifiers, not E472a-specific risk quantification [S13].
- **Mechanistic concerns (indirect class evidence)**:
  - 2024 mouse study reported metabolic and gut-microbiome effects for common emulsifiers, including mono-/diglycerides; relevance to E472a at real-world human doses remains uncertain [S11].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure framing**:
  - EFSA calculated exposure scenarios for E472 additives and concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels in the 2020 re-evaluation context [S3].
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - Classical numerical ADI exceedance analysis is not applicable for E472a because EFSA set no numerical ADI for E472a/b/c and JECFA uses "not limited" [S3, S6, S7].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - Children/infants are generally higher per-kg consumers of food additives; in the 2025 EFSA specification follow-up, infant/young-child scenarios were included in impurity exposure considerations without identifying a safety concern under proposed limits [S2].

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA no numerical ADI for E472a; JECFA ADI not limited; regulatory toxicology indicates hydrolysis to common dietary constituents [S3, S6, S7].
  - Evidence against this tier: modern E472a-specific human longitudinal safety evidence is sparse; class-level emulsifier concerns exist (mostly indirect) [S11, S12, S13].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: authorized by EFSA and FDA, with explicit manufacturing/specification controls; no major regulator identified current safety concern at permitted uses [S1, S2, S4].
  - Evidence against this tier: residual uncertainty from indirect emulsifier literature and limited contemporary E472a-targeted studies [S11, S12].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: indirect signals from broader emulsifier literature (microbiome/metabolic and some observational cancer associations for E471) justify caution [S11, S12].
  - Evidence against this tier: those signals are not specific, causal, or sufficient to overturn current regulatory conclusions for E472a [S1, S2, S3].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: little to none in consulted primary regulatory datasets.
  - Evidence against this tier: no IARC carcinogen group assignment found, no major-jurisdiction ban found, no EFSA/FDA/JECFA withdrawal signal [S1, S2, S4, S6, S8, S9, S10].

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest direct evidence is still from regulatory toxicology and specifications work, which is broadly reassuring for approved uses.
- The main uncertainty is not a demonstrated high hazard signal, but the limited amount of modern E472a-specific human data and indirect class-level emulsifier concerns.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**.
