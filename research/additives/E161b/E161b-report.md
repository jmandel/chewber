# E161b (Lutein) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E161b (lutein).
- **Primary chemical:** Lutein (xanthophyll carotenoid).
- **CAS number(s):** 127-40-2 (lutein from *Tagetes erecta*).
- **Chemical class:** Oxygenated carotenoid (xanthophyll).
- **Common synonyms:** Vegetable lutein, vegetable luteol, Bo-Xan; chemical names include all-trans-lutein and beta,epsilon-carotene-3,3'-diol.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **natural-source** for food-additive context (marigold/*Tagetes erecta* extract), with related synthetic carotenoids evaluated in group assessments.

**Source basis:** FAO JECFA Monograph 22 (2018) lutein specification page and WHO JECFA chemical page for "LUTEIN from TAGETES ERECTA".

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Lutein pigments absorb blue-violet light and impart yellow-orange color (carotenoid chromophore behavior).
- **Primary technological function:** Colour additive.
- **Secondary function:** Nutrient supplement.
- **Common use categories identified in retrieved regulatory texts:**
  - Broad food-additive uses in EU context (EFSA re-evaluation of authorised uses).
  - Chicken feed pigmentation use in US CFR (yellowing chicken skin/eggs).

**Source basis:** EFSA re-evaluation context (authorised uses), FAO JECFA Monograph 22 "Functional uses: Colour, nutrient supplement", and eCFR 21 CFR 73.295.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval/authorization context:** EFSA 2010 re-evaluation states lutein (E161b) under "currently authorised uses" (i.e., authorized additive context in EU).
- **ADI:** **1 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **ADI basis:** Derived from a NOAEL of **200 mg/kg bw/day** from rat chronic/two-generation studies with an uncertainty factor of 200.
- **Evaluation year(s):**
  - **2010**: Full re-evaluation opinion (EFSA Journal 8(5):1634).
  - **2012**: EFSA follow-up statement on new use-level exposure data (EFSA Journal 10(3):2589), with refined exposure estimates.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2010 EFSA re-evaluation established ADI 1 mg/kg bw/day and concluded high-end exposure could exceed ADI in some child groups under certain assumptions.
  - 2012 follow-up statement reported refined estimates generally below ADI, except high-level pre-school/school children.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 73.295** (Tagetes (Aztec marigold) meal and extract).
- **Regulatory status in CFR:** **Approved color additive (animal feed context), exempt from certification**.
- **Conditions of use (from 21 CFR 73.295):**
  - May be safely used **in chicken feed**.
  - Intended effect: enhance yellow color of chicken skin and eggs.
  - Feed must satisfy ethoxyquin tolerance conditions (cross-reference to §573.380).
- **GRAS status check in CFR Title 21:**
  - Scan of Part 184 (GRAS direct human food ingredients) found **no lutein/tagetes listing**.
  - Therefore, in CFR text reviewed here, lutein is not codified as a standalone Part 184 GRAS ingredient; it appears as a color additive regulation under Part 73.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Current JECFA ADI:** **Not specified** (group ADI for lutein from *Tagetes erecta*, lutein esters from *Tagetes erecta*, and synthetic zeaxanthin).
- **Last evaluation year:** **2018** (86th JECFA).
- **Prior ADI history:** 2004 group ADI **0-2 mg/kg bw** (superseded by 2018 "not specified" group ADI).

### IARC
- Checked IARC Monographs classification pages and current classification dataset bundle.
- **Finding:** No lutein-specific classification entry identified in the retrieved IARC list dataset.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified from the retrieved EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and toxicology
- A recent rodent toxicology study (PMID 41596229, 2026) reported acute oral LD50 > 2000 mg/kg and no treatment-related adverse effects in a 6-month oral study of a lutein preparation.
- Note: this study used a specific novel lutein matrix (silk lutein extract), so external validity to all E161b commercial preparations is limited.

### Human/clinical evidence
- 2025 pediatric RCT (PMID 41342624) reported favorable safety framing for 6-month lutein ester supplementation.
- 2025 infant formula non-inferiority safety trial including lutein among formula components (PMID 41406156) reported similar total adverse events vs comparator.
- 2025 Cochrane review in preterm infants (PMID 40292760) reported **no adverse effects** in included studies of lutein/zeaxanthin supplementation.

### Mechanistic/regulatory concerns
- The main recurring concern in regulatory documents is **exposure vs ADI in high-consuming children**, rather than strong intrinsic toxicity signals at ordinary intake levels.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2010:** exposure estimates based on maximum permitted levels were above ADI in multiple age groups; estimates based on reported use levels were lower, but high-end child exposure could still exceed ADI in some cases.
- **EFSA 2012 statement (new use-level data):** refined estimates were lower than ADI for most groups, with exceedance concern persisting for high-level pre-school and school children.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** present mainly for **high consumers among children**.
- **Vulnerable populations:** toddlers/pre-school/school children with high additive exposure; preterm infant populations remain clinically sensitive (though trials reviewed did not show adverse events).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - JECFA group ADI is "not specified" (2018), which is generally a low-concern signal.
  - Recent clinical and toxicology publications did not show clear treatment-related adverse outcomes at studied doses.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA identified scenarios where high child exposure may exceed ADI.
  - EFSA maintained a numerical ADI (1 mg/kg bw/day), so this is not a no-limit EU conclusion.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks support continued use under defined conditions.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - Human studies reviewed did not report strong safety signals.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA exposure caveat in high-consuming children is a non-trivial regulatory concern.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA explicitly identified potential ADI exceedance in some child subpopulations.
  - Regulatory acceptability is contingent on exposure controls and use conditions.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - JECFA 2018 group ADI not specified and lack of robust harm signals in recent studies suggest risk is not broadly high.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Limited support: child high-end exposure exceedance concerns exist in EFSA assessments.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban found.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found for lutein.
  - EFSA and JECFA did not conclude general unsafe status at authorized/intended uses.

### 2. Rationale
The additive remains accepted by major regulatory systems with defined conditions, but EFSA exposure modeling repeatedly flags high-consuming child groups as the primary risk-management issue. This places E161b above a simple "limited" profile but clearly below "high" risk.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
