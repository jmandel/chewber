# E435 (Polysorbate 60) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E435
- **Name**: Polysorbate 60
- **INS**: 435
- **CAS number**: 9005-67-8
- **Chemical class**: Non-ionic surfactant/emulsifier (polyoxyethylene sorbitan fatty acid ester)
- **Key synonym(s)**: Polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monostearate; Tween 60
- **Origin**: **Semi-synthetic/synthetic** (made by esterifying sorbitol/sorbitan with stearic acid, then ethoxylation with ethylene oxide)

Data extracted from the WHO/JECFA chemical record and 21 CFR 172.836 text.

## Function in Food
- **Primary role**: Emulsifier/surfactant that lowers interfacial tension and stabilizes oil-water systems.
- **Mechanistic role**: The lipophilic fatty-acid ester region and hydrophilic polyoxyethylene chains allow dispersion/stabilization of fat droplets in aqueous matrices.
- **Typical food categories from U.S. permitted uses (21 CFR 172.836)**:
  - Cakes/cake mixes, cake icings/fillings
  - Confectionery coatings and cacao products
  - Nonstandardized dressings
  - Shortenings and edible oils
  - Frozen desserts (ice cream, sherbet, etc.)
  - Whipped edible oil toppings
  - Gelatin/pudding mixes and some beverage-related uses

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Approved food additive in the EU; re-evaluated by EFSA ANS Panel.
- **Opinion year**: 2015 (EFSA Journal 2015;13(7):4152).
- **ADI**: **Group ADI 25 mg/kg bw/day** for polysorbates E432-E436.
- **Key conclusions (from EFSA abstract/indexed text)**:
  - No safety concern for reported uses/use levels in the re-evaluation framework.
  - ADI based on NOAEL 2500 mg/kg bw/day and uncertainty factor 100.
  - Exposure in refined scenarios did not exceed ADI for some groups, but exceedance was reported in infants/children (and high consumers among adolescents in one brand-loyal scenario).

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal/toxicology evidence
- **Direct older toxicology signal (rat intestinal model)**: Tween 60 at experimental concentrations increased lysosomal enzyme release and slightly increased intestinal permeability in rat ileum preparations (Food Chem Toxicol, 1984; PMID 6094320). This indicates potential mucosal-barrier perturbation at tested conditions.
- **Historical high-dose studies in JECFA monograph corpus**: very high dietary levels of related polysorbates can produce GI effects (e.g., diarrhea, growth effects in some models), while lower levels were generally better tolerated.

### Human/epidemiological evidence
- **Direct human epidemiology specific to polysorbate 60 is limited** in the retrieved set.
- Recent human-relevant dietary work is mostly on **emulsifier classes** or mixed-polysorbate exposure rather than isolated E435 outcomes.

### Recent PubMed-retrieved evidence
- **Food Chem 2025 (Korean population, 9 emulsifiers)**: estimated intake for polysorbate 60 was low relative to ADI (reported as ~1.14% of ADI in whole population scenario), with no immediate safety concern under current intake patterns.
- **Nutrients 2020 (Crohn's context; mixed emulsifiers)**: supports biological plausibility that some emulsifiers may affect intestinal inflammatory markers, but effects were strongest for specific other emulsifiers (not a direct E435-only causal dataset).

### Mechanistic concerns
- Surfactant/emulsifier class concerns around gut barrier and microbiome are plausible, but **E435-specific modern causal evidence remains limited** compared with data for some other emulsifiers.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Health-based guidance value**: 25 mg/kg bw/day (EFSA group ADI and JECFA ADI range for the relevant group).
- **Population intake context**:
  - EFSA re-evaluation scenarios indicated possible ADI exceedance in younger groups under some high/brand-loyal scenarios.
  - A recent national intake study (Korea, 2025) estimated polysorbate 60 exposure well below ADI in the assessed population.
- **Vulnerable populations**:
  - Infants/children (higher food intake per kg body weight and scenario sensitivity).
  - Potentially high-intake "brand-loyal" consumers in specific food patterns.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Regulatory approvals remain in place (EFSA/FDA/JECFA).
  - ADI exists and many population estimates are below ADI.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA scenario language includes potential ADI exceedance in some younger groups.
  - Mechanistic/animal concerns about gut barrier effects at sufficient exposure.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved additive with established ADI and defined use limits.
  - Recent mixed-emulsifier intake data show low ADI utilization in at least one national dataset.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Not all scenarios are uniformly below ADI (per EFSA re-evaluation text for some younger groups).

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved substance but with caveat that ADI exceedance may occur in specific subpopulations/scenarios.
  - Some biological plausibility for GI/mucosal effects from surfactant-emulsifier behavior and older direct animal findings.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No strong, consistent modern human causal evidence showing major harm at typical current intake.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None strong from this dataset (no major bans; no IARC 2A/2B classification specific to E435 identified).
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Regulatory approvals and ADI frameworks remain active.

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are: (a) broad regulatory acceptance with ADI and use limits, (b) scenario-dependent exposure concerns in younger populations from EFSA, and (c) limited but non-zero mechanistic/animal concern signals for gut barrier effects.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate** (confidence: moderate).
