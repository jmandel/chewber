# E175 (Gold) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E175
- **Name:** Gold (metallic)
- **CAS number(s):** 7440-57-5 (EFSA); JECFA page currently shows no CAS value in its field for the entry but identifies INS 175.
- **Chemical class:** Elemental metal color additive (metallic pigment/coating).
- **Common synonyms:** Gold, metallic gold, gold leaf, Aurum, CI 77480.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily refined elemental gold from natural ore (processed to food-grade purity).

**Evidence**
- EFSA identifies gold as “elemental form of metal gold” and gives CAS 7440-57-5 / CI 77480 in the E175 re-evaluation abstract snippet [S1].
- JECFA entry is “GOLD (metallic)” with INS 175 [S4][S5].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Decorative surface coloring via inert metallic particles/leaf that reflect light and provide metallic appearance; not a nutritive or preservative function.
- **Common food categories:** Surface coating/decoration of confectionery, chocolates, and liqueurs.

**Evidence**
- EFSA notes EU use for external coating of confectionery and decoration of chocolates [S1].
- FDA/eCFR allows use “for the coating and decorative coloring of confections and liqueurs” (with GMP and max level constraints) [S2].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in the EU for specific decorative uses (quantum satis context in EFSA summary).
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation year:** 2016 (EFSA Journal opinion on E175).
- **ADI:** Not numerically defined by EFSA (no numeric mg/kg bw/day value set).
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA reported very low estimated exposure and considered E175 of minimal toxicological concern, while also noting uncertainty due to limited data and inability to exclude nanoparticle presence from particle-size data.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** No GRAS listing identified for gold in 21 CFR Part 184; instead, it is regulated as an approved **color additive exempt from certification**.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR § 73.129 (Gold)**.
- **Conditions of use (food):** Coating/decorative coloring of confections and liqueurs; level consistent with GMP, not more than 0.2% by weight in foods, excluding weight of any alcoholic beverage component [S2].

### JECFA / WHO
- **Entry:** GOLD (metallic), INS 175 [S4][S5].
- **ADI:** “NO ADI ALLOCATED” (1977 entry), with comment “Very limited use. Not considered to present a hazard” [S5].
- **Last listed year on current page:** 2018 note states “Not re-evaluated by JECFA, but the provisions for gold was withdrawn at CCFA50” [S5].
- **Interpretation:** Latest record is administrative (2018), while toxicological ADI language comes from older evaluation history.

### IARC
- **Classification for metallic gold/E175:** No direct IARC Monographs classification entry found for metallic gold (CAS 7440-57-5) in the current list dataset.
- IARC list data (updated 2026-02-25) includes “Goldenseal root powder” (Group 2B), which is unrelated to metallic gold food additive E175 [S6][S7].

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban was confirmed in the reviewed sources.
- JECFA page indicates Codex-related withdrawal of provisions for gold at CCFA50 in 2018 [S5].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **90-day oral food-grade gold in mice (2023):** Sex-dependent changes in gut microbiota and local gut immune system reported at human-relevant exposure framing [S8].
- **90-day oral AuNP study in rats (2018):** No mortality or major hematology/biochemistry shifts, but gold accumulation and renal findings at higher exposure conditions were reported [S9].

### Epidemiological data
- No robust human epidemiological dataset specific to dietary E175 safety was identified in the reviewed regulatory/PubMed sources [S1][S8][S9].

### Mechanistic concerns
- Nanoparticle-related uptake and tissue interaction are recurring concerns in the literature; review literature highlights that nanoscale fractions can alter toxicokinetics vs bulk particles [S10][S11].

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA’s exposure estimates (as reported in the EFSA abstract snippet) were low:
  - **Maximum estimate:** 1.32 microg/kg bw/day
  - **Refined estimate:** 0.33 microg/kg bw/day
- EFSA noted large uncertainty because use-level data were sparse and not representative [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** No numeric ADI exists (EFSA and JECFA both non-numeric/no ADI allocation), so classic percent-of-ADI exceedance is not directly applicable.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of decorated confectionery (especially children) remain the logical subgroup for uncertainty analysis, but direct exceedance metrics are unavailable due absent numeric ADI.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: metallic gold is minimally reactive; EFSA and JECFA did not derive a restrictive numeric ADI and described low concern/very limited hazard context [S1][S5].
  - Evidence against: EFSA highlighted data gaps and uncertainty; recent animal work reports microbiome/immune perturbations and dose-dependent organ findings in nanoparticle-focused settings [S1][S8][S9].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved in EU/US with constrained decorative uses; very low estimated dietary exposure; no clear human signal of severe harm in reviewed sources [S1][S2][S3].
  - Evidence against: unresolved nanoparticle characterization and modern mechanistic findings prevent calling risk trivial [S1][S8][S10].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: approved but with nontrivial evidence gaps; credible animal findings (gut microbiome/immune effects; renal findings at higher exposures in some studies); JECFA record includes non-re-evaluation/withdrawal note [S5][S8][S9].
  - Evidence against: current regulatory permissions remain in place for food decoration; no strong human epidemiological harm signal identified [S2][S3].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: none strong in reviewed evidence.
  - Evidence against: no confirmed major-jurisdiction ban for E175 and no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for metallic gold itself in current list data [S6][S7].

2. **Rationale**
- The central decision driver is the mismatch between historically low-regulatory-concern framing and newer nanoparticle-focused animal/mechanistic signals, combined with sparse modern human exposure/safety data.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
