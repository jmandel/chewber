# E297 (Fumaric acid) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E297 (INS 297)
- **Name**: Fumaric acid
- **CAS number**: 110-17-8
- **Chemical class**: Unsaturated dicarboxylic acid (trans-butenedioic acid; 2(trans)-butenedioic acid)
- **Common synonyms**: allomaleic acid; boletic acid; (E)-butenedioic acid; (E)-1,2-ethylenedicarboxylic acid
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Naturally occurring metabolite (tricarboxylic acid cycle; reported in cells and foods) but food-additive material is typically manufactured industrially (treated here as synthetic commercial origin).

## Function in Food
- **Primary role**: Acidity regulator/acidulant (INS functional class: acidity regulator).
- **Other roles**: Flavouring-related use contexts and preservative-related technological use are described in JECFA/EFSA materials.
- **Mechanism of action**: Lowers product pH (acidifying effect), shaping sour taste and helping suppress growth conditions for some microorganisms.
- **Common food categories**: Powdered beverage bases, fruit juice drinks, dry gelatin dessert systems, and other acidified foods where permitted use levels are applied.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Permitted in the EU; re-evaluation is ongoing (call for data published in 2024).
- **ADI**:
  - EFSA OpenFoodTox records the historical SCF ADI as **0-6 mg/kg bw/day** (opinion year **1990**), with endpoint basis including rat nephrocalcinosis and NOAEL 600 mg/kg bw/day (UF 100).
  - EFSA 2024 re-evaluation background document also notes SCF confirmation of 6 mg/kg bw/day and the JECFA historical pathway (temporary 0-6 mg/kg bw/day in 1974, then changed later).
- **Latest EFSA activity year**: 2024 (data call/re-evaluation process; not a final new ADI).
- **Key EFSA conclusions/data points**:
  - Additive is currently permitted in food in EU legislation while re-evaluation is pending.
  - Preliminary refined exposure assessment (MPL-based, conservative) reported high-end intakes up to **54.7 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers** (95th percentile), with children also high.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status**: **Approved** direct food additive under **21 CFR 172.350**.
- **GRAS status**:
  - In the current Title 21 eCFR structure, fumaric acid appears under §172.350 rather than as a dedicated Part 184 GRAS fumaric-acid section.
  - Part 184 includes many GRAS entries (and ferrous fumarate at §184.1307d), but no separate “fumaric acid” GRAS section heading was found in the accessed 2026-02-25 Title 21 XML.
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 172.350)**:
  - Fumaric acid purity: minimum 99.5% (anhydrous basis).
  - Calcium/magnesium/potassium/sodium salts: minimum 99% (anhydrous basis).
  - General use level: not more than amount reasonably required to accomplish intended effect.
  - Ferrous fumarate: permitted as iron source in foods for special dietary use, consistent with good nutrition practice.

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI**: **Not specified** (group ADI for fumaric acid and salts).
- **Evaluation timeline**:
  - JECFA database entry indicates ADI “NOT SPECIFIED (1989)”.
  - The chemical page shows evaluation year 1999 with statement that the 1989 group ADI “not specified” was maintained at the 53rd meeting.
- **Key conclusion**: No safety concern at current levels of intake when used as a flavouring agent (1999 comment text).

### IARC
- **Classification**: No fumaric acid entry was identified on the IARC “Agents classified by the IARC” page (no Group 1/2A/2B/3 listing found for fumaric acid in the accessed page content).

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction bans were identified in the consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies and toxicology basis
- EFSA OpenFoodTox (SCF-derived endpoint) reports critical effect **nephrocalcinosis** in rats, with NOAEL **600 mg/kg bw/day** from a 2-year rat study and ADI derivation (UF 100) to 0-6 mg/kg bw/day.
- JECFA evaluations (1989, maintained in 1999) concluded no safety concern at flavouring-use exposure and maintained ADI “not specified”.

### Epidemiological/human data
- No robust modern epidemiological signal specific to dietary E297 intake was identified in the consulted regulatory and PubMed sources.

### Mechanistic/other concerns
- EFSA feed-additive renewal opinion (2024; PubMed record) noted fumaric acid is irritant to skin/eyes/respiratory tract and considered skin/respiratory sensitiser in occupational handling context (nickel impurity context), while concluding safety under authorized terrestrial-animal-use conditions.
- A 2025 PubMed safety signal paper concerns **fumaric acid esters (drugs)** and serious GI adverse reactions; this is chemically related but not equivalent to E297 dietary use, so relevance is indirect.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2024 background document gives conservative preliminary dietary exposure (mg/kg bw/day):
  - **Toddlers**: mean 6.4-24.6; 95th percentile 20.6-54.7
  - **Children**: mean 3.9-17.7; 95th percentile 11.8-42.9
  - **Adolescents**: mean 2.0-9.1; 95th percentile 5.6-23.2
  - **Adults**: mean 1.4-6.6; 95th percentile 4.1-15.5
  - **Elderly**: mean 1.4-6.2; 95th percentile 3.8-14.5
- Relative to the historical SCF ADI (0-6 mg/kg bw/day), high-end intake estimates can exceed ADI, especially toddlers/children.
- Relative to JECFA’s group ADI “not specified,” regulatory concern is lower, but EFSA re-evaluation remains open.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups**: toddlers and children (highest estimated intake per body weight).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Endogenous metabolite and naturally present in foods.
  - JECFA group ADI “not specified,” maintained with no safety concern statement in flavouring context.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - SCF-derived EFSA ADI is numerical (0-6 mg/kg bw/day), not “not specified.”
  - Conservative EFSA exposure estimates exceed 6 mg/kg bw/day in younger groups.
  - Re-evaluation is still pending.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - EU and US authorization remains in place.
  - FDA conditions constrain purity and functional use level to amount reasonably required.
  - No clear IARC carcinogenic classification.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - Historical toxicology endpoint includes nephrocalcinosis at high-dose animal testing.
  - EFSA preliminary intake estimates indicate possible ADI exceedance (SCF benchmark), especially children.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Approved additive but with unresolved EFSA re-evaluation process.
  - ADI exceedance risk appears possible in children/toddlers under conservative EFSA scenario.
  - Credible animal toxicology endpoint exists (renal effect at high doses).
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - JECFA maintained “not specified” ADI and reported no safety concern at flavouring-use intake.
  - No direct modern epidemiological harm signal for typical dietary E297 exposure was identified.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Limited support: there is an animal toxicology basis and conservative exposure exceedance vs SCF ADI.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification identified.
  - Regulatory approvals remain active.

### 2. Rationale
The main tension is between older SCF numerical ADI (0-6 mg/kg bw/day; renal endpoint basis) and JECFA’s group ADI “not specified” maintained in 1999. EFSA’s 2024 background exposure modeling shows potentially high intake in toddlers/children under conservative assumptions, and EFSA’s food-additive re-evaluation is still unfinished. This supports caution above “limited,” but evidence does not justify “high.”

### 3. Recommended tier
- **moderate**
