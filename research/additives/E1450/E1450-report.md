# E1450 Research Report: Starch sodium octenyl succinate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1450 (INS 1450) [1][5]
- **Name:** Starch sodium octenyl succinate (also referred to in JECFA as **octenyl succinic acid (OSA)-modified starch**) [1][5]
- **CAS number(s):** 66829-29-0 (ECHA entry for “Starch, hydrogen octenylbutanedioate, sodium salt”) [12]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (starch ester; OSA-modified starch) [1][5]
- **Common synonyms:** Starch sodium octenyl succinate; SSOS; OSA-modified starch; INS 1450; E 1450 [1][5]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** **Semi-synthetic** (native starch chemically esterified with octenyl succinic anhydride) [1][4][5]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic starch derivative used to stabilize oil-water systems; functions as emulsifier/stabilizer/thickener [1][5].
- **Common food categories:**
  - Infant/special medical formula categories in EU authorizations discussed by EFSA [1]
  - Beverages and beverage bases (specific modified-starch treatment pathways in U.S. CFR) [4]
  - Broader food additive roles as emulsifier/stabilizer/thickener (JECFA functional class) [5]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorized food additive with re-evaluation follow-up completed for infants and broader population uses [1][2].
- **Latest EFSA opinion/re-evaluation found:** 2020 EFSA FAF opinion (EFSA Journal 2020;18(8):5874) [1][13].
- **ADI:** EFSA reiterated that there is **no need for a numerical ADI** (historically aligned with “not specified” framing for modified starches) [1][2].
- **Key conclusions (2020):**
  - No indication for safety concern for infants below 16 weeks at exposures within the clinical-study range (up to **2,725 mg/kg bw/day**) [1].
  - Clinical data quality was limited (mostly high risk of bias), and a formal reference point could not be derived [1].
  - EFSA noted exposure estimates at reported/max use levels can exceed the high end of clinical-trial exposure ranges in some infant scenarios [1].
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA journal pages on `efsa.europa.eu` were visited but blocked in this VM by anti-bot challenge; EFSA conclusions were extracted from PubMed/PMCID EFSA records [1][2][13][14].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not listed as a standalone GRAS affirmation in the retrieved CFR context; handled as a **regulated food additive** under modified starch provisions [3][4].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR § 172.892** [3][4].
- **Conditions of use (from retrieved CFR text):**
  - Modified starch esterification with **1-octenyl succinic anhydride** is listed with limits (e.g., not to exceed **3%** in the relevant treatment pathway) [4].
  - A listed OSA-modified pathway is limited to use as stabilizer/emulsifier in **beverages and beverage bases** [4].
  - In the eCFR section page retrieved via browser tool for this additive name, use in nonalcoholic beverages/beverage bases was shown at **not more than 0.2% by weight of the finished beverage** and with nonstandardized-food labeling language for the additive name [3].

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA entry:** OSA-modified starch chemical record (INS 1450) [5].
- **ADI:** **Not specified** (explicitly shown in toxicological evaluation entries, including 2016/2014; originally assigned at earlier meeting and reaffirmed) [5].
- **Last evaluation year shown:** **2018** (noted as “considered for specification only”); ADI entries remain “not specified” [5].

### IARC
- **Classification result:** No specific IARC monograph classification entry found for starch sodium octenyl succinate / OSA-modified starch in the current IARC classification list dataset reviewed [6][7].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in EFSA/JECFA/eCFR sources reviewed in this run [1][3][4][5].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA/JECFA):**
  - EFSA 2020: no clear adverse effect signal in submitted infant clinical/piglet datasets, but uncertainty prevented deriving a reference point [1].
  - EFSA 2017 modified-starch group re-evaluation: no safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no numerical ADI needed [2].
  - JECFA: ADI not specified across evaluations for INS 1450 record [5].
- **Animal evidence:**
  - Neonatal piglet dietary study (2014) reported no treatment-related adverse effects up to 20 g/L in formula over 3 weeks [8].
- **Recent PubMed safety-relevant studies (mostly formulation/biomaterials context, not chronic regulatory toxicology):**
  - 2024 in vitro digestion/cell work reported lack of cytotoxicity in tested formulations [9].
  - 2025 comparative nanoemulsion work reported non-toxicity in tested gastric cell model [10].
  - 2025 digestibility work indicates altered starch digestibility/resistant starch behavior, not direct hazard evidence [11].
- **Epidemiology:** No robust human epidemiological signal specifically implicating E1450 was identified in the retrieved PubMed records [15].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Infants <16 weeks, FSMP (EFSA 2020):**
  - Regulatory maximum scenario: mean ~4,000 and high ~5,200 mg/kg bw/day [1].
  - Refined scenario (mean reported use): mean ~1,676 and high ~2,179 mg/kg bw/day [1].
- **Carry-over exposure in infant formula category (EFSA 2020):**
  - Regulatory maximum scenario: mean ~220 and high ~286 mg/kg bw/day [1].
  - Refined scenario (mean reported use): mean ~12 and high ~16 mg/kg bw/day [1].
- **ADI exceedance framing:** There is no numerical ADI, but EFSA’s “no indication for concern” conclusion in infants was tied to exposure range represented by available clinical data (up to ~2,725 mg/kg bw/day), and EFSA noted some exposure scenarios could exceed this range [1].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Infants consuming special medical formulas at high-end or maximum permitted use levels are the most relevant at-risk subgroup for uncertainty-driven exceedance of the clinical-exposure evidence range [1].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA/JECFA conclusions are broadly permissive and non-numerical ADI framing (“not specified”/no numerical ADI) is consistent with low concern under intended uses [1][2][5].
  - Evidence against placement: Additive is chemically modified (semi-synthetic), and infant datasets had important uncertainty/data-quality limitations; EFSA could not derive a firm reference point [1].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting placement: Regulatory approvals remain in place (EU/US/JECFA context), no IARC classification signal found, and no consistent adverse-effect pattern in core animal/clinical datasets used by regulators [1][3][4][5][6][7][8].
  - Evidence against placement: Infant high-end exposure scenarios can exceed the range directly represented by clinical data used in EFSA’s conclusion [1].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting placement: There are explicit caveats for infants (data gaps, risk-of-bias concerns, and potential exposure above clinical-evidence range) [1].
  - Evidence against placement: Regulators still concluded no indication for safety concern within evaluated conditions and did not move to restriction/withdrawal [1][2][5].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting placement: Very limited; no major-jurisdiction bans or IARC Group 2A/2B classification of this additive were found [5][6][7].
  - Evidence against placement: Core regulatory bodies continue to permit use with no carcinogenic classification signal specific to this additive [1][3][4][5].

### 2. Rationale
The evidence base supports regulatory acceptance with low-to-moderate uncertainty. The main uncertainty is concentrated in infant subpopulations where evidence quality is weaker and modeled high-end exposures may exceed clinical-study exposure ranges. Outside this caveat, authoritative regulatory evaluations remain consistently permissive.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`.**
