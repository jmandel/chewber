- **Primary function:** Colour additive (black pigment).

- **Regulatory functional class evidence:** WHO/JECFA lists functional class as **Food Additives / COLOUR**.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- **2018 (Particle and Fibre Toxicology):** Intragastric exposure to food-grade E153 in rats was associated with modest but statistically significant vasomotor changes (coronary artery/aorta responses), without clear systemic oxidative-stress biomarker differences.
  - URL (PubMed abstract): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29482579/
- **2012 (Toxicological Sciences):** Repeated oral gavage of carbon black nanoparticles in lean/obese Zucker rats associated with endothelial dysfunction (reduced acetylcholine-mediated vasorelaxation).
  - URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22610611/
- **2012 review (Current Molecular Medicine):** Summarized evidence for oxidative DNA damage after GI exposure to insoluble particles including carbon black.
  - URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22292440/
- **1987 (Toxicology Letters):** Reported co-carcinogenic signal in a rat colon-tumour model under high-fat diet + DMH conditions with carbon-black ingestion.
  - URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3603592/

### Toxicokinetics / counter-evidence
- **2022 (Particle and Fibre Toxicology):** Radiolabelled carbon black oral gavage study in rats found ~98–99% recovery in feces and no detectable blood/systemic organ radioactivity above quantitation limits.
  - URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36242080/

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** Could not be robustly quantified from accessible official sources in this session.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not computable against a numerical ADI, because WHO shows “NO ADI ALLOCATED” and EFSA re-evaluation did not establish a numeric ADI from accessible data.
- **Main uncertainty driver:** EFSA-reported absence of reliable exposure data and insufficient oral toxicology evidence for risk characterization.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of foods containing black pigments, children, and people with vascular/metabolic vulnerability are plausible concern groups by analogy to animal vascular findings; however, human intake-risk quantification is currently weak.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: WHO/JECFA did not set a positive numeric ADI requirement in the current record (“NO ADI ALLOCATED”); 2022 oral toxicokinetic data suggest minimal systemic translocation in rats.
  - Evidence against: EFSA reported insufficient data to assess safety; animal oral studies show vascular effects and oxidative-stress-related findings.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Historical international use as colour additive; JECFA record exists; some kinetic data suggest low systemic uptake after oral exposure.
  - Evidence against: U.S. food regulatory position is not supportive (termination of provisional carbon-black listing for food-use context), and EFSA did not confirm safety due to data gaps.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Regulatory ambiguity/caveats (EFSA data insufficiency), plus credible animal oral studies indicating vascular and oxidative-DNA-effect signals at repeated exposures.
  - Evidence against: Lack of strong human epidemiology for oral dietary E153 risk and limited systemic absorption in recent rat toxicokinetic work.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: IARC carbon black is Group 2B (possible carcinogenic hazard), and there is historical U.S. termination language for provisional food-use listing.
  - Evidence against: IARC classification is hazard-based (not specific to oral dietary doses), and current evidence does not show consistent high-certainty oral carcinogenic risk at food-use levels.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest determinants are: (a) EFSA inability to complete a positive safety assessment due to data deficiencies, (b) non-supportive U.S. food regulatory history for carbon black, (c) animal oral studies showing biologically relevant vascular/oxidative signals, and (d) partial counterbalance from low oral systemic translocation data.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
