# E416 (Karaya gum) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E416 (INS 416) [S1][S4]
- **Primary name:** Karaya gum (sterculia gum) [S1][S4][S5]
- **CAS number(s):** 9000-36-6 [S4][S5]
- **Chemical class:** Natural plant exudate **acidic polysaccharide hydrocolloid** from *Sterculia* species [S3][S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Karaya; Gum sterculia; Sterculia; Kadaya; Katilo; Kullo; Kuterra; Gum karaya [S4][S5]
- **Origin:** Natural (tree exudate) [S3][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Water-swellable hydrocolloid that increases viscosity and stabilizes texture/emulsions; JECFA monograph describes minimal digestion/degradation and mechanical laxative behavior at high intakes [S6].
- **Regulatory functional classes:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [S4][S5].
- **Common food categories (US CFR maximum-use table):**
  - Frozen dairy desserts and mixes: 0.3%
  - Milk products: 0.02%
  - Soft candy: 0.9%
  - All other food categories: 0.002% [S3]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorized food additive in EU; EFSA states E416 is authorised for food use and re-evaluated it under Regulation (EU) No 257/2010 [S1].
- **ADI:** **No numerical ADI required** (EFSA: no need for a numerical ADI) [S1].
- **Evaluation year:** **2016** (adopted 31 May 2016; published 26 July 2016) [S1].
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No concern with respect to genotoxicity.
  - No adverse effects in carcinogenicity studies at highest tested doses.
  - No safety concern for the general population at refined exposure for reported uses/levels.
  - Adults should not consume more than about **7,000 mg/person/day** because abdominal discomfort was reported at this intake [S1].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS framework under **21 CFR Part 184** (direct food substances affirmed as generally recognized as safe), specifically **21 CFR 184.1349 Karaya gum (sterculia gum)** [S2].
- **Conditions of use:** FDA CFR section provides explicit maximum use levels by food category (see Function section) and requires food-grade specifications [S3].
- **Access note:** `ecfr.gov/current/.../section-184.1349` returned an access challenge in this environment; data were extracted from the official eCFR XML endpoint [S7][S2][S3].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic toxicology
- JECFA (FAS 24) reported lack of toxicity in high-dietary-level studies and described minimal degradation/absorption, with ADI "not specified" at the evaluation stage [S6].
- JECFA mutagenicity section reported no consistent mutagenic signal in the reviewed test battery summaries [S6].

### Human data
- JECFA monograph describes short-term human tolerance at **10.5 g/day for 21 days** without adverse biochemical/haematological findings in the cited study; older data also reported abdominal discomfort in some subjects at 7 g/day granules [S6].
- EFSA used human tolerance information and advised avoiding intakes above ~7,000 mg/day in adults due to abdominal discomfort [S1].

### Allergy / hypersensitivity signals
- JECFA monograph cites historical reports of inhalation/oral allergic sensitivity (asthma/dermatitis/GI symptoms) in exposed individuals [S6].

### Recent PubMed safety signal check
- Recent PubMed search (2015-2026) returns mostly materials/drug-delivery papers, with limited direct oral food-additive toxicology [S10].
- Relevant case-based signals include:
  - Drug-induced severe liver injury case linked to *Sterculia versicolor* exposure (non-food context, weak direct relevance to E416 food use) [S11].
  - Esophageal bezoar due to karaya gum granules used as laxative (high-dose medicinal use context) [S12].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** Numerical population intake distributions were not directly retrievable from the EFSA full text in this environment; however, EFSA reports no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses/use levels [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not directly applicable for EFSA/JECFA because ADI is non-numerical/not specified [S1][S5][S6].
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Individuals consuming high medicinal/laxative bolus doses (GI discomfort/obstruction risk context).
  - Individuals with hypersensitivity to gum karaya exposure [S6][S11][S12].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Natural plant gum with long regulatory use history.
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed, no general-population safety concern at refined exposure.
  - JECFA: ADI not specified [S1][S5][S6].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Historical hypersensitivity reports exist.
  - GI discomfort reported at high intakes (adult ~7 g/day threshold in EFSA context) [S1][S6].

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EU authorized and re-evaluated positively.
  - US CFR placement in Part 184 GRAS framework with defined limits.
  - JECFA ADI not specified and no strong systemic toxicity signal in reviewed data [S1][S2][S3][S5][S6].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Non-zero allergy/hypersensitivity evidence and high-dose GI effects [S1][S6].

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Credible but limited case-level concerns (allergy, high-dose GI events, isolated non-food liver injury case) [S6][S11][S12].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No regulator-set restrictive ADI reduction in current EFSA/JECFA conclusions.
  - No broad-population exposure warning from EFSA refined exposure assessment [S1][S5].

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - No substantial evidence from reviewed regulatory datasets.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions.
  - No identified IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing.
  - EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain permissive [S1][S5][S8][S9].

### 2. Rationale
Regulatory consensus (EFSA, FDA/eCFR, JECFA) indicates low toxicological concern at authorized food-use levels, with non-numerical/unspecified ADI approaches and no broad population risk signal. The remaining concerns are mostly high-dose tolerance and historical hypersensitivity reports rather than strong evidence of chronic systemic toxicity from normal dietary exposure.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
