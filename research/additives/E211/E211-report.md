# E211 (Sodium benzoate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E211.
- **Name:** Sodium benzoate.
- **CAS number(s):** 532-32-1.
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salt of benzoic acid (aromatic carboxylate preservative salt).
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium benzoate; sodium salt of benzenecarboxylic acid; sodium salt of phenylcarboxylic acid; monosodium benzoate; benzoic acid sodium salt. [R5, R10]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** as a food additive (manufactured by neutralizing benzoic acid; CFR states the salt is not found to occur naturally). [R4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Preservative / antimicrobial agent. [R4, R5]
- **Mechanism of action:** Acts as a weak-acid preservative (benzoic acid/benzoate system), with antimicrobial effect primarily in acidic foods and beverages.
- **Common food categories:** Carbonated drinks (major contributor in one field exposure study), juices, cheeses, yogurts, and other acidic processed foods. [R8]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (re-evaluated within the benzoic acid/benzoate group: E210-E213).
- **Latest identified EFSA re-evaluation:** 2016 EFSA Journal opinion (DOI: `10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4433`). [R1, R2]
- **ADI:** **5 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as benzoic acid).
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA reported mean intakes below ADI in refined scenarios, but high-percentile intakes exceeded ADI in some younger groups (toddlers/children/adolescents), with broader exceedance when other benzoic-acid sources were included. [R2]
- **Access note:** The EFSA journal page was Cloudflare-blocked (HTTP 403) in this environment; metadata and conclusion text were captured via the EFSA-indexed search snippet plus DOI metadata. [R1, R2]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS framework applies (Part 184 substances are affirmed GRAS).
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 184.1733 (Sodium benzoate)**.
- **Conditions of use:**
  - Antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent/adjuvant uses.

- **Last evaluation year:** **2021** (92nd meeting); JECFA states this replaced the previous group ADI 0-5 mg/kg bw. [R5]

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- **Endocrine/reproductive toxicity signals (rats):** Recent studies reported dose-related hormone changes, gonadal histopathology, sperm impairment, oxidative stress, and neuroendocrine axis (kisspeptin/RFRP-3) disruption at tested doses. [R11, R12, R13]
- **Pancreatic/inflammatory mechanism:** Mouse/cell data linked sodium benzoate exposure with beta-cell inflammation/apoptosis and histone benzoylation-related signaling, indicating a plausible mechanistic pathway (preclinical evidence). [R14]
- **Cytogenotoxicity literature synthesis:** A 2025 systematic review in mammalian-cell studies reported frequent sodium-benzoate-associated genotoxic/cytotoxic findings in included datasets; interpretation remains limited by heterogeneity and model systems. [R9]

### Epidemiological / human-behavioral evidence
- **Randomized trial context (mixture exposure):** The Southampton trial (2007) found increased hyperactivity scores in children with test mixes containing artificial colors plus sodium benzoate; the design does not isolate sodium benzoate as the sole causal component. [R15]

### Regulatory-weighted interpretation
- EFSA and JECFA maintain ADI-based authorization, indicating no basis for outright prohibition at authorized exposures, but both frameworks support intake control and cumulative-exposure caution. [R2, R5]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (2016 opinion):** Mean exposures were below ADI, but high consumers among younger groups exceeded ADI in some scenarios; adding non-additive benzoic-acid sources broadened exceedance risk. [R2]
- **Recent field data (Algeria, 2024):** Mean estimated intakes were below ADI for sodium benzoate, while 95th-percentile scenarios exceeded ADI under some assumptions; carbonated drinks were the top contributor for E211 exposure. [R8]
- **Vulnerable populations:** Toddlers, children, adolescents, and high consumers of preservative-containing drinks/processed foods. [R2, R8]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Longstanding regulatory acceptance (EFSA/FDA/JECFA), no IARC carcinogenic classification.
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA high-percentile ADI exceedance in younger groups; recent animal/mechanistic signals prevent a "no credible harm" position.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Approved/GRAS status with established ADIs and explicit use controls; no major regulatory ban.
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA exceedance scenarios and a growing preclinical signal base (reproductive, inflammatory, cytogenotoxic endpoints) argue beyond "minor concerns only."

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Matches criteria directly: approved additive with caveats, ADI exceedance in some child/high-consumer scenarios, and credible animal/mechanistic concerns.
- **Evidence against this tier:** Human causal evidence for major chronic outcomes remains limited/inconclusive; many adverse findings are preclinical and dose/model dependent.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Some newer studies report biologically significant adverse endpoints.
- **Evidence against this tier:** No major-jurisdiction ban; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for sodium benzoate; EFSA/JECFA continue ADI-based authorization.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight is regulatory consensus plus exposure caveats: sodium benzoate remains authorized, but ADI exceedance risk in younger/high-intake subgroups and nontrivial recent preclinical toxicity signals justify caution above a simple limited-risk framing.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
