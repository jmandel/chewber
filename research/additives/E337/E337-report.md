# E337 (Sodium potassium tartrate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E337
- **Primary name:** Sodium potassium tartrate (also called potassium sodium L(+)-tartrate)
- **CAS number(s):** 304-59-6 (tetrahydrate; listed in 21 CFR 184.1804), 6381-59-5 (anhydrous form listed in PubChem RN cross-references)
- **Chemical class:** Tartrate salt (mixed sodium/potassium salt of L(+)-tartaric acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Rochelle salt, Seignette salt, potassium sodium dextro-tartrate, INS 337
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **natural-derived** (CFR identity states it is obtained as a byproduct of wine manufacture), with industrial purification/formulation for food use.

## Function in Food
- **Functional roles:** Emulsifier and pH control agent (21 CFR 184.1804(c)(1)).
- **Mechanism of action:** As a tartrate salt, it contributes buffering/acidity control and metal-ion complexation behavior typical of tartrates; in cheese systems, FDA standards list it among permitted emulsifying salts to maintain homogeneous texture and melt behavior.
- **Common food categories (documented):**
  - Cheeses and jams/jellies under GMP via GRAS affirmation (21 CFR 184.1804(c)(2)).
  - Pasteurized process cheese, pasteurized process cheese food, and pasteurized process cheese spread as an emulsifying salt blend component (21 CFR 133.169(c), 133.173(e)(1), 133.179(e)); solids from emulsifying agents capped at 3% of finished product.

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest evaluation found:** EFSA Journal opinion `10.2903/j.efsa.2020.6030` (2020), covering E334/E335/E336/**E337**/E354.
- **Approval status:** **Approved** in EU food additive system (E-number remains authorized; EFSA re-evaluation concluded no safety concern at reported uses).
- **ADI:** **240 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI for L(+)-tartaric acid-tartrates, expressed as tartaric acid).
- **Key conclusions:** No indication of genotoxicity; no carcinogenicity signal in chronic rat study at highest tested dose; refined exposure estimates did not exceed the group ADI.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal studies
- EFSA re-evaluation reports no toxic effects (including nephrotoxicity) in studies where the L(+)-form was tested.
- Chronic rat study cited by EFSA reported no carcinogenicity signal for monosodium L(+)-tartrate at 3,100 mg/kg bw/day.
- Reproductive/developmental data reviewed by EFSA did not show relevant effects; explicit reproductive-toxicity dataset was limited.

### Human/epidemiological data
- Direct long-term human epidemiology specific to E337 is limited.
- High-dose case reports exist for related tartrate ingestion patterns:
  - Cream of tartar (potassium bitartrate) ingestion causing life-threatening hyperkalemia in humans (case reports).
  - Veterinary case-series evidence linking tartaric acid exposure to acute kidney injury in dogs after cream of tartar/tamarind ingestion.
- These involve unusual/high exposures and/or different tartrate salts, not typical E337 dietary use at GMP.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Potential concern at extreme intake scenarios appears linked to electrolyte load (for potassium-rich tartrate preparations) and high-dose tartaric acid exposure, rather than normal food-additive intake levels for E337.
- EFSA concluded no genotoxicity concern for tartaric acid and sodium/potassium tartrates.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA population exposure conclusion (2020):** Refined non-brand-loyal exposure estimates for assessed population groups did **not exceed** the group ADI of 240 mg/kg bw/day (as tartaric acid).
- **Additional dietary context:** French TDS publication on tartaric acid (E334) reported ADIs for assessed additives were generally not exceeded for tartaric acid under the study assumptions.
- **ADI exceedance risk for E337 specifically:** Low in general population at reported current use levels.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Individuals with conditions that increase sensitivity to electrolyte disturbances may warrant caution in extreme/non-typical intake situations.
  - Animal toxicology evidence in dogs should not be directly extrapolated to routine human E337 intake, but supports caution against concentrated tartrate ingestion.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Naturally occurring tartaric-acid chemistry and long-standing food use.
  - EFSA found no genotoxicity and no carcinogenicity signal in reviewed studies.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Formal ADIs are set by EFSA and JECFA (not “ADI not specified”).
  - High-dose adverse effects are documented for related tartrate exposures.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - FDA GRAS affirmation with GMP-based conditions in 21 CFR 184.1804.
  - EFSA group ADI established (240 mg/kg bw/day) and exposure not exceeding ADI in refined scenario.
  - No strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in major reviews.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Historical ADI differences (JECFA 0-30 mg/kg bw vs EFSA 240 mg/kg bw/day as tartaric acid) indicate uncertainty in harmonization.
  - Human epidemiological evidence directly on E337 remains limited.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Some uncertainty from sparse modern human data directly specific to E337.
  - Case reports on related tartrate forms show toxicity at excessive intakes.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No EFSA finding of ADI exceedance at reported uses.
  - No major regulatory downgrades, withdrawals, or targeted restrictions specific to E337.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified (no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for E337, no major bans found).
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Regulatory approval/GRAS status persists across major frameworks reviewed.
  - Core hazard datasets reviewed by EFSA do not indicate high-level concern at food-use exposures.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence supports a low-to-limited risk profile: major regulators continue authorization, EFSA’s modern re-evaluation set a group ADI with no exceedance in refined exposure estimates, and key toxicology endpoints (genotoxicity/carcinogenicity) did not trigger high concern. Available toxicity signals are mainly from atypical high-dose exposures involving related tartrate preparations rather than standard E337 use.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
