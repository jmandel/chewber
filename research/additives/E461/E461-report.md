# E461 (Methyl cellulose) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E461
- **Name:** Methyl cellulose
- **CAS number(s):** 9004-67-5
- **Chemical class:** Modified cellulose ether (cellulose methyl ether; hydrocolloid polysaccharide derivative)
- **Common synonyms:** Cellulose methyl ether; methyl ether of cellulose; methylcellulose
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Semi-synthetic (chemically modified cellulose)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrophilic polymer that increases viscosity, stabilizes dispersions, and helps emulsification/texture by water binding and gel-forming behavior.
- **Common food categories:** Broad processed-food use as a thickener/stabilizer/emulsifier; recent food studies explicitly include plant-based burger formulations using methylcellulose as a binder.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Approved (as part of the celluloses group E460-E469).
  - **Latest EFSA re-evaluation found:** 2018 (EFSA Journal 16(1):5047; DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2018.5047).
  - **ADI:** EFSA concluded no need for a numerical ADI (consistent with "ADI not specified" approach for celluloses).
  - **Key EFSA conclusions:** No genotoxic concern; low acute toxicity; chronic NOAELs in celluloses data set up to 9,000 mg/kg bw/day; no carcinogenic signal detected for microcrystalline/modified celluloses; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
  - **Exposure note from EFSA abstract:** Combined refined (brand-loyal) 95th percentile exposure up to 506 mg/kg bw/day; indicative total exposure around 660-900 mg/kg bw/day.
  - **Access note:** Direct EFSA Journal page on `efsa.europa.eu` redirected to Wiley and was blocked from this VM (403/Cloudflare). Data were extracted from the PubMed-indexed EFSA abstract for the same DOI.
- **FDA / eCFR (U.S.):** GRAS in 21 CFR Part 182.
  - **Citation:** 21 CFR 182.1480 (Methylcellulose), within **PART 182 - Substances Generally Recognized as Safe**.
  - **Conditions of use:** "Generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice."
  - **Specification in 182.1480(a):** USP methylcellulose with methoxy content 27.5%-31.5% (dry-weight basis).
  - **Access note:** eCFR web search/UI was CAPTCHA-blocked, but official eCFR API endpoints were accessible and used.
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - WHO JECFA database entry (Chemical 700) lists methyl cellulose (INS 461, CAS 9004-67-5).
  - **ADI:** NOT SPECIFIED.
  - **Last evaluation year listed:** 1989 (with previous evaluations listed, including 1984 and 1973).
  - InChem JECFA summary also reports ADI "not specified" for modified celluloses including methyl cellulose.
- **IARC:** No methyl cellulose entry found in the IARC Monographs classification dataset/list reviewed (Volumes 1-140 listing infrastructure).
- **Notable bans:** None identified in major jurisdictions reviewed (EU, U.S., JECFA context).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology and carcinogenicity (regulatory dataset):**
  - EFSA reports low acute toxicity, no genotoxic concern, and no carcinogenic properties detected in the evaluated celluloses data package; chronic NOAELs reported up to 9,000 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Reproductive/developmental effects were not observed at doses >1,000 mg/kg bw/day (often highest dose tested) in the celluloses evidence base.
- **Human data:**
  - A 2026 randomized placebo-controlled trial in healthy adults assessed methylcellulose versus psyllium with inulin co-administration; methylcellulose altered fermentation dynamics but was not shown non-inferior to psyllium for reducing early breath hydrogen rise.
  - A 2015 clinical practice review includes methylcellulose among fiber options and advises gradual dose increases to reduce GI adverse effects (e.g., bloating/gas context).
- **Recent food-system evidence:**
  - A 2025 plant-based burger study used methylcellulose-containing formulations and focused safety concerns on mycotoxin occurrence in raw materials rather than methylcellulose-specific toxic effects.
- **Mechanistic concerns identified:** Mainly gastrointestinal tolerance/fermentation behavior (gas production, fiber-gel behavior), not systemic toxicity at food-additive use levels.
- **Epidemiology:** No strong additive-specific epidemiological harm signal for E461 was identified in retrieved sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake / exposure (best available from retrieved regulatory source):**
  - EFSA abstract reports combined celluloses exposure at 95th percentile (refined, brand-loyal) up to 506 mg/kg bw/day and indicative total exposure around 660-900 mg/kg bw/day.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA and JECFA do not set a numerical ADI for this celluloses group (no numerical ADI / ADI not specified), so classical "numerical ADI exceedance" is not the primary risk metric.
  - Based on retrieved EFSA conclusion, exposures considered by EFSA did not indicate safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
- **Potentially sensitive populations:**
  - Individuals sensitive to fermentable fibers/GI gas symptoms may experience tolerance-related symptoms at higher intakes (functional GI effects rather than toxicological hazard).

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA and JECFA outcomes are non-numerical ADI/not specified with no safety concern at reported uses; no IARC listing found.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: E461 is a modified (semi-synthetic) polymer rather than endogenous/naturally absorbed nutrient; GI tolerance effects (gas/bloating context) can occur at higher intake.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EU approved with EFSA re-evaluation showing no safety concern; U.S. GRAS status in 21 CFR 182.1480 with GMP conditions; JECFA ADI not specified; no carcinogenic classification identified.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: Direct additive-specific long-term modern human outcome studies are limited; much toxicology is read-across within celluloses.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting placement: GI symptom potential in susceptible individuals and limited additive-specific epidemiologic evidence.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: Regulatory toxicology package is extensive for celluloses and did not identify major hazard signals at use levels.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting placement: No major supportive evidence found.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: No major bans identified; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing found for methyl cellulose; EFSA/JECFA conclusions are low concern.

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest evidence is consistent multi-jurisdiction regulatory assessment (EFSA, FDA/eCFR, JECFA) indicating low toxicological concern at authorized use levels. Residual concern is mainly GI tolerance in some users, not a demonstrated serious toxic/carcinogenic hazard.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
