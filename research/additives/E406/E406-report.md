# E406 Research Report: Agar

## Identity
- **E-number**: E406 (INS 406)
- **Name**: Agar (agar-agar)
- **CAS number(s)**: 9002-18-0
- **Chemical class**: Hydrocolloid polysaccharide (galactan-rich phycocolloid from red algae)
- **Common synonyms**: Agar-agar; Japan agar; Bengal/Ceylon/Chinese/Japanese isinglass; layor carang; gelose
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Natural** (extracted from red algae / Rhodophyceae)

Key source data: JECFA agar entry and 21 CFR 184.1115 list CAS/origin/synonyms/INS ([JECFA AGAR, Chemical ID 1163](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1163); [21 CFR 184.1115 via eCFR API](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=184&section=184.1115)).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Agar hydrates and forms a gel/viscous hydrocolloid matrix, supporting thickening and structure stabilization in aqueous food systems.
- **Regulatory functional class**:
  - JECFA: emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener.
  - US CFR table: drying agent, flavoring agent, stabilizer, thickener, surface finisher, formulation aid, humectant (category-dependent).
- **Common food categories (US CFR-listed examples)**:
  - Baked goods and baking mixes
  - Confections and frostings
  - Soft candy
  - All other food categories (with lower general limit)

Sources: [JECFA AGAR](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1163); [21 CFR 184.1115 via eCFR API](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=184&section=184.1115).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Approved/authorised food additive in the EU (re-evaluated by EFSA ANS Panel).
- **ADI**: EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** (effectively ADI not specified).
- **Evaluation year**: **2016** (EFSA Journal 2016;14(11):4645).
- **Key conclusions**:
  - Agar (E406) was not of safety concern at the reported uses/use levels.
  - No numerical ADI considered necessary.
  - Exposure estimates were highest in infants/toddlers in certain scenarios; despite this, EFSA did not identify a safety concern at reported uses.

EFSA exposure values reported in the abstracted text include (mg/kg bw/day):
- General population refined scenario: means ~0.1 (elderly) to 66 (infants); high-level up to 214 (toddlers).
- Brand-loyal infants/toddlers: means ~5-67; high-level ~13-892.
- Non-brand-loyal infants/toddlers: means ~2-55; high-level ~6-168.

Source URLs visited: [EFSA journal page](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4645), [DOI landing page](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4645).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status**: **GRAS-affirmed direct food ingredient** in Part 184.
- **CFR citation**: **21 CFR 184.1115 (Agar-agar)**, read together with **21 CFR 184.1**.
- **Conditions of use**:
  - Baked goods/baking mixes: up to 0.8%
  - Confections/frostings: up to 2.0%
  - Soft candy: up to 1.2%
  - All other food categories: up to 0.25%
  - Must be used under 184.1(b)(2) GRAS conditions with specified limitations.

Sources: [21 CFR 184.1115 via eCFR API](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=184&section=184.1115), [21 CFR 184.1 via eCFR API](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=184&section=184.1), [version history endpoint](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/versions/title-21.json).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies and regulatory toxicology
- EFSA re-evaluation (2016) concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels and no need for a numerical ADI.
- JECFA lists agar with ADI “NOT LIMITED,” consistent with low toxicological concern at food-use levels.

Sources: [EFSA 2016 entry](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4645), [JECFA AGAR](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1163).

### Epidemiological/clinical human evidence (PubMed, recent)
- **2021 RCT in children** (xyloglucan + gelose/agar-agar + ORS): reported treatment as effective and safe; adverse events were judged unrelated to study medication.
- **2015 case report**: agar-agar bezoar causing solid food intolerance and gastric pouch dilation after Roux-en-Y bypass, suggesting a mechanical risk in susceptible postoperative anatomy/high-gelling exposure contexts.
- **2021/2025 review literature**: agar is generally discussed as low-dispute/nontoxic versus some other seaweed hydrocolloids, while broader seaweed-polysaccharide literature notes persistent gaps in long-term digestion/physicochemical safety modeling.

Sources: [PMID 33028102](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33028102/), [PMID 26077699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26077699/), [PMID 34281484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34281484/), [PMID 41295380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41295380/).

### Mechanistic concerns
- Main plausible concern from available evidence is not classic systemic toxicity but **dose/formulation-dependent gastrointestinal mechanical effects** (e.g., bezoar formation risk in vulnerable individuals) and uncertainty around fermentation behavior in diverse matrices.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reported highest modelled exposures in infants/toddlers (including very high brand-loyal high-percentile scenarios), but still concluded no safety concern at reported use levels.
- Because EFSA and JECFA did not require/set a numerical ADI for agar, conventional ADI exceedance framing is limited.
- **Vulnerable populations** to monitor pragmatically:
  - Infants/toddlers (highest modelled dietary exposure in EFSA scenarios)
  - Individuals with altered GI anatomy/motility (based on agar bezoar case report)

Primary source: [EFSA 2016 entry](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4645); clinical signal: [PMID 26077699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26077699/).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting placement**:
  - EFSA (2016): no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses.
  - JECFA: ADI “NOT LIMITED.”
  - Natural origin (red algae polysaccharide) with long-standing authorised food use.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
- **Evidence arguing against placement**:
  - Human case report of agar-agar bezoar indicates non-zero harm potential in specific high-gel/susceptible contexts.
  - Recent literature still notes gaps in standardized long-term digestion/safety modeling for seaweed polysaccharides.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting placement**:
  - Broad regulatory acceptance (EU re-evaluated, US Part 184 GRAS, JECFA not limited).
  - Identifiable minor concern signals are context-dependent rather than systemic toxicity.
- **Evidence arguing against placement**:
  - The regulatory toxicology profile is stronger than a typical “limited” uncertainty case because both EFSA and JECFA did not require a numerical ADI.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting placement**:
  - Very high modelled exposure values can occur in specific infant/toddler scenarios.
  - Isolated clinical adverse event exists (bezoar case).
- **Evidence arguing against placement**:
  - No reduced ADI, no regulatory withdrawal, and no EFSA/JECFA conclusion of unresolved safety at authorised use levels.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting placement**:
  - None compelling in current evidence set.
- **Evidence arguing against placement**:
  - No major bans identified.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found.
  - EFSA/JECFA conclusions are not consistent with high-risk categorization.

### 2. Rationale
The core regulatory signal is consistently low concern (EFSA no numerical ADI needed; JECFA ADI not limited; FDA GRAS-affirmed listing with use limits). The main caveat is that adverse outcomes can still occur under atypical high-gelling exposure in susceptible GI conditions, which is better characterized as contextual/mechanical risk rather than intrinsic systemic toxicity.

### 3. Recommended tier
**risk_free**
