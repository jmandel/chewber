# E129 (Allura Red AC) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E129 (INS 129) [S6]
- **Primary name:** Allura Red AC [S6]
- **CAS number:** 25956-17-6 [S6]
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic **monoazo** sulfonated dye (disodium salt) [S6]
- **Common synonyms:** FD&C Red No. 40, Red 40, C.I. Food Red 17, C.I. 16035 [S6]
- **Origin:** Synthetic (not natural/endogenous) [S6]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Color additive used to provide red coloration in foods [S2, S6]
- **Mechanism of action:** Azo-conjugated chromophore absorbs visible light; sulfonate groups improve water solubility for use in aqueous food matrices [S6]
- **Common food categories:** In U.S. federal regulation, FD&C Red No. 40 is permitted in "foods generally" unless a food standard excludes added color; use must follow GMP levels [S2]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Evaluated and retained in the EU additives framework (no EFSA recommendation to withdraw). [S1, S3, S4]
- **ADI:** **7 mg/kg bw/day**. [S1, S3]
- **Evaluation timeline:**
  - **2009 re-evaluation:** ADI set/retained at 7 mg/kg bw/day. High-level exposure scenarios could exceed ADI in some children (1-10 years). [S1]
  - **2013 statement on new studies:** EFSA concluded there was **no reason to revise** the ADI of 7 mg/kg bw/day. [S3]
  - **2015 refined exposure assessment:** Refined estimates reported **below ADI** across assessed population groups using improved use-level data. [S4]
- **Key conclusion:** Current EFSA position keeps ADI at 7 mg/kg bw/day, with exposure outcome depending on assessment assumptions/data quality. [S1, S3, S4]

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **GRAS status:** FDA states there is **no GRAS provision for color additives**. Color additives are handled via listing/certification rules, not GRAS pathways. [S5]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 74.340** (FD&C Red No. 40). [S2]
- **Conditions of use (from CFR text):**
  - Subject to **batch certification** [S2]
  - May be used in foods generally unless prohibited by a specific standard [S2]
  - Must comply with **current good manufacturing practice** (minimum needed) [S2]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-7 mg/kg bw** [S7]
- **Last evaluation year:** **2016** (latest listed in WHO JECFA database entry) [S7]
- **Notes:** WHO/JECFA entry also shows earlier evaluations and specification updates. [S7]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No specific IARC group classification identified for Allura Red AC/E129 in the current IARC classified-agents dataset (Volumes 1-140). [S8, S9, S10]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction blanket ban was identified in the specific EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed for this task. [S1, S2, S7, S8]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic studies
- **Mouse colitis susceptibility (2022, Nature Communications):** Chronic Allura Red AC exposure increased susceptibility to experimental colitis, linked to intestinal serotonin pathways and microbiota effects. [S11]
- **Mouse DNA damage/inflammation study (2023, Toxicology Reports):** Reported DNA damage, microbiome perturbation, and low-grade colonic inflammation under experimental conditions. [S12]

### Reviews and broader concerns
- **2024 review (Chemosphere):** Summarized potential neurotoxicity mechanisms for multiple synthetic dyes including Allura Red AC; evidence is largely mechanistic/animal and not definitive for human causal harm at regulated intakes. [S13]

### Evidence that does not confirm harm under tested conditions
- **2025 in vitro study (J Food Sci):** Short-term in vitro exposure to Allura Red AC did not show significant cytotoxicity or major microbiota-composition shifts under tested conditions; authors still call for chronic-exposure research. [S14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2009:** Non-brand-loyal/high-end scenarios indicated potential ADI exceedance in some children (1-10 years). [S1]
- **EFSA 2015 refined assessment:** With reported industry use levels, estimated exposures were below ADI for assessed groups. [S4]
- **Interpretation:** ADI exceedance risk appears **scenario-dependent** (conservative assumptions vs refined use data). [S1, S4]
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Children with frequent intake of highly colored products and populations with high consumption of ultra-processed foods. [S1, S11, S12]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Established ADIs by EFSA and JECFA; refined EFSA exposure assessment below ADI in assessed groups. [S4, S7]
- **Evidence against this tier:** Synthetic azo dye (not endogenous), plus multiple recent animal/mechanistic signals (colitis susceptibility, DNA damage/inflammation). [S6, S11, S12]

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Approved/listed by EFSA framework, FDA CFR, and JECFA with a defined ADI and no IARC carcinogen classification entry. [S1, S2, S7, S8]
- **Evidence against this tier:** Nontrivial mechanistic/animal safety signals and historical EFSA scenarios showing possible ADI exceedance in children. [S1, S11, S12]

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Approved additive with caveats: credible experimental signals (gut inflammation, DNA damage/microbiome effects), plus child-focused exposure sensitivity in conservative EFSA scenarios. [S1, S11, S12]
- **Evidence against this tier:** Regulators have not moved to withdrawal/ban; ADI remained unchanged and refined exposure estimates were below ADI. [S3, S4, S7]

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Limited. No strong support from the reviewed regulatory datasets.
- **Evidence against this tier:** No major-jurisdiction ban in consulted core sources; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing found for Allura Red AC; ADI remains in place across EFSA/JECFA. [S3, S7, S8, S10]

### 2. Rationale
The additive remains authorized with harmonized ADIs (EFSA/JECFA at 7 mg/kg bw/day), but there are persistent caveats: (a) animal/mechanistic studies indicating potential GI/inflammatory/genotoxic signals under some exposure conditions, and (b) historically conservative exposure scenarios that can place children near/above ADI. This supports a middle-risk, caveated position rather than risk-free or high-risk.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
