# E223 (Sodium metabisulfite) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E223
- **Name:** Sodium metabisulfite
- **CAS number(s):** 7681-57-4
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfite salt (sulfur dioxide-releasing preservative)
- **Common synonyms:** sodium pyrosulfite, sodium disulfite, disodium pyrosulfite, sodium metabisulphite
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **synthetic** food additive (commercially manufactured inorganic salt).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative/antioxidant.
- **Mechanism of action:** Releases sulfur dioxide/bisulfite species that inhibit oxidation, enzymatic browning, and microbial growth.
- **Common food categories:** Dried fruits/vegetables, wine, beer, some meat preparations, fruit/vegetable juices, and flavored drinks (category-level evidence is largely for the sulfite group E220-E228).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in the EU as part of the sulfite group (E220-E228), with ongoing risk-management review.
- **ADI:**
  - 2016 EFSA re-evaluation set a **temporary group ADI of 0.7 mg/kg bw/day (as SO2 equivalents)**.
  - 2022 EFSA follow-up judged the database insufficient for an ADI and **withdrew the temporary ADI**; EFSA used a **MOE approach (target MOE >= 80)** and identified concern for high consumers.
- **Evaluation year(s):** 2016 re-evaluation; 2022 follow-up (latest scientific opinion).
- **Key conclusions:** High consumers (notably infants/toddlers/children under several scenarios) may be of concern; sulfite-sensitive and asthmatic individuals are a concern group.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** **GRAS** when used in accordance with GMP.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3766 (Sodium metabisulfite)**.
- **Conditions of use (from 21 CFR 182.3766(c)):** Not used in meats; not used in foods recognized as vitamin B1 sources; not used on fruits/vegetables intended to be served or sold raw as fresh.
- **Related labeling threshold:** 21 CFR 101.100(a)(4) treats sulfiting agents >=10 ppm as detectable, relevant for labeling/exemption logic.

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **0-0.7 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as sulfur dioxide for sulfites including sodium metabisulfite).
- **Last evaluation year shown on chemical page:** **1998** (with prior evaluations listed).

### IARC
- **Sodium metabisulfite-specific classification:** No direct listing found in IARC classification list.
- **Related agent:** Sulfur dioxide is listed by IARC as **Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans).

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No broad major-jurisdiction ban identified for E223 itself.
- Significant use restrictions exist (e.g., 21 CFR 182.3766(c) limitations in the US).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **Rat neurobehavioral study (2025, Toxicol Lett):** Oral sulfite (25/100/260 mg/kg for 35 days) associated with anxiety/depression-like behavior at higher doses and reduced NO pathway markers in hippocampus.
- **Mouse toxicology study (2023, J Toxicol):** 98 mg/kg/day SMB for 40 days associated with oxidative stress, hematotoxicity, and liver/kidney injury markers (ameliorated by antioxidant co-treatment).

### In vitro / mechanistic
- **Cell toxicity study (2022, J Food Sci):** SMB showed dose-dependent cytotoxicity and apoptosis in cultured cells; dried plum samples in that market survey showed substantial SMB residues.
- Mechanistic pattern across studies: oxidative stress and redox disruption are recurring concerns at higher experimental exposures.

### Epidemiological / clinical evidence
- Direct large-scale epidemiology specific to E223 is limited.
- Clinical and regulatory evidence supports **hypersensitivity risk** in susceptible individuals (especially some asthmatics) for sulfites as a class.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2016:** Temporary ADI exceedance reported in some population groups, especially toddlers/children at high exposure scenarios.
- **EFSA 2022 follow-up:** Because ADI could not be maintained, EFSA used MOE and reported concern in high consumers across scenarios and specific concern for sulfite-sensitive/asthmatic individuals.
- **EFSA 2025 exposure update (group E220-E228; from DOI metadata abstract due direct page access limits):**
  - Highest DS-MPL1 scenario in toddlers: mean 2.63 and P95 8.09 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day.
  - Refined scenarios (DSA1/DSA2): highest mean 0.43 and P95 1.40 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day (toddlers).
  - Analytical-data-heavy scenarios (MDSD1/MDSD2): elderly up to P95 0.61 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Sulfite-sensitive individuals (notably some asthmatics), infants/toddlers/children under high-consumption scenarios.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free placement:** Long history of permitted use; JECFA group ADI exists; FDA GRAS status under GMP.
- **Evidence against risk_free placement:** EFSA withdrew temporary ADI (2022), concerns remain for high consumers and sulfite-sensitive groups; documented hypersensitivity risk.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited placement:** Continued approvals (US GRAS with restrictions; EU authorization), no IARC carcinogenic classification for sodium metabisulfite itself.
- **Evidence against limited placement:** EFSA could not confirm safety via an ADI-based approach and required MOE framework; repeated exceedance/concern in higher exposure groups.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate placement:** Approved but with clear caveats (EFSA ADI withdrawn, MOE concerns in high consumers, known hypersensitivity subgroup risk, and consistent high-dose toxicological signals in animal data).
- **Evidence against moderate placement:** Lack of broad regulatory bans and absence of strong human causal evidence of severe harm at typical population exposures.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high placement:** Presence of toxicological findings at experimental doses and subgroup sensitivity concerns.
- **Evidence against high placement:** No major-jurisdiction blanket ban for E223; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for this additive; current regulation still allows use with limits.

### 2. Rationale
The evidence best fits an additive that is legally permitted but not risk-free: regulators maintain approval with restrictions, while EFSA's latest risk framing indicates unresolved uncertainty and concern in certain exposure/sensitivity contexts rather than universal high hazard.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
