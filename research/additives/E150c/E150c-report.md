# E150c (Ammonia Caramel) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS**: E150c / INS 150c. [S5]
- **Primary name**: Ammonia caramel (caramel colour, Class III). [S5]
- **CAS number(s)**: 8028-89-5 (listed in JECFA class III entry). [S5]
- **Chemical class**: Class III caramel colour (ammonia process); complex process-derived brown colorant from heated carbohydrates. [S3, S5]
- **Common synonyms**: Ammonia caramel; caramel colour, Class III. [S5]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Synthetic/process-derived** (manufactured by controlled heat treatment of food-grade carbohydrates with permitted acids/alkalis/salts including ammonium reagents). [S3]

## Function in Food
E150c is used as a **brown food color additive**. Controlled thermal treatment of carbohydrates creates complex caramelization products (highly colored polymers) that darken beverages and foods. [S3, S5]

Common categories include soft drinks, sauces/seasonings, and other processed foods where brown coloration is desired. [S3, S9, S12]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Authorized in the EU as part of caramel colours E150a-d. [S1]
- **ADI**: **300 mg/kg bw/day** as a **group ADI** for E150a, E150b, E150c, and E150d. [S1]
- **Evaluation year**:
  - 2011 re-evaluation opinion.
  - 2012 EFSA refined exposure statement (follow-up). [S1, S2]
- **Key conclusions**:
  - EFSA retained the group ADI at 300 mg/kg bw/day in the re-evaluation. [S1]
  - In EFSA's refined 2012 exposure statement, mean exposures were below ADI, but high-level exposure in some child/toddler scenarios remained above ADI under conservative assumptions. [S2]

### FDA / eCFR (United States)
- **Color additive status**: Caramel is listed in **21 CFR 73.85** for coloring foods, exempt from certification, with use under GMP. [S3]
- **GRAS status**: Caramel is also listed as GRAS in **21 CFR 182.1235** when used in accordance with GMP. [S3]
- **Conditions of use / composition-relevant points**:
  - 21 CFR 73.85(a)(2) permits ammonium hydroxide and certain ammonium salts in manufacture.
  - 21 CFR 73.85(c) allows use for coloring foods generally under GMP (except where standards of identity disallow added color).
  - 21 CFR 73.85(e) states exemption from certification. [S3]

### JECFA / WHO
- **Additive entry**: CARAMEL COLOUR, Class III / INS 150c. [S5]
- **ADI**: **0-200 mg/kg bw** (comment: **0-150 mg/kg bw on a solids basis**). [S5]
- **Last evaluation year shown**: **1985** (meeting 29). [S5]

### IARC
- No direct IARC monograph classification was found for **ammonia caramel/E150c** itself in the IARC list dataset.
- Related process contaminant **4-methylimidazole (4-MEI)** appears in IARC data as **Group 2B** (possibly carcinogenic to humans). [S7, S8]

### Notable bans
- No broad major-jurisdiction ban (EU/US) was identified in consulted regulatory sources; use remains ADI/GMP-managed. [S1, S3, S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/mechanistic evidence
- A 2024 mouse study reported immunotoxic effects of **2-acetyl-4-tetrahydroxybutylimidazole (THI)**, a by-product associated with Class III caramel, including reduced lymphocyte-related endpoints and stronger effects in vitamin B6-deficient mice. [S10]
- 4-MEI remains a key mechanistic concern discussed for caramel-color processes; IARC classifies 4-MEI as Group 2B. [S8]

### Human/epidemiological evidence
- Direct human epidemiology specific to E150c alone is limited in the searched records.
- A 2025 prospective cohort reported an association between one **food-additive mixture** (including sulphite ammonia caramel/E150d and other additives) and type 2 diabetes incidence; this is mixture-level observational evidence and not causal evidence for E150c specifically. [S11]

### Exposure/risk studies
- A 2026 U.S. exposure update for 4-MEI estimated lower current dietary exposure from caramel-color sources than earlier FDA-era estimates, reflecting reformulation/specification changes. [S9]
- A 2019 Chinese intake assessment estimated caramel colour intake and 4-MEI/THI exposure below relevant ADI-based concern levels. [S12]
- A 2025 reproductive/developmental 4-MEI risk assessment reported MOEs above common concern thresholds, but included industry-linked conflict-of-interest disclosure. [S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Group ADI context**: EFSA uses a group ADI (300 mg/kg bw/day) for E150a-d. [S1]
- **Potential exceedance**: EFSA refined exposure work still indicated possible ADI exceedance in some high-exposure child/toddler conservative scenarios. [S2]
- **Recent external assessments**:
  - U.S. 4-MEI dietary exposure estimates have declined in recent updates. [S9]
  - Chinese population assessment reported low concern for caramel colours and by-products at estimated intakes. [S12]

### Vulnerable populations
- **Toddlers/children** with high intake patterns of caramel-colored foods/beverages are the main potential ADI-exceedance groups in EFSA's conservative scenarios. [S2]
- Nutritional status (e.g., low vitamin B6 in animal models) may modify THI-related immunotoxic response, but human translation is uncertain. [S10]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Ongoing authorization in EU/US; no major ban identified. [S1, S3]
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA maintains a finite group ADI with modeled exceedances in some child/toddler scenarios. [S1, S2]
  - Relevant by-products (THI/4-MEI) have toxicology flags; 4-MEI is IARC Group 2B. [S8, S10]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Regulatory frameworks in EU/US/JECFA continue to permit use with ADI/GMP controls. [S1, S3, S5]
  - Recent exposure assessments show lower estimated 4-MEI exposure than older estimates. [S9]
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA still flagged possible ADI exceedance in conservative high-end child/toddler scenarios.
  - Mechanistic/animal concerns for Class III by-products persist. [S2, S10]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Approved additive, but with meaningful caveats: ADI exceedance risk in some modeled pediatric scenarios and credible by-product toxicology concerns (THI immunotoxicity; 4-MEI carcinogenic concern signal). [S2, S8, S10]
  - Epidemiological signal exists at additive-mixture level (not definitive for E150c alone), adding uncertainty rather than reassurance. [S11]
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - No direct ban/withdrawal and no direct IARC classification for E150c itself. [S3, S7]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Presence of IARC Group 2B classification for a related contaminant (4-MEI). [S8]
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - No direct IARC Group 2A/2B classification for E150c itself, and no major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - Existing regulatory bodies retain authorization under managed conditions. [S1, S3, S5, S7]

### 2. Rationale
E150c remains permitted by EFSA/FDA/JECFA, but it is not a "no-concern" profile: EFSA's group-ADI framework still shows potential high-end pediatric exceedance in conservative scenarios, and there are credible toxicological concerns around Class III-related by-products (especially THI and 4-MEI). Current evidence does not support a "high" tier because major regulators have not withdrawn authorization and direct IARC classification for E150c is absent.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `moderate`**
