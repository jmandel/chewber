# E303 (Potassium ascorbate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E303 (INS 303)
- **Name**: Potassium ascorbate
- **CAS number(s)**: 15421-15-5 (as listed in PubChem synonyms for potassium ascorbate)
- **Chemical class**: Potassium salt of L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C derivative; ascorbate salt)
- **Common synonyms**: Potassium L-ascorbate, Monopotassium ascorbate, INS 303
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Commercially produced ingredient (industrial neutralization of ascorbic acid to potassium salt; not typically isolated directly from foods)

**Data notes**:
- JECFA lists INS 303 as "POTASSIUM ASCORBATE" and functional class antioxidant.
- PubChem synonym list includes "E-303" and CAS 15421-15-5.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function**: Antioxidant.
- **Mechanism**: Ascorbate salts act as reducing agents and oxygen scavengers, limiting oxidative reactions in food matrices.
- **Common food categories**: Historically used in antioxidant roles similar to other ascorbates; however, current international status is mixed (Codex provision withdrawn per JECFA database comment).

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Targeted EFSA record found**: EFSA Journal opinion on **ascorbic acid (E300), sodium ascorbate (E301), and calcium ascorbate (E302)** (2015; doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4087).
- **E303-specific status**: No dedicated EFSA scientific opinion/re-evaluation page for **potassium ascorbate (E303)** was identified in accessible EFSA materials during this search.
- **ADI**: For E300-E302 re-evaluation, EFSA reported no need for a numerical ADI (group considered low concern at reported uses).
- **Evaluation year**: 2015 (for E300-E302, not E303).
- **Key EFSA conclusion (for E300-E302)**: No safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no concern for genotoxicity.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status for potassium ascorbate**: No explicit potassium ascorbate listing found in current Title 21 full text search.
- **Related GRAS citations in Part 182**:
  - **21 CFR 182.3189** - Calcium ascorbate: generally recognized as safe when used per GMP.
  - **21 CFR 182.3731** - Sodium ascorbate: generally recognized as safe when used per GMP.
- **Conditions of use (related ascorbate salts in CFR)**: "generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice."

### JECFA / WHO
- **Chemical record**: Potassium ascorbate (INS 303).
- **ADI**:
  - 1981 entry: **ADI "NOT SPECIFIED"**, with comment: group ADI for ascorbic acid and its sodium, potassium, and calcium salts.
  - 2015 entry comment: "Not re-evaluated by JECFA, but the provisions for potassium ascorbate was withdrawn at CCFA47."
- **Last evaluation year shown on entry**: 2015 (commentary entry), with ADI basis from prior evaluation.

### IARC
- Checked IARC classification resources (list-of-classifications app/data).
- **Result**: No IARC group entry was identified for potassium ascorbate.

### Notable bans/withdrawals
- No major-jurisdiction "ban" was identified in the sources reviewed.
- **Codex-related withdrawal noted**: JECFA database comment says provisions for potassium ascorbate were withdrawn at CCFA47.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology consensus (related ascorbates)**:
  - EFSA (E300-E302): no genotoxic concern and no safety concern at reported uses; no numerical ADI needed.
  - JECFA: group ADI not specified for ascorbic acid and sodium/potassium/calcium salts.
- **Direct potassium ascorbate literature (PubMed)**:
  - Sparse and mostly not food-additive toxicology.
  - Query for "potassium ascorbate" in title/abstract returned 12 records, mostly clinical/biomedical contexts.
  - Query combining potassium ascorbate with toxicity/safety/adverse terms returned no items.
- **Mechanistic/related evidence**:
  - Recent food-system study (sausage model) evaluated ascorbic acid level, storage, and packaging effects on volatile N-nitrosamines; indicates nitrosamine risk context in cured products is multifactorial.
  - Human cohort evidence on high vitamin C intakes (supplemental, especially high dose) shows increased kidney stone risk in men; this is relevant mainly for high-dose exposure contexts, not typical additive-level intake.

## Exposure Assessment
- **E303-specific intake datasets**: Not identified in the retrieved sources.
- **Proxy from EFSA E300-E302 exposure assessment**:
  - Reported mean and high-percentile intakes were highest in toddlers/children and lower in adults/elderly, with no safety concern conclusion for E300-E302.
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - JECFA group ADI and EFSA related-assessment framing indicate "ADI not specified/no numerical ADI" for related ascorbates.
  - Therefore classic ADI exceedance framing is limited for this compound class.
- **Potential vulnerable populations**:
  - Individuals with kidney stone susceptibility may warrant caution with high total vitamin C intake (especially supplemental high doses), though this is not specific evidence of harm at normal food-additive exposure.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free**:
  - Potassium ascorbate is an ascorbate (vitamin C salt) with longstanding use context.
  - JECFA group ADI: "not specified" for ascorbic acid and sodium/potassium/calcium salts.
  - Related EFSA ascorbate re-evaluation found no genotoxic concern and no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Evidence against risk_free**:
  - E303-specific modern toxicology and exposure datasets are sparse.
  - JECFA note indicates Codex provision withdrawal context rather than straightforward ongoing endorsement.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited**:
  - Strong related-compound regulatory reassurance (EFSA E300-E302, JECFA group ADI not specified).
  - No IARC classification identified.
  - No clear human harm signal at typical food-additive exposure levels from retrieved sources.
- **Evidence against limited**:
  - U.S. CFR has no explicit potassium ascorbate listing (while sodium/calcium ascorbate are explicitly listed in 21 CFR Part 182).

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate**:
  - Regulatory ambiguity across jurisdictions/documents (e.g., Codex withdrawal comment; no explicit CFR listing for potassium ascorbate).
  - Limited direct modern food-safety toxicology on potassium ascorbate specifically.
- **Evidence against moderate**:
  - No credible high-strength toxic hazard signal was found.
  - Group ascorbate toxicology remains broadly reassuring.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high**:
  - None identified in the retrieved authoritative sources.
- **Evidence against high**:
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - No EFSA/JECFA indication of major toxic concern for the ascorbate group.

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are: (a) reassuring group-level toxicology for ascorbates, (b) lack of strong direct hazard signals, and (c) some regulatory listing gaps/withdrawal notes that prevent a "risk_free" conclusion for E303 specifically.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
