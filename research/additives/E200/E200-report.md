# E200 (Sorbic acid) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E200
- **Name:** Sorbic acid
- **CAS number:** 110-44-1 (JECFA and PubChem synonym listing)
- **Chemical class:** Unsaturated monocarboxylic (hexadienoic) organic acid; preservative-class weak acid
- **Common synonyms:** (2E,4E)-hexa-2,4-dienoic acid; trans,trans-sorbic acid; E 200
- **Natural vs synthetic:** The trans,trans isomer occurs naturally (e.g., in some berries), but additive-grade sorbic acid for food manufacturing is industrially produced at scale.

**Evidence used:**
- JECFA entry identifies sorbic acid, CAS 110-44-1, and functional class "Preservative" (WHO/JECFA database).
- PubChem synonym and record data include CAS, synonyms, natural occurrence note, and industrial production/use context.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antimicrobial preservative.
- **Mechanistic action:** As a weak organic acid preservative, sorbic acid/sorbates are used to suppress spoilage organisms (especially yeasts and moulds; also some bacteria), particularly in acidic foods.
- **Common food categories:** Acidified and shelf-stable foods where mould/yeast control is needed; EFSA follow-up opinion specifically addressed additional use in pre-packed prepared salads and dairy-based desserts.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised in the EU (E200/E202 listed in Annex II and III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 in EFSA re-evaluation context).
- **Latest opinion found:** EFSA follow-up opinion (EFSA Journal, 2018; publication date shown as 30 Nov 2018 in EFSA Open).
- **ADI:** Group ADI **11 mg/kg bw/day** for sorbic acid (E200) and potassium sorbate (E202).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern for sorbic acid and potassium sorbate.
  - At proposed extended uses, estimated exposure remained below the ADI (mean 0.3-6.0 mg/kg bw/day; high 0.7-7.6 mg/kg bw/day, highest in toddlers/other children).
- **Re-evaluation history:** 2015 EFSA re-evaluation had established a temporary group ADI of 3 mg/kg bw/day pending additional data; later follow-up replaced this with 11 mg/kg bw/day.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3089 (Sorbic acid)**.

- **Related feed citation:** 21 CFR 582.3089 (same GRAS-with-GMP framing for feed use).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology baseline
- EFSA follow-up (2018) supports ADI 11 mg/kg bw/day and reports exposure estimates below ADI at evaluated uses.
- EFSA also states no genotoxicity concern for sorbic acid/potassium sorbate.

### Recent human epidemiology (PubMed)
- **NutriNet-Sante prospective cohort studies (2026):**
  - Reported associations between preservative additive intakes (including potassium sorbate) and higher incidence of type 2 diabetes.
  - Separate cohort analysis reported associations between preservative additive intake patterns and cancer incidence.
- **Interpretation limits:** Observational design; mixture-exposure context; residual confounding cannot be fully excluded.

### Recent mechanistic/experimental signals (PubMed)
- **Systematic review (2025):** Cytogenotoxicity studies in mammalian cells reported mixed but notable signals for preservatives including potassium sorbate.
- **Rat study (2025):** Combined preservative exposure (including potassium sorbate) reported reproductive toxicity/apoptosis markers.
- **Network tox + in vitro study (2025):** Suggested hepatotoxic/carcinogenic pathway hypotheses for potassium sorbate.
- **Interpretation limits:** Several studies are mechanistic/high-dose or mixed-additive designs and are not direct proof of harm at regulated dietary exposure levels.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA quantitative exposure (follow-up scenario):**
  - Mean: **0.3-6.0 mg/kg bw/day**
  - High (95th percentile): **0.7-7.6 mg/kg bw/day**
  - Highest groups: toddlers and other children
- **ADI exceedance risk (current EFSA opinion context):** Estimated exposures in that follow-up opinion were **below** the group ADI of 11 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Young children with high consumption of preserved products (highest estimated per-kg exposures).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Longstanding regulatory acceptance (EFSA, FDA GRAS, JECFA).
  - EFSA follow-up found exposure below ADI in assessed scenarios.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - EFSA did not set "ADI not specified"; a numeric ADI is required.
  - New epidemiologic/mechanistic literature raises unresolved signals.

#### limited
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Approved/GRAS status with explicit ADI and GMP conditions.
  - EFSA no-genotoxicity concern for sorbic acid/potassium sorbate.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - Evidence base now includes observational associations and mechanistic studies suggesting potential adverse endpoints.
  - Historical EFSA ADI revision history indicates non-trivial toxicology reassessment over time.

#### moderate
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Approved but with caveats: ADI-based control and monitoring remain necessary.
  - EFSA ADI history moved from temporary lower ADI to updated ADI after additional data, reflecting meaningful uncertainty management.
  - Emerging human cohort associations (not causal proof) and supportive mechanistic literature justify caution, especially for high-exposure subgroups.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - Core regulators still permit use; EFSA follow-up exposure remained below ADI at assessed uses.

#### high
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Some experimental studies report toxicity signals.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - No major bans identified.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for sorbic acid found.
  - Regulatory bodies continue to allow use with ADI/GMP controls.

### 2. Rationale
The additive remains broadly authorised, but not in a "risk-free" framing. EFSA/JECFA use numeric ADIs, FDA applies GRAS-with-GMP conditions, and recent epidemiologic/mechanistic studies introduce non-zero uncertainty. The current evidence profile is best described as approved with caveats rather than either negligible risk or high-hazard status.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
