# E296 (Malic acid) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E296 (INS 296).
- **Name:** Malic acid.
- **CAS number(s):** 97-67-6 (L-malic acid), 617-48-1 (DL-malic acid), 6915-15-7 (malic acid entry used in WHO JECFA and Open EFSA substance database).
- **Chemical class:** Alpha-hydroxy dicarboxylic acid (hydroxybutanedioic acid).
- **Common synonyms:** DL-malic acid, L-malic acid, hydroxybutanedioic acid, hydroxysuccinic acid, pomalous acid.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Both. L-malic acid occurs naturally in foods; racemic DL-malic acid does not occur naturally and is commercially produced (e.g., hydration of fumaric or maleic acid).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Acidulant/pH control and sourness contributor; also used as flavor enhancer/flavoring adjuvant.
- **Common food categories (FDA CFR examples):** Nonalcoholic beverages, chewing gum, jams/jellies, processed fruits/fruit juices, hard/soft candy, gelatins/puddings/fillings, and other foods under cGMP limits.
- **Key US condition:** Use in food **except baby food**, at levels not exceeding good manufacturing practice; specific maxima are listed in 21 CFR 184.1069(d).

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU): Approved, but re-evaluation is still ongoing**
  - Open EFSA question EFSA-Q-2011-00598 is in **"Ongoing Risk Assessment"** status (last modified 2026-01-19).
  - EFSA’s status note states E296 is already authorized in the EU and that re-evaluation work continues after a call for data that closed on **31 December 2024**.
  - **ADI in current EFSA re-evaluation:** no finalized EFSA ADI value published yet (ongoing process).

- **FDA / CFR (US): GRAS**
  - Citation: **21 CFR 184.1069 (Malic acid)** within **21 CFR Part 184** (direct food substances affirmed as GRAS).
  - Conditions of use: flavor enhancer, flavoring agent/adjuvant, and pH control agent; use in food (except baby food) under cGMP with listed category maxima.
  - Access note: direct `ecfr.gov/current/...` pages were CAPTCHA-blocked from this VM, but official eCFR renderer API endpoints were accessible and used.

- **JECFA/WHO**
  - JECFA database entry for MALIC ACID (INS 296) reports **ADI: "Not limited"**.
  - JECFA entry shows **year: 1967** for this evaluation record.

- **IARC**
  - No malic acid listing found in IARC Monographs “List of Classifications” dataset (checked current list app dataset).
  - Therefore, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B/3 assignment was identified for E296.

- **Notable bans**
  - No major-jurisdiction bans identified in reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory/toxicology baseline (strongest evidence):**
  - JECFA assigns ADI "not limited," consistent with low concern at normal use levels.
  - FDA affirms GRAS status under specified cGMP conditions.
  - EFSA indicates authorization remains in place while formal re-evaluation is ongoing.

- **Animal studies / older toxicology literature:**
  - PubMed-indexed safety review data (older) report low systemic toxicity profile for malic acid at typical use contexts, with irritation concerns primarily linked to concentrated/low-pH topical exposure contexts.

- **Epidemiological data (recent):**
  - A 2025 prospective cohort analysis (NutriNet-Sante) found higher type 2 diabetes incidence for certain **food additive mixtures**; one mixture included malic acid among many additives. This is not ingredient-specific causality for E296 but is a relevant mixture-safety signal.

- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - For malic acid itself, major systemic toxicity signals are weak.
  - Local irritation and acid-load effects are plausible at high local concentrations/low pH.
  - Mixture interactions (rather than E296 alone) remain an active research issue.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:**
  - No finalized up-to-date EFSA quantitative intake output for E296 is available yet in the ongoing re-evaluation.
  - FDA provides category-specific maximum-use levels under cGMP, which constrain typical use.

- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not limited," so numeric ADI exceedance analysis is not the usual primary risk trigger for this additive.
  - Current concern focus is more about cumulative dietary patterns and additive mixtures than isolated E296 overexposure.

- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - **Infants (US regulatory context):** FDA section excludes use in baby food.
  - Individuals with acid sensitivity (e.g., oral/dental or GI sensitivity) may be more susceptible to local effects in highly acidic products.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting:
    - Naturally occurring metabolite and present in normal biochemistry.
    - JECFA ADI is "not limited."
    - FDA GRAS affirmation under cGMP.
  - Evidence arguing against:
    - EFSA food-additive re-evaluation is not finalized yet.
    - Recent mixture epidemiology raises broader additive-mixture questions.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting:
    - Approved/authorized in EU and US (GRAS) with established use controls.
    - No robust ingredient-specific severe hazard signal.
    - Regulatory uncertainty is procedural (ongoing EFSA review), not a suspension/ban.
  - Evidence arguing against:
    - Still lacks a current finalized EFSA re-evaluation output/ADI statement.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting:
    - Could be argued from mixture-level epidemiologic signal and unresolved cumulative-effects questions.
    - Baby-food exclusion in US indicates some population-specific conservatism in use conditions.
  - Evidence arguing against:
    - No clear ingredient-specific moderate-risk toxicology profile for E296 itself.
    - No ADI tightening or regulatory withdrawal identified.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting:
    - None found (no ban, no IARC 2A/2B classification for malic acid, no EFSA safety failure conclusion published).
  - Evidence arguing against:
    - Existing JECFA/FDA positions and current EFSA status are inconsistent with a high-risk categorization.

2. **Rationale**
- E296 is still legally authorized and widely regulated as a low-concern organic acid under controlled use conditions. The strongest uncertainty is not classical single-agent toxicity, but incomplete EFSA re-evaluation closure and emerging additive-mixture epidemiology.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
