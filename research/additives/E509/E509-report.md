# E509 (Calcium chloride) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E509
- **Name:** Calcium chloride
- **CAS numbers identified:** 10043-52-4 (anhydrous calcium chloride), 10035-04-8 (calcium chloride dihydrate)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic chloride salt (alkaline-earth metal chloride)
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium dichloride, calcium chloride anhydrous, CaCl2, INS 509 / E509
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Primarily industrially manufactured mineral salt** (commercially obtained from Solvay-process streams, natural salt brines, or prepared by substitution reactions).

Evidence:
- 21 CFR 184.1193 identifies both CAS numbers and manufacturing routes (Solvay byproduct, natural brines, substitution reactions). [S3]
- PubChem lists calcium chloride identity, formula (CaCl2), IUPAC name (calcium dichloride), and synonyms including E/INS naming. [S9][S10]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Firming agent (JECFA functional class) and broad multifunctional processing aid in U.S. regulation.
- **Mechanism of action (inferred from regulatory functions + chemistry):** Provides calcium/chloride ions that modulate ionic strength and pH behavior, supports texture/firmness, and can influence processing chemistry (including Maillard/caramelisation pathways in some heat-processed systems).
- **Common food categories (U.S. CGMP maxima listed):** baked goods, beverages, cheese, processed fruit/vegetables, sauces/gravies, jams/jellies, meat products, plant protein products, and others.

Evidence:
- 21 CFR 184.1193(c)-(d) lists functional classes and food-category maximum as-served levels under CGMP. [S3]
- JECFA chemical entry lists calcium chloride as a firming agent food additive. [S5]
- Recent process-model studies report CaCl2 effects on acrylamide/HMF and flavor-compound formation under heating conditions. [S12][S13]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion/re-evaluation found:** EFSA Journal scientific opinion on chlorides (E507/E508/E509/E511), published **2019**.
- **Approval status:** **Approved/authorised in EU**; EFSA opinion states chlorides are authorised under Annex II and III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008.
- **ADI / health-based guidance:** No explicit classical ADI statement located in the opinion abstract; EFSA identified a **reference value of 40 mg chloride/kg bw/day** for assessment.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity was reported in the panel summary.
  - Mean exposure estimates across age groups were at/below the 40 mg/kg bw/day reference value.
  - High-percentile exposure was slightly above this value in toddlers/children/adolescents.
  - Overall, EFSA concluded chloride exposure from E507/E508/E509/E511 at reported uses does not raise a safety concern.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and experimental toxicology
- EFSA summarized chlorides as having low acute oral toxicity, with no concern for genotoxicity or carcinogenicity in its reviewed database for the chloride group (E507-509/E511).
- EFSA abstract notes no developmental effects in rats for magnesium chloride hexahydrate at 800 mg/kg bw/day (same chloride-group assessment context).
- A 2022 zebrafish embryo food-safety toxicology paper reported adverse developmental/cardiac/neural effects above 0.4% CaCl2, with complete embryo mortality at 0.8%-1.6% at 24 hpf.

### Epidemiological / human-relevant evidence
- EFSA indicates animal data suggested possible chloride-related blood-pressure effects but considered human data more appropriate for deriving a no-concern intake reference (40 mg chloride/kg bw/day).
- No robust human epidemiological studies specifically isolating E509 intake and long-term outcomes were identified in this focused search.

### Mechanistic/process concerns
- Two recent food-system heating studies indicate CaCl2 can reduce acrylamide in some matrices but may increase HMF/furan-family process contaminants under certain conditions, indicating a process-chemistry tradeoff rather than a direct systemic toxicity signal at approved use levels.

Evidence:
- EFSA opinion abstract via PubMed (PMID 32626371). [S8]
- Zebrafish toxicology study (PMID 36123048). [S11]
- Heating model studies with CaCl2 and process-contaminant shifts (PMID 37049974; PMID 36608554). [S12][S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA reported exposure (chlorides as food additives E507-509/E511):**
  - Mean: 2 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) to 42 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers)
  - 95th percentile: 5 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) to 71 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers)
- **Reference benchmark used by EFSA:** 40 mg chloride/kg bw/day.
- **ADI exceedance risk interpretation:**
  - Mean exposures were at/below the EFSA reference value.
  - High-percentile exposures were slightly above it in toddlers, children, and adolescents.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** high-consuming children/toddlers in the EFSA exposure scenarios.

Evidence:
- EFSA scientific opinion abstract text (PMID 32626371) and EFSA journal publication page reference. [S1][S8]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - JECFA ADI is “NOT LIMITED.”
  - EFSA did not identify genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern in reviewed data.
  - Calcium/chloride are normal dietary ions and E509 remains authorized.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - EFSA high-percentile exposure estimates exceeded the 40 mg/kg bw/day reference value in some child groups.
  - Experimental data show toxicity at sufficiently high concentrations (e.g., zebrafish embryo model).
  - Process studies suggest potential tradeoffs (e.g., HMF/furan increases in some heated systems).

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported use/use levels.
  - FDA status is GRAS with explicit CGMP and category limits in 21 CFR 184.1193.
  - JECFA ADI remains NOT LIMITED.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - EFSA identified high-percentile subgroup exposure near/above its reference value.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA exposure exceedance at the 95th percentile in children/toddlers/adolescents adds some uncertainty at high intake.
  - Chloride-related blood-pressure mechanistic signal (from animal data reviewed by EFSA) warrants monitoring in high-intake contexts.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Regulators reviewed here did not identify a broad safety failure at authorized levels.
  - No major jurisdictional bans or IARC carcinogenic classification were identified.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None identified from the reviewed regulatory and literature sources.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Ongoing authorization across EFSA/FDA/JECFA contexts.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B entry found for calcium chloride.

### 2. Rationale
The total evidence supports **low-to-limited risk under regulated food-use conditions**. Strong regulatory convergence (EFSA no-safety-concern conclusion at reported use levels, FDA GRAS, JECFA NOT LIMITED) weighs heavily toward low concern, while subgroup high-percentile exposure and high-concentration experimental effects argue against a pure risk-free classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
