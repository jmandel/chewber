# E470a (Sodium/potassium/calcium salts of fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E470a
- **Name:** Sodium, potassium and calcium salts of fatty acids
- **CAS number(s):**
  - Sodium stearate: **822-16-2** (PubChem RN cross-reference)
  - Potassium stearate: **593-29-3** (PubChem RN cross-reference)
  - Calcium stearate: **1592-23-0** (PubChem RN cross-reference)
  - Note: the eCFR text for 21 CFR 184.1229 reports calcium stearate as **1529-23-0**.
- **Chemical class:** Alkali/alkaline-earth metal salts of long-chain fatty acids (soap-like fatty acid salts; emulsifier/anticaking class)
- **Common synonyms:** Salts of myristic, palmitic and stearic acids (calcium, potassium, sodium); sodium stearate; potassium stearate; calcium stearate; salts of fatty acids
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured salts derived from food fatty acids from edible fats/oils)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic fatty-acid salts reduce interfacial tension and improve powder flow and lubrication.
- **Functional roles:** Emulsifier, binder, anticaking agent (explicit in 21 CFR 172.863), plus lubricant/release and stabilizing uses (for calcium stearate in 21 CFR 184.1229).
- **Common food categories:** Broadly used across multiple authorized categories in EU at quantum satis; EFSA notes widespread but not universal use, with frequent use in solid supplement/confectionery style categories.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised.
- **Latest core safety re-evaluation found:** **2018** (EFSA Journal 16(3):5180).
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for E470a/E470b and that these additives are of no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
- **Key conclusions (2018):**
  - No mutagenicity concern from available data (limited dataset; negative bacterial assays cited).
  - Sodium oleate chronic study did not support carcinogenic potential, though NOAEL could not be derived.
  - Estimated contribution of E470a/E470b fatty-acid moieties was up to about **5%** of total saturated fatty-acid intake.
- **Additional EFSA item (2023):** NDA panel reassessed allergenicity context for mustard-derived behenic acid used to manufacture emulsifiers including E470a and concluded reaction risk in mustard-allergic individuals is extremely unlikely (<=1% probability) under proposed conditions.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** Approved for use as food additive in the salts-of-fatty-acids form; calcium stearate separately affirmed GRAS.
- **CFR citations and conditions:**
  - **21 CFR 172.863 (Salts of fatty acids):** permits aluminum, calcium, magnesium, potassium, and sodium salts of qualifying fatty acids; use as binder/emulsifier/anticaking agent under GMP; labeling conditions required.
  - **21 CFR 184.1229 (Calcium stearate):** affirmed GRAS as a direct human food ingredient; use under current GMP.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Database entry used:** “SALTS of MYRISTIC, PALMITIC and STEARIC ACIDS (CALCIUM, POTASSIUM, SODIUM)” (JECFA chemical page ID 3487).
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED** (database) / historically **Not limited** (WHO Food Additives Series monograph text).
- **Evaluation year shown:** **1985** (with later specification-history entries, including 1988 revision code in the record).

### IARC
- No IARC monograph classification was found for **sodium stearate**, **potassium stearate**, or **calcium stearate** in the current IARC classifications dataset (last update shown in dataset: 2026-02-25).
- A distinct compound, **glycidyl stearate**, appears separately in IARC as Group 3; this is not E470a.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology backbone (EFSA 2018):**
  - Salts dissociate in GI tract to fatty-acid carboxylates + cations.
  - Limited direct chronic/repro tox data for salts, but read-across to fatty acids plus available genotoxicity/carcinogenicity data supported no-safety-concern conclusion at reported uses.
- **Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity:**
  - Available mutagenicity studies (calcium caprylate, potassium oleate, magnesium stearate) were negative.
  - Sodium oleate rat study: pancreatic adenomas noted; EFSA considered no carcinogenic potential signal but NOAEL not identifiable from that study design.
- **Recent PubMed signal:**
  - Search specific to E470a/food-additive context yields very limited direct new toxicology literature; most recent directly relevant item is EFSA-related risk work (e.g., 2023 mustard-behenic-acid allergenicity context including E470a), not new systemic-toxicity evidence for E470a itself.
- **Mechanistic concern noted by EFSA:**
  - EFSA highlighted that other emulsifiers have shown microbiota-related effects in some studies, but no additive-specific microbiome studies were available for E470a/E470b at the time.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA considered E470a/E470b authorized broadly (quantum satis) but actually used in a subset of categories.
- EFSA estimated the **fatty-acid moiety contribution** from E470a/E470b to be up to around **5%** of overall saturated fatty-acid intake at mean exposure.
- Because EFSA and JECFA do not set a numerical ADI for this additive group (no need/not specified), classical “ADI exceedance” framing is limited.
- **Potentially relevant subgroups:**
  - High consumers of supplement-like categories where use levels were frequently reported.
  - Mustard-allergic individuals in the specific scenario of emulsifiers manufactured from mustard-derived behenic acid (separate allergen question; EFSA 2023 judged risk extremely unlikely under proposed conditions).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA (2018) and JECFA (not specified/not limited) indicate low toxicological concern at reported uses; additive is based on common dietary fatty-acid chemistry.
  - Evidence against: Direct additive-specific chronic/reproductive datasets are limited; EFSA itself notes evidence gaps.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Major regulators continue to permit use; EFSA concluded no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses; FDA has explicit allowed uses (21 CFR 172.863) and calcium stearate GRAS affirmation (21 CFR 184.1229).
  - Evidence against: Some uncertainty remains from limited direct long-term data and specific raw-material impurity/source questions.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Data gaps (limited dedicated tox package), plus scenario-specific allergen considerations when mustard-derived behenic acid is involved in emulsifier manufacture.
  - Evidence against: No major regulator currently flags systemic toxicity concern at approved uses; no IARC signal for E470a salts.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Very limited.
  - Evidence against: No major bans, no IARC listing for sodium/potassium/calcium stearates, and no EFSA/JECFA withdrawal due to systemic toxicity concerns.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence is dominated by consistent regulatory conclusions of low concern at current uses, balanced against acknowledged toxicology data limitations and specific impurity/source-allergen context considerations.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
