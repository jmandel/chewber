# E950 (Acesulfame K) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E950
- **Name:** Acesulfame potassium (Acesulfame K)
- **CAS number(s):** 55589-62-3
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of an oxathiazinone dioxide high-intensity sweetener (sulfamate sweetener)
- **Common synonyms:** Acesulfame K, Potassium acesulfame, Acesulfame potassium, E 950, INS 950
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic (industrial sweetener)

Evidence: JECFA chemical entry identity fields and CAS; eCFR section identity line; PubChem synonyms/property records [S3][S4][S10][S11].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Non-nutritive high-intensity sweetener used at low concentrations to provide sweetness; also used as a flavor enhancer [S6].
- **Common food categories:** Foods generally (except meat and poultry under US CFR), including beverages and sugar-reduced products [S2][S6].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion/re-evaluation found:** EFSA FAF Panel re-evaluation published **2025** (PLS published 2025-04-30; linked full opinion DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2025.9317) [S1].
- **Approval status:** Approved/re-evaluated food additive in EU re-evaluation programme [S1].
- **ADI:** **15 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **Change from prior ADI:** Replaces SCF 2000 ADI of 9 mg/kg bw/day [S1].
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - ADI based on rat NOAEL 1500 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Highest EU exposure estimate generally below ADI for all population groups (no safety concern).
  - Recommended tightening impurity specifications (e.g., 5-chloro-acesulfame, acetylacetamide, Pb/Hg limits) [S1].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not listed as a GRAS-affirmed direct food substance in Part 184 search; regulated as an **approved food additive** in Part 172 [S8][S2].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.800**.
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 172.800):**
  - May be safely used as a **general-purpose sweetener and flavor enhancer** in foods generally, **except meat and poultry**.
  - Use must follow current good manufacturing practice and not exceed amount reasonably required for technical effect.
  - Identity/specification conditions include purity not less than 99% (dry basis) [S2].

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-15 mg/kg bw** (interpreted as daily intake framework for food additive ADIs).
- **Last evaluation year in JECFA database entry:** **1990** (Meeting 46; TRS 806 link shown on entry).
- **Historical note on entry:** prior years include earlier ADI history and an initial period with no ADI allocated [S3][S4].

### IARC
- **Classification result:** No acesulfame/acesulfame potassium entry found in the current IARC list dataset used by the official "List of Classifications" app.
- **Cross-check:** Aspartame appears in the same dataset (Group 2B), supporting that the dataset itself is populated and searchable [S5][S7].

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in sources reviewed (EFSA, eCFR/FDA, JECFA, IARC dataset) [S1][S2][S3][S5].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2025 used toxicology data including a rat NOAEL of 1500 mg/kg bw/day and concluded no safety concern at estimated EU exposures under the updated ADI [S1].
- A 2026 mechanistic/in vitro study reported **synergistic nephrotoxicity signals** when acesulfame K was co-exposed with benzoic acid (CTNNB1/STAT3 axis), but this is not standalone human causal evidence [S13].

### Human and epidemiological evidence
- A 2024 study reported an association between higher acesulfame K intake and increased central precocious puberty risk in girls, with accompanying experimental data; observational design and confounding risk remain relevant limitations [S14].
- A 2022 study reported links between higher acesulfame K intake and preterm-risk/uterine contractility signals with supporting experimental work; interpretation is constrained by dose/context and design limitations [S15].

### Carcinogenicity/genotoxicity context
- A 2025 NSS carcinogenicity review and a 2020 acesulfame potassium review reported no consistent human-relevant carcinogenic signal for Ace-K in reviewed evidence, but both are literature syntheses and should be interpreted with study-quality/conflict context in mind [S12][S16].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark:** EFSA ADI 15 mg/kg bw/day [S1].
- **Typical/high-end intake conclusion (EU):** EFSA reported highest exposure estimates were generally below ADI across population groups [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not indicated by EFSA for EU populations in the 2025 re-evaluation [S1].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations to monitor despite below-ADI estimates:**
  - High consumers of products containing multiple non-nutritive sweeteners.
  - Children/adolescents (because of emerging endocrine-related observational signals in recent literature).
  - Pregnancy-related contexts where additive co-exposure or high intake could matter [S14][S15].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA 2025 concluded exposure was generally below ADI with no safety concern [S1].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Numeric ADI is still required (not "ADI not specified").
  - Newer studies report potential endocrine/reproductive and mixture-toxicity signals that warrant monitoring [S13][S14][S15].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved by EFSA/FDA framework/JECFA with established ADIs and explicit use conditions [S1][S2][S3].
  - No IARC classification entry identified for acesulfame in current list dataset [S5][S7].
  - EFSA exposure conclusion indicates no current ADI exceedance concern at population level [S1].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Mechanistic and observational studies continue to raise specific hypothesis-generating concerns [S13][S14][S15].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Credible newer studies suggest potential endocrine/reproductive and co-exposure renal concerns [S13][S14][S15].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EFSA did not reduce ADI; it increased from 9 to 15 mg/kg bw/day and still found no safety concern at estimated exposure [S1].
  - No broad regulatory withdrawal/bans identified [S1][S2][S3].

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None compelling from current regulatory sources.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Not banned in major jurisdictions in reviewed sources.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification found for acesulfame itself in current dataset [S5][S7].
  - EFSA/JECFA retain ADI-based approvals [S1][S3].

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence remains regulatory-approval dominant: EFSA (2025), FDA CFR approval route, and JECFA all maintain permitted use with ADI/GMP controls. Newer studies raise plausible concerns (especially endocrine/reproductive signals and mixture nephrotoxicity), but these do not currently overturn the main regulatory safety determination at typical exposure levels.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
