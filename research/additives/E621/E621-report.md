# E621 (Monosodium Glutamate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E621 (INS 621).
- **Primary name**: Monosodium L-glutamate (MSG).
- **CAS number(s)**: 142-47-2 (listed in WHO/JECFA database entry for MONOSODIUM L-GLUTAMATE).
- **Chemical class**: Sodium salt of the amino acid L-glutamic acid.
- **Common synonyms**: Sodium glutamate, MSG, glutamic acid monosodium salt (monohydrate naming appears in JECFA chemical names).
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Glutamate is naturally present in foods and human metabolism; commercial MSG is generally produced by fermentation of carbohydrate feedstocks (starch/sugar beet/sugar cane/molasses), i.e., industrial biomanufacture of a molecule identical to naturally occurring glutamate.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Provides/enhances umami (savory) taste via free glutamate.
- **Regulatory functional class**: WHO/JECFA classifies MSG as a **food additive, flavour enhancer**.
- **Common food categories**: EFSA indicates broad use in EU foods and highlights key contributors to exposure such as fine bakery wares, soups/broths, sauces, meat and meat products, seasonings/condiments, and food supplements.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Approved/authorised in the EU as part of glutamates (E620-E625); re-evaluated by EFSA.
- **Latest re-evaluation year found**: **2017**.
- **ADI**: **30 mg/kg body weight/day** (group ADI for E620-E625, expressed as glutamic acid).
- **Key conclusions**:
  - No genotoxicity concern identified.
  - NOAEL used for ADI derivation: 3,200 mg MSG/kg bw/day (neurodevelopmental study), UF=100.
  - EFSA concluded exposure can exceed the ADI (and in some scenarios doses associated with adverse human effects) for certain population groups; EFSA recommended reviewing maximum permitted levels.

### FDA / U.S. CFR
- **Status**: GRAS.
- **CFR citation**: **21 CFR 182.1** (Title 21) explicitly lists monosodium glutamate among common ingredients regarded as safe for intended use.
- **Conditions of use**:
  - Under **21 CFR 182.1**, use is tied to **good manufacturing practice (GMP)**, including using no more than reasonably required for intended technical effect.
  - Under **21 CFR 101.22(a)(5)**, any added monosodium glutamate must be declared as "monosodium glutamate" in ingredient labeling.
- **FDA consumer-facing statement**: FDA states MSG is considered GRAS and reports inconsistent reproducibility of sensitivity reactions in controlled studies.

### JECFA (WHO/FAO)
- **ADI**: **Not specified** (group ADI context for L-glutamic acid and relevant salts).
- **Last evaluation year shown in WHO JECFA entry**: **1987**.

### IARC
- No IARC Monographs group classification for monosodium glutamate was identified in the current IARC "List of Classifications" dataset (last-update metadata in retrieved script: 2026-02-25 CET); "monosodium glutamate" and CAS 142-47-2 were not present.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC materials.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **EFSA re-evaluation (2017)**: Broad toxicology dataset reviewed; no genotoxicity concern; ADI derived from a neurodevelopmental NOAEL of 3,200 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology (2025, rat GLP feeding studies)**: 28-day and 90-day dietary studies reported no toxicologically significant findings; NOAEL at highest tested dose (approx. >=3,170 mg/kg bw/day males; >=3,620 mg/kg bw/day females in the 90-day study). Note: publication discloses industry affiliation/funding.
- **J Xenobiotics (2025, rat high-dose overconsumption model)**: Oral doses 0.8-3 g/kg bw/day for 8 weeks associated with dose-dependent hepatic/renal/cardiac toxicity markers and histopathologic changes.

### Epidemiological / human data
- **J Allergy Clin Immunol (2000, multicenter DBPC challenge)**: Large bolus doses (often without food) could trigger symptoms in some self-identified sensitive participants, but responses were inconsistent on retesting and no persistent or serious effects were observed.
- **FDA/FASEB summary**: Mild short-term symptoms may occur in sensitive people at >=3 g MSG without food; typical serving with added MSG is reported as <0.5 g.

### Mechanistic concerns
- **Systematic review (Food Sci Nutr, 2024)** reports potential links between MSG exposure and gut microbiota/metabolic dysbiosis mainly from limited preclinical literature, with weak direct human evidence.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake context (FDA)**:
  - Average adult intake of glutamate from protein foods: ~13 g/day.
  - Added MSG intake estimate: ~0.55 g/day.
- **EFSA exposure concern**:
  - Refined exposure assessment indicated potential ADI exceedance among high consumers in all population groups and among toddlers/children with medium exposure.
  - High exposure in infants/children/adolescents may reach levels associated with some adverse effects in humans.
- **Recent national assessment (China, 2023)**:
  - Measured-intake estimate: 17.63 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Apparent-consumption estimate: 40.20 mg/kg bw/day (authors note likely overestimation due to cooking loss not considered).

## Risk Assessment
- **Recommended Chewber tier**: **moderate**.
- **Rationale**:
  - Strong regulatory acceptance remains (EU authorised, FDA GRAS, JECFA ADI not specified).
  - However, EFSA established a finite ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day) and explicitly identified potential exceedance in some populations, plus human symptom signals at high bolus intake and mixed newer literature including high-dose animal toxicity reports.
  - Net interpretation: generally safe within intended use/GMP, but not "risk_free" due exposure exceedance scenarios and dose-dependent adverse findings at high intakes.
