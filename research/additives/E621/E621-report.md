# E621 (Monosodium Glutamate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E621
- **Name:** Monosodium glutamate (MSG); in JECFA listing: **Monosodium L-glutamate**
- **CAS number(s):** 142-47-2 (WHO JECFA record)
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salt of L-glutamic acid; amino-acid salt used as a flavour enhancer
- **Common synonyms:** MSG; sodium glutamate; glutamic acid monosodium salt monohydrate; monosodium L-glutamate monohydrate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Glutamate occurs naturally in foods and in the body, while commercial MSG is produced industrially by fermentation (FDA)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Flavour enhancer (umami/savoury taste)
- **Mechanism of action:** Umami perception is mediated by dedicated umami taste receptors (T1R1/T1R3) responsive to glutamate compounds, including MSG.
- **Common food categories:** Seasonings/condiments, soups/broths, sauces, meat/meat products, fine bakery products, and other processed foods where savoury flavour enhancement is desired.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved food additive (as part of glutamic acid/glutamate group E620-E625).
- **Latest re-evaluation found:** 2017 scientific opinion (EFSA ANS Panel).
- **ADI:** **30 mg/kg body weight/day** (group ADI, expressed as glutamic acid).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern identified.
  - ADI derived from NOAEL 3,200 mg MSG/kg bw/day with uncertainty factor 100.
  - EFSA noted that exposure for some population groups could exceed the ADI and even doses associated with adverse effects in humans; EFSA recommended reviewing maximum permitted levels.

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **GRAS status:** FDA states added MSG is **"generally recognized as safe" (GRAS)**.
- **CFR citation:**
  - **21 CFR 182.1(a)**: Commissioner regards monosodium glutamate as safe for intended use.
  - **21 CFR 182.1(b)**: Use must follow good manufacturing practice (amount reasonably required for intended effect, food-grade quality, etc.).
  - **21 CFR 101.22(h)(5)**: If used as an ingredient, it must be declared as "monosodium glutamate" on label.
- **Conditions of use (practical):** Intended-use/GMP framework and mandatory ingredient-name declaration.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Record found:** WHO JECFA database entry for **MONOSODIUM L-GLUTAMATE** (INS 621).
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED** (group ADI for glutamic acid and ammonium/calcium/monosodium/magnesium/potassium salts).
- **Last evaluation year on record:** **1987**.

### IARC
- **Classification:** No IARC Monographs classification found for monosodium glutamate in the current IARC "List of Classifications" dataset checked (updated date shown in dataset).

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed here.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA 2017 (regulatory re-evaluation) found no adverse effects across short-term, subchronic, chronic, reproductive, and developmental studies except increased kidney/spleen weights without adverse histopathology; this was not treated as adverse.
- A 2025 rat perinatal study (PubMed) reported developmental neurotoxicity findings at **very high dose** levels (LOAEL 6000 mg/kg bw; NOAEL 3000 mg/kg bw).
- A 2025 rat study reported dose-dependent reproductive toxicity/sperm-quality impairment over 30 days at 30-1000 mg/kg bw.

### Epidemiological / human evidence
- FDA notes blinded challenge studies in self-identified sensitive individuals have not consistently reproduced reactions to MSG.
- FDA cites historical adverse-event reports but indicates causality with MSG was not confirmed.

### Mechanistic / broader evidence
- Recent review papers raise hypotheses for obesity/metabolic and developmental concerns, but repeatedly emphasize limited high-quality human data.
- A 2025 toxicology methods paper (39 MSG toxicity/amelioration studies) concluded none were "high" quality for direct human-safety inference due dose-model relevance and reporting limitations.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake signal (U.S. FDA communication):** Average adult intake estimated at about **13 g/day glutamate from protein foods** and around **0.55 g/day added MSG**.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA reported that estimated exposure for some groups may exceed the 30 mg/kg bw/day group ADI.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Children/high consumers of flavoured processed foods; individuals who report sensitivity under high bolus intakes (especially without food).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not specified" (group ADI context).
  - Glutamate is endogenous and naturally present in many foods.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA set a finite ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day) and flagged exceedance risk for some groups.
  - Recent animal studies continue to report dose-related adverse findings.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved in EU/U.S.; FDA GRAS position maintained.
  - Human challenge evidence does not consistently show reproducible MSG-specific reactions.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA explicitly highlighted possible ADI exceedance and risk-management need.
  - Credible, though dose-sensitive, animal toxicity signals remain in recent literature.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved additive but with clear caveats: EFSA ADI and exceedance concern in some groups.
  - New animal studies suggest potential neurodevelopmental/reproductive effects at elevated or prolonged exposures.
  - Evidence-quality review shows substantial uncertainty in existing toxicology literature quality.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification.
  - JECFA ADI "not specified" and long-standing widespread regulatory acceptance.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Some recent animal findings indicate potentially serious endpoints (developmental/reproductive) at higher doses.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification for MSG.
  - EFSA did not conclude inability to confirm safety; instead it established an ADI and requested exposure-control review.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence supports a regulated-but-cautious profile: major regulators continue to permit MSG, but EFSA’s 2017 re-evaluation introduced a group ADI and explicitly noted potential exceedance in some populations. Recent animal literature provides additional concern signals but is limited by model relevance and study-quality heterogeneity.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
