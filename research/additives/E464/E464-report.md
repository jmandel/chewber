# E464 (Hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E464
- **Primary name:** Hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose (HPMC), also called hypromellose
- **CAS number(s):** 9004-65-3
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified cellulose ether (non-ionic cellulose derivative)
- **Common synonyms:** Hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, hypromellose, HPMC
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from cellulose, then chemically substituted)

Evidence used:
- 21 CFR section text identifies hydroxypropyl methylcellulose and CAS Reg. No. 9004-65-3 ([21 CFR 172.874, e-CFR text mirror, URL](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/172.874)).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** HPMC hydrates in water to form viscous colloidal systems and films, increasing viscosity and stabilizing dispersed phases.
- **Common technological functions:** Emulsifier, film former, protective colloid, stabilizer, suspending agent, thickener.
- **Common food categories:** Broad multi-category use where texture/stability are needed (e.g., sauces/dressings, bakery systems, desserts, fillings, and other processed foods under GMP use patterns).

Evidence used:
- FDA CFR function/use roles in 21 CFR 172.874(b) ([URL](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/172.874)).
- EFSA re-evaluation considered broad authorised food-additive uses under MPL and refined exposure scenarios ([EFSA Journal opinion listing URL](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/5047); abstract accessible via PubMed [URL](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28719429/)).

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved food additive in the EU (E464), re-evaluated by EFSA ANS Panel.
- **Latest re-evaluation:** 2018 EFSA Journal opinion on celluloses (E460-E466).
- **ADI:** Panel concluded there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for E464 and related celluloses.
- **Key conclusions:** No safety concern at reported uses/exposure; no need for numerical ADI; concern remained for toxic-element specifications (arsenic/lead/cadmium/mercury) and microbiological criteria in specifications.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA 2018):** No numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at reported uses/exposure.
- **JECFA historical position:** ADI not specified/not limited, with caveat that some high-substitution products lacked sufficient data in a 1985 follow-up context.
- **Animal toxicology (direct HPMC):** Older PubMed-indexed studies include 3-month repeated oral rat administration and other toxicity/teratology studies (titles indicate toxicology focus; detailed abstracts limited for older records).
- **Recent literature trend:** Recent PubMed review evidence emphasizes gut-microbiome concern signals mainly from some food additives broadly (including certain cellulose-based thickeners), but direct high-quality causal harm evidence specific to HPMC remains limited.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA (2018) exposure modelling for celluloses including E464 reported high-end estimated intakes in children substantially above adult means, but **still concluded no need for a numerical ADI** and no safety concern at reported uses.
- Reported EFSA abstract ranges (mg/kg bw/day):
  - Regulatory maximum level scenario: mean up to 191 (children), 95th percentile up to 506 (children).
  - Refined scenarios: lower, but children remained the highest-exposed group.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not directly framed as exceedance because EFSA/JECFA did not set a numeric ADI for E464.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers among infants/children due bodyweight-normalized exposure; people with sensitive GI tolerance may experience effects at very high fiber/gum intakes (evidence strength limited for HPMC-specific harm).

Source:
- EFSA opinion abstract via PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28719429/

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Supports this tier:**
  - EFSA concluded no need for a numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses.
  - JECFA historical ADI not specified/not limited.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - Not an endogenous molecule; it is a chemically modified polymer.
  - EFSA highlighted specification-related contaminant concerns (toxic elements, microbiological criteria), meaning risk control depends on quality/spec compliance.

#### limited
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Approved in major jurisdictions (EU and US) with regulatory frameworks.
  - FDA has explicit use conditions in 21 CFR 172.874 and GMP-based function-limited use.
  - No strong human carcinogenic/genotoxic regulatory signal from EFSA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - Old JECFA note on inadequate data for some high-substitution products indicates residual uncertainty for edge-case materials.

#### moderate
- **Supports this tier:**
  - High exposure estimates in children (mg/kg bw/day) suggest margin-sensitive populations.
  - Emerging mechanistic literature on food-additive/gut-microbiome interactions creates a plausible theoretical concern space for some cellulose-derived additives.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - EFSA still found no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at those modeled exposure levels.
  - No strong direct clinical harm signal specific to HPMC identified in reviewed records.

#### high
- **Supports this tier:**
  - Very limited support in current reviewed evidence.
- **Argues against this tier:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban found.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found.
  - No EFSA conclusion of unresolved safety for normal food uses.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence is the EFSA 2018 re-evaluation (no numerical ADI needed, no safety concern at reported uses) plus longstanding JECFA acceptance and active FDA authorization with use conditions. Countervailing signals are mostly specification-quality caveats and indirect microbiome-mechanistic concerns rather than robust direct harm evidence for E464 itself.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
