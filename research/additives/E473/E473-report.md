# E473 (Sucrose esters of fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E473 / INS 473. [S6][S7]
- **Name:** Sucrose esters of fatty acids (also written as sucrose fatty acid esters). [S1][S6]
- **CAS number(s):** No single CAS was reported in the WHO/JECFA entry reviewed (`CAS_NO: null`), consistent with E473 being a variable mixture of mono-, di-, and tri-esters rather than one discrete molecule. [S6][S7]
- **Chemical class:** Non-ionic sucrose fatty acid ester emulsifier mixture (mono-, di-, tri-esters of sucrose with fatty acids). [S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Sucrose fatty acid esters; SEFs; INS 473. [S4][S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (industrially esterified from sucrose plus edible fatty-acid sources). [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic sucrose esters lower oil-water interfacial tension and stabilize emulsions/foams; they can also act as texturizers and coating agents depending on formulation. [S4]
- **Common food categories from regulatory text:**
  - Baked goods and baking mixes
  - Chewing gum
  - Coffee/tea beverages with dairy ingredients or analogues
  - Confections and frostings
  - Dairy analogues and frozen dairy desserts
  - Whipped milk products
  - Surimi-based fabricated seafood products
  - Protective coatings for selected fruits [S4]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest EFSA opinion identified:** 2023 follow-up re-evaluation (EFSA Journal 21(4):e07961 / 7961). [S1][S2]
  - **ADI:** group ADI **40 mg/kg bw/day** (for E473 and E474), used by EFSA in 2010/2012/2018 and referenced in the 2023 follow-up. [S1][S2][S3]
  - **Key conclusions (2023):**
    - Technical data supported amending EU specifications.
    - E473 was reported as not used in infant FC 13.1.1/13.1.5.1/13.1.5.2, so infant <16 weeks safety assessment for those FCs was not performed.
    - Updated exposure estimates exceeded the group ADI for many population groups. [S1][S2]
  - **Overall status:** approved in the EU food additive framework, with exposure/specification caveats. [S1][S2]
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Status:** approved direct food additive (not GRAS-affirmed as an E473-specific Part 184 entry in the CFR text reviewed).
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 172.859 (Sucrose fatty acid esters)**.
  - **Conditions of use (examples):** must meet compositional/impurity limits (e.g., ester content and solvent/heavy-metal limits), permitted in listed food categories, and used under GMP at no more than reasonably required. [S4][S5]
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **0-30 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI for sucrose esters of fatty acids and sucroglycerides; 1997 evaluation entry).
  - **Later evaluation note (2020):** high dietary estimate for children 3-9 years (113 mg/kg bw/day for SEFs+SOEs) exceeded ADI by about 4x, but JECFA noted the estimate was conservative and requested refined exposure assessment. [S6]
- **IARC:**
  - No specific IARC Monographs classification entry found for sucrose esters of fatty acids / E473 in the current IARC classification dataset used by the list page. [S8][S9]
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in EFSA/JECFA/FDA/IARC sources reviewed. [S1][S4][S6][S8]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal studies:**
  - A chronic rat feeding/carcinogenicity study (2 years, up to 5% diet) reported no treatment-related increase in tumors and concluded no toxicity/carcinogenicity under tested conditions. [S11]
- **Epidemiological data:**
  - No robust human epidemiological studies directly linking dietary E473 exposure to adverse outcomes were identified in the PubMed search used here. [S10]
- **Mechanistic / cellular evidence:**
  - Caco-2 work reported sucrose esters increased membrane permeability at non-toxic concentrations and altered membrane/cytoskeletal behavior, indicating biological activity at epithelial barriers, but this was not direct evidence of systemic toxicity from food-level exposure. [S12]
- **Regulatory exposure signal:**
  - EFSA (2018, 2023) and JECFA (2020 comment) each flagged modeled high-end intakes that can exceed the ADI in some groups, especially children/high consumers, while also noting conservative assumptions likely overestimate real-world exposure. [S1][S3][S6]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (2018):** estimates exceeded the 40 mg/kg bw/day ADI for many groups, especially toddlers and children; EFSA stated estimates were likely overestimates due to conservative assumptions/data gaps. [S3]
- **EFSA follow-up (2023):** updated estimates still exceeded the ADI for many groups; no assessment was performed for infant categories where use was reportedly absent. [S1]
- **JECFA (2020, Meeting 89):** high estimate for children 3-9 years at 113 mg/kg bw/day for SEFs+SOEs (about 4x ADI), with explicit note that assumptions were conservative and refinement was requested. [S6]
- **Vulnerable populations:** high-consuming toddlers/children and scenarios with broad additive-presence assumptions. [S1][S3][S6]

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** long regulatory history; no IARC listing for E473 found; no carcinogenic signal in the cited rat chronic study.
  - **Evidence against this tier:** repeated EFSA/JECFA high-end intake scenarios exceed established group ADIs in some populations, so a strict "risk_free" placement is not supported. [S1][S3][S6][S9][S11]
- **limited**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** approved by EFSA and FDA frameworks; JECFA has an established ADI rather than prohibition; no strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in key reviewed sources.
  - **Evidence against this tier:** exposure exceedance findings are recurring across EFSA/JECFA assessments, especially for children/high-end consumers. [S1][S3][S4][S6]
- **moderate**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** additive is approved but with meaningful caveats, including ADI exceedance in modeled subpopulations and repeated regulator requests for improved exposure refinement/spec updates.
  - **Evidence against this tier:** regulators still maintain authorization and did not conclude an unmanageable hazard at authorized uses. [S1][S3][S6]
- **high**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** none found (no major bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment for E473 itself, no regulatory withdrawal signal).
  - **Evidence against this tier:** active authorizations remain in EU/US frameworks and JECFA retains an ADI-based risk-management approach. [S4][S6][S8][S9]

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest current signal is not intrinsic severe toxicity but **exposure-management risk**: high-end modeled intakes can exceed group ADIs in children/toddlers under conservative assumptions. That pattern is stronger than a "limited"-only signal, but does not reach "high" because major authorities still authorize E473 and did not classify it as carcinogenic.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
