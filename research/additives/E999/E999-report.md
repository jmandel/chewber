## Function in Food

**Primary function:** Emulsifier / Foaming agent

**Mechanism of action:** Quillaia saponins are amphiphilic triterpenoid glycosides. Their molecular structure — hydrophobic aglycone (quillaic acid) linked to hydrophilic sugar chains — allows them to adsorb at oil–water and air–water interfaces, stabilising emulsions and generating persistent foams.

**Common food categories:**
- Water-based flavoured drinks (FC 14.1.4) — primary use as foaming agent (e.g., ginger beer, root beer, cream soda)
- Cider and perry (FC 14.2.3)
- Alcoholic beverages
- Chewing gum, confections, frostings
- Frozen dairy desserts, fruit and water ices
- Condiments and relishes
- Jams and jellies
- Hard and soft candy
- Dietary/food supplements (proposed extension)
- Coffee and tea products

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (European Union)

- **Status:** Approved (Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008, Annex II)
- **ADI:** **3 mg saponins/kg bw per day** (established 2019, replacing the former SCF ADI of 0–5 mg spray-dried extract/kg bw/day from 1978)
- **Last evaluation year:** 2019 (re-evaluation); 2024 follow-up + 2024 extension of uses opinion
- **Basis of ADI:** NOAEL of 1,500 mg quillaia extract/kg bw per day from a 2-year rat study, with conservative assumption that extract contained 20% saponins (= 300 mg saponins/kg bw/day), divided by uncertainty factor of 100
- **Key finding (2019):** No concern for genotoxicity. No indication of carcinogenicity. Toxicity attributed to saponin content. None of the refined brand-loyal exposure estimates exceeded the ADI. Panel recommended revising EU specifications to differentiate Type 1 and Type 2 extracts and to lower limits for toxic elements.
- **Key finding (2024 follow-up):** Panel recommended lowering maximum limits for lead, mercury, arsenic; adding maximum limit for cadmium and calcium oxalate (expressed on saponins basis). At certain high-exposure scenarios (95th percentile), ADI exceedance was possible but scenarios were considered overestimates.
- **Key finding (2024 extension):** Extension of uses in food supplements (FC 17.1, 17.2) would not result in ADI exceedance when using typical reported use levels.
- **Authorised food categories in EU:** FC 14.1.4 (Flavoured drinks) and FC 14.2.3 (Cider and perry)

### FDA (United States)

- **Status:** GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) via self-determination supported by FDA "no questions" letters
- **GRN 165:** Quillaia extract Type 1 — foaming agent in semi-frozen carbonated and non-carbonated beverages at ≤500 mg/kg dry weight. Filed by American Beverage Association, closed November 9, 2005. FDA had no questions.
- **GRN 903:** Quillaia extract Type 2 — emulsifier in beverages, chewing gum, confections, dairy analogs, fats/oils, frozen desserts, dietary supplements, etc. at 30–185 mg/100g. Filed by Naturex SA, closed April 21, 2021. FDA had no questions.
- **CFR citation:** GRAS notification per 21 CFR §170.30(a)(b); no specific dedicated CFR section for quillaia extract. Not listed in 21 CFR Part 172/173 as a food additive; status is via GRAS self-determination.

### JECFA (Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives)

- **ADI:** **0–1 mg quillaia saponins/kg bw** (group ADI for Type 1 and Type 2 combined)
- **Last substantive evaluation:** 65th meeting (2005); specifications revised at 79th meeting (2014)
- **Key history:** Originally evaluated at 26th meeting. At 29th meeting, ADI of 0–5 mg/kg bw was set for unpurified extract. In 2001 (57th meeting), the Committee could not allocate an ADI for the semi-purified product due to limited compositional data. At 61st meeting (2003), extracts were split into Type 1 and Type 2. At 65th meeting (2005), group ADI of 0–1 mg saponins/kg bw was established.
- **Intake concern (JECFA):** At 500 mg/kg proposed GSFA level, high-percentile consumers (especially children) could exceed the ADI. At actual manufacturer use levels (95–100 mg/kg), intakes were generally below the ADI.

### IARC (International Agency for Research on Cancer)

- **Classification:** **Not classified** — Quillaia extract does not appear in IARC Monographs Volumes 1–140.

### Notable Bans or Restrictions

- No known bans in major jurisdictions. Quillaia extract is approved in the EU, USA, Australia/New Zealand, and Codex Alimentarius.
- Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) adopted the JECFA group ADI of 0–1 mg saponins/kg bw.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal Toxicology

- **Acute toxicity (mice):** Oral LD₅₀ ≈ 1,600 mg/kg bw; much less toxic orally than by parenteral routes (subcutaneous LD₅₀ 650, IV 280 mg/kg bw) (Efimova et al., 1966).
- **13-week rat study:** NOAEL = 0.6% in diet (~400 mg/kg bw/day). At 2% and 4%, reduced relative liver weight and increased stomach weight, but no histopathological changes (Gaunt et al., 1974).
- **84-week mouse study:** NOAEL = 0.5% (~700 mg/kg bw/day). At 1.5%, slightly lower body-weight gain in males. No compound-related histopathological effects. No carcinogenicity (Phillips et al., 1979).
- **2-year rat study:** NOAEL = 1,500 mg/kg bw/day (highest dose tested, 3% in diet). Lower body weights in males at highest dose attributed to reduced food consumption (palatability). No compound-related histopathological changes. No carcinogenicity. Variety of tumours found at similar incidence in control and treated groups (Drake et al., 1982).
- **90-day gavage study (saponins):** At 1,200 mg saponins/kg bw (by gavage, not in diet), severe toxic effects including inflammatory changes in forestomach, larynx, trachea, and lungs. The Panel noted gavage vs. dietary administration and different test material composition may explain differences.

### Genotoxicity

- EFSA 2019: Available genotoxicity data did not indicate a concern for genotoxicity.

### Carcinogenicity

- No indication of carcinogenicity in either the 84-week mouse study or the 2-year rat study.

### Reproductive/Developmental Toxicity

- No dedicated reproductive or developmental toxicity studies were available. However, in the chronic rat study, no adverse effects were observed in reproductive organs.

### Gut Effects

- Saponins affect gut permeability in vitro — reduce transmural potential difference in rat jejunal mucosa (Gee et al., 1989). Several studies showed changes in GI organ weights, but these were not accompanied by consistent treatment-related histopathological changes.

### Haemolytic Activity

- Quillaia saponins are haemolytic in vitro (crude extract causes haemolysis at ~20 µg/mL in chicken erythrocytes). In vivo haemolytic activity is much weaker due to low oral absorption. Individual saponin fractions (QH-A, QH-B, QH-C) differ widely in potency.

### ADME

- EFSA considered that intact saponins are absorbed to a low extent, are hydrolysed in the GI tract, and the aglycone (quillaic acid) is absorbed only to a limited extent. Conclusions based on read-across from structurally similar saponins.

### Epidemiological Data

- No epidemiological studies specific to quillaia extract were identified.

## Exposure Assessment

### Typical Dietary Intake

- **At actual use levels (95–100 mg/kg):** Estimated intakes in the USA were 0.3 mg/kg bw/day (mean consumers) and 0.54 mg/kg bw/day (90th percentile), well below any ADI.
- **At GSFA proposed level (500 mg/kg):** Intakes could be significantly higher, especially for heavy consumers of flavoured drinks.

### ADI Exceedance Risk

- **Under JECFA ADI (0–1 mg saponins/kg bw):** At the GSFA 500 mg/kg level, high-percentile consumers in Australia and UK children aged 1.5–4 years exceeded the ADI. At manufacturer use levels (~100 mg/kg), most populations remained below the ADI, though UK children at 97.5th percentile were marginal (5.2 mg extract/kg bw/day ≈ 105% of old ADI).
- **Under EFSA ADI (3 mg saponins/kg bw):** None of the refined brand-loyal exposure estimates exceeded the ADI for any population group (2019 evaluation). The 2024 follow-up found potential exceedance only in highly conservative scenarios (all flavoured drinks assumed to contain quillaia, 95th percentile) for children, adolescents, and elderly.

### Vulnerable Populations

- **Children (especially toddlers and young children):** Higher relative consumption of flavoured beverages per kg body weight. JECFA and EFSA both identified children as the population group most likely to approach or exceed ADI.
- **High consumers of soft drinks/flavoured beverages.**

## Risk Assessment

### Tier-by-Tier Analysis

#### risk_free
**Evidence for:** Quillaia extract is of natural origin, has been used traditionally in beverages, and shows low acute oral toxicity. No genotoxicity concern, no carcinogenicity signal, and EFSA confirmed safety at current use levels.
**Evidence against:** An ADI has been established (not "ADI not specified"), indicating the substance is not considered completely without hazard potential. Saponins have known biological activity (haemolysis, gut permeability effects). JECFA's ADI is relatively low (0–1 mg saponins/kg bw). This is not a "risk_free" profile.

#### limited ✓
**Evidence for:** Approved with established ADI by both EFSA and JECFA. FDA has accepted GRAS status with no questions. No serious safety signals in chronic studies — the NOAEL in the 2-year rat study was the highest dose tested (1,500 mg/kg bw/day). No genotoxicity, no carcinogenicity, no reproductive organ effects in chronic studies. IARC has not classified it. At typical use levels, dietary exposure does not exceed the ADI for any population group. The main concern (saponin toxicity) manifests only at high doses, especially via parenteral or gavage administration.
**Evidence against:** Some scenarios show potential ADI exceedance in children at high consumption. The JECFA ADI (0–1 mg saponins/kg bw) is relatively conservative. No reproductive/developmental toxicity studies are available. Saponins have documented biological activity on gut epithelium.

#### moderate
**Evidence for:** The JECFA ADI is quite low (0–1 mg saponins/kg bw), and at the Codex proposed use level (500 mg/kg), ADI exceedance is clearly possible for children and high consumers. EFSA's 2024 follow-up found potential ADI exceedance at 95th percentile in children, adolescents, and elderly under conservative scenarios. No reproductive/developmental toxicity data exist. Saponins affect gut permeability.
**Evidence against:** The EFSA ADI (3 mg saponins/kg bw) is more permissive, and EFSA's refined exposure assessments found no exceedance. The 2024 extension opinion concluded no ADI exceedance at typical use levels. The scenarios showing exceedance were acknowledged as overestimates by the Panels. The lack of repro/developmental studies is partially compensated by the absence of reproductive organ effects in the 2-year study.

#### high
**Evidence for:** None applicable. Not banned anywhere. No IARC classification. EFSA confirmed safety. No strong mechanistic evidence of serious harm.
**Evidence against:** All evidence argues against a "high" classification.

### Rationale

Quillaia extract (E 999) is approved by all major regulatory bodies (EFSA, FDA, JECFA, FSANZ, Codex). EFSA's 2019 re-evaluation established an ADI of 3 mg saponins/kg bw/day and found no exceedance in refined exposure scenarios. The 2024 follow-up and extension opinions reaffirmed safety at typical use levels. FDA has accepted GRAS status without questions. Chronic animal studies show no carcinogenicity or compound-related histopathology at tested doses, with very high NOAELs. The main safety consideration is saponin-related biological activity (haemolysis in vitro, gut permeability effects), but these manifest primarily at high doses or parenteral administration, not at dietary exposure levels. The JECFA ADI is more conservative (0–1 mg/kg bw), and under worst-case assumptions (500 mg/kg in all flavoured drinks), children could exceed it — but actual use levels are much lower (95–100 mg/kg). The absence of dedicated reproductive/developmental toxicity studies is a data gap but not a safety signal.

### Recommended Tier: **limited**
