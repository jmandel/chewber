# E312 (Dodecyl gallate / Lauryl gallate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E312
- **Name:** Dodecyl gallate
- **CAS number(s):** 1166-52-5
- **Chemical class:** Alkyl gallate (long-chain ester of gallic acid), synthetic phenolic antioxidant
- **Common synonyms:** Lauryl gallate; dodecyl 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic** food antioxidant (classified among synthetic phenolic antioxidants in the toxicology literature) [Sources 8, 14].

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antioxidant/preservative in fat-containing foods.
- **Mechanism of action:** Chain-breaking phenolic antioxidant behavior that slows lipid oxidation/rancidity (described in synthetic phenolic antioxidant literature) [Source 14].
- **Common food categories identified in current regulatory texts reviewed:**
  - **Margarine/oleomargarine** in the US standard of identity context (21 CFR 166.110(b)(5)) [Source 6].
  - **Chewing gum category context in EU follow-up legal text** (as discussed in Regulation (EU) 2018/1481 recitals) [Source 4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation identified:** 2015 EFSA Journal opinion on dodecyl gallate (E312) [Sources 1, 2].
- **Evaluation year:** 2015 [Sources 1, 2].
- **ADI (current interpretation from EFSA opinion abstract):** EFSA reported it could not complete the assessment with the available reproductive/developmental toxicity data and did not support maintaining the prior group ADI as valid; therefore a current numeric ADI is not reliable for risk benchmarking [Source 1].
- **Key conclusions from EFSA abstract:**
  - No safety concern was identified for the **reported uses and use levels** at that time.
  - But the toxicological database was insufficient to complete full safety assessment and support a valid group ADI [Source 1].
- **Approval status context (EU legal follow-up):** Regulation (EU) 2018/1481 documents this EFSA uncertainty and tightened authorized uses (recitals indicate removal of uses without submitted data, with a narrow retained context discussed for chewing gum) [Source 4].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status from Title 21 search:** No dedicated dodecyl gallate GRAS affirmation section was found in the full Title 21 XML snapshot reviewed.
- **Specific CFR citation found:** **21 CFR 166.110(b)(5)** (margarine standard), where propyl/octyl/dodecyl gallates are allowed as preservatives with a combined/individual limit of **0.02% by weight of finished food** [Source 6].
- **Practical status:** **Use-specific approved** in margarine standard; not explicitly identified as a standalone GRAS section in the searched Title 21 corpus [Source 6].

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA entry found:** DODECYL GALLATE (chemID 1885) [Sources 7, 8].
- **ADI:** **NO ADI ALLOCATED** [Sources 7, 8].
- **Last evaluation year:** **1996** [Source 8].
- **JECFA note:** temporary ADI from 1993 was not extended [Source 8].

### IARC
- Checked IARC classification resources and the current classification dataset script feed.
- No dodecyl/lauryl gallate listing was identified in the checked IARC resources [Sources 9, 10, 11].

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No explicit global IARC-style carcinogenic classification found.
- EU legal follow-up indicates significant restriction/removal actions tied to unresolved data gaps in EFSA follow-up context [Source 4].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory uncertainty signal (strongest weight):** EFSA 2015 opinion abstract indicates insufficient reproductive/developmental toxicity data for full assessment and that prior group ADI support was not maintained, despite no concern at then-reported use levels [Source 1].
- **Broader SPA toxicology review (includes lauryl/dodecyl gallate among SPAs):** Animal high-dose SPA exposure is discussed with potential DNA-damage/cancer-relevant concerns; this is broad-class evidence, not a direct chronic dietary E312 cohort finding [Source 14].
- **In vitro mechanistic study (2011):** Dodecyl gallate showed oxidative stress/apoptosis signaling in a melanoma cell model at experimental concentrations, indicating biological activity but not direct evidence of harm at typical food-exposure levels [Source 13].
- **In vivo/in vitro pharmaceutical context (2018):** Dodecyl gallate in a therapeutic model reported low toxicity in tested systems and reduced toxicity in one formulation context; relevance to long-term food additive exposure is limited [Source 12].
- **Recent studies (2025) remain mostly mechanistic/therapeutic rather than dietary safety cohorts** [Source 15].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake (quantitative):** No robust recent population intake estimate (mg/kg bw/day) was found in the collected sources.
- **ADI exceedance framing:**
  - EFSA: prior group ADI was not supported as valid in the re-evaluation context [Source 1].
  - JECFA: no ADI allocated [Source 8].
  - Therefore, classic ADI exceedance quantification is limited.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Frequent consumers of food categories where use remains legally possible (jurisdiction-dependent), including children/high consumers of confectionery/chewing-gum-type products.
  - Individuals with higher cumulative intake of synthetic phenolic antioxidants.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: No direct IARC classification found; EFSA did not identify concern for reported use levels in the specific 2015 context.
  - Evidence against: EFSA also stated insufficient data for full safety confirmation and invalidity of prior group ADI support.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: US has a specific permitted use context (margarine standard at capped level); no definitive global carcinogen classification found.
  - Evidence against: EU follow-up regulatory tightening and EFSA unresolved data gaps go beyond a simple low-concern profile.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved/allowed in some settings with caveats; evidence of mechanistic effects exists but is mostly high-dose or non-dietary context.
  - Evidence against: EFSA inability to confirm full safety and ADI validity concerns are stronger than a typical moderate profile.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA re-evaluation reported inability to complete safety assessment and did not support continued validity of prior group ADI; EU follow-up enacted strong restriction/removal measures in response to missing data.
  - Evidence against: Not universally banned in all major jurisdictions; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing identified.

### 2. Rationale
The deciding factors are regulatory-scientific, not just mechanistic toxicology: EFSA’s latest re-evaluation for E312 flagged insufficient toxicological data for full safety confirmation and ADI validity, and EU follow-up legal measures tightened/remodeled authorizations accordingly. This outweighs the fact that limited use remains in some jurisdictions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**high**
