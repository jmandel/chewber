# E1405 (Enzyme-treated starch) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1405 (INS 1405 in JECFA/Codex naming).
- **Name:** Enzyme-treated starches.
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-84-9 (listed on JECFA chemical page as "STARCH, SOLUBLE").
- **Chemical class:** Enzymatically modified starch (partially hydrolyzed starch carbohydrate polymer).
- **Common synonyms:** "STARCHES, ENZYME-TREATED" (JECFA synonym), "ENZYME treated STARCHES".
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic / processed natural polymer** (derived from edible starch and modified by enzymatic treatment rather than full chemical substitution).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Enzymatic treatment (e.g., alpha-amylase, beta-amylase, glucoamylase, isoamylase, pullulanase in U.S. CFR context) partially hydrolyzes starch, changing molecular size and rheology; in FDA's food starch-modified framework, enzymatic treatment can yield a nonsweet nutritive saccharide polymer with dextrose equivalent (DE) < 20.
- **Primary technological role:** Thickener/texturizer (JECFA functional class: Food Additives - THICKENER).
- **Common food categories:** Processed foods using starch texturization (soups/sauces, bakery/confectionery systems, beverage systems and other formulated foods where modified starch functionality is used).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA / EU
- **EFSA search outcome:** Direct EFSA search and EFSA Journal page access from this environment returned 403/challenge responses, so direct page extraction from efsa.europa.eu was limited.
- **Latest relevant EFSA scientific opinion found:** 2017 EFSA re-evaluation of **12 modified starches** (E1404, E1410, E1412, E1413, E1414, E1420, E1422, E1440, E1442, E1450, E1451, E1452), retrieved via PubMed record of the EFSA paper (DOI: 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4911). E1405 is **not listed** in that EFSA re-evaluation set.
- **Approval status for E1405 in EU additive framework (interpreted from legislation):** EU food additive regulation text states the Regulation does not apply to "starch treated by amylolitic enzymes" (unless used as food additives), and the queried Union list amendment text did not show E1405 entries. This supports treating E1405 as **not in the current EFSA additive re-evaluation set / not explicitly listed as a currently authorised standalone additive in the queried Union list text**.
- **ADI (EFSA):** No E1405-specific EFSA numerical ADI identified in the queried sources. For the 2017 EFSA modified-starch group (excluding E1405), conclusion was no need for a numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Evaluation year (EFSA):** No dedicated EFSA E1405 re-evaluation identified; nearest relevant EFSA modified-starch opinion year is **2017** (not E1405-specific).
- **Key conclusion:** No dedicated EFSA E1405 opinion located; nearest EFSA modified-starch opinion is favorable but excludes E1405.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **Regulatory status:** **Approved food additive use** under **21 CFR 172.892 (Food starch-modified)**, not a standalone GRAS listing for "enzyme-treated starch" in queried Part 184 text.
- **GRAS status:** No direct E1405/enzyme-treated-starch GRAS entry found in queried **21 CFR Part 184** XML text.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.892**.
- **Conditions of use (key):**
  - Food starch-modified "may be safely used in food" under listed treatment limits.
  - Enzymatic modification subsection lists alpha-amylase, beta-amylase, glucoamylase, isoamylase, and pullulanase.
  - Limitation text indicates enzyme must be GRAS or approved for the purpose, and resulting nonsweet nutritive saccharide polymer has DE < 20.

- No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified in the checked sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (strongest):**
  - JECFA historical evaluations for enzyme-treated starches: ADI not specified (2016, 1982), with 2018 review focused on specifications.
  - EFSA 2017 modified-starch group opinion (not E1405-specific) reported no safety concern at reported uses and no need for numerical ADI for that re-evaluated group.
- **Human epidemiology (indirect to E1405):**
  - NutriNet-Sante 2025 cohort analysis found higher type 2 diabetes incidence for two additive-mixture patterns; one mixture included "modified starches." Authors note residual confounding and no causal proof from one observational study.
  - 2021 NutriNet-Sante exposure characterization reported modified starches among the most widely consumed additives (>90% consumers in that cohort), supporting high population exposure to modified-starch category additives, but not E1405-isolated risk.
- **Mechanistic/other literature:**
  - 2023 review of chemically modified starches discusses broad functionality and regulatory context but is not an E1405-specific hazard demonstration.

## Exposure Assessment
- **E1405-specific intake data:** No dedicated quantitative EFSA/JECFA population intake dataset for E1405 alone was identified in the queried materials.
- **Proxy evidence:** Cohort work indicates broad modified-starch exposure in the food supply (high prevalence of consumption in studied population), but this is category-level rather than E1405-specific.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA ADI framing is "not specified" (non-numerical), so classical numeric ADI exceedance calculations are not directly applicable.
  - No identified evidence in retrieved sources that E1405-specific exposures exceed a numeric health-based guidance value.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - High consumers of ultra-processed/additive-rich foods may have higher cumulative mixture exposure.
  - Children can have higher body-weight-normalized additive intakes in general; however, no E1405-specific numeric exceedance dataset was found.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA historical ADI classification for enzyme-treated starches is favorable (ADI not specified), and no IARC carcinogenic classification was found.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Data are not fully additive-specific in modern epidemiology; mixture-level observational signals (not causal) suggest some uncertainty for additive combinations.
  - EU regulatory handling suggests scope/classification complexity rather than straightforward pan-jurisdiction additive listing.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - U.S. has explicit allowed use framework under 21 CFR 172.892 with conditions.
  - JECFA evaluations remain broadly reassuring (non-numerical ADI approach, specification updates).
  - No direct carcinogenic classification signal from IARC checks.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Limited modern E1405-specific toxicology/epidemiology in the retrieved recent literature.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Observational cohort data on additive mixtures found associations with T2D incidence for patterns including modified starches, which could justify caution for cumulative exposure contexts.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Evidence is mixture-level and non-causal; no direct regulatory downgrading of E1405 from JECFA/FDA sources found.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified in retrieved EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC checks (no major ban pattern, no IARC 2A/2B/1 signal identified).
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Existing regulatory datasets are largely permissive/low-concern under intended use conditions.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest direct evidence (JECFA classification and FDA permitted-use framework) supports a low-concern profile when used as intended. The main uncertainty is not a direct E1405 hazard signal but limited modern additive-specific human outcome data and emerging mixture-level epidemiology that is hypothesis-generating rather than causal.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
