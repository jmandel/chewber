# E479b (Thermally oxidized soya bean oil interacted with mono- and diglycerides) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E479b
- **Primary name:** Thermally oxidized soya bean oil interacted with mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids (TOSOM)
- **CAS number(s):** 884318-13-2 (reported in EFSA re-evaluation; sourced from SciFinder in the EFSA document)
- **Chemical class:** Complex mixture of glycerol esters containing fatty acids from thermally oxidized soybean oil and mono-/diglycerides
- **Common synonyms:** TOSOM; oxidised/oxidized soya bean oil interacted with mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids; INS 479 (JECFA naming)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (made from edible soybean oil and mono-/diglycerides, then chemically processed by oxidation/interesterification/deodorization)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Emulsifier (JECFA functional class).
- **Mechanism of action (inference from composition + regulatory use):** Amphiphilic glyceride/oxidized lipid esters help stabilize fat-containing emulsions and can modify fat crystallization/release behavior.
- **Common food categories:** In the EU, EFSA states authorization is limited to **FC 02.2.2** (other fat and oil emulsions including spreads/liquid emulsions), with a restriction relevant to frying fat emulsions and MPL use scenario at 5,000 mg/kg.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest re-evaluation:** EFSA FAF Panel opinion, published 2018 (EFSA Journal 16(10):e05420).
- **Status:** Authorized in EU but **restricted** (single authorized category; limited market use reported).
- **ADI:** Historical ADIs were SCF 25 mg/kg bw/day and JECFA 30 mg/kg bw/day, but EFSA concluded available toxicology data were **insufficient to support the current ADI** (missing ADME and developmental toxicity data).
- **Key conclusion:** Despite ADI uncertainty, EFSA estimated toddler high exposure at ~10 mg/kg bw/day and compared it to NOAEL 5,400 mg/kg bw/day (rat chronic/carcinogenicity), yielding MoS ~540; EFSA concluded current use is of **no safety concern** under limited use conditions.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Chronic/carcinogenicity (rats):** 2.5-year dietary study cited by EFSA (and indexed in PubMed, 1993 Food Chem Toxicol) reported no treatment-related carcinogenic effect; EFSA used NOAEL 5,400 mg/kg bw/day (males; 7,400 females).
- **Subchronic (pigs):** EFSA cites a 98-day pig study with no adverse effects up to 4,000 mg/kg bw/day (limited design).
- **Genotoxicity/developmental data gaps:** EFSA reports no direct genotoxicity dataset for TOSOM and no reliable developmental toxicity studies; ADME data also unreliable/missing.
- **Mechanistic concern addressed by EFSA:** Potential reactive by-products (e.g., epoxides) were considered theoretically relevant, but EFSA notes low specification limits and no carcinogenic signal in chronic study; concern at first-contact site considered alleviated in context.
- **PubMed recency check:** Direct PubMed title/abstract search terms for E479b/TOSOM returned very limited literature (mainly EFSA 2018 re-evaluation and older 1993 carcinogenicity paper), with no new direct post-2018 primary safety studies found in that targeted query.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA dietary exposure (regulatory maximum scenario):**
  - Mean: <0.1 to 4.6 mg/kg bw/day (highest in toddlers)
  - P95: <0.1 to 10.1 mg/kg bw/day (highest in toddlers)
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Versus historical ADIs (25-30 mg/kg bw/day): modeled P95 exposure did not exceed ADI.
  - Caveat: EFSA stated available data were insufficient to support/retain the current ADI approach.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Toddlers are the highest modeled-exposure group; uncertainty remains because exposure estimates were conservative and based on MPL assumptions.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence for: No carcinogenic signal in chronic rat study; EFSA concluded no safety concern at current use; modeled MoS ~540.
  - Evidence against: Core data gaps (ADME, genotoxicity package, developmental toxicity) prevent a strong "risk-free" determination.

- **limited**
  - Evidence for: EU-authorized restricted use; JECFA ADI 0-30 mg/kg bw; no clear severe hazard signal in available in vivo studies; estimated exposure below historical ADIs.
  - Evidence against: EFSA could not support the historical ADI due to missing studies, which is a stronger caveat than a typical "limited-risk" profile.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence for: Regulatory acceptance exists, but with meaningful uncertainty: EFSA explicitly found toxicological package insufficient for ADI support; exposure conclusions rely on margin-of-safety logic and limited market use.
  - Evidence against: No evidence of carcinogenicity in long-term rat data; no current signal of population-level harm from authorized uses.

- **high**
  - Evidence for: Theoretical concern over reactive oxidation by-products and incomplete toxicology package.
  - Evidence against: No major-jurisdiction ban found; no IARC carcinogenic classification found; EFSA still concluded no safety concern under current limited use.

### 2. Rationale
The additive has a weak modern data package (missing ADME/developmental/genotoxicity dataset), which prevents a robust ADI-based confidence statement, but currently available chronic animal evidence and EFSA exposure modeling do not indicate an immediate high-risk profile at authorized use levels. The strongest uncertainty driver is **evidence incompleteness**, not a demonstrated severe hazard.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: moderate**
