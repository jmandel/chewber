# E1452 Research Report: Starch aluminium octenyl succinate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1452. [2]
- **Name:** Starch aluminium octenyl succinate (also written as starch aluminum octenyl succinate / aluminum starch octenylsuccinate). [2][13]
- **CAS number(s):** `9087-61-0` (reported in PubMed-indexed safety assessments for aluminum starch octenylsuccinate). [12][13]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (aluminum salt of octenyl-succinate substituted starch). [13]
- **Common synonyms:** Aluminum starch octenylsuccinate; Aluminium starch octenylsuccinate; E1452; starch aluminum octenyl succinate. [2][12][13]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (native starch chemically modified with octenyl succinic anhydride, then associated with aluminum). [13]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** OSA-modified starches act as amphiphilic starches used for emulsion stabilization and texture control; FDA’s food-starch framework covers this through controlled chemical modification limits. [4][11]
- **Primary technological roles:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener. [8][11]
- **Common food categories:**
  - Broad “food starch-modified” uses in foods under 21 CFR 172.892 (subject to reagent limits). [4]
  - Infant formula / formula for special medical purposes (JECFA discussion for OSA-modified starch at up to 20 g/L). [8]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Included in EFSA’s re-evaluation of authorized modified starch food additives (including E1452) and not identified as a safety concern at reported uses/use levels. [1][2]
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for the modified starch group (historically aligned with ADI “not specified”). [2]
- **Evaluation year:** **2017** (EFSA Journal 15(10):4911). [1][2]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxic concern in the EFSA read-across/in silico framework.
  - No treatment-related effects relevant for human risk assessment in high-dose animal data cited by EFSA.
  - Exposure estimate reported for E1452 food-supplement consumers (95th percentile): up to 22.1 mg/kg bw/day. [2]

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **GRAS status:** No dedicated Title 21 GRAS section naming “starch aluminium/aluminum octenyl succinate” was identified in this run; the additive is handled as an **approved food additive use** under the food-starch-modified regulation. [3][4]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR § 172.892 (Food starch-modified).** [3][4]
- **Conditions of use extracted:**
  - Food starch-modified may be safely used in food, subject to amount limitations needed for technical effect.
  - Specific relevant modification option: **1-octenyl succinic anhydride ≤ 2% and aluminum sulfate ≤ 2%**. [4]

### JECFA / WHO
- **Direct E1452 record:** No dedicated JECFA database entry was found for “starch aluminium/aluminum octenyl succinate” in the current WHO public database queries. [6]
- **Closest related JECFA record:** OSA-modified starch / starch sodium octenylsuccinate (INS 1450) shows ADI **NOT SPECIFIED**. [7][8]
- **ADI (closest related record):** **Not specified** (non-numerical). [7][8]
- **Last evaluation year (closest related record):** **2018** (specification-only); last explicit ADI entry shown: 2016. [8]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No entry found for E1452 or starch aluminium/aluminum octenyl succinate in IARC’s current list-of-classifications dataset. [9][10]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified from EFSA/eCFR/WHO-JECFA sources reviewed here. [2][4][8]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA 2017 group re-evaluation (includes E1452):** no genotoxic concern; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no numerical ADI needed. [2]
- **Neonatal piglet dietary study (2014):** OSA-modified starch up to 20 g/L in formula for 3 weeks was well tolerated, with no adverse effects on growth, clinical pathology, or pathology endpoints. [11]
- **Cosmetic safety reassessments (2002, 2023):** PubMed-indexed CIR reviews report no new signal sufficient to overturn prior safety conclusions for aluminum starch octenylsuccinate in cosmetic use contexts (not oral-food risk assessments, but relevant toxicological context). [12][13]
- **Epidemiological data:** No strong E1452-specific human epidemiology was identified in this run. [15]
- **Mechanistic concerns:** Broader aluminum exposure burden is a known dietary toxicology topic (EFSA 2008 aluminum opinion), but this is not an E1452-specific carcinogenic classification finding. [14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate (E1452):** 95th percentile exposure for food-supplement consumers up to **22.1 mg/kg bw/day** (EFSA abstract text). [2]
- **Infant-use context (related OSA-modified starch JECFA entry):** JECFA comments indicate no concern for infant formula use levels up to **20 g/L** in the evaluated context. [8]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA/JECFA use non-numerical ADI framing (“no numerical ADI needed” / “not specified”), so classical numeric ADI exceedance cannot be calculated directly. [2][8]
  - Potentially vulnerable populations to monitor: high food-supplement consumers; infants consuming specialized formulas; people with high cumulative aluminum exposure from multiple dietary sources. [2][8][14]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA and JECFA-related evaluations use non-numerical ADI framing and report low concern at intended uses. [2][8]
  - Evidence against: This is a chemically modified additive with an aluminum component, and evidence is partly read-across/group-based rather than extensive E1452-only datasets. [2][14]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: EU re-evaluation found no safety concern at reported uses; U.S. CFR includes explicit permitted conditions; no IARC listing for this additive. [2][4][9][10]
  - Evidence against: WHO/JECFA public database currently lacks a clearly separate E1452-specific entry, requiring reliance on closest related OSA-modified starch records for some international ADI context. [6][8]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Broader aluminum toxicology concerns (separate EFSA aluminum opinion) justify caution when total dietary aluminum burden is high. [14]
  - Evidence against: No direct EFSA/JECFA conclusion indicating unsafe E1452 use at authorized levels; no IARC 2A/2B/1 classification found for this additive. [2][9][10]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None found (no major bans, no IARC carcinogenic classification specific to this additive, no EFSA finding of inability to confirm safety at authorized uses). [2][9][10]
  - Evidence against: Multiple regulatory evaluations remain permissive at intended use levels. [2][4][8]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest direct regulatory evidence (EFSA 2017) indicates no safety concern for E1452 under reported uses and no need for a numerical ADI. U.S. use is controlled via explicit reagent limits in 21 CFR 172.892. The main residual uncertainty is that WHO/JECFA public records are clearer for INS 1450/OSA-modified starch than for a standalone INS 1452 entry, and aluminum-related risk is mostly a cumulative-diet issue rather than an E1452-specific hazard signal.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
