# E150a (Plain Caramel) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS**: E150a / INS 150a. [S5]
- **Primary name**: Plain caramel (caramel colour, Class I). [S5, S6]
- **CAS number(s)**: Not explicitly stated in the consulted EFSA/eCFR/JECFA primary pages for E150a (complex mixture; no single class-specific CAS confirmed from the required sources). [S1, S3, S5]
- **Chemical class**: Class I caramel colour; complex mixture formed by controlled heat treatment of food-grade carbohydrates (process colorant). [S3, S6]
- **Common synonyms**: Plain caramel; caustic caramel; caramel colour, Class I. [S5, S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic/process-derived** (made from food-grade carbohydrates via thermal processing, optionally with acids/alkalis/salts). [S3, S6]

## Function in Food
E150a functions as a **brown color additive**. Thermal treatment of carbohydrates creates high-molecular-weight colored products (caramelization/Maillard-derived chromophores) that impart brown color in foods and beverages. [S3, S6]

Common categories include soft drinks, sauces, bakery products, confectionery, seasonings, and other processed foods where brown coloration is desired. [S1, S2, S3]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Authorized food color in the EU (E150a is part of the caramel colours group E150a-d evaluated by EFSA). [S1]
- **ADI**: **300 mg/kg bw/day** as a **group ADI** for the sum of E150a, E150b, E150c, and E150d (2011 re-evaluation). [S1]
- **Evaluation year(s)**:
  - **2011** re-evaluation opinion (EFSA Journal 2004).
  - **2012** refined exposure statement (EFSA Journal 3092). [S1, S2]
- **Key conclusions**:
  - EFSA set a group ADI of 300 mg/kg bw/day and concluded that earlier conservative exposure scenarios could exceed ADI in some high consumers. [S1]
  - In EFSA's refined 2012 exposure update, mean exposure estimates were substantially below ADI across age groups; high-percentile toddlers at the upper bound were near/slightly above ADI in conservative assumptions. [S2]

### FDA / eCFR (United States)
- **GRAS status**: **GRAS** for caramel under **21 CFR 182.1235** when used according to GMP. [S3]
- **Color-additive status**: Caramel is also listed as a color additive for foods under **21 CFR 73.85**, with exemption from batch certification. [S3]
- **Conditions of use (Title 21)**:
  - **21 CFR 182.1235**: "generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice." [S3]
  - **21 CFR 73.85(c)**: May be safely used for coloring foods generally under GMP, except where standards of identity do not permit added color.
  - **21 CFR 73.85(e)**: Exempt from certification. [S3]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI**: **Not specified** for INS 150a (Plain caramel) in the JECFA food additive database entry. [S5]
- **Last evaluation year**: **1974** (as listed on the INS 150a JECFA entry page). [S5]
- **Related JECFA class page**: Class I caramel (plain/caustic caramel) entry confirms class identity and historical evaluations. [S6]

### IARC
- No IARC monograph classification was identified for **plain caramel / E150a** itself in the IARC classification dataset.
- A related contaminant of concern, **4-methylimidazole (4-MEI)**, is listed by IARC as **Group 2B** (possibly carcinogenic to humans). [S7, S8]

### Notable bans
- No broad ban of E150a in major jurisdictions (EU/US) was identified in the consulted regulatory sources; it remains ADI/GMP-managed. [S1, S3, S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- Historical carcinogenicity concern in mice is largely tied to **4-MEI** findings (not specific to plain caramel production chemistry), with mode-of-action uncertainty and non-genotoxic interpretations in some analyses. [S10]
- Newer animal studies report reproductive and gamete-related effects for 4-MEI at experimental doses in rodents (e.g., oocyte maturation and sperm motility effects). [S12, S13]
- A 2025 risk-assessment paper on reproductive/developmental toxicity reported margins of exposure above common concern thresholds using U.S. dietary exposure assumptions. [S11]

### Epidemiological / human evidence
- Direct human epidemiological evidence specifically linking **E150a** intake to adverse outcomes remains limited in the consulted PubMed records.
- Recent exposure-focused literature emphasizes declining 4-MEI exposure from caramel-color sources due reformulation/specification changes. [S9]

### Mechanistic concerns
- Main mechanistic concern in caramel-color discussions is **4-MEI** (and historically THI for some classes), which is primarily associated with ammonia-process caramel classes (III/IV), making relevance to Class I plain caramel comparatively lower. [S1, S5, S7, S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA refined 2012 estimates (caramel colours group, not E150a-only) reported mean exposure around:
  - toddlers: ~10 mg/kg bw/day
  - children: ~9.5 mg/kg bw/day
  - adolescents: ~6 mg/kg bw/day
  - adults: ~3.8 mg/kg bw/day
  - elderly: ~1.8 mg/kg bw/day
  - very elderly: ~1.5 mg/kg bw/day [S2]
- High-percentile (95th) estimates were approximately:
  - toddlers: ~100 mg/kg bw/day
  - children: ~60 mg/kg bw/day
  - adolescents: ~20 mg/kg bw/day
  - adults/elderly/very elderly: ~10 mg/kg bw/day [S2]

### ADI exceedance risk
- For the **group ADI** (300 mg/kg bw/day), refined estimates are generally below ADI, with conservative upper-bound high-consumer toddler scenarios nearest to or slightly above ADI. [S2]

### Vulnerable populations
- Toddlers and children with high intakes of caramel-colored products are the most relevant higher-exposure groups in EFSA modeling. [S1, S2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - JECFA lists ADI as **not specified** for INS 150a, consistent with low toxicological concern in normal use. [S5]
  - FDA lists caramel as GRAS under GMP and as permitted color additive under GMP. [S3]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - EFSA maintains a quantitative group ADI (300 mg/kg bw/day) and previously flagged high-consumer scenarios in conservative models. [S1, S2]
  - Related contaminant (4-MEI) has IARC Group 2B classification (though not specific to E150a). [S7, S8]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - EU/US/JECFA all allow use (ADI/GMP-managed), with no major-jurisdiction ban identified. [S1, S3, S5]
  - Refined EFSA exposure is mostly below the group ADI; concern is concentrated in conservative high-end scenarios. [S2]
  - Main toxicological signals in recent literature largely concern 4-MEI rather than plain caramel chemistry itself. [S9, S10, S11, S12, S13]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - Some high-consumer child/toddler scenarios approach or exceed the group ADI under upper-bound assumptions. [S2]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - EFSA history includes exceedance concerns in conservative scenarios and continued ADI management across caramel classes. [S1, S2]
  - Credible animal evidence exists for related compounds (4-MEI), even if direct relevance to E150a is limited. [S10, S12, S13]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - Regulatory consensus remains permissive (GRAS/GMP and JECFA ADI not specified for 150a).
  - E150a-specific harm signals are weaker than for ammonia caramel classes/contaminants. [S3, S5, S6]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Presence of an IARC Group 2B classification for related contaminant 4-MEI can trigger higher concern in contaminant-focused assessments. [S7, S8]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - No direct IARC classification for E150a itself.
  - No major regulatory ban/withdrawal identified; EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks still permit use. [S1, S3, S5]

### 2. Rationale
E150a is widely permitted and managed under GMP/ADI frameworks, with JECFA listing ADI as not specified for INS 150a and FDA retaining GRAS/color-additive authorization. The strongest concerns come from related contaminants (especially 4-MEI) and conservative high-consumer scenarios in grouped caramel-colour exposure models, rather than robust E150a-specific toxicity signals.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`**
