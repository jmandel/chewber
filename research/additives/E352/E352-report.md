# E352 (Calcium malates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E352 (calcium malates; commonly split as E352(i)/E352(ii) in international nomenclature).
- **Primary name used in international evaluations:** Calcium DL-malate / monocalcium DL-malate (JECFA entries).
- **Chemical class:** Salts of malic acid (hydroxy-dicarboxylic acid salts).
- **Synonyms found in regulatory/scientific databases:**
  - Calcium DL-malate; Hydroxybutanedioic acid calcium salt; Calcium-L-hydroxysuccinate; DL-monocalcium malate (JECFA).
- **CAS numbers:** Not consistently provided in the primary regulatory pages successfully retrieved in this session for E352-specific entries (JECFA chemical pages did not expose CAS fields in page content).
- **Natural vs synthetic origin:**
  - **Mixed/industrial:** related parent acid context indicates L-malic acid occurs naturally while DL-malic acid is commercially produced; calcium malates in food additive use are industrial salts produced from malic acid sources (inference from CFR malic acid identity + JECFA naming).

## Function in Food
- **Functional role:** acidity regulator and flavour enhancer (JECFA functional class fields for calcium DL-malate).
- **Mechanism of action (practical):** contributes acidity buffering/acidification and sourness modulation via malate anion and calcium salt form.
- **Common food categories (evidence available):**
  - Direct E352 category-level EU exposure tables were not retrievable from accessible EFSA pages in this session.
  - Related U.S. CFR text for **malic acid** (not calcium malate) indicates use across beverages, chewing gum, confectionery, jams/jellies, fruit products, etc., at GMP-constrained levels.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA scientific opinion found for calcium malate context:** EFSA ANS Panel (2018) on **di-calcium malate (DCM)** as novel food/source of calcium.
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA found insufficient evidence that proposed DCM differed from already authorised calcium malate source(s), and therefore EFSA was unable to complete a full novel-food safety assessment for DCM as presented.
- **ADI:** No numeric EFSA ADI identified for E352 from accessible EFSA pages in this session.
- **Evaluation year:** 2018 (for DCM opinion).
- **Access issues encountered:** two EFSA call/consultation URLs specific to malic acid/malates re-evaluation currently returned “Page not found” (404/archive-style page), so no newer EFSA re-evaluation document could be extracted from those URLs.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** JECFA group ADI “not specified” for DL-malic acid and sodium/potassium/calcium salts supports low concern at normal food-use levels.
- **EFSA data-quality caveat (2018 DCM opinion):** inability to fully assess DCM novel-food dossier due insufficient characterization/distinction from existing calcium malate sources; this is a **data-adequacy concern**, not a direct hazard confirmation.
- **Human epidemiology (indirect signal):** a 2025 prospective cohort on **food additive mixtures** (not single-additive causal proof) reported higher type 2 diabetes incidence for two additive-mixture patterns; one mixture included malic acid among multiple additives.
- **Recent direct calcium malate literature profile:** recent PubMed hits are dominated by production/absorption/technology studies, with limited new dedicated chronic toxicology for E352 as a single additive.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake:** no robust recent single-additive intake distribution for E352 could be extracted from accessible EFSA re-evaluation outputs in this session.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA: ADI “not specified” (no numerical threshold to compare against routine intake).
  - EFSA 2018 DCM opinion: exposure to DCM from proposed uses could not be calculated from submitted information.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - High consumers of ultra-processed foods with multiple acidulants/additives.
  - Individuals with high supplement intake where calcium salts are combined from multiple sources.
  - Data are insufficient for precise E352-only vulnerability quantification.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free placement:**
  - JECFA group ADI “not specified” for malic acid salts.
  - Malate is a known endogenous metabolite class context.
- **Evidence arguing against risk_free placement:**
  - EFSA did not provide a clean, complete positive safety conclusion for DCM dossier due characterization/exposure gaps.
  - Limited recent single-additive human safety datasets specific to E352.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited placement:**
  - Longstanding international regulatory use context and JECFA “not specified” ADI.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified for calcium malate/malic acid in checked monograph classification data.
  - No major bans identified in reviewed sources.
- **Evidence arguing against limited placement:**
  - EU re-evaluation clarity for E352-specific modern exposure/safety outputs is incomplete from currently accessible EFSA pages in this session.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate placement:**
  - Data gaps (exposure quantification and dossier characterization issues in EFSA DCM opinion).
  - Indirect epidemiological mixture-level findings including malic acid among many co-exposures.
- **Evidence arguing against moderate placement:**
  - No strong single-agent toxicological red flag for calcium malates from JECFA/IARC reviewed records.
  - Evidence for harm is indirect and not additive-specific.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high placement:**
  - None identified from reviewed authoritative regulatory sources.
- **Evidence arguing against high placement:**
  - No major regulatory bans found.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment identified for calcium malate.
  - JECFA ADI not specified does not align with high-risk classification.

### 2. Rationale
The main decision drivers are: (a) JECFA’s longstanding “ADI not specified” group assessment for malate salts, (b) absence of direct carcinogenic classification evidence, and (c) lack of compelling additive-specific harm signals. Counterbalancing this are evidence-quality gaps: EFSA dossier characterization limitations (for DCM) and sparse recent E352-only toxicology/exposure datasets.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
