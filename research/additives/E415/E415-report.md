# E415 Research Report: Xanthan gum

## Identity
- **E-number:** E415
- **Name:** Xanthan gum
- **CAS number(s):** 11138-66-2
- **Chemical class:** Microbial exopolysaccharide (high-molecular-weight hydrocolloid gum)
- **Common synonyms:** Xanthan gum, xanthan, INS 415, E415
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Natural (produced by fermentation with *Xanthomonas campestris*, then purified)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Xanthan gum hydrates in water and forms highly viscous, shear-thinning solutions that stabilize dispersions/emulsions and improve texture/suspension.
- **Common food functions/categories:** Stabilizer, emulsifier, thickener, suspending agent, bodying agent, foam enhancer; used broadly in foods under GMP.
- **CFR-described uses:** 21 CFR 172.695(e) explicitly allows use under GMP as stabilizer/emulsifier/thickener/suspending agent/bodying agent/foam enhancer where standards of identity do not preclude it.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorised as E415 in the EU (re-evaluated by EFSA panels).
- **Latest scientific opinion found:** EFSA Journal 2023 (doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2023.7951), follow-up of the 2017 re-evaluation.
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI ("no need for a numerical ADI" in 2017 re-evaluation; 2023 follow-up used MOE/reference-point approach for infants in FC 13.1.5.1).
- **Evaluation years:** 2017 (general re-evaluation), 2023 (infants below 16 weeks in FSMP/formulae and follow-up).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2017: no safety concern for general population at refined exposure; no genotoxicity concern; not applicable to infants under 12 weeks.
  - 2023: no safety concerns for infants below 16 weeks consuming FC 13.1.5.1 at assessed exposure; technical data supported specification amendments.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not listed as a direct-food GRAS substance in 21 CFR Part 184; instead listed as an approved direct food additive in **21 CFR 172.695**.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.695 (Xanthan gum)**.
- **Conditions of use (from 21 CFR 172.695):**
  - Produced from nonpathogenic, nontoxic *X. campestris* strain and free of viable cells.
  - Must meet specifications (e.g., residual isopropyl alcohol <= 750 ppm; viscosity and identity tests).
  - Use under GMP in permitted food technological functions.
  - Labeling: container must identify additive as "food grade" and provide adequate use directions.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Database record:** WHO JECFA chemical record for XANTHAN GUM (Chemical ID 802).
- **ADI:** NOT SPECIFIED.
- **Last evaluation year shown:** 2016 (with earlier 1986 entry also shown).
- **Key point:** 2016 comments indicate no safety concern for infant formula/FSMP use at the evaluated maximum use level (1000 mg/L), using neonatal piglet data and MOE considerations.

### IARC
- **Classification status:** No IARC monograph classification identified for xanthan gum.
- Checked IARC classification pages/dataset; no xanthan entry found in the current classified-agent dataset snapshot.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified from EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/regulatory toxicology
- **EFSA 2017:** No adverse effects reported at highest tested doses in chronic/carcinogenicity studies; no genotoxicity concern.
- **JECFA 2016 infant-focused entry:** Used neonatal piglet NOAEL and infant exposure estimates; concluded no safety concern for assessed infant-formula context.

### Human/clinical and epidemiological signals
- **Infant NEC case reports (2012):** Case-series/case-report literature described late-onset NEC in premature infants exposed to xanthan-containing thickener products (signal-generating, not definitive causality).

### Mechanistic/biological studies (PubMed)
- **Nat Microbiol 2022:** Human gut microbiomes can metabolize xanthan gum; additive can shift microbial ecology (Ruminococcaceae-linked xanthan degradation pathways).
- **mSphere 2020 (murine):** Xanthan gum diet altered microbiota dynamics and interacted with antibiotic effect, reducing *C. difficile* colonization in that model.
- **Curr Res Food Sci 2025 (rat model with processed meat matrix):** Xanthan-containing diet changed microbiome composition; study reported reduced oxidative-stress and down-regulated inflammatory pathways in that context.
- **Nutrients 2026 in vitro:** Among 12 additives tested, xanthan gum reduced total bacterial load in healthy-control fermentations (in vitro finding).

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2017 refined exposure estimates:** up to 64 mg/kg bw/day (children, general population), 38 mg/kg bw/day (children as supplement consumers), and 115 mg/kg bw/day (infants consuming FSMP/special formulae).
- **JECFA 2016 infant context:** conservative infant intake estimate cited as 220 mg/kg bw/day for fully formula-fed infants when deriving MOE context.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Classical ADI exceedance comparison is not applicable because EFSA/JECFA set ADI as not specified/no numerical ADI.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Premature/very young infants (historical NEC signal context; later regulatory follow-up in infant-specific categories)
  - High-intake infants on specialized formula categories
  - Individuals sensitive to GI effects at high short-term intakes (abdominal discomfort noted in EFSA summary)

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA/JECFA ADI not specified (no numerical ADI required).
  - No genotoxicity concern and no chronic/carcinogenic adverse effects at tested high doses in EFSA summary.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Historical NEC signal in premature infants (case reports/surveillance context).
  - Emerging microbiome-modulation findings create biologically plausible, though not clearly adverse, effects.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved in EU and US (CFR section with defined manufacturing/specification/use controls).
  - JECFA and EFSA conclude no safety concern under assessed uses.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Infant-prematurity context required dedicated follow-up risk assessment.
  - Newer microbiome studies suggest measurable biological effects, requiring continued monitoring.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Presence of infant-specific caution history and dedicated post-re-evaluation review could be viewed as a regulatory caveat.
  - Some mechanistic/in vitro and animal data indicate microbiome-related perturbations.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No reduced ADI, no ADI exceedance framework, and no conclusion of unsafe use by EFSA/JECFA in permitted contexts.
  - No major bans/withdrawals.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Very limited: isolated severe-infant case reports are serious clinical events.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 signal for xanthan gum.
  - No broad regulatory prohibition; instead ongoing authorization with safety conclusions.

### 2. Rationale
The total evidence supports broad regulatory acceptance with no numerical ADI requirement and no confirmed major toxicological hazard at permitted uses. The main cautionary area is very young/premature infant contexts and evolving microbiome evidence, which does not currently overturn regulatory conclusions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
