# E218 (Methyl p-hydroxybenzoate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E218
- **Primary name:** Methyl p-hydroxybenzoate (methylparaben)
- **CAS number:** 99-76-3
- **Chemical class:** Paraben preservative (p-hydroxybenzoic acid ester)
- **Common synonyms:** methylparaben; methyl 4-hydroxybenzoate; methyl p-oxybenzoate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic** for food use (produced by methanol esterification of p-hydroxybenzoic acid per 21 CFR text).

## Function in Food
- **Technological function:** Preservative / antimicrobial agent in food.
- **Mechanism of action:** As a paraben preservative, it inhibits microbial growth (particularly yeasts/moulds and some bacteria) via membrane/enzyme disruption; regulatory texts classify it functionally as an antimicrobial preservative.
- **Common food-use context:** Preservative use where authorized under additive legislation and GMP limits; FDA text specifies a GMP maximum of 0.1% in food.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest scientific opinion located:** EFSA AFC Panel opinion (2004) on E214-219.
  - **ADI:** EFSA set a **full group ADI of 0-10 mg/kg bw/day** for the **sum of methyl and ethyl p-hydroxybenzoic acid esters and their sodium salts**.
  - **Key conclusion:** Propyl paraben was **excluded** from that group ADI; EFSA stated it could not recommend an ADI for propyl paraben due to no clear NOAEL.
  - **Approval status for E218:** E218 remains listed in EU additive legislation tables (E214-219 group entries include E218: methyl p-hydroxybenzoate).
  - **Access note:** Direct current EFSA Journal/Wiley pages were not fully accessible from this VM; EFSA opinion text was extracted from archived EFSA PDF URL and validated against DOI metadata.
- **FDA / CFR (US):**
  - **Status:** GRAS-affirmed direct food substance.
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1490 (Methylparaben)** within **21 CFR Part 184** (Direct food substances affirmed as GRAS).
  - **Conditions of use:** antimicrobial agent; use at levels not exceeding GMP; FDA text states current GMP gives a **maximum 0.1% in food**.
  - **Access note:** `ecfr.gov/current/...` human-facing page redirected to an unblock page in this VM, but official eCFR API endpoints were accessible and used.
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - **Substance record:** JECFA evaluation page for methyl p-hydroxybenzoate (INS 218).
  - **ADI:** **0-10 mg/kg bw** (group ADI as sum of ethyl, methyl, and propyl esters of p-hydroxybenzoic acid).
  - **Latest evaluation year on methyl-specific page:** **1973**.
  - **Supporting monograph:** WHO Food Additives Series 5 monograph reports 0-10 mg/kg bw estimate for ethyl/methyl/propyl esters.
- **IARC:**
  - No listing found for methylparaben / methyl p-hydroxybenzoate in current IARC list-of-classifications dataset (updated 2026-02-25 in the loaded list app).
  - **Classification:** No IARC group classification identified for E218.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major food-use ban for E218 identified in EU/US from the retrieved sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology base (EFSA 2004):**
  - No developmental toxicity signal in reviewed methylparaben studies up to tested high doses in multiple species.
  - In vitro estrogenic activity exists for parabens, but EFSA reported no in vivo uterotrophic activity for methyl/ethyl/propyl in classical assays.
  - EFSA identified concern primarily with **propyl** paraben male reproductive findings (LOAEL 10 mg/kg bw/day), while methyl/ethyl showed NOAEL 1000 mg/kg bw/day in juvenile rat male reproductive endpoints.
- **Recent literature (PubMed):**
  - 2025 scoping review (Food Chemistry) reports measurable dietary paraben occurrence across food categories; notes potential cumulative-intake scenarios where ADI exceedance could occur in some settings.
  - 2026 review (Toxicology and Industrial Health) describes weak estrogenic potency vs endogenous hormones but highlights uncertainty from repeated/cumulative exposure.
  - Newer animal/experimental studies (e.g., zebrafish and rodent models) report reproductive/metabolic effects under experimental exposure conditions; external validity to typical dietary human exposure remains uncertain.
  - Human epidemiology signal is limited and mixed; a small pilot prenatal EDC study reported no clear methyl/propyl paraben-specific significant association for hypospadias.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Historical regulatory exposure context:** EFSA 2004 text indicates then-available Tier-1 intake estimates were below 10 mg/kg bw/day, but EFSA also noted human food exposure assessment was not adequately characterized.
- **Current evidence direction:** Recent review evidence suggests geographically variable dietary contamination profiles, with potential cumulative exposure concerns in high-consumption patterns.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - **Possible** in specific high cumulative-intake scenarios (especially where multiple food sources contribute), but robust contemporary EU-wide intake quantification for E218 specifically is limited in the retrieved material.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** children (lower body weight), high consumers of processed/paraben-containing foods, and potentially pregnancy-related endocrine-sensitive windows (uncertain evidence strength).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports:** Approved by major regulators; longstanding use history; no strong human causal harm signal from retrieved evidence.
  - **Against:** Endocrine/reproductive mechanistic concerns exist in experimental literature; EFSA itself separated propyl from methyl/ethyl safety basis and noted exposure data gaps.
- **limited**
  - **Supports:** FDA GRAS affirmation with explicit GMP cap; EFSA group ADI exists for methyl/ethyl; JECFA group ADI exists.
  - **Against:** Recent literature raises cumulative exposure and mechanistic concern themes; evidence is not only trivial/high-dose-noise.
- **moderate**
  - **Supports:** Approved status with caveats; credible animal/mechanistic endocrine and reproductive concern literature; uncertain cumulative exposure margin in some populations; EFSA exposure-assessment limitations explicitly noted.
  - **Against:** No major jurisdictional food ban for E218; strong human causal evidence remains limited.
- **high**
  - **Supports:** Some mechanistic concern narratives exist.
  - **Against:** No IARC Group 2A/2B listing for E218 found; no major food-regulatory ban identified; ADI-based frameworks still in place.

### 2. Rationale
E218 has a stable regulatory acceptance profile (EU/FDA/JECFA frameworks) and defined intake limits, which argues against high-risk categorization. However, it does not fit a risk-free profile because the broader paraben toxicology literature includes endocrine/reproductive concerns and exposure uncertainty, and EFSA identified data limitations in exposure characterization. The most defensible classification from retrieved evidence is a middle category.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
