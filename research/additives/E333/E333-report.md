# E333 (Calcium citrates) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E333
- **Name:** Calcium citrates
- **CAS number(s):** 813-94-5 (JECFA calcium citrate entry) [7]
- **Chemical class:** Calcium salts of citric acid (tricarboxylate salts); JECFA lists tricalcium citrate naming variants [7]
- **Common synonyms:** Tricalcium citrate; Tricalcium salt of 2-hydroxy-1,2,3-propanetricarboxylic acid; Tricalcium salt of beta-hydroxytricarballylic acid [7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercially produced by neutralizing citric acid with calcium hydroxide or calcium carbonate (semi-synthetic industrial manufacture) [5]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Acidity regulator (pH control)
- **Secondary roles:** Firming agent; sequestrant [7]
- **Mechanism of action:** Citrate buffering/acid-base control and metal-ion complexation; calcium citrate contributes calcium and modulates acidity.
- **Common food categories:** Broad direct food ingredient use under GMP in the US CFR entry; can also be used in infant formula per section 412 provisions cited in CFR [5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- A 2026 pediatric nephrology study reported that higher urinary calcium-to-citrate ratio was associated with greater stone risk in infants/toddlers under nephrology evaluation (marker study context, not food-additive-dose causality) [14].
- A 2025 systematic review on dietary interventions in urolithiasis discusses management of calcium/citrate intake and urinary risk parameters, supporting that calcium/citrate balance can be clinically relevant in stone-prone populations [13].

### Human tolerability evidence
- A 2024 prospective multicenter study in elderly osteopenic subjects using oral calcium citrate reported mostly mild adverse reactions (mainly GI), low serious-safety signal, and acceptable adherence in the study context [12].

### Food processing safety context
- A 2025 Food Chemistry study found calcium salts (including calcium citrate) reduced benzo[a]pyrene formation in barbecued pork sausages in that model, suggesting a potential food-process safety benefit under tested conditions [15].

### Epidemiological data
- No strong E333-specific population epidemiology isolating this additive at normal dietary exposure was identified in the retrieved set.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA ADI not specified/not limited; FDA GRAS framework and GMP use conditions [5][7].
  - Evidence against: EFSA’s current re-evaluation record for E333 is ongoing (no final output in retrieved record), and renal/lithogenic context studies indicate caution at susceptible physiological states [3][13][14].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Strong regulatory acceptance in FDA/JECFA sources; no major ban identified; human tolerability data generally favorable in supplementation contexts [5][7][12].
  - Evidence against: Data gap remains for a currently retrievable final EFSA re-evaluation output/ADI; some evidence indicates stone-risk-relevant calcium/citrate physiology in vulnerable groups [3][13][14].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Potential concern for specific susceptible populations (stone formers) and lack of directly retrieved finalized EFSA output for the current re-evaluation entry [3][13][14].
  - Evidence against: No broad regulatory restriction pattern and no strong signal of serious toxicity at typical food-use context in retrieved sources [5][7][12].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None identified from retrieved sources.
  - Evidence against: FDA GRAS part/section listing, JECFA non-numerical permissive ADI, and absence of identified IARC-specific calcium citrate classification [5][7][10].

### 2. Rationale
The overall evidence profile is permissive from FDA/JECFA, with no strong high-severity safety signal in retrieved records. However, EFSA’s latest retrievable E333 re-evaluation record is ongoing with no published output object in OpenEFSA, and renal-stone-related literature supports some caution for susceptible subgroups.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`**
