# E227 Research Report (Calcium hydrogen sulfite / Calcium bisulfite)

## Identity
- **E-number**: E227.
- **Primary name**: Calcium hydrogensulfite (synonym: calcium bisulfite).
- **CAS number (primary)**: **13780-03-5**.
- **Chemical class**: Inorganic sulfite/bisulfite salt; sulfiting agent.
- **Common synonyms**: calcium bisulfite, calcium hydrogen sulfite.
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Synthetic** food additive in practice (industrial sulfiting preparation).  
  Source basis: JECFA chemical entry identifies the substance and functional class; origin classification here is an inference from industrial additive use context.

Evidence: JECFA Chemical 81 page lists chemical name, synonym, CAS, INS 227, and functional class ([WHO/JECFA Chemical 81](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/81)).

## Function in Food
- **Regulatory/functional classes**: Preservative and firming agent (JECFA).
- **Mechanism of action (inference)**:
  - Sulfiting agents reduce oxidative/enzymatic browning and can inhibit microbial spoilage.
  - They also participate as reducing/antioxidant chemistry in processed foods.
- **Typical food categories**: Sulfite-regulated foods generally include products like dried fruits and wine-related uses (exposure contributors in population studies).

Evidence:
- JECFA functional class for E227 ([WHO/JECFA Chemical 81](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/81)).
- Population exposure studies identifying major sulfite contributors such as wine/dried fruits ([PubMed 22264192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22264192/), [PubMed 19680935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19680935/)).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest scientific re-evaluation/follow-up year**: **2022** (EFSA Journal follow-up).
- **ADI status**:
  - 2016 re-evaluation had a **temporary group ADI of 0.7 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day**.
  - 2022 follow-up concluded database remained inadequate and **withdrew that temporary ADI**; EFSA used an MOE approach and reported safety concern at estimated exposures.
- **Key conclusion**: potential safety concern under assessed exposure scenarios; recommendation to tighten toxic element limits in specifications.

Evidence:
- EFSA follow-up abstract (PubMed record of EFSA Journal article) ([PubMed 36440381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36440381/)).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (strong evidence for concern at population level)**:
  - EFSA 2022 follow-up withdrew temporary ADI and reported MOE-based concern at assessed exposures ([PubMed 36440381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36440381/)).
- **Human sensitivity/allergenicity signal (moderate evidence)**:
  - 2025 clinical retrospective patch-test study reported significantly more systemic symptoms after high-sulfite foods in sodium disulfite patch-test-positive patients vs controls ([PubMed 41040044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41040044/)).
- **Mechanistic/genotoxic in vitro concern (weak-to-moderate translational weight)**:
  - 2025 lymphocyte in vitro study found sodium sulfite-associated CA/MN increases at higher concentrations ([PubMed 39958174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39958174/)).
- **Animal evidence nuance**:
  - 2016 rat dietary study did **not** reproduce older reported gastric lesions from sodium metabisulfite exposure; uncertainty remained on critical endpoint ([PubMed 27401986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27401986/)).

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2022**: MOEs below EFSA’s comparison value in many groups/scenarios, interpreted as safety concern under assessed exposure assumptions ([PubMed 36440381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36440381/)).
- **Country dietary studies (contextual, older)**:
  - Taiwan TDS (2016): high-intake sulfite exposure estimated at 17.8–19.7% of ADI ([PubMed 28959578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28959578/)).
  - Austria (2012): high-intake scenario could exceed ADI for sulfites in adults ([PubMed 22264192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22264192/)).
  - New Zealand (2009): most groups below ADI; 95th percentile exceedance noted for boys 5–12 in sulfite estimates ([PubMed 19680935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19680935/)).
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - Sulfite-sensitive individuals (especially respiratory/contact-reactive subsets).
  - High-intake consumers depending on food-pattern mix.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Supports: JECFA has a defined group ADI (0–0.7 mg/kg bw).
  - Against: EFSA (2022) withdrew temporary ADI and flagged concern; this conflicts with “no credible evidence of harm.”
- **limited**
  - Supports: Sulfites remain regulated/allowed in multiple systems with use conditions.
  - Against: EFSA’s inability to maintain ADI and MOE concern exceeds “minor concerns only at high doses.”
- **moderate**
  - Supports: Some evidence resembles moderate-tier triggers (population exceedance in scenarios, sensitivity concerns, caveats).
  - Against: EFSA’s explicit inability to confirm safety/derive ADI pushes beyond moderate by the provided rubric.
- **high**
  - Supports: Provided rubric explicitly includes “EFSA unable to confirm safety” as high-tier trigger; that criterion is met by 2022 follow-up.
  - Against: No IARC Group 2A/2B signal and no broad multi-jurisdiction ban specifically for E227.

### 2. Rationale
The dominant driver is the latest EFSA follow-up (2022): temporary ADI withdrawn due insufficient toxicology certainty, with MOE-based safety concern at estimated exposures. Additional evidence supports sensitivity and potential high-dose cellular toxicity concerns, while counterevidence exists for some endpoints and jurisdictions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**high** (with moderate confidence, because evidence is strong at regulatory-assessment level but heterogeneous across study types and compounds in the sulfite group).
