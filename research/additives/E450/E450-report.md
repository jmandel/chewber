# E450 (Diphosphates / Pyrophosphates) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E450 (group name in EU: diphosphates/pyrophosphates).
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic phosphate salts (di-/pyrophosphates), typically sodium, potassium, calcium (and some magnesium forms) salts.
- **Common synonyms:** Diphosphates, pyrophosphates, disodium dihydrogen diphosphate (acid sodium pyrophosphate), trisodium diphosphate, tetrasodium diphosphate, tetrapotassium diphosphate, dicalcium diphosphate.
- **CAS numbers (variant-specific, from JECFA chemical records):**
  - 7758-16-9 (disodium dihydrogen diphosphate / acid sodium pyrophosphate; INS 450i)
  - 7722-88-5 (tetrasodium pyrophosphate / tetrasodium diphosphate; INS 450iii)
  - 7320-34-5 (tetrapotassium diphosphate / pyrophosphate; INS 450v)
  - 35405-51-7 (dicalcium diphosphate / pyrophosphate; INS 450vi)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Used as food additives in manufactured form (synthetic/industrial salts), although phosphate is also an essential endogenous nutrient.

## Function in Food
EFSA lists phosphates (including E450 group members) as multi-functional additives used as antioxidants, acidity regulators, emulsifiers, raising agents, stabilisers, thickeners, sequestrants, humectants, and colour-retention agents. In practice, E450 salts are used to:
- buffer/control pH,
- chelate minerals (process stability),
- support leavening systems,
- improve texture/water retention.

Common food categories include bakery and processed products, processed meats, some dairy-analog/process foods, and packaged/ultra-processed foods where phosphate additives are common.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (authorised in EU additive framework; re-evaluated).
- **Latest major re-evaluation:** 2019 EFSA FAF opinion (phosphates E338-341, E343, E450-452).
- **ADI:** Group ADI **40 mg/kg bw/day**, expressed as phosphorus.
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA concluded the ADI is protective, but estimated exposures exceeded ADI for some child groups (mean and/or 95th percentile) and supplement-related exposure can exceed ADI.
- **Access note:** In this environment, direct extraction from the EFSA Journal landing path redirected to Wiley and browser access was constrained; data were cross-checked from the EFSA publication URL plus the PubMed-indexed abstract of the same EFSA opinion.

### FDA / CFR (US)
US regulation is ingredient-specific rather than E-number-based.
- **GRAS examples in 21 CFR Part 182:**
  - 21 CFR 182.1087 (sodium acid pyrophosphate)
  - 21 CFR 182.6787 (sodium pyrophosphate)
  - 21 CFR 182.6789 (tetrasodium pyrophosphate)
  - 21 CFR 182.8223 (calcium pyrophosphate)
- **GRAS condition language:** “generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice.”
- **Specific use condition example:** 21 CFR 161.190 (canned tuna) allows sodium acid pyrophosphate up to **0.5% by weight** of finished food to inhibit struvite crystal development.
- **Access note:** The interactive `ecfr.gov/current/...` pages were bot-protected in this environment; Title 21 regulatory text was extracted from the official eCFR API XML endpoint.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Status:** Evaluated across phosphate/diphosphate compounds.
- **ADI/TDI basis:** Group **MTDI 70 mg/kg bw (as phosphorus)**.
- **Evaluation timing:** JECFA chemical pages for E450 salts show toxicological basis centered on historical group evaluations (commonly 1982), with some entries carrying later specification-related updates (e.g., 2001) while retaining the MTDI framework.

### IARC
- **Classification:** No specific IARC monograph listing/classification identified for E450 diphosphate/pyrophosphate entries in the IARC classification dataset checked.

### Notable bans

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and toxicology evidence
- EFSA’s 2019 re-evaluation reported adequate toxicity data, low acute oral toxicity for phosphates, no concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity, and no developmental-toxicity signals in the assessed dataset; this underpins the group ADI.

### Epidemiological / human evidence context
- 2023-2025 PubMed literature indicates widespread phosphate additive presence in packaged foods and raises concern that highly absorbable inorganic phosphate additives may contribute to cardiorenal risk, especially where total exposure is high or in CKD-prone groups.
- Evidence strength is mixed: strong for “widespread exposure and plausibility,” weaker/moderate for direct causal harm from E450 specifically in healthy populations.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Recent reviews describe rapidly absorbable inorganic phosphates as potentially affecting mineral-regulating pathways (e.g., FGF23/PTH axis) and vascular calcification pathways, providing mechanistic plausibility for cardiorenal concern at high cumulative exposure.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA estimated whole-diet phosphate exposure (as phosphorus) from approximately **251 mg P/person/day (infants)** to **1,625 mg P/person/day (adults)**; high exposure ranged from **331 to 2,728 mg P/person/day**.
- EFSA reported ADI exceedance in some younger populations: infants/toddlers/children at mean exposure, and infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high-percentile exposure.
- Recent US market analyses found high prevalence of phosphate additives in packaged foods (2023 and 2025 analyses), indicating persistent real-world exposure opportunities.

### Vulnerable populations
- Infants, toddlers, children (per EFSA exceedance modeling).
- People with CKD or impaired phosphate handling (clinical concern in recent literature).
- High consumers of ultra-processed/packaged foods and supplement users.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier: Regulatory approval by EFSA/FDA/JECFA; no strong genotoxic/carcinogenic concern in EFSA re-evaluation.
- Evidence against this tier: EFSA modeled ADI exceedance in multiple child populations and supplement scenarios; newer literature flags cardiorenal plausibility at higher exposure.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier: Approved/GRAS in major jurisdictions; clear ADI/MTDI frameworks exist; no major carcinogenic classification signal.
- Evidence against this tier: Exceedance risk is not purely theoretical and occurs in specific populations; exposure burden from processed foods is high.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier: Matches the rubric criterion of “approved but with caveats,” including ADI exceedance in children and credible mechanistic concerns in cardiorenal pathways.
- Evidence against this tier: Direct, additive-specific severe toxicity signals are limited; large regulatory bodies still permit use under limits/GMP.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier: Persistent concerns about cumulative phosphate exposure and potential vulnerability in CKD contexts.
- Evidence against this tier: No major-jurisdiction ban identified; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found; EFSA/JECFA retained acceptability under ADI/MTDI frameworks.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest decision drivers are: (a) approved status with established ADI/MTDI, (b) consistent evidence that some subpopulations can exceed guidance values, and (c) mechanistic/epidemiologic concern concentrated around high exposure and cardiorenal vulnerability rather than universal severe toxicity.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
