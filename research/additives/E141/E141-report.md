# E141 (Copper complexes of chlorophylls) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E141 (mainly E141(i): copper complexes of chlorophylls; related E141(ii): copper complexes of chlorophyllins, sodium/potassium salts) [S5][S6].
- **Primary name:** Copper complexes of chlorophylls (INS 141(i)) [S5].
- **CAS number(s):** 65963-40-8 (reported on WHO/JECFA entry for copper chlorophyll/copper phaeophytins; INS 141(i)) [S5].
- **Chemical class:** Copper-substituted chlorophyll derivatives (porphyrin-type colorants; copper phaeophytin A/B and copper chlorophyllin derivatives) [S5][S6][S3].
- **Common synonyms:** Copper chlorophyll, copper phaeophytin, C.I. Natural Green 3; sodium copper chlorophyllin; potassium copper chlorophyllin [S5][S6].
- **Natural vs synthetic origin:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from plant chlorophyll, then chemically modified by saponification and copper substitution) [S3].

## Function in Food
- **Main function:** Green food colorant [S5][S6].
- **Mechanism of action:** Copper replacement in the chlorophyll macrocycle increases green color stability versus native chlorophyll, especially during processing/storage. **Inference based on identity/process descriptions** [S3][S5].
- **Common food categories:**
  - US food use in CFR is specifically citrus-based dry beverage mixes (for sodium copper chlorophyllin) [S3].
  - Broader color additive use exists internationally for green coloring (E141 family). **Inference from INS/JECFA classification and EFSA re-evaluation scope** [S5][S6][S1].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest re-evaluation found:** EFSA Journal opinion on E140/E141, year **2015** [S1].
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA stated that, due to significant data gaps, the Panel could not assess safety adequately and the **existing ADI should be withdrawn** [S1].
- **ADI (value + unit):** Previously **15 mg/kg bw/day** (as copper chlorophylls), but EFSA concluded this ADI should be withdrawn [S1].
- **Exposure conclusion:** In EFSA’s re-evaluation text, maximum-level scenarios were above the then-current ADI for several age groups (toddlers/children/adolescents/adults), not elderly [S1].
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA publication pages redirected to Wiley and were blocked in this environment; EFSA URL was still reached and indexed text was extracted [S1].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not identified as a GRAS listing in searched Title 21 chlorophyll/chlorophyllin entries; in food it is handled as a **color additive approval** rather than GRAS [S3][S4].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 73.125** (Sodium copper chlorophyllin) [S3].
- **Conditions of use (food):** May color citrus-based dry beverage mixes at **≤0.2% in dry mix**; additive is exempt from batch certification under this section [S3].

### JECFA / WHO
- **Entry found (INS 141(i)):** CHLOROPHYLLS, COPPER COMPLEXES [S5].
- **ADI:** **0-15 mg/kg bw** [S5].
- **Last evaluation year:** Latest year listed in “Previous Years” for 141(i) is **1987**; related 141(ii) listing shows updates through 1988 [S5][S6].

### IARC
- **Monograph classification:** No chlorophyll/chlorophyllin copper-complex entry identified in the IARC Monographs classification dataset checked [S7][S8].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban was identified in the sources checked here.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA re-evaluation concerns (core regulatory evidence):**
  - Significant uncharacterized fraction in tested materials.
  - Lack of adequate toxicological datasets for current specifications.
  - Genotoxicity concern for unidentified fractions could not be excluded.
  - Copper exposure contribution may be relevant for upper-limit exceedance in high consumers (especially young children). [S1]
- **Recent PubMed safety/biological studies (contextual, not decisive for food-additive re-authorization):**
  - 2025 preclinical GLP toxicology paper reported high acute/subacute tolerance in rodents (NOAEL above tested subacute doses) [S10][S11].
  - 2026 first-in-human high-dose study reported tolerability in healthy male volunteers in a therapeutic context [S10][S11].
  - 2019 in vitro human blood study reported no cyto/genotoxicity at tested ranges with radioprotective effects [S10][S11].
- **Interpretation:** These newer studies are mainly pharmacologic/therapeutic contexts, not comprehensive long-term dietary additive reassessment under current EU specification uncertainties.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** EFSA indicated it was not possible to estimate exposure for E141(i) from use-level data in that re-evaluation workflow [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA reported maximum-level scenarios above the then-current ADI for younger groups (toddlers/children/adolescents; adults also above in that scenario), with elderly lower [S1].
- **Vulnerable populations:** Children/toddlers are the principal concern group from EFSA’s exposure conclusions [S1].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA has an established numerical ADI (0-15 mg/kg bw) and long historical use [S5].
  - Evidence against: EFSA could not confirm safety under available data and recommended withdrawal of the prior ADI; unresolved uncertainty on composition/genotoxicity [S1].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: US CFR approval exists for a defined food use (21 CFR 73.125) with explicit limits; JECFA ADI exists [S3][S5].
  - Evidence against: EFSA’s re-evaluation identified major unresolved toxicology/specification issues and unfavorable exposure scenarios [S1].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Additive remains regulated/usable in some systems and there is some recent experimental tolerance data [S3][S10][S11].
  - Evidence against: EFSA did not just tighten ADI; it concluded safety could not be assessed and the previous ADI should be withdrawn [S1].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Prompt-defined criterion is met because EFSA was unable to confirm safety in the re-evaluation and highlighted unresolved genotoxicity/composition concerns [S1].
  - Evidence against: No IARC carcinogenic classification found; no major-jurisdiction outright ban identified; JECFA and FDA frameworks still show allowance/ADI/conditions [S3][S5][S7][S8].

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factor is the EFSA 2015 re-evaluation outcome: inability to complete a safety assessment with recommendation to withdraw the previous ADI, plus exposure concerns in younger populations under maximum-level assumptions. This outweighs older JECFA tolerance values and limited newer therapeutic-context studies.

### 3. Recommended tier
**high**
