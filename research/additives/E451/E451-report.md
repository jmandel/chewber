# E451 (Triphosphates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E451 (E451(i) pentasodium triphosphate; E451(ii) pentapotassium triphosphate). [EFSA-1][JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic condensed phosphates (triphosphates/polyphosphates). [EFSA-1][INCHEM-1]
- **CAS number(s):**
  - **7758-29-4** (E451(i), pentasodium triphosphate / sodium tripolyphosphate). [JECFA-1]
  - **13845-36-8** (E451(ii), pentapotassium triphosphate / potassium tripolyphosphate). [JECFA-2]
- **Common synonyms:** sodium tripolyphosphate, sodium triphosphate, pentasodium triphosphate; potassium tripolyphosphate, potassium triphosphate, pentapotassium triphosphate. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Treated as **synthetic** in food additive context (manufactured inorganic phosphate salts). [EFSA-1][INCHEM-1]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Triphosphates function primarily as sequestrants and texture agents; they bind polyvalent cations, affect ionic balance/pH, and support water retention/texture in formulated foods. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][INCHEM-1]
- **JECFA functional classes:**
  - E451(i): sequestrant, texturizer, thickener.
  - E451(ii): texturizer, thickener. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- **Common food categories:** EFSA's phosphate group assessment covers broad processed-food uses and food supplements; U.S. CFR also lists sodium tripolyphosphate under GRAS uses and in modified starch processing, indicating broad industrial use in processed foods. [EFSA-1][FDA-1][FDA-2][PUBMED-1]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Phosphates (including E451) are authorised in the EU and were re-evaluated by EFSA in 2019. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **ADI:** EFSA established a **group ADI of 40 mg/kg bw/day (as phosphorus)** for E338-341, E343, E450-452 (includes E451). [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Evaluation year:** 2019. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No concern for genotoxicity or carcinogenicity for the phosphate additive group.
  - Group ADI set at 40 mg/kg bw/day as phosphorus.
  - Exposure based on analytical phosphorus data exceeded ADI for infants/toddlers/children at mean intake, and for infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high intake.
  - Food-supplement exposure can exceed ADI.
  - No safety concern identified for infants below 16 weeks consuming formula/FSMP containing phosphates.
  - ADI not applicable to people with moderate-to-severe renal impairment. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Access note:** The EFSA Journal page on Wiley was anti-bot protected from this VM; the EFSA press page and the PubMed Central mirror of the EFSA scientific opinion were used as accessible alternatives. [EFSA-2][EFSA-3][EFSA-4]

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** **GRAS** (sodium tripolyphosphate) under Title 21. [FDA-1][FDA-2]
- **Primary CFR citation:** **21 CFR 182.1810** (Sodium tripolyphosphate).
- **Condition of use (quoted in CFR text):** generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice. [FDA-2]
- **Additional relevant CFR entries:**
  - **21 CFR 182.6810**: sodium tripolyphosphate listed with same GRAS/GMP condition.
  - **21 CFR 172.892**: use of sodium tripolyphosphate (with sodium trimetaphosphate) in modified food starch with residual phosphate limits (<= 0.4% as phosphorus).
  - **21 CFR 173.310**: potassium tripolyphosphate appears in permitted boiler-water additives for steam contacting food. [FDA-2]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI/MTDI:** Group **MTDI 70 mg/kg bw/day (as P)** for phosphorus from all sources (naturally present + additives). [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]
- **Last evaluation year:**
  - E451(i) (pentasodium triphosphate): latest entry shows **2023** (specifications-focused update; MTDI unchanged).
  - E451(ii) (pentapotassium triphosphate): **1982** (group MTDI basis shown). [JECFA-1][JECFA-2]

### IARC
- No IARC monograph entry/classification was identified for E451 names (triphosphates/sodium tripolyphosphate/potassium tripolyphosphate) in the IARC list/classification dataset reviewed. [IARC-1][IARC-2]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban for E451 identified in the EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology baseline
- EFSA identified kidney calcification/tubular nephropathy as the key adverse effect for phosphate risk characterization and set a group ADI accordingly; no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern for the phosphate additive group. [EFSA-1]

### Human/epidemiologic and exposure evidence
- **2025 cross-sectional U.S. packaged food study:** phosphate additives were present in 56% of products from top manufacturers, supporting high potential population exposure; authors highlight label-data gaps for phosphorus. [PUBMED-1]
- **2023 review (cardiorenal mechanism):** proposes inorganic phosphate additives in ultra-processed foods as a plausible contributor to cardiorenal risk pathways (vascular calcification and mineral-hormone signaling), while noting exposure quantification gaps. [PUBMED-2]
- **2023 branded-food database analysis:** phosphate-salt additives were associated with higher phosphorus density in several food groups versus products without phosphate salts. [PUBMED-3]

### Animal/mechanistic recent signal
- **2025 mouse study:** chronic high-phosphate diet induced anxiety-like behavior and neuroimmune/gene-expression changes, suggesting potential neurobiological effects at high intake (preclinical evidence). [PUBMED-4]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA whole-diet phosphorus exposure (analytical-data scenario):** 251 to 1,625 mg P/person/day (mean by age group), and 331 to 2,728 mg P/person/day (95th percentile). [EFSA-1]
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal additive scenario:** 1 to 48 mg P/kg bw/day (mean), and 3 to 62 mg P/kg bw/day (95th percentile). [EFSA-1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA reported exceedance of the 40 mg/kg bw/day ADI in infants/toddlers/children (mean), and infants/toddlers/children/adolescents (95th percentile); supplement users may also exceed ADI. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants and children (higher exposure per kg bw), regular food-supplement users, and individuals with reduced renal function. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** E451 is included in long-standing EU and international phosphate authorizations; phosphorus is an essential nutrient.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** EFSA set a finite ADI (not “ADI not specified”) and found exceedance in several child/high-intake groups; renal-function caveat remains. [EFSA-1]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** FDA GRAS status for sodium tripolyphosphate with GMP condition; JECFA and EFSA provide numeric intake guidance (MTDI/ADI) rather than ban-level conclusions. [FDA-2][JECFA-1][JECFA-2][EFSA-1]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** Documented ADI exceedance in young populations and plausible cardiorenal mechanisms associated with high inorganic phosphate additive exposure. [EFSA-1][PUBMED-2]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** E451 is approved but with meaningful caveats: EFSA ADI exceedance in some populations (especially children/high consumers), supplement-related exceedance concern, and biologically plausible renal/cardiovascular and emerging neurobehavioral signals at high intake. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][PUBMED-2][PUBMED-4]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** No major regulator has banned E451; FDA/JECFA continue to permit use under established conditions. [FDA-2][JECFA-1][JECFA-2]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** High-exposure concerns exist, but not accompanied by major-jurisdiction bans or IARC carcinogenic classification specific to E451.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** No IARC listing for E451 names and continued regulatory authorization in EU/U.S./JECFA frameworks. [IARC-2][FDA-2][EFSA-1]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight is regulatory: E451 remains authorised, but EFSA’s 2019 group ADI is exceeded in some population scenarios (especially younger groups and high consumers), and exposure from supplements may exceed ADI. This places E451 above a simple “limited concern” profile but below “high” risk criteria.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
