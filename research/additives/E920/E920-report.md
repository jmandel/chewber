# E920 (L-cysteine) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E920.
- **Name:** L-cysteine (including L-cysteine hydrochloride forms in additive nomenclature).
- **CAS number(s):** 52-90-4 (L-cysteine), 52-89-1 (L-cysteine hydrochloride), 7048-04-6 (L-cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical class:** Sulfur-containing amino acid / amino-acid-derived dough conditioning additive.
- **Common synonyms:** L-cysteine, L-cysteine monohydrochloride, E920, INS 920.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commonly produced industrially (including fermentation/industrial processing); chemically identical to endogenous L-cysteine.

**Evidence:** OpenEFSA substance metadata for L-cysteine and hydrochloride forms (CAS values) [3][4]; eCFR identity definitions in 21 CFR 184.1271 and 184.1272 [5].

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Flour treatment / dough conditioning aid in bakery applications.
- **Mechanism of action:** Used in dough processing to condition dough handling/performance; US CFR defines use as a dough strengthener in yeast-leavened baked goods and baking mixes.
- **Common food categories:** Yeast-leavened baked goods and baking mixes.

**Evidence:** 21 CFR 184.1271 and 184.1272 specify use conditions and category (yeast-leavened baked goods and baking mixes) [5].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Authorised in EU food additive database entry for E920 [1].
- **ADI:** Listed as **"not specified"** in EU additive details for E920 [1].
- **Latest EFSA opinion year (available in EU additive entry):** **2006** (last opinion date shown as 26 September 2006) [1].
- **Current re-evaluation status:** OpenEFSA record for "Re-evaluation of E920 L-Cysteine" (EFSA-Q-2011-00710) is still in "Ongoing Risk Assessment"; comment updated on **19 January 2026** states re-evaluation has been delayed and a new call for data is planned for 2026-2027 [3].

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **Status:** GRAS-affirmed direct food substances for specific uses.
- **Citations:**
  - **21 CFR 184.1271** (L-cysteine)
  - **21 CFR 184.1272** (L-cysteine monohydrochloride)
- **Conditions of use:** Up to **0.009 part total L-cysteine per 100 parts of flour** in dough as a dough strengthener, for yeast-leavened baked goods and baking mixes.

**Evidence:** Official eCFR API text for Title 21 section 184.1271/184.1272 [5].

### JECFA / WHO
- **Direct WHO/JECFA database finding:** L-CYSTEINE entry (WHO JECFA DB) shows evaluation year **2004** and conclusion **"No safety concern at current levels of intake when used as a flavouring agent"** (non-numeric ADI expression for that entry) [7][8].
- **Additional search note:** A FAO search result for "L-cysteine and hydrochlorides" indicates an older JECFA additive record context, but the referenced FAO detail page did not render extractable record content in this environment [9].

### IARC
- **Classification found for E920/L-cysteine:** No specific L-cysteine / E920 entry identified in IARC public classification lists searched.

**Evidence:** IARC "Agents classified by the IARC" and "List of classifications" pages searched with no cysteine/E920 hit [10][11].

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified from the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** EFSA/EU status remains authorised with ADI not specified in the EU additive record [1], and FDA maintains GRAS-affirmed specific-use CFR entries [5].
- **Recent literature from PubMed (most relevant hits):**
  - 2025 EFSA FEEDAP assessment: L-cysteine and L-cysteine hydrochloride in feed considered safe for target species up to defined inclusion levels; no consumer/environment concern under assessed conditions, while user-handling irritation/corrosivity concerns were noted for hydrochloride forms [12][13].
  - 2025 renewal opinion for cats/dogs: additive remains safe under current conditions; user irritation risk remains relevant [12][14].
  - 2023 mechanistic food study: L-cysteine hydrochloride inhibited *A. flavus* growth and aflatoxin biosynthesis in vitro/in food-safety context; this is not evidence of dietary toxicity in consumers [12][15].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Use-pattern constraint:** FDA use condition is narrowly defined (yeast-leavened baked goods and baking mixes) with a low flour-based formulation limit [5].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** No numeric EFSA ADI was set ("not specified"), so classic ADI exceedance calculations are not directly applicable from that EFSA entry [1].
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - **Occupational handling** of concentrated hydrochloride forms (irritation/corrosivity concerns in FEEDAP user safety sections) [12][13][14].
  - For general consumers at authorised food-use levels, sources reviewed did not identify a strong modern signal of dietary harm.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Endogenous amino acid identity and long-standing regulatory acceptance.
  - EFSA/EU record reports ADI not specified for E920 [1].
  - FDA GRAS-affirmed specific food uses in CFR [5].
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA re-evaluation remains ongoing and delayed (data refresh still pending) [3].
  - Concentrated hydrochloride forms can pose user-handling hazards (irritation/corrosive effects) [13][14].

**limited**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/authorised by major regulators with explicit use conditions [1][5].
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found [10][11].
  - Recent relevant assessments (feed context) continue to conclude safety under specified conditions [13][14].
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Consumer-specific modern re-evaluation for EU food-additive context is still incomplete (ongoing EFSA file) [3].

**moderate**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Ongoing re-evaluation indicates unresolved modernisation of evidence package in EU process [3].
  - Documented user hazards for concentrated forms suggest non-zero risk in some exposure settings [13][14].
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Current regulatory findings for intended food/feed uses do not indicate broad consumer harm signals [1][5][13][14].

**high**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - No strong support found.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major bans identified.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification identified.
  - Authorised status persists in EU and US frameworks [1][5][10][11].

### 2. Rationale
E920 has strong regulatory acceptance history and specific use limits, with no clear carcinogenic classification signal found. The main counterweights are process lag in EFSA re-evaluation and handling risks for concentrated hydrochloride forms rather than clear consumer dietary-toxicity findings at authorised use levels.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
