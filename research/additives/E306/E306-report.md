# E306 (Tocopherols / Vitamin E) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E306 (INS 306)
- **Name**: Tocopherol-rich extract (mixed tocopherols; vitamin E concentrate)
- **CAS number(s)**:
  - 59-02-9 (vitamin E / alpha-tocopherol listed on JECFA INS 306 record)
  - 10191-41-0 (dl-alpha-tocopherol listed in 21 CFR 184.1890)
- **Chemical class**: Tocopherols (lipophilic phenolic antioxidants; vitamin E compounds)
- **Common synonyms**: Mixed tocopherols concentrate, tocopherol-rich extract, vitamin E concentrate
- **Natural vs synthetic**:
  - E306 is generally the natural mixed-tocopherol fraction (JECFA synonym: vitamin E concentrate; FDA describes d-alpha-tocopherol source from vegetable oil distillation)
  - Other tocopherol forms can be synthetic (e.g., dl-alpha-tocopherol in 21 CFR 184.1890)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Antioxidant activity that limits lipid oxidation/rancidity in fats/oils and lipid-containing foods (JECFA functional class: ANTIOXIDANT).
- **Common food categories**:
  - Fats/oils and fat-containing foods as antioxidants
  - Specific U.S. GRAS affirmation in 21 CFR 184.1890: use as inhibitor of nitrosamine formation in pump-cured bacon

- **Evaluation year**: **2015**.
- **Key conclusion**: No safety concern for reported uses/use levels for E306-E309; group ADI established.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status**: GRAS listings present for tocopherols in Title 21.
- **CFR citations and conditions**:
  - **21 CFR 182.3890**: Tocopherols are generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice (GMP).
  - **21 CFR 182.8890**: Tocopherols are generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with GMP.
  - **21 CFR 184.1890** (alpha-tocopherols): GRAS affirmation limited to use as inhibitors of nitrosamine formation, specifically in pump-cured bacon at levels not exceeding current GMP.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology consensus for food-additive use**:
  - EFSA re-evaluation (2015) concluded no safety concern at reported uses for E306-E309 and set a group ADI.
  - JECFA INS 306 record has an older ADI of 0-2 mg/kg bw.
- **Recent human epidemiology (PubMed)**:
  - 2026 NutriNet-Sante cohort (Nat Commun) reported associations between several preservatives and incident type 2 diabetes, including **alpha-tocopherol** exposure; this is observational and not causal proof.
- **Recent related additive assessment**:
  - 2025 EFSA opinion on vitamin E TPGS (different vitamin E derivative, not E306) reported no safety concern at proposed uses.
- **Mechanistic/clinical concern signal at high supplemental doses**:
  - SELECT trial (JAMA, 2011): 400 IU/day all-rac-alpha-tocopheryl acetate increased prostate cancer incidence (HR 1.17).
  - Cochrane review (2020) found no lung-cancer prevention benefit from vitamin E supplementation and reported increased hemorrhagic stroke risk in one large trial.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory reference points**:
  - EFSA group ADI: 300 mg/kg bw/day (E306-E309, 2015 re-evaluation).
  - JECFA ADI: 0-2 mg/kg bw for INS 306 (older evaluation framework).
- **Typical dietary/additive exposure**:
  - Direct contemporary intake estimates specific to E306 from EFSA full text were not retrievable here due access blocking.
  - Available regulatory conclusions indicate no safety concern at authorized food-additive uses.
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - For ordinary food-additive exposure, exceedance risk appears low based on EFSA no-safety-concern conclusion.
  - Potential concern is more plausible with high-dose vitamin E supplementation rather than routine additive intake.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - High-dose supplement users.
  - Populations with cardiometabolic vulnerability (observational signal needs confirmation, not causal).

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Tocopherols are vitamin E compounds, naturally present in foods.
  - EFSA 2015 re-evaluation concluded no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Evidence against**:
  - JECFA ADI is finite (0-2 mg/kg bw), not "ADI not specified".
  - Human high-dose supplementation literature shows non-zero adverse-signal history.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Approved/GRAS status across EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification entry found.
  - Core regulatory conclusions do not indicate additive-level safety failure.
- **Evidence against**:
  - Recent observational epidemiology flagged alpha-tocopherol among preservatives associated with T2D incidence (non-causal).

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Divergence between EFSA and older JECFA ADIs introduces uncertainty.
  - High-dose supplemental vitamin E evidence (not strictly additive-level) includes adverse endpoints.
- **Evidence against**:
  - No direct regulator-driven restriction or withdrawal of E306 itself identified.
  - No confirmed additive-level harm signal strong enough to overturn approvals.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - None from current regulatory classifications.
- **Evidence against**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing specific to E306/mixed tocopherols identified.
  - EFSA and FDA regulatory pathways remain permissive for defined uses.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight is still on regulatory acceptance and lack of confirmed additive-level toxicity at authorized use levels. However, uncertainty is not zero because of conflicting historical ADI frameworks (EFSA vs older JECFA basis) and adverse signals in high-dose vitamin E supplement literature plus new observational epidemiology.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
