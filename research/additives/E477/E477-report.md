# E477 Research Report — Propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids

## Identity
- **E-number**: E477
- **Name**: Propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids
- **Synonyms**: Propylene glycol esters of fatty acids; propylene glycol mono- and diesters of fats and fatty acids
- **INS**: 477
- **Chemical class**: Mixed mono-/di-esters of propylene glycol with edible fatty acids; non-ionic emulsifier system
- **CAS number(s)**: No single CAS was listed in the WHO/JECFA chemical entry for INS 477, and the FAO JECFA detail page showed a blank CAS field for this additive mixture.
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured by esterification/transesterification of food fatty acids/fats with propane-1,2-diol)

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role**: Emulsifier (WHO/JECFA functional class).
- **Mechanism**: Amphiphilic esters reduce oil-water interfacial tension and improve dispersion/stability, especially in fat-containing systems.
- **Regulatory use principle (US)**: Under 21 CFR 172.856, use is allowed in food at levels "not in excess of that reasonably required to produce the intended effect."
- **Common food categories (EU exposure datasets)**: Edible ices, fine bakery wares, confectionery, chewing gum, decorations/coatings/fillings (including whipped dessert toppings), some dairy analogues and emulsified fat systems.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Status**: Approved food additive (authorized in Annex II; re-evaluated by EFSA FAF Panel).
  - **ADI**: EFSA confirmed ADI equivalent to **25 mg/kg bw/day expressed as propane-1,2-diol**, corresponding to **80 mg/kg bw/day for E477** (assuming max 31% free+bound propane-1,2-diol in specification).
  - **Evaluation year**: 2018 (EFSA re-evaluation publication; PubMed-indexed EFSA Journal record and EFSA portal entry).
  - **Key conclusion**: No safety concern at reported use levels under the refined non-brand-loyal exposure scenario.
  - **Access note**: Direct EFSA Journal URL returned anti-bot challenge from shell in this environment; EFSA content was cross-checked via EFSA-indexed PubMed/PMC record and EFSA portal search results.

- **FDA / eCFR (US)**:
  - **Status**: Approved as a **direct food additive** (not listed by name as a stand-alone GRAS substance in the reviewed GRAS sections; instead regulated under direct additive provisions).
  - **Citation**: **21 CFR 172.856** — "Propylene glycol mono- and diesters of fats and fatty acids."
  - **Conditions**: Produced from specified edible fat/fatty acid sources and used only at levels reasonably required for intended effect.
  - **Access note**: The eCFR website front-end returned anti-bot access gating in this environment; data were extracted from the official eCFR API endpoints.

- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **ADI**: **0-25 mg/kg bw** (comment: "As propylene glycol").
  - **Last evaluation year**: **1973** in JECFA evaluation entry (with earlier/administrative status history also listed).

- **IARC**:
  - No IARC Monographs Group 1/2A/2B/3 classification entry was identified for "propylene glycol esters of fatty acids" / "propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids" in the current IARC list-of-classifications dataset reviewed.

- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology**:
  - EFSA summary reports no adverse effects in short-term studies in rats and dogs at highest tested doses.
  - EFSA summary includes a 13-week rat study with propane-1,2-diol monostearate identifying a NOAEL at the highest tested dose (6,768 mg/kg bw/day).

- **Genotoxicity**:
  - EFSA concluded no genotoxicity concern for E477 based on available in vitro data plus supporting evaluation of released propane-1,2-diol.

- **Data gaps / uncertainties**:
  - EFSA reported no chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive, or developmental studies directly on E477.
  - EFSA recommended specification updates (toxic elements; trans fatty acids; glycidyl esters/glycidol; 3-MCPD esters; erucic acid limits).

- **Epidemiology / human studies**:
  - No robust E477-specific epidemiological safety signal was identified in the reviewed sources.

- **PubMed (recent safety literature check)**:
  - Direct E477 safety/toxicity papers in recent years are sparse; the main indexed comprehensive safety source is the EFSA re-evaluation article.
  - Recent papers retrieved for related terms (e.g., propylene glycol monostearate) were mostly formulation/food-technology studies rather than toxicological hazard studies.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reports E477 is authorized across 13 EU food categories, with use-level data quality varying by category and data source.
- Main labeled usage in market datasets was in **bakery** and **desserts/ice cream** categories.
- In EFSA refined non-brand-loyal modeling, estimated exposure did **not** exceed the E477 ADI in any group; a reported high-end estimate in children was 16.7 mg/kg bw/day (below 80 mg/kg bw/day ADI for E477).
- **ADI exceedance risk**: Low based on EFSA refined scenario.
- **Potentially vulnerable considerations**: Children had higher modeled intake than adults; combined exposure to released propane-1,2-diol from E477, E1520, and E405 was flagged by EFSA for future grouped assessment.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Hydrolysis to common dietary components (fatty acids + propane-1,2-diol), no EFSA genotoxicity concern, no ADI exceedance in EFSA refined scenario.
  - Evidence against: EFSA identified notable dataset gaps (no chronic/carcinogenicity/reproductive/developmental studies directly on E477) and requested tighter impurity specifications.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved by EFSA/FDA/JECFA; explicit ADIs established; EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported use levels; no IARC carcinogenic classification identified.
  - Evidence against: Evidence base relies heavily on older and/or indirect datasets; limited modern long-term endpoint coverage.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Presence of uncertainty drivers (data gaps, impurity-spec concerns, need to consider cumulative propane-1,2-diol exposure from multiple additives).
  - Evidence against: No clear regulatory finding of current-use harm, no ADI exceedance in EFSA refined exposure, and no direct carcinogenic classification signal.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None identified from reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.
  - Evidence against: Not banned in reviewed major frameworks; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B entry found for this additive; existing ADI-based frameworks remain in place.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence indicates regulatory acceptance with ADI-based control and no current-use safety concern in EFSA refined exposure assessment. The principal limitations are uncertainty/data-gap related rather than demonstrated harm at permitted exposure levels.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
