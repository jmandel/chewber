# E966 (Lactitol) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E966 (lactitols). [S1]
- **Name:** Lactitol.
- **CAS number:** 585-86-4. [S3]
- **Chemical class:** Sugar alcohol (polyol), specifically a hydrogenated lactose derivative (disaccharide polyol). [S3][S13]
- **Common synonyms:** Lactit, lactositol, lactobiosit; chemical name 4-O-beta-D-galactopyranosyl-d-glucitol. [S3][S4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** in commercial use (industrial hydrogenation of lactose feedstock). [S13]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Sweetener. [S3][S4]
- **Other technological roles:** Texturizer/thickener. [S3]
- **Mechanism of action:** Provides sweetness with incomplete small-intestinal absorption; substantial colonic fermentation contributes to low glycaemic response and potential laxative effects at higher doses. [S5]
- **Common food categories:** Sugar-free/low-sugar foods, especially confectionery and chewing gum contexts for noncariogenic sweetener claims. [S2]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Authorized EU sweetener (E966) listed by EFSA on its sweeteners topic page.
- **Latest EFSA status text:** "Re-evaluation ongoing" for E966 (Lactitols). [S1]
- **ADI (EFSA):** No completed EFSA re-evaluation ADI value published yet for E966 on the current EFSA sweeteners status table (last reviewed 17 February 2026). [S1]
- **Key conclusion:** E966 remains authorized while EFSA re-evaluation is still in progress. [S1]

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Title 21 findings:** Lactitol appears in:
  - **21 CFR 101.9(c)(1)(i)(F):** caloric factor for lactitol = **2.0 kcal/g**.
  - **21 CFR 101.80(c)(2)(ii)(A):** listed among eligible sugar alcohols for dental-caries health claims. [S2]
- **Conditions of use identified in CFR context:** For 21 CFR 101.80 claims, foods must meet sugar-content requirements and, where other carbohydrates are present, must not lower plaque pH below 5.7 under the specified test. [S2]
- **GRAS-specific codification note:** No lactitol-specific "may be safely used" section was identified in the 2026-02-25 full Title 21 text (unlike xylitol at 21 CFR 172.395). [S2]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **Not specified**. [S3][S4]
- **Latest evaluation year:** **1983**. [S3][S4]
- **Functional class at JECFA:** Sweetener, texturizer/thickener. [S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **JECFA toxicology basis (legacy, foundational):**
  - Very low general toxicity in evaluated animal/human data.
  - Not mutagenic in the Salmonella/microsome test.
  - Rat teratogenicity signal not established; multigeneration rat data noted embryotoxic/foetotoxic effects at higher dietary percentages.
  - Human tolerance in older data: 24 g/day tolerated; diarrhea reported at 50 g loading in healthy adults. [S5]
- **Recent mechanistic/genotoxic evidence:**
  - 2023 in vitro study in human lymphocytes reported no significant genotoxic signal for lactitol across tested concentrations; mitotic index decreased at higher concentrations. [S9]
- **Recent human safety signals:**
  - 2019 randomized controlled trial in constipated adults reported treatments including lactitol were well tolerated with no serious adverse events.
  - 2020 multicenter clinical study in constipated patients showed symptom improvement and microbiota shifts after lactitol.
  - 2026 case report described severe AKI/oxalate nephropathy after prolonged intake of a lactitol-containing supplement plus persistent diarrhea (single-patient evidence, not incidence data). [S10][S11][S8]
- **Epidemiological evidence:** No large prospective population cohort directly establishing chronic high-risk outcomes from food-additive-level lactitol exposure was identified in the searched sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark context:** JECFA ADI is "not specified," so risk management is not ADI-numeric-limited in the standard way. [S3][S4]
- **Dose/tolerance observations from human data:**
  - 24 g/day tolerated in older JECFA-cited human observations.
  - 50 g acute loading caused diarrhea in healthy subjects in older observations.
  - Mild flatulence reported in portal-systemic encephalopathy patients at 36-96 g/day in older therapeutic contexts. [S5]
- **ADI exceedance risk framing:** Classical ADI exceedance is **not applicable** because JECFA ADI is not specified; practical risk is dose-related GI intolerance, and potentially dehydration/secondary complications in susceptible individuals at high intakes. [S5][S8]
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Children (lower body mass), people with GI sensitivity, and people with renal vulnerability if prolonged high-dose laxative use leads to persistent diarrhea/dehydration. [S5][S8]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports:** JECFA ADI "not specified" and long-standing regulatory acceptance support low intrinsic toxicological concern at intended food-use levels. [S3][S4][S5]
  - **Argues against:** Clear dose-related GI effects (diarrhea/laxation) are documented; newer case-level severe renal outcome exists after prolonged high intake, so "no credible evidence of harm" is too strong. [S5][S8]

- **limited**
  - **Supports:** Broad regulatory acceptance; no established carcinogenic classification in IARC; recent genotoxicity study did not show significant genotoxicity under tested conditions; clinical studies generally report tolerability with expected GI effects. [S1][S2][S6][S7][S9][S10][S11]
  - **Argues against:** Rare but serious adverse outcome has been reported in a case report at high/prolonged exposure context. [S8]

- **moderate**
  - **Supports:** Documented GI adverse effects at higher doses and a recent serious case report could justify a cautionary upgrade in sensitive populations or misuse scenarios. [S5][S8]
  - **Argues against:** No strong evidence of population-level severe toxicity at normal additive-use exposure; JECFA kept ADI "not specified." [S3][S4][S5]

- **high**
  - **Supports:** Very limited (mainly single-case severe toxicity context). [S8]
  - **Argues against:** No major-jurisdiction bans, no IARC carcinogenic group classification, and no broad regulatory inability to confirm safety at intended uses. [S1][S2][S3][S6][S7]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence pattern is long-standing regulatory acceptance with "ADI not specified," plus predictable GI intolerance at higher doses rather than systemic toxicity at normal use. A single recent severe case report raises caution for misuse/high-dose contexts but does not outweigh the broader evidence base.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
