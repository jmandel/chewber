# E225 (Potassium sulfite) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E225 [S6][S9]
- **Name:** Potassium sulfite [S6][S9]
- **CAS number(s):** 10117-38-1 [S6][S9]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfite salt (sulfurous acid salt; SO2-releasing sulfite additive class) [S6][S10]
- **Common synonyms:** Dipotassium sulfite, potassium sulphite, sulfurous acid dipotassium salt, INS 225 [S9][S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily synthetic/manufactured food additive. [S6][S9]

## Function in Food
Potassium sulfite is used as a **preservative and antioxidant**. Sulfite chemistry (SO2-releasing species) supports antimicrobial action, antioxidant/reducing action, and anti-browning effects in suitable foods. [S6][S10]

Common food categories reported across regulatory/review sources include beverages and beverage bases, fruit products (for example preserved cherries), condiments/relishes, frozen potato products, confectionery/syrups, dried fruit/vegetables, seafood, and wine-related products. [S1][S3][S10]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/mechanistic
- A 2022 toxicology study reported sodium bisulfite-induced biothiol imbalance and inflammatory signaling in colonic cell/mouse models, suggesting plausible sulfite-class intestinal toxicity mechanisms under experimental conditions. [S11]

### Human/clinical/observational
- A 2024 clinical review (Allergen of the Year) highlights sulfites as relevant allergens/contact sensitizers and notes exposure from foods, medicines, and personal care products. [S12]
- A 2025 prospective cohort study on **food additive mixtures** reported increased type 2 diabetes risk with some mixtures that included sulphite ammonia caramel; this is mixture-level evidence and not specific causal proof for E225 alone. [S13]

### Review evidence
- A comprehensive sulfites-in-food review (2020) summarizes antimicrobial/antioxidant functionality and discusses hypersensitivity/allergic reactions and other possible adverse effects at higher exposures. [S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reported high-consumer concern for sulfites in 2022 MOE analysis (MOE below 80 in most groups), and its 2022 communication stated potential exceedance of what would be considered safe by up to 12.5% in children and up to 60% in adults (for high consumers in that assessment context). [S1]
- EFSA also reported major data limitations and withdrew the existing temporary ADI in the 2022 follow-up. [S1][S2]
- JECFA maintains a group ADI of 0-0.7 mg/kg bw for sulfites including potassium sulfite. [S6]
- **Vulnerable populations:** high consumers of sulfite-containing foods and sulfite-sensitive/allergic individuals. [S1][S10][S12]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Long-standing authorization history in multiple systems and established functional role. [S3][S6]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA sulfite follow-up withdrew temporary ADI and identified high-consumer safety concerns.
  - Documented hypersensitivity/allergenicity concerns in susceptible individuals. [S1][S2][S12]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - FDA GRAS listing (21 CFR 182.3798) with explicit GMP limits.
  - JECFA group ADI (0-0.7 mg/kg bw) includes potassium sulfite. [S3][S6]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA follow-up for sulfites identified unresolved data gaps and concern at high exposure.
  - Mechanistic/animal evidence raises additional caution signals for sulfite-class additives. [S1][S2][S11]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved/permitted in some systems but with clear caveats and high-exposure concerns.
  - EFSA ADI withdrawal in sulfite follow-up and MOE concern pattern.
  - Clinically relevant allergenicity/hypersensitivity concerns. [S1][S2][S12]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - IARC does not classify sulfites as Group 1/2 (Group 3 listing).
  - No major blanket ban found in visited core regulatory datasets. [S8]

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Regulatory concern signals exist for high consumers in EFSA sulfite assessment context. [S1][S2]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for potassium sulfite itself; sulfites are Group 3.
  - No major-jurisdiction blanket ban identified in visited sources. [S8]

### 2. Rationale
Potassium sulfite does not fit `risk_free` because sulfite-class risk assessment shows unresolved uncertainty and exposure-driven concerns in EFSA's latest follow-up context. It does not fit `high` because major blanket bans and IARC high-risk carcinogenic classifications are absent. The best fit is **moderate**: permitted with conditions, but with meaningful caveats (exposure and sensitivity concerns, data limitations, and nontrivial mechanistic signals). [S1][S2][S3][S6][S8][S12]

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
