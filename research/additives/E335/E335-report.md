# E335 (Sodium tartrates) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E335
- **Name:** Sodium tartrates (E335)
- **CAS number(s):** 868-18-8 (sodium tartrate listed in 21 CFR 184.1801)
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salts of tartaric acid (tartrate salts)
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium tartrate; disodium L(+)-tartrate; sodium tartrates
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **semi-synthetic / natural-derived**. CFR text states sodium tartrate is obtained as a byproduct of wine manufacture, then used as a food-grade salt.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Acidity/pH control and sequestration/complexation of metal ions; in US CFR it is explicitly listed as an emulsifier and pH control agent.
- **Common food categories (CFR-listed):** Cheeses, fats and oils, jams and jellies (at GMP levels).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA
- **Search and access note:** The EFSA opinion URL was found (`https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/6030`) but direct page retrieval was blocked by anti-bot/rate limiting in this environment. I used the PubMed-indexed EFSA article record for the same opinion to extract core data.
- **Latest re-evaluation year:** 2020 (EFSA Journal 18(3):6030).
- **Status:** Approved/re-evaluated; EFSA retained use with updated risk assessment.
- **ADI:** **240 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI for L(+)-tartaric acid-tartrates E334-337 and E354, expressed as tartaric acid).
- **Key conclusions:** No indication of genotoxicity; no carcinogenicity signal in chronic rat data at tested doses; refined exposure estimates did not exceed the group ADI.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status:** Yes (direct food substance affirmed as GRAS).
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1801 (Sodium tartrate)**.
- **Conditions of use:** Used in food with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice; CFR lists use as emulsifier and pH control agent, including cheeses, fats/oils, jams/jellies.
- **Related feed citation:** 21 CFR 582.6801 states sodium tartrate is GRAS when used according to good manufacturing or feeding practice.

### JECFA / WHO
- **WHO/JECFA database record used:** L(+)-tartaric acid entry that explicitly covers sodium, potassium, and potassium sodium tartrate salts.
- **ADI:** **0-30 mg/kg bw** (group ADI, expressed as tartaric acid).
- **Last evaluation year:** **2017** (with earlier 1977 group ADI history shown on-page).
- **Key note:** 84th JECFA concluded metatartaric acid should be included without changing the established group ADI.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **EFSA re-evaluation (2020):** No toxic effects (including nephrotoxicity) in the assessed L(+)-tartrate studies; no genotoxic signal; chronic rat study reported no carcinogenicity for monosodium L(+)-tartrate at the highest tested dose (3,100 mg/kg bw/day sodium tartrate).
- **Rat nephropathy model (2015):** DL-potassium hydrogen tartrate caused obstructive nephropathy in F344 rats at high dietary levels (>=0.5% in diet). This is relevant as high-dose tartrate-salt hazard context, but it is not a direct dietary E335 use-level study.
- **FemTA study (2013):** A complex product containing iron trichloride + sodium tartrate showed GI/inflammation and organ-weight/clinical chemistry changes at mid/high doses, interpreted as iron-overload related; reproductive/developmental endpoints showed no adverse effect at highest tested dose.

### Epidemiological / human data
- No robust E335-specific epidemiological harm signal at normal dietary exposure was identified in the retrieved sources.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Species-dependent kinetics are reported (higher absorption in rats than humans), which EFSA used to derive a chemical-specific uncertainty approach.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake:** Numeric population intake values were not retrieved from full EFSA tables in this environment due EFSA page access blocking, but the EFSA abstract reports refined non-brand-loyal exposure estimates did not exceed the group ADI.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA framework: no exceedance in refined scenario reported.
  - JECFA framework: lower group ADI (0-30 mg/kg bw), so conservative benchmark remains lower than EFSA's 240 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Potential vulnerable populations:** Individuals with renal vulnerability/high mineral-salt exposure patterns may warrant caution at high intakes, based on high-dose animal tartrate-salt findings.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Longstanding regulatory acceptance in EU/US/JECFA; EFSA found no genotoxic/carcinogenic concern and no ADI exceedance in refined exposure.
  - Evidence against this tier: JECFA ADI is numerical (not "ADI not specified"); high-dose tartrate-salt animal nephropathy findings exist for related salts.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: FDA GRAS with explicit GMP conditions; EFSA approval with a defined ADI and no major safety signal at reported use levels; JECFA group ADI maintained.
  - Evidence against this tier: Divergence between EFSA and JECFA ADI values introduces uncertainty in conservative benchmarking.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Related high-dose animal nephropathy data for tartrate salts and interspecies kinetic differences suggest hazard at sufficiently high exposure.
  - Evidence against this tier: No major regulatory withdrawal/restriction; no strong human evidence of harm at normal food additive exposures.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: None identified (no major bans; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment for sodium tartrate).
  - Evidence against this tier: Regulatory status remains permissive across EFSA/FDA/JECFA and available data do not indicate high-severity risk at authorized use levels.

### 2. Rationale
Core regulators continue to permit E335/sodium tartrate use, and the latest EFSA re-evaluation established a high group ADI with no refined-scenario exceedance. Available concern signals are mainly from high-dose or related-salt animal models, not from typical dietary use levels.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
