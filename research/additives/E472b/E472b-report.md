# E472b (Lactic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E472b / INS 472b. (WHO/JECFA chemical page, 1973 entry metadata) [S5]
- **Name:** Lactic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides (also listed as "lactic and fatty acid esters of glycerol"). (WHO/JECFA, 1973 metadata) [S5]
- **CAS number(s):** 31566-31-1 is listed on the WHO/JECFA page (shown as glyceryl stearate); this additive is a mixture and does not have a single universally used CAS across all commercial compositions. (WHO/JECFA) [S5]
- **Chemical class:** Esterified mono-/diglyceride emulsifier (glycerol fatty-acid esters further esterified with lactic acid). (EFSA re-evaluation text + JECFA name/functional class) [S3][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Lactoglycerides; lactic and fatty acid esters of glycerol; lactic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides. (WHO/JECFA) [S5]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (industrial esterification of food lipids/mono-diglycerides with lactic acid). (eCFR manufacturing description + EFSA chemistry framing) [S3][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Emulsifier. (WHO/JECFA functional class; eCFR section heading) [S4][S5]
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic ester mixture that lowers oil-water interfacial tension and stabilizes fat/water systems; helps texture and processing stability. (General emulsifier function + regulatory classification) [S4][S5]
- **Common food categories:** Broadly permitted in foods unless a standard of identity excludes it (US CFR language), commonly used in bakery/fat-containing processed foods and cream-like systems. (eCFR + mouse cream-formulation study context) [S4][S9]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Status:** Approved in EU food-additive framework; EFSA re-evaluated E472a-f and concluded **no safety concern for E472b at reported uses/use levels**. (EFSA Journal re-evaluation, 2020) [S1][S3]
  - **ADI:** EFSA concluded there was **no need for a numerical ADI** for E472a, E472b, E472c, E472e, E472f (separate group ADI set only for E472d as tartaric acid basis). (EFSA 2020 abstract/full text) [S1][S3]
  - **Evaluation year:** 2020. [S1]
  - **Key conclusion:** No genotoxicity concern identified for E472a-f, and no safety concern for E472b at reported use levels. [S1][S3]
- **FDA/CFR (US):**
  - **Status:** **Approved** direct food additive (not handled as a standalone GRAS affirmation in this citation set).
  - **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.852** (Lactic acid esters of mono- and diglycerides).
  - **Conditions of use:** May be safely used in food where not excluded by standard of identity; must be made by esterification of glycerides with lactic acid; use must follow GMP and not exceed amount reasonably required for intended technical effect. [S4]
  - **GRAS status note:** In the checked source set, regulation is explicit under Part 172 food additive listing, so this profile is treated as **approved food additive** rather than a distinct Part 182/184 GRAS listing. [S4]
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **NOT LIMITED** (interpreted as no numerical mg/kg bw/day limit specified).
  - **Evaluation year:** **1973** (meeting 17 shown on page).
  - **Comment:** ADI context given as a sum with related glycerol esters/acids and a tartaric-acid intake condition in historical wording. [S5]
- **IARC:** No IARC monograph classification found for this additive in the checked IARC classification list. [S7]
- **Notable bans:** No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in the EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources checked. [S1][S4][S5][S7]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (strongest evidence base):**
  - EFSA 2020: no concern for genotoxicity for E472a-f; no safety concern for E472b at reported uses; no numerical ADI needed for E472b. [S1][S3]
  - JECFA: ADI "NOT LIMITED" with 1973 evaluation record. [S5]
- **Animal/mechanistic evidence:**
  - Mouse cream study using lactic acid esters of mono-/diglycerides (with carrageenan in one arm) found no deleterious outcomes for lipid metabolism, intestinal permeability, or liver disorder markers after 4 weeks, but reported modulation of mucus-cell counts and intestinal ER-stress gene expression, suggesting mechanistic signals that warrant follow-up. (J Dairy Sci, 2018) [S9]
- **Epidemiological data:**
  - No robust human cohort/intervention safety studies specific to E472b were identified in the targeted PubMed searches; most hits were regulatory or analytical chemistry papers. [S8][S10]
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - Class-level emulsifier hypotheses around gut barrier/microbiome effects exist, but direct E472b-specific human causal evidence is limited in the searched literature. [S8][S9]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimates (re-evaluation context):**
  - EFSA reported E472b exposure estimates in refined scenarios, with highest mean/high-percentile values in toddlers (reported in abstract text up to ~99 mg/kg bw/day mean and ~237 mg/kg bw/day high percentile). [S1][S3]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Because EFSA and JECFA do not currently use a numerical ADI for E472b (EFSA: no numerical ADI needed; JECFA: NOT LIMITED), classic "ADI exceedance" is not directly applicable for current regulatory interpretation. [S1][S3][S5]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Young children/toddlers can have higher exposure per kg body weight due to diet pattern and body size; monitoring is still prudent in high ultra-processed-food diets. [S1][S3]

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** EFSA found no safety concern for E472b at reported uses and no need for a numerical ADI; JECFA lists ADI as NOT LIMITED. [S1][S3][S5]
  - **Evidence against this tier:** E472b is a manufactured ester mixture (not simply an endogenous single molecule), and direct long-term human E472b-specific epidemiology is sparse; limited mechanistic signals in animal work exist. [S8][S9]
- **limited**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** EU/US/JECFA frameworks permit use; EFSA and JECFA outcomes are reassuring; no IARC classification found; available animal study did not show clear overt toxicity at tested conditions. [S1][S3][S4][S5][S7][S9]
  - **Evidence against this tier:** Residual uncertainty from sparse modern human data and class-level emulsifier mechanistic concerns. [S8][S9]
- **moderate**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** High per-kg child exposure estimates and mechanistic intestinal signals can justify cautionary placement if one emphasizes uncertainty. [S1][S3][S9]
  - **Evidence against this tier:** Regulators did not identify a need to restrict E472b via a numerical ADI; no major bans or carcinogenic classifications were found. [S1][S4][S5][S7]
- **high**
  - **Evidence supporting this tier:** No strong direct evidence from checked sources.
  - **Evidence against this tier:** No major bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification identified, and EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain permissive. [S1][S5][S7]

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest, additive-specific evidence remains regulatory toxicology (EFSA/JECFA), which is generally reassuring for E472b at reported uses. Uncertainty remains due to sparse modern human E472b-specific studies and some mechanistic animal observations, but these are insufficient to outweigh current regulatory conclusions.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
