# E309 (Delta-tocopherol) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E309 (delta-tocopherol) [S1][S10]
- **Primary CAS number:** `119-13-1` (PubChem synonym list for delta-tocopherol). [S10]
- **Other CAS reported in synonym datasets:** `251562-63-7` (stereochemical form entry). [S10]
- **Chemical class:** Tocopherol (vitamin E isoform), lipophilic phenolic antioxidant. [S1][S10]
- **Common synonyms:** delta-Tocopherol, (+)-delta-Tocopherol, 8-Methyltocol, RRR-delta-tocopherol, delta-vitamin E. [S10]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Delta-tocopherol exists naturally (vegetable-oil tocopherol fraction); commercial food-additive material can be natural extract-derived and/or manufactured as a purified ingredient. [S1][S10]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antioxidant/preservative against lipid oxidation in foods. [S1][S3]
- **Mechanism:** Chain-breaking antioxidant behavior in lipid phases (interrupts propagation of oxidative radical reactions). [S1]
- **Common food categories (regulatory/inferred):** fats and oils and other lipid-containing processed foods where tocopherols are used as antioxidants under GMP. [S1][S3][S4]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest additive re-evaluation found:** EFSA Journal opinion for E306-E309 (2015), publication `4247`. [S1]
  - **Approval/safety status:** EFSA concluded exposure to tocopherols (E306-E309) as food additives was **not of safety concern** at reported uses/use levels. [S1]
  - **ADI:** EFSA reported **no need for a numerical ADI** for tocopherols as food additives in this opinion; EFSA also referenced a historical group ADI context (2 mg/kg bw/day) for vitamin E source use. [S1]
  - **Practical access note:** direct EFSA/Wiley full-text endpoints were blocked in this environment (Cloudflare / robots), so extraction relied on accessible indexed EFSA metadata/search snippets plus title-level records. [S1][S2]
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**

  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3890 (Tocopherols)**.
  - **Conditions of use:** used in accordance with **good manufacturing practice**; GMP limitations are further described in **21 CFR 182.1(b)** (amount reasonably required for intended technical effect, food-grade quality, minimization of carryover). [S3][S4]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA):** 2015 EFSA re-evaluation for E306-E309 concluded no safety concern for reported food-additive uses/use levels; additive-use ADI not numerically set. [S1]
- **Recent human observational signal (delta-specific):** CKD cohort analysis (PMID 40053500, 2025) reported a **U-shaped association** between dietary delta-tocopherol intake and CKD progression risk, supporting possible non-linear dose-response in a vulnerable population (observational, non-causal). [S12]
- **Broader additive epidemiology signal (not delta-specific):** NutriNet-Sante cohort (PMID 41501013, 2026) found positive associations between several preservatives and incident type 2 diabetes, including alpha-tocopherol exposure; this does not establish causality and does not isolate E309 specifically. [S13]
- **Short-term supplementation safety context:** phase-0 trial with gamma-rich vitamin E mixture (including delta increase in tissue levels) reported **no adverse events** over 7-14 days (PMID 33319628). [S14]
- **Mechanistic dietary intervention context:** randomized diet trial (PMID 41361349, 2025) suggested alpha-tocopherol supplementation attenuated some oxidative/inflammatory biomarker shifts in a high-meat dietary context (not an E309 safety trial). [S15]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake (regulatory context):** EFSA indexed abstract metadata for the 2015 E306-E309 opinion reported mean and high-percentile exposures from all tocopherol sources (mean ~0.30-2.36 mg/kg bw/day; high percentile ~0.70-8.49 mg/kg bw/day across groups). [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - For additive use specifically, EFSA did not require a numerical ADI in the 2015 opinion. [S1]
  - Relative to older vitamin-E-source group ADI context (2 mg/kg bw/day), high-end total-source exposure estimates can exceed 2 mg/kg bw/day in some populations; additive-specific exceedance remains uncertain. [S1]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Individuals with chronic kidney disease (based on recent observational non-linear associations for tocopherol isoforms including delta-tocopherol). [S12]
  - High consumers of ultra-processed/additive-dense diets pending stronger additive-specific exposure attribution. [S13]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting placement: delta-tocopherol is a vitamin E isoform; EFSA did not identify safety concern at reported additive use levels and did not set a numerical additive ADI. [S1]
  - Evidence arguing against placement: observational human data suggest non-linear risk patterns in CKD for dietary delta-tocopherol; some epidemiological preservative studies signal possible metabolic associations for the broader preservative set. [S12][S13]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA no-safety-concern conclusion for additive use; FDA GRAS/GMP framework; no IARC carcinogenic listing; no major ban identified. [S1][S3][S4][S8][S9]
  - Evidence arguing against placement: direct modern toxicology specifically isolating E309 additive exposure remains limited; JECFA lacks a clear standalone delta-tocopherol entry. [S5][S7]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting placement: recent cohort evidence suggests non-linear (U-shaped) association for dietary delta-tocopherol in CKD progression; uncertainty persists on high-end exposures from all sources. [S12][S1]
  - Evidence arguing against placement: signals are observational and not causal; regulators currently retain permissive positions for tocopherol use. [S1][S3]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting placement: none identified from EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC core checks.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for delta-tocopherol, no major regulatory ban, and no regulatory conclusion that safety cannot be confirmed at current uses. [S1][S8][S9]

### 2. Rationale
The evidence supports continued regulatory acceptance with low acute concern, but not a "risk-free" designation, because additive-specific modern exposure/toxicity granularity is limited and newer observational data suggest possible non-linear risk in vulnerable CKD populations.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
