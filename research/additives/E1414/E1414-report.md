# E1414 Research Report: Acetylated distarch phosphate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1414 (INS 1414) [5]
- **Name:** Acetylated distarch phosphate [5]
- **CAS number(s):** 9067-33-8, 68130-14-3, 113894-91-0 (modified amylopectin entry) [6]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch; acetylated and phosphate-crosslinked starch derivative [6]
- **Common synonyms:** Acetylated distarch phosphate; INS 1414; modified starch (regulatory contexts) [5][6]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from food starch, then chemically esterified/crosslinked with reagents such as sodium trimetaphosphate/phosphorus oxychloride and acetic anhydride/vinyl acetate) [6]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Crosslinking and acetylation modify starch functionality (improving process stability/texture behavior under food-processing conditions) and support thickening/stabilization/emulsification uses [5][6].
- **JECFA functional class:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [5].
- **Common food categories (from searched literature/use context):** Sauces and sauce systems, protein/starch gels (surimi/meat systems), and other processed foods needing viscosity/stability control [11].

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorized in EU as part of modified starch food additives under Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008, with EFSA re-evaluation completed in 2017 [2].
- **ADI:** EFSA reports ADI "not specified" and concludes there is **no need for a numerical ADI** for the modified starch group including E1414 [2].
- **Evaluation year:** 2017 (EFSA Journal opinion doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4911) [2].
- **Key conclusion:** No safety concern at reported uses/use levels for the general population [2].
- **Access note:** The EFSA page URL was visited but redirected to Wiley pages that were bot-protected from this VM; data were recovered from the PubMed record of the same EFSA opinion and DOI metadata [1][2].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not identified as a separate GRAS listing in searched 21 CFR parts 182/184 text; use is regulated under food additive regulation for modified starch [3][4].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.892 (Food starch-modified)** [3].
- **Conditions of use (relevant excerpts):**
  - Food starch-modified "may be safely used in food" under listed modification limits and GMP-type constraints [3].
  - For reagent systems relevant to acetylated/phosphate-modified starches, limits include e.g. phosphorus oxychloride (<=0.1%), subsequent acetic anhydride (<=8%) or vinyl acetate (<=7.5%), and residual phosphate limits (including 0.04% phosphorus for sodium trimetaphosphate route) [3].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA 2017):**
  - Modified starches (including E1414) assessed via read-across; no genotoxic concern in silico; no treatment-related effects relevant for human risk assessment in rats up to 31,000 mg/kg bw/day; no safety concern at reported uses; no numerical ADI needed [2].
- **Older animal signal (1982 rat study):**
  - 30% dietary acetylated distarch phosphate associated with caecal enlargement and renal mineralization-type findings, but effects were less than lactose comparator and study authors discussed diet mineral-balance confounding [10].
- **Recent PubMed landscape:**
  - Recent records are predominantly food-technology/functional-property studies, with limited new compound-specific chronic toxicology evidence [11].
- **Food-contact context (EFSA 2018):**
  - Acetylated distarch phosphate in absorbent pad mixtures was concluded not of safety concern under specified non-direct-contact/functional-capacity conditions [9].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure context:** For combined modified starches E1404-E1451, EFSA reported 95th percentile refined exposure up to 3,053 mg/kg bw/day; separate figure reported for E1452 supplement consumers (22.1 mg/kg bw/day) [2].
- **E1414-specific intake quantification:** Not separately quantified in the extracted accessible records; available values are largely grouped modified-starch exposure [2].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** For EFSA/JECFA, ADI is "not specified" (no numerical ADI), so classic exceedance calculations against a numeric ADI are not applicable [2][5].
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** High consumers of processed foods may have higher exposure within group estimates; direct E1414-specific vulnerable-population quantification was not found in accessible records from this run [2][11].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA/JECFA both indicate ADI not specified/no numerical ADI required for modified starch context; no major genotoxic/carcinogenic signal identified by regulators [2][5].
  - Evidence against: E1414 is a chemically modified additive (not simply endogenous/native starch), with some high-dose animal findings in legacy studies and limited modern compound-specific chronic datasets [6][10][11].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Broad regulatory acceptance (EU, US via 21 CFR food additive framework, JECFA); EFSA concludes no safety concern at reported uses; no IARC listing identified [2][3][5][8].
  - Evidence against: Evidence base for E1414-specific modern epidemiology and long-term human outcomes remains sparse; many recent papers are functional/technological rather than toxicological [11].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Historical animal findings at high dietary inclusion (renal/caecal effects) could justify caution in conservative interpretations [10].
  - Evidence against: Findings were at high-dose dietary conditions with confounding discussion, and regulatory bodies did not conclude current-use safety concern [2][10].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None found (no major-jurisdiction ban, no IARC 2A/2B/1 assignment found for this agent itself, and no EFSA/JECFA reversal indicating inability to confirm safety) [2][5][8].
  - Evidence against: Multiple authoritative bodies maintain permissive status with non-numerical ADI framing [2][3][5].

### 2. Rationale
Main drivers are: (a) consistent permissive conclusions by EFSA/JECFA and U.S. CFR listing, (b) absence of compound-specific IARC carcinogenic classification, and (c) lack of convincing modern evidence of serious hazard at approved food-use exposures. Counterweight is limited additive-specific modern epidemiology/toxicology depth.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`**.
