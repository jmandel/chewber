# E212 (Potassium benzoate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E212.
- **Name:** Potassium benzoate.
- **CAS number(s):** 582-25-2. [S8, S17]
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of benzoic acid (aromatic carboxylate preservative salt). [S8, S17]
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium benzoate; benzoic acid, potassium salt; potassium salt of benzenecarboxylic acid; INS 212. [S8, S17]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** as a commercial food additive (produced as a benzoate salt for preservative use). [S8, S17]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Preservative / antimicrobial agent. [S8]
- **Mechanism of action:** Benzoate preservatives act as weak-acid antimicrobials, with strongest activity in acidic foods where undissociated benzoic acid inhibits microbial growth.
- **Common food categories:** Acidic beverages and other acidic foods in benzoate-preservative use patterns; EFSA exposure contributors for benzoates include non-alcoholic flavoured drinks, fermented milk products, and sauces. [S1]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in the EU food additive framework; latest identified EFSA re-evaluation for E210-E213 is 2016. [S1, S2]
- **ADI:** **5 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as benzoic acid). [S1]
- **Evaluation year:** 2016 (EFSA Journal opinion `10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4433`). [S1, S3]
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA maintained the group ADI but reported that high-level exposure could exceed the ADI in younger groups (notably toddlers/children), and exceedance became broader when additional benzoic-acid sources were included. [S1]
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA journal page access from this environment returned anti-bot/403 responses; data were extracted from EFSA-indexed search result content and DOI metadata. [S1, S2, S3]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Direct GRAS status for potassium benzoate:** Not identified as a standalone direct-food GRAS substance in **21 CFR part 184** (Part 184 list includes benzoic acid and sodium benzoate, but not potassium benzoate as its own section). [S4, S5, S6]
- **Specific CFR citation for potassium benzoate:** **21 CFR 177.1210** (indirect food additive context: closures with sealing gaskets for food containers). [S7]
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 177.1210):** Potassium benzoate is listed among optional closure-sealing gasket substances with a listed limit of **1 percent by weight** of gasket composition. [S7]

- **Last evaluation year:** **2021** (92nd meeting), with note that the previous group ADI 0-5 mg/kg bw was withdrawn. [S8]

## Key Safety Evidence

### Direct potassium-benzoate or benzoate-focused findings
- **In vitro genotoxicity signal (2011):** Human lymphocyte study reported increased chromosomal aberrations/SCE/MN and comet-assay DNA damage for sodium benzoate and potassium benzoate under tested concentrations. [S12]
- **Regulatory pharmacokinetic reassessment (2017):** Human/rodent PK analysis argued that default interspecies uncertainty could be reduced, supporting a less conservative ADI basis for benzoic acid and salts; this aligns with later JECFA ADI increase. [S11, S8]

### Human and broader benzoate evidence (contextual)
- **Child behavior trial (2013):** Randomized crossover trial in Hong Kong children did not find significant behavioral adverse effects for sodium benzoate alone in that study design. [S13]
- **Neuro-oxidative animal signal (2018):** Mouse study reported learning/memory and oxidative-stress changes with sodium benzoate exposure. Evidence is preclinical and not potassium-benzoate-specific. [S14]
- **Recent review trend (2025):** Systematic review of mammalian-cell food-preservative studies reported frequent cytogenotoxic findings, with sodium benzoate commonly represented; relevance to real-world potassium benzoate exposure remains uncertain. [S15]
- **Recent computational study (2024):** Docking-based genotoxicity-oriented analysis included potassium benzoate but is mechanistic/theoretical rather than direct toxicological proof. [S16]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (benzoates group):** Reported exposure could reach high values in younger groups (e.g., high-percentile estimates up to about 29.7 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers and 18.5 mg/kg bw/day in children in one scenario), exceeding EFSA's group ADI of 5 mg/kg bw/day. [S1]
- **Additional-source scenario:** EFSA also reported higher potential totals when benzoic-acid sources beyond additive use were included. [S1]
- **ADI exceedance interpretation:**
  - Versus EFSA ADI (5): exceedance risk exists in high consumers, especially children.
  - Versus JECFA ADI (20): exceedance risk narrows but can still occur at the upper tail in some groups.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Toddlers/children/adolescents and high consumers of benzoate-containing beverages/acidic processed foods. [S1]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting `risk_free`:** No IARC carcinogenic classification identified; JECFA currently uses a higher group ADI (0-20 mg/kg bw).
- **Evidence against `risk_free`:** EFSA reported high-consumer ADI exceedance in children under its EU ADI basis; in vitro genotoxic signals exist for potassium benzoate/related benzoates.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting `limited`:** Regulatory approval/authorization exists (EU/JECFA context), and FDA CFR includes a specific permitted use in food-contact gaskets.
- **Evidence against `limited`:** EFSA exceedance findings in younger high consumers and recurring mechanistic/genotoxic concerns are more than minor edge-case noise.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting `moderate`:** Fits criteria directly: approved substance with caveats, ADI exceedance risk in some populations, and credible non-clinical toxicity/genotoxicity signals.
- **Evidence against `moderate`:** Human causal evidence for serious long-term outcomes specific to potassium benzoate is limited, and major regulators have not withdrawn authorization.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting `high`:** Some experimental studies report biologically relevant adverse endpoints in benzoate research.
- **Evidence against `high`:** No major-jurisdiction ban found; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for potassium benzoate; EFSA/JECFA continue ADI-based management rather than prohibition.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence supports continued regulated use with caution: there is no strong signal for immediate high-tier hazard classification, but EFSA exposure exceedance in children and repeated genotoxic/mechanistic concerns argue against a low-concern classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
