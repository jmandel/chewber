# E404 (Calcium alginate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E404
- **Name:** Calcium alginate
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-35-0
- **Chemical class:** Alginic acid salt (hydrocolloid polysaccharide; alginate)
- **Common synonyms:** Alginic acid, calcium salt; calcium salt of alginic acid; INS 404
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic / naturally derived** (alginic acid is a natural constituent of brown algae; calcium alginate is produced by neutralizing purified alginic acid or by metathesis from sodium alginate).

Evidence:
- 21 CFR 184.1187 defines calcium alginate as the calcium salt of alginic acid, a natural constituent of brown algae, and describes manufacturing route. URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2025-10-16/title-21.xml
- JECFA chemical record lists CAS 9005-35-0 and INS 404. URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/2806

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** hydrocolloid thickening/stabilizing; gel network formation via alginate polymer interactions (including ionic gel behavior in alginate systems).
- **Common technological functions:** emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener.
- **Common food categories (US CFR explicit limits in 21 CFR 184.1187):** baked goods, alcoholic beverages, confections/frostings, egg products, fats/oils, gelatins/puddings, gravies/sauces, jams/jellies, sweet sauces, and other categories.

Evidence:
- JECFA functional classes: emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener. URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/2806
- 21 CFR 184.1187 food-category table and maximum levels. URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2025-10-16/title-21.xml
- Review discussing gelling/thickening mechanisms of marine algal polysaccharides (includes alginate context). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40973263/

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised in EU (E400-E404 authorised under Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008; re-evaluated by EFSA).
- **Latest key EFSA re-evaluation year:** **2017** (EFSA Journal 15(11):5049).
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for alginic acid and salts (E400-E404).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No safety concern at refined exposure estimates for reported uses/levels.
  - For infants/young children consuming foods for special medical purposes (FC 13.1.5.1/13.1.5.2), available data were insufficient for an adequate safety assessment.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology backbone:** EFSA 2017 re-evaluation of E400-E404 found no need for a numerical ADI and no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses, with a data gap for infants/young children in specific medical-purpose foods.
  - Source: EFSA Journal re-evaluation (2017). URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625343/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010146/
- **Animal evidence (calcium alginate-specific arm):** Rat study reported calcium alginate reduced Sr/Cs absorption/exposure and stated calcium alginate appeared safer than sodium alginate in that experimental setting.
  - Source: Biol Pharm Bull (2013). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23318531/
- **Recent mechanistic/dietary literature:** 90-day rat dietary sodium alginate work (2025) showed microbiome and lipid-metabolism modulation, with sex/viscosity dependence; these are biological-effect signals, not clear harm findings.
  - Source: J Sci Food Agric (2025). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40600413/
- **Evidence gap:** Limited recent human epidemiology specifically isolating E404 intake and long-term adverse outcomes.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (E400-E404 group):**
  - Non-brand-loyal mean: up to **26.7 mg/kg bw/day** (toddlers)
  - Non-brand-loyal high (95th): up to **79.3 mg/kg bw/day** (infants)
  - Brand-loyal mean: up to **40.6 mg/kg bw/day** (toddlers)
  - Brand-loyal high (95th): up to **158.1 mg/kg bw/day** (infants)
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not directly applicable numerically because EFSA and JECFA use "ADI not specified / no numerical ADI needed".
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants and young children consuming foods for special medical purposes (FC 13.1.5.1/13.1.5.2) remain the key uncertainty due to insufficient dataset in EFSA's 2017 re-evaluation.

Evidence URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010146/

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed and no safety concern at refined exposure.
  - JECFA: ADI not specified.
  - Substance is derived from natural alginate from brown algae and widely authorized.
- **Evidence arguing against risk_free:**
  - EFSA identified unresolved evidence gap for infants/young children consuming special medical-purpose foods.
  - Limited modern human long-term epidemiology specifically for E404.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - Authorized in EU; GRAS/regulated in US CFR.
  - No major carcinogenic classification signal from IARC for this agent.
  - No consistent serious toxicity signal in reviewed regulatory/animal evidence at authorized use context.
- **Evidence arguing against limited:**
  - Remaining uncertainty for specific pediatric medical-food contexts.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - EFSA data-gap caveat for infants/young children in specific medical-purpose categories.
  - Emerging gut-microbiome modulation literature (biological-effect signals), though not clearly adverse.
- **Evidence arguing against moderate:**
  - No regulatory finding of general-population harm at authorized uses.
  - ADI frameworks from EFSA/JECFA are permissive (not specified), not restrictive.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - No substantial direct evidence found.
- **Evidence arguing against high:**
  - Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment identified for calcium alginate.
  - EFSA/JECFA conclusions do not indicate high concern under normal food-use scenarios.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence comes from formal regulatory assessments (EFSA/JECFA/FDA), which indicate low concern at authorized uses and no numerical ADI requirement. The main counterweight is not demonstrated harm but uncertainty in specific infant medical-food use contexts and limited modern human outcome data specific to E404.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
