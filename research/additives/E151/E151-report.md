# E151 (Brilliant Black BN) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E151
- **Name:** Brilliant Black BN
- **CAS number(s):** 2519-30-4
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic sulfonated bis-azo dye (food colour)
- **Common synonyms:** Brilliant Black PN, Black PN, C.I. Food Black 1, INS 151
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic**

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action (technological):** A synthetic azo chromophore system absorbs visible wavelengths and imparts dark blue-black/purple tone to foods.
- **Primary role:** Colour additive.
- **Common food categories:** Products where dark colour standardization is needed (e.g., confectionery, desserts, sauces/condiments, and selected beverages), subject to local regulatory permissions.

**Evidence and URLs visited:**

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved in EU framework; re-evaluated by EFSA.
- **ADI:** **5 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **Evaluation year(s):**
1. **2010** re-evaluation (E151).
2. **2015** refined exposure assessment (latest EFSA output found specific to E151).
- **Key conclusions:**
1. 2010 EFSA opinion concluded there was no reason to revise the ADI of 5 mg/kg bw/day; long-term carcinogenicity studies were negative; a positive in vitro micronucleus/Comet signal was not considered indicative of carcinogenicity; refined exposure estimates were generally below ADI, but high consumers among children could exceed it (reported up to 6.9 mg/kg bw/day at upper percentile).
2. Latest EFSA item is the 2015 refined exposure assessment for E151.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic findings
- A 2004 study reported genotoxic signals (micronucleus and Comet assay contexts) for Brilliant Black BN and Quinoline Yellow in test systems.
- EFSA 2010 acknowledged in vitro positive micronucleus/Comet findings but considered long-term carcinogenicity evidence negative and did not revise the ADI.
- A 2010 pharmacology paper found allosteric receptor-modulating activity of Brilliant Black BN at adenosine A1/A3 receptors in vitro (mechanistic activity, not a direct chronic dietary toxicity endpoint).

### Human/epidemiology
- No strong modern human epidemiology isolating E151 dietary risk was found in the reviewed sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2010:** intake generally below ADI, but high-percentile exposure in children could exceed ADI (explicit value reported in abstract: 6.9 mg/kg bw/day in children under 10 years at upper percentile).
- **EFSA 2015 refined exposure assessment:** latest EFSA E151 output indicates continued exposure-focused reassessment after the 2010 ADI framework.
- **At-risk groups:** High consumers among children/toddlers are the primary group of concern for potential ADI exceedance scenarios.

**Evidence and URLs visited:**

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Supports placement:** Established ADIs by EFSA/JECFA and no IARC monograph classification found for E151 itself.
- **Argues against placement:** Not "ADI not specified"; EFSA identified potential high-end child intake exceedance scenarios; published genotoxic signals exist in some test systems.

#### `limited`
- **Supports placement:** Approved in EU with ADI, JECFA ADI retained, and no direct IARC classification.
- **Argues against placement:** Child high-percentile intake can exceed EFSA ADI in some scenarios; in vitro genotoxic findings prompted regulatory discussion.

#### `moderate`
- **Supports placement:** Meets the project’s moderate-tier triggers: approved but with caveats, ADI exceedance concern in high-consuming children, and credible mechanistic/genotoxicity signals requiring interpretation.
- **Argues against placement:** No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified; long-term carcinogenicity conclusion in EFSA 2010 did not support revising ADI downward.

#### `high`
- **Supports placement:** Would require stronger corroboration (major bans, strong carcinogenic classification/regulatory action, or inability to establish safety).
- **Argues against placement:** Current evidence does not show IARC Group 2A/2B assignment for E151 itself, nor major-jurisdiction blanket ban aligned with a high-tier profile.

### 2. Rationale
E151 has formal ADIs and remains permitted in important regulatory frameworks, but exposure caveats (especially in high-consuming children) plus recurring mechanistic/genotoxic discussion indicate non-trivial uncertainty at upper intake tails.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
