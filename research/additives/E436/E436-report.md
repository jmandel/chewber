# E436 (Polysorbate 65) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E436 (INS 436). [S4]
- **Primary name:** Polysorbate 65. [S3][S4]
- **CAS number:** 9005-71-4. [S4]
- **Chemical class:** Non-ionic emulsifier; polyoxyethylene sorbitan fatty acid ester mixture (polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan tristearate / mixed stearic acid esters of sorbitol anhydrides). [S3][S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Polysorbate 65; polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan tristearate; INS 436 / E436. [S3][S4]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** Industrially manufactured (reaction of stearic acid + sorbitol, then ethoxylation with ethylene oxide); classified here as **synthetic** for risk abstraction purposes. [S3]

## Function in Food
Polysorbate 65 functions as an **emulsifier/surfactant** (reduces oil-water interfacial tension and stabilizes dispersed fat/water systems). [S3][S4]

Common regulated U.S. food uses include: [S3]
- Ice cream, frozen custard, ice milk, fruit sherbet, nonstandardized frozen desserts (max 0.1%, alone or with polysorbate 80).
- Cakes and cake mixes (limits specified; polysorbate 65 alone up to 0.32% dry basis).
- Whipped edible oil toppings (up to 0.4%).
- Solid edible vegetable fat-water coffee whitener substitutes (up to 0.4%).
- Cake icings and fillings (polysorbate 65 alone up to 0.32%; combination caps also specified).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA scientific opinion for E436 group:** 2015 re-evaluation covering E432-E436; EFSA page also shows a 2018 correction notice (supplementary pagination issue). [S1][S2]
- **ADI:** EFSA set a **temporary group ADI of 10 mg/kg bw/day** for E432-E436. [S1]
- **Key conclusions:** No concern for genotoxicity; no concern from chronic/carcinogenicity and developmental toxicity datasets; reproductive toxicity database considered insufficient, so ADI set as temporary with additional uncertainty. [S1]
- **Exposure conclusion:** Highest estimated exposure occurred in toddlers and approached/slightly exceeded the temporary group ADI in high-consumption scenarios. [S1]
- **Approval status interpretation:** **Approved/restricted-use additive** in EU context with ADI-based risk management (not withdrawn in the cited EFSA record). [S1]

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis (directly relevant to E436 group):** EFSA re-evaluation found no genotoxicity concern and no concern from chronic/carcinogenicity or developmental toxicity, but noted reproductive-toxicity data limitations (temporary ADI). [S1]
- **Direct recent PubMed safety evidence on Polysorbate 65 itself:** very limited. PubMed query for polysorbate-65-specific toxicity/safety returned no direct hits, and the term-specific corpus is dominated by pharmaceutical formulation papers rather than food toxicology. [S7][S8]
- **Mechanistic concern from related polysorbate emulsifiers (extrapolation, not E436-specific):**
  - 2025 placebo-controlled human trial of several emulsifiers reported shifts in SCFA patterns but no clear rise in systemic/inflammatory endpoints over the trial window. [S9]
  - 2025 in vitro microbiome model paper found dose-dependent microbiome/barrier effects for polysorbate 80 and CMC. [S10]
  - 2021 rat/cell study reported polysorbate 80-associated intestinal barrier changes and increased absorption of MEHP (a contaminant metabolite), suggesting potential co-exposure interaction concerns. [S11]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA exposure estimates in regular consumers (group of E432-E436) ranged roughly from low values in elderly to highest in toddlers; high-consumption toddler estimates were around the temporary group ADI (about 10 mg/kg bw/day). [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** possible for high-consuming toddlers depending on scenario assumptions; lower concern for average adult consumers. [S1]
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** toddlers/young children and high consumers of foods where emulsifier use is common (e.g., bakery/frozen dessert/sauce patterns referenced by EFSA exposure contributors). [S1]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting placement: major regulators (EFSA/JECFA/FDA) allow use; no clear genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in EFSA review. [S1][S3][S4]
- Evidence against placement: EFSA ADI is **temporary** (data gap on reproductive toxicity), and high-end toddler exposure can approach/exceed ADI; therefore not "no credible harm" territory. [S1]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting placement: approved by FDA and internationally evaluated with numerical ADIs; no severe hazard classification from IARC listing search. [S3][S4][S5][S6]
- Evidence against placement: temporary ADI + near-ADI high-end toddler exposure and emerging mechanistic concerns from related emulsifiers argue beyond purely minor concerns. [S1][S9][S10][S11]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting placement: approved but with caveats (temporary ADI due data limitations), exposure at upper tails near/exceeding ADI in toddlers, plus class-based mechanistic signals from related polysorbates. [S1][S9][S10][S11]
- Evidence against placement: direct recent E436-specific harm evidence is sparse; most newer mechanistic evidence is from polysorbate 80 or mixed emulsifier designs. [S8][S9][S10][S11]

**high**
- Evidence supporting placement: some mechanistic concern signals exist for related emulsifiers. [S9][S10][S11]
- Evidence against placement: no major bans found, no IARC Group 1/2 classification for E436 identified, and regulators continue to allow controlled uses with ADIs. [S1][S3][S4][S5][S6]

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factors are: (a) continued regulatory approval with quantitative intake limits, (b) EFSA's temporary ADI and data-gap caveat, (c) high-end child exposure proximity to the ADI, and (d) limited but non-zero mechanistic concern from related emulsifiers rather than direct E436-specific human harm evidence.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `moderate`** (confidence: moderate, not high).
