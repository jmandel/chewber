# E1400 (Dextrin) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1400
- **Name:** Dextrin (roasted starch; INS 1400)
- **CAS number:** 9004-53-9
- **Chemical class:** Partially hydrolyzed starch carbohydrates (starch degradation products)
- **Common synonyms:** Dextrin, pyrodextrin, roasted starch
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** **Semi-synthetic** in food-additive context (made from starch by controlled roasting/partial hydrolysis; starch feedstock is natural)

Evidence:
- JECFA database entry for Dextrin (CAS 9004-53-9, synonyms, INS 1400): https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1778
- FDA identity statement for dextrins: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-184/section-184.1277

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Dextrins are lower-molecular-weight starch fragments that increase solids and viscosity, improve texture/body, and can function as stabilizing/carrier carbohydrates in formulations.
- **Functional classes (JECFA):** Anticaking agent, carrier, emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener.
- **Common food categories:** Broad use under GMP-type use patterns (powdered foods, formulated foods requiring bulking/texture/carrier functions).

Evidence:
- JECFA functional classes for dextrin: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1778
- FDA CFR indicates use with no specific limitation other than current GMP: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-184/section-184.1277

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology baseline
- JECFA assigns ADI “Not limited” and states no safety concern at current GMP use levels.
  - Source: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1778

### Recent PubMed evidence (safety-relevant)
1. **2025 systematic review/meta-analysis (T2D RCTs, resistant dextrin):** HbA1c reduction signal; no major new hazard signal reported in abstract; authors call for larger high-quality trials.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40346635/
2. **2022 human intervention (resistant dextrin, 14 g/day):** transient increase in gas/sensations followed by adaptation and lower gas production after adaptation period.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36364873/
3. **2020 randomized, placebo-controlled trial (resistant maltodextrin beverage):** abstract reports no serious adverse events or adverse clinical findings.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33132344/

### Animal studies / epidemiology / mechanistic concerns
- **Animal studies:** Some recent animal papers on resistant dextrin derivatives focus on metabolic outcomes rather than toxicity endpoints; no strong new harm signal for food-use exposures was identified in this targeted search.
- **Epidemiology:** No robust prospective epidemiology specifically isolating E1400 exposure was identified in this run.
- **Mechanistic concerns:** Main recurring concern is gastrointestinal intolerance (gas/bloating) at higher fermentable carbohydrate intakes, typically mild/transient.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake data for E1400 specifically:** Not found in accessible EFSA/JECFA/FDA sources reviewed here.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA uses “Not limited,” so classic numeric ADI exceedance calculation is not applicable.
  - Based on available sources, population-level toxicological exceedance concern appears low under GMP use.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:**
  - Individuals with IBS/functional GI sensitivity may report more gas/bloating with higher resistant dextrin intake.
  - People with very high supplemental fiber intake may experience GI discomfort before adaptation.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI is “Not limited,” with a “no safety concern at current levels” statement.
  - FDA lists dextrins as GRAS with GMP-based use.
  - No direct IARC carcinogenic classification found for dextrin.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No clearly retrievable EFSA E1400-specific re-evaluation output in this run.
  - Mild GI intolerance signals exist at higher intakes in some dextrin/resistant dextrin studies.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Strong regulatory acceptance in US (GRAS) and JECFA (ADI not limited).
  - Human studies show mostly mild/transient GI effects rather than serious toxicity.
  - Lack of convincing carcinogenic/genotoxic regulatory signal for dextrin itself.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EU/EFSA status for E1400 specifically could not be fully resolved from accessible EFSA materials.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Could be argued if EU uncertainty is treated conservatively and GI effects are emphasized.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No ADI reduction, no major regulatory warning, no clear serious toxicology signal in reviewed sources.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No major bans found, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for dextrin itself, and no strong mechanistic harm signal at normal food-use levels.

### 2. Rationale
The main drivers are: (a) JECFA “Not limited” ADI and explicit no-safety-concern language; (b) FDA GRAS status with GMP conditions; (c) absence of direct IARC classification for dextrin; balanced against (d) unresolved EFSA E1400-specific data availability in this run and (e) mostly mild GI tolerance limitations at higher intakes.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`** (not `risk_free`) due to unresolved EFSA-specific evidence retrieval for E1400 and modest GI-tolerance caveats, despite otherwise low concern.
