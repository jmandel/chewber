# E102 (Tartrazine) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E102
- **Name:** Tartrazine
- **CAS number(s):** 1934-21-0
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic azo dye (sulfonated monoazo food color)
- **Common synonyms:** FD&C Yellow No. 5, INS 102, C.I. Food Yellow 4, Acid Yellow 23
- **Origin:** Synthetic

**Evidence:**
- 21 CFR §74.705 identifies FD&C Yellow No. 5 and gives CAS 1934-21-0 plus synthetic manufacturing route ([S3], [S4]).
- WHO/JECFA entry is for TARTRAZINE with ADI listing ([S6]).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Color additive (yellow coloring agent).
- **Mechanism of action in foods:** The azo chromophore absorbs visible light and imparts a yellow hue; used to standardize/restore product color.
- **Common food categories:** Beverages, confectionery, desserts, snacks, sauces/condiments, and dietary supplements.

**Evidence:**
- FDA allows FD&C Yellow No. 5 for coloring foods generally under GMP (with restrictions) ([S4]).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation located:** 2009 Scientific Opinion, \"Scientific Opinion on the re-evaluation Tartrazine (E 102)\" ([S1], [S2]).
- **Status:** Approved (re-evaluated; ADI retained).
- **ADI:** 7.5 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Key EFSA conclusion:** Available toxicology database was considered more extensive than previous evaluations; no reason to revise the ADI; impurity exposure at current specs was not considered a safety concern.

**Access note:** direct EFSA/Wiley opinion page was not machine-accessible from this environment (bot challenge/403); data above were taken from EFSA result metadata/snippet and EFSA DOI metadata pages that were accessible ([S1], [S2]).

### FDA / eCFR (United States)
- **Status:** Approved color additive, **not GRAS-listed**.
- **Primary citation:** **21 CFR §74.705** (FD&C Yellow No. 5) ([S3], [S4]).
- **Conditions of use (foods):** \"may be safely used for coloring foods (including dietary supplements) generally\" under GMP, with standard-of-identity restrictions; labeling declaration required; batch certification required ([S4]).
- **GRAS inference:** Tartrazine appears as a certified color additive in Part 74 and was not found as a GRAS substance by additive name in Title 21 text extraction. This is an inference from the eCFR structure/search results, not a single explicit \"not GRAS\" sentence ([S4]).

### JECFA / WHO
- **Entry:** TARTRAZINE, JECFA database chemical page ([S6]).
- **Latest evaluation year:** 2016.
- **ADI:** 0-10 mg/kg bw.
- **Key conclusion:** JECFA established ADI 0-10 mg/kg bw (NOAEL 984 mg/kg bw/day, UF 100), withdrew previous ADI 0-7.5 mg/kg bw/day, and stated estimated dietary exposure (including children 1-10 years) was below the upper ADI bound ([S6]).

### IARC
- **Finding from IARC Monographs classification resources:** No tartrazine entry found in the current \"List of Classifications\" dataset (updated 2026-02-25 in the served script) ([S7], [S8], [S9]).
- **Interpretation:** No current IARC group classification could be confirmed from the live IARC classification dataset accessed.

### Notable bans
- No current major-jurisdiction ban was confirmed in the sources collected for this run.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- Multiple recent animal studies report oxidative stress, histopathology, or neurobehavioral changes with tartrazine exposure, often at high dose and/or short-to-medium study duration (e.g., pancreatic and brain injury models) ([S12], [S13], [S14]).
- A 2026 OECD/AOP-oriented paper reported endocrine-activity signals primarily from in silico/in vitro integration, indicating hypothesis-generating concern rather than definitive clinical risk quantification ([S11]).

### Human/epidemiological evidence
- The 2007 Southampton randomized trial found increased hyperactivity scores with **mixtures** of artificial colors plus sodium benzoate in children; causality for tartrazine alone is not isolated ([S15]).
- A 2022 review (OEHHA-linked) found many challenge studies indicating behavioral associations for synthetic food dyes and highlighted concern for susceptible children ([S10]).

### Allergic/hypersensitivity concern
- U.S. labeling rules include explicit warning language for FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine) sensitivity in certain susceptible persons, including bronchial-asthma-type reactions and higher prevalence among aspirin-hypersensitive patients (drug-label context in Title 21 text) ([S4]).

## Exposure Assessment
- **JECFA (2016):** exposure estimates for general population, including children 1-10 years, were reported below the upper ADI bound ([S6]).
- **ADI exceedance risk:** not clearly demonstrated in the regulatory sources collected here for the general population; high-intake subgroups remain a practical concern in principle.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children with high intake of dyed foods/beverages.
  - Individuals with aspirin hypersensitivity or known dye sensitivity.

Carcinogenic classification evidence from IARC was not confirmed as positive in current accessed data.
- **Confidence:** Moderate (source quality is mixed across regulatory and experimental evidence; EFSA full opinion text was not directly retrievable in this environment).
