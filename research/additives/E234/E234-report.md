# E234 (Nisin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E234
- **Name:** Nisin
- **CAS number:** 1414-45-5
- **EC number:** 215-807-5
- **Chemical class:** Polycyclic lantibiotic (antimicrobial peptide/bacteriocin)
- **Common synonyms:** Nisin, Nisin A, INS 234, E 234
- **Origin:** Natural fermentation product (derived from *Streptococcus lactis* / *Lactococcus lactis* cultures)

**Evidence:**
- OpenEFSA substance record for Nisin lists CAS 1414-45-5 and common name E 234.
- eCFR 21 CFR 184.1538 states nisin preparation is derived from pure culture fermentations and contains nisin (CAS 1414-45-5).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antimicrobial preservative (biopreservative)
- **Mechanism of action:** Nisin is a lantibiotic peptide that inhibits susceptible Gram-positive bacteria (including *Clostridium botulinum*) through membrane-targeting/antimicrobial peptide activity.
- **Common food categories:**
  - Pasteurized cheese spreads and pasteurized process cheese spreads (US CFR-defined uses)
  - EFSA 2017 opinion also assessed proposed EU extension to unripened cheese and heat-treated meat products

**Evidence:**
- eCFR 21 CFR 184.1538(c) defines antimicrobial use to inhibit *C. botulinum* outgrowth and toxin formation in specific cheese spread categories.
- EFSA 2017 opinion abstract (via PubMed record of EFSA Journal article) explicitly discusses proposed extension to unripened cheese and heat-treated meat products.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved food additive (E234) with re-evaluation/opinion in 2017.
- **Latest identified EFSA opinion:** EFSA-Q-2017-00097, output ON-5063 (published December 2017).
- **ADI:** 1 mg/kg bw/day (as nisin A), derived from 90-day rat NOAEL 225 mg/kg bw/day with uncertainty factor 200.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - Overall dietary exposure estimate was below the new ADI for all population groups.
  - Proposed extension of use in unripened cheese (12 mg/kg) and heat-treated meat products (25 mg/kg) was not considered a safety concern.

**Access note:** Direct EFSA/Wiley article URL returned Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) in this environment; ADI and conclusions were extracted from the PubMed indexed abstract of the same EFSA opinion and linked back to the EFSA output record.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS (affirmed under Part 184 direct food substances).
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1538** (Nisin preparation).
- **Conditions of use:**
  - Antimicrobial agent in specified pasteurized cheese spread/process cheese spread categories.
  - Use at GMP, with current GMP corresponding to max **250 ppm nisin** in finished product.

### JECFA (FAO/WHO)

- **ADI:** 0-2 mg/kg bw (full ADI status on FAO JECFA additive detail page).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic data
- **Regulatory toxicology base:** EFSA 2017 considered a new 90-day oral rat study with no adverse effects at the highest tested dose (NOAEL 225 mg/kg bw/day), supporting ADI derivation at 1 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Recent concern signal (preclinical):** 2026 zebrafish model study reported that dietary nisin worsened diabetic vascular complications via microbiota dysbiosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and NF-kB signaling.
- **Recent potentially beneficial signal (preclinical):** 2025 *Apc(Min/+)* mouse study reported reduced small-intestinal tumor burden with nisin Z and microbiome/metabolite shifts; authors also described anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Additional recent animal feeding data:** 2025 rabbit supplementation study reported improved growth and antioxidant/intestinal parameters with microbiome changes; this is not a direct chronic human safety study.

### Epidemiology/human data
- No strong contemporary human epidemiological safety signal identified in the sources reviewed.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Gut microbiome modulation is plausible and observed in recent animal models, with mixed directionality (potentially adverse in disease-specific models, beneficial in others).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2017 exposure calculations (current and proposed uses) were reported as below the new ADI for all population groups.
- US exposure is additionally constrained by narrow food-category authorization and 250 ppm maximum GMP level under CFR.
- Main uncertainty in 2026 literature context: disease-specific susceptibility hypotheses (for example, diabetes-related vascular outcomes) are preclinical and not yet integrated into regulatory ADI revisions from the cited authorities.

### ADI exceedance risk
- **General population:** Low likelihood based on EFSA 2017 exposure conclusion and current use restrictions.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Individuals with relevant disease states (for example diabetes) remain a research uncertainty due to limited translational human evidence.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:** Longstanding regulated use; natural fermentation-derived peptide; no IARC cancer classification found.
- **Evidence against:** Regulators still maintain numeric ADIs (not “ADI not specified”); emerging microbiome-related mechanistic concerns in some animal disease models.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - EFSA-approved with defined ADI and explicit “no safety concern” conclusion for evaluated uses.
  - FDA GRAS with clear CFR conditions and concentration limits.
  - JECFA full ADI established.
  - No corroborated human epidemiologic harm signal from reviewed sources.
- **Evidence against:** New preclinical studies suggest context-dependent microbiome and inflammatory effects that warrant monitoring.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:** Credible recent animal/mechanistic publications (including a diabetic zebrafish model) suggest potential adverse effects in specific biological contexts.
- **Evidence against:** No demonstrated ADI exceedance in EFSA exposure assessment; no parallel major regulatory restriction/bans; findings not yet corroborated by robust human data.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:** Minimal to none from current regulatory evidence.
- **Evidence against:** No major-jurisdiction bans found, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification identified, and no EFSA/JECFA inability-to-confirm-safety finding in reviewed documents.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence is still the convergent regulatory position (EFSA/FDA/JECFA) supporting controlled use with ADIs/conditions. New mechanistic animal data introduce uncertainty about potential subgroup-specific effects (especially microbiome-mediated pathways), but current evidence is insufficient for a moderate/high classification in the absence of human corroboration or regulatory downgrades.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
