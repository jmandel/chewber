# E960 (Steviol glycosides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E960 (currently split in EU into E960a-d classes; includes enzymatically produced steviol glycosides as E960c). [S1][S2]
- **Primary name:** Steviol glycosides.
- **Representative CAS numbers (for major constituents):**
  - Stevioside: **57817-89-7** [S16]
  - Rebaudioside A: **58543-16-1** [S17]
  - Steviol (aglycone/equivalent basis): **471-80-7** [S18]
- **Chemical class:** diterpene glycosides (steviol-based glycosides).
- **Common synonyms/components:** stevioside, rebaudioside A, rebaudioside D, rebaudioside M, glucosylated steviol glycosides, enzyme-modified steviol glycosides. [S2][S6][S7][S8]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** traditionally extracted from *Stevia rebaudiana* leaves; additional commercial forms are produced by enzymatic/fermentation bioconversion processes (semi-synthetic/biotech routes). [S2][S6][S8]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** high-intensity sweetener (typically ~200-400x sweeter than sucrose, depending on component/profile). [S3]
- **Mechanism of action:** provides sweet taste via sweet-taste receptor pathway (non-caloric sweetening); used to replace sugar while maintaining sweetness. [S3][S14]
- **Common food categories:** table-top sweeteners, flavored drinks and other general-purpose sweetened foods; EFSA notes authorization across many EU categories (32 categories in 2024 opinion context). [S1][S2][S6][S7][S8]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** approved/authorized food additive class in the EU (E960a-d; E960c process variants also evaluated). [S1][S2]
- **ADI:** **4 mg/kg bw/day** (as steviol equivalents) retained.
- **Latest major ADI re-evaluation outcome (2024):** EFSA assessed requests to raise ADI (to 6 or 16 mg/kg bw/day) and concluded available ADME evidence was insufficient to increase the ADI; current ADI of 4 mg/kg bw/day remained. [S1]
- **Latest scientific opinion (2026, process modification):** EFSA concluded **no safety concern** for the proposed E960c manufacturing modification (new GM *E. coli* production strains; negligible residual enzyme-TOS exposure; no viable cells/DNA in final product). [S2]
- **Exposure implication in EFSA 2024 assessment:** conservative 95th percentile exposure estimates reached 4.1 mg/kg bw/day (infants) and 6.9 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers) under proposed expanded uses, increasing ADI exceedance concern for toddlers. [S1]

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** high-purity steviol glycosides are handled via the **GRAS notice** pathway; FDA has issued multiple “no questions” responses under intended conditions of use. [S3][S6][S7][S8]
- **CFR citation:**
  - **21 CFR 170.30** (eligibility for GRAS determination)
  - **21 CFR 170.203** (GRAS notice definitions)
  - No steviol-glycoside-specific listing was found in Title 21 eCFR text search for “steviol”/“rebaudioside.” [S4][S5]
- **Conditions of use (examples from FDA GRAS inventory):**
  - General-purpose/table-top sweetener use, typically excluding infant formula and often excluding meat/poultry unless explicitly addressed. [S6][S7][S8]
  - Example GRN 790 includes cured meat use up to 2,500 ppm. [S7]
- **Related U.S. restriction:** whole-leaf and crude stevia extracts are not permitted as sweeteners (Import Alert pathway); this is distinct from high-purity steviol glycosides. [S3]

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **0-4 mg/kg bw** (expressed as steviol). [S9][S10]
- **Last evaluation year listed:** **2023** (meeting 96 entry on the WHO JECFA chemical page). [S10]
- **Key notes from evaluation history:** JECFA entries repeatedly confirm ADI continuity and report no new safety issues for qualifying specifications/methods; earlier history documents high-consumer young-child scenarios near/exceeding ADI in conservative modeling. [S10]

### IARC
- **Classification:** no steviol/stevioside/rebaudioside entry located in IARC monographs classification dataset (Volumes 1-140 list feed). [S11][S12][S13]

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified for high-purity steviol glycosides used within regulatory specifications.
- U.S. restriction applies to crude/whole-leaf stevia extracts rather than purified steviol glycosides. [S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology backbone:**
  - JECFA history documents ADI derivation around a long-term animal NOEL and maintenance of 0-4 mg/kg bw ADI (as steviol equivalents). [S10]
  - EFSA 2024 did not support ADI increase; EFSA 2026 found no new concern for E960c process update. [S1][S2]
- **Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity evidence (recent synthesis):**
  - A 2025 review of nonsugar sweeteners reports no consistent genotoxic/carcinogenic signal for steviol glycosides within the broader NSS evidence base. [S14]
- **Mechanistic/gut-related evidence (recent studies):**
  - 2025 in vitro work found barrier-disruptive effects for miraculin/mogroside V but **not stevia** under tested conditions. [S15]
- **Human clinical signal examples (recent):**
  - A 2025 randomized crossover trial using sucrose/stevia beverage conditions reported differential hormonal responses versus sucrose-only drink, without a clear acute harm signal in healthy adults. [S19]

## Exposure Assessment
- **ADI benchmark:** 4 mg/kg bw/day (steviol equivalents) in EFSA/JECFA frameworks. [S1][S9][S10]
- **EU conservative scenarios:** EFSA 2024 projected possible 95th percentile exceedance in toddlers under proposed expanded uses/MPL increases. [S1]
- **JECFA historical high-consumer scenario:** young children at high intake were estimated around 4.0-4.4 mg/kg bw/day (up to ~110% of ADI in that modeled context). [S10]
- **Practical intake framing from FDA communication:** FDA public table expresses steviol-glycoside ADI equivalence using rebaudioside A packet examples (for consumer context, not a direct risk finding). [S3]
- **Vulnerable populations:** toddlers/high-consuming children are the primary group where ADI exceedance can occur in conservative modeled scenarios. [S1][S10]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: broad regulatory acceptance in EU/U.S./JECFA frameworks; no IARC listing found; multiple evaluations report no major new hazard signal under approved specifications. [S1][S2][S3][S10][S11][S13]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: ADI is finite (not “ADI not specified”), and conservative exposure models show possible exceedance in young children/toddlers. [S1][S10]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: approved/GRAS pathways in major jurisdictions; longstanding ADI; no major carcinogenic/genotoxic confirmation signal in recent review-level evidence. [S1][S3][S4][S6][S7][S8][S14]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: specific modeled child subgroups can exceed ADI, which introduces meaningful caveat beyond “minor concerns only at high doses.” [S1][S10]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: regulatory acceptance with clear caveat that ADI can be exceeded in high-end child exposures in conservative scenarios (EFSA and JECFA history); EFSA declined ADI increase in 2024 due insufficient ADME justification. [S1][S10]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: no strong evidence of serious toxicity at approved use patterns; recent EFSA process-modification opinion found no safety concern. [S2]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: none robustly identified (no major-jurisdiction ban of high-purity steviol glycosides; no IARC Group 2A/2B steviol-specific classification located).
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: extensive regulatory acceptance and repeated ADI confirmations; safety conclusions remain generally favorable when intake stays within ADI/specifications. [S1][S2][S3][S10]

### 2. Rationale
Steviol glycosides are strongly supported by major regulatory bodies, but not in a way that justifies a “risk_free” classification because the ADI is explicit and exceedance risk is documented for some high-intake child scenarios in conservative modeling. The best fit is a middle category that recognizes both broad approval and the child-exposure caveat.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
