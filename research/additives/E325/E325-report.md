# E325 (Sodium lactate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E325
- **Name:** Sodium lactate
- **CAS number(s):** 72-17-3 (CAS Reg. No. in FDA CFR and OpenEFSA substance record) [S3, S4]
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salt of lactic acid (organic acid salt; lactate) [S3, S7]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium 2-hydroxypropanoate; Sodium lactate solution; E 325 [S3, S4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** in food use (commercially prepared by neutralizing lactic acid with sodium hydroxide; lactic acid is typically fermentation-derived) [S7]

## Function in Food
- **Main technological roles:** humectant, pH control agent, flavor enhancer, flavoring agent/adjuvant, emulsifier (US CFR functional categories) [S7]
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - As a lactate salt, it supports pH control and water activity management in processed foods.
  - In food systems, sodium lactate is widely used as an antimicrobial hurdle (especially for refrigerated/RTE meats), often reducing growth/survival of *Listeria* and spoilage flora in matrix-specific studies [S11, S12].
- **Common food categories:** processed meat/poultry and other moisture-managed processed foods; broader direct-food use under GMP in US CFR (except infant foods/formula) [S7].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation record found:** EFSA question **EFSA-Q-2011-00600** (“Re-evaluation of E325 Sodium lactate”).
- **Current phase/status:** **Ongoing Risk Assessment**; no final output number posted yet [S1].
- **Approval status signal:** EFSA record comment states this additive is “already authorised for use in the EU” and under delayed re-evaluation workload [S1].
- **ADI (EFSA):** No EFSA ADI value published in this ongoing E325 re-evaluation record (no final output yet) [S1, S2].
- **Evaluation timing:** dossier received 28-04-2011; dossier valid 12-05-2011; record updated 19-01-2026; expected completion date shown as 31-12-2027 [S1, S2].
- **Key EFSA conclusion currently available:** re-evaluation is pending; a new data call is planned and a joint output is foreseen with related lactates/lactic acid questions [S1].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** **GRAS** as a direct human food ingredient in **21 CFR 184.1768** [S7].
- **Conditions of use:**
  - No limitation other than current good manufacturing practice (cGMP).
  - **Not authorized in infant foods and infant formulas**.
  - Functional uses include emulsifier, flavor enhancer, flavoring agent/adjuvant, humectant, and pH control agent [S7].

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA ADI:** **Not limited** (database entry and toxicological monograph context) [S8, S9].
- **Last evaluation year (database):** **1974** [S8].
- **Monograph context:** WHO Food Additives Series 5 concludes no numerical ADI limit needed, with infant caution for D(-)/DL-lactic acid use [S9].

### IARC
- **Classification:** No explicit sodium lactate listing identified on IARC “Agents Classified by the IARC” page during this search; no IARC group assignment found for sodium lactate [S10].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction blanket ban identified in the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and human metabolism/toxicology (legacy core evidence)
- JECFA monograph (WHO Food Additives Series 5) reports lactate as a normal metabolic intermediate and includes legacy animal/human metabolism and tolerance observations; it states no numerical ADI limit needed for the evaluated lactate group context [S9].
- Legacy short-term data in that monograph include rat dosing with sodium lactate (1000 and 2000 mg/kg bw/day, 14–16 days) without reported tissue accumulation in that study summary [S9].

### Infant-specific caution
- The same JECFA monograph flags concern for infant handling of D(-)/DL-lactic acid and states these forms should not be used in infant foods; FDA CFR independently disallows sodium lactate use in infant foods/formula [S7, S9].

### Recent PubMed evidence (food-system safety context)
- Recent studies are mostly food-microbiology/application focused rather than direct chronic human toxicology for E325 intake:
  - 2024 *Food Microbiology*: preservative exposure including sodium lactate changed *Salmonella* adaptation patterns, but authors reported no antibiotic cross-resistance selection [S11].
  - 2025 *Journal of Food Protection*: sodium lactate/diacetate film improved *Listeria* control in deli meat storage [S12].
  - 2022 *PLOS ONE*: sodium lactate-containing dips reduced *Listeria* growth potential in refrigerated frankfurters [S13].
- No strong recent epidemiological signal specifically linking dietary sodium lactate (E325) intake to chronic human adverse outcomes was identified in the searched PubMed set [S14].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Quantitative intake data:** A current EFSA finalized exposure estimate specific to E325 was not located because EFSA re-evaluation is still ongoing [S1, S2].
- **ADI exceedance framing:** JECFA’s ADI is “not limited,” so classical ADI exceedance framing is less informative than use-pattern and population-context assessment [S8, S9].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - **Infants**: explicit infant-use restrictions in US CFR and historical infant D-/DL-lactate handling concerns in JECFA monograph context [S7, S9].
  - **Sodium-restricted consumers**: practical dietary caution is reasonable for high-intake processed-food patterns (sodium contribution is formulation-dependent).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Endogenous lactate metabolism is well-established.
  - JECFA ADI is “not limited.”
  - Long regulatory history in major jurisdictions [S7, S8, S9].
- **Evidence against:**
  - EFSA E325 re-evaluation remains unfinished.
  - Infant-related cautions/restrictions exist [S1, S7, S9].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Approved/GRAS status with cGMP conditions and explicit regulatory boundaries.
  - JECFA “not limited” ADI and absence of strong modern human harm signal.
  - Recent food-microbiology work does not show antibiotic cross-resistance emergence from sodium-lactate preservative exposure [S7, S8, S9, S11].
- **Evidence against:**
  - EFSA final re-evaluation output still pending; this leaves uncertainty around updated EU exposure modeling [S1, S2].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Pending EFSA reassessment and historical infant metabolic caveats justify some caution for specific subpopulations [S1, S7, S9].
- **Evidence against:**
  - No current regulatory downgrades, no ban trend, no robust carcinogenic/genotoxic signal identified in reviewed sources.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - None identified in reviewed regulatory and PubMed sources.
- **Evidence against:**
  - No IARC carcinogen group assignment found for sodium lactate; no major-jurisdiction ban located; JECFA ADI remains “not limited” [S8, S10].

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence is consistent with **low concern under authorized uses**, with key caveats centered on infants and the fact that EFSA’s modernized re-evaluation is still in progress. Historical toxicology and metabolism evidence plus longstanding regulatory acceptance support a low-risk baseline, while pending EFSA completion prevents a stronger “risk_free” conclusion.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
