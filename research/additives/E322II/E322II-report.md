# E322II (Partially hydrolysed lecithin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E322(ii) (partially hydrolysed lecithin; EFSA discusses hydrolysed lecithins under E322). Source: EFSA 2017 re-evaluation abstract (PubMed record for EFSA Journal article) and keywords listing hydrolysed lecithins under E322. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed
- **CAS number(s):** 85711-58-6 (hydrolysed lecithins, EFSA keywords); 8002-43-5 (general lecithin and JECFA entry for partially hydrolyzed lecithin). URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed ; https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1478
- **Chemical class:** Complex phospholipid/phosphatide mixture (phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylinositol and related phospholipids). Sources: 21 CFR 184.1400 and JECFA 1478 entry. URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml ; https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1478
- **Common synonyms:** Phosphatides; phospholipids; lecithins; hydrolysed lecithins. URLs: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1478 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Best classified as **semi-synthetic** for E322(ii): base lecithin is naturally sourced (e.g., soy/corn/safflower oils), then partially hydrolysed/modified. URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic phospholipids reduce interfacial tension and act as emulsifiers/synergists; hydrolysis products also include choline-containing compounds. URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010002/
- **Regulatory functional class:** Emulsifier (CFR/JECFA), antioxidant synergist/synergist (JECFA classes). URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml ; https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1478
- **Common food categories:** General foods (GMP-limited in US), infant formula and foods for special medical purposes in EU risk assessments (FC 13.1.1 and 13.1.5.1). URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33204307/?format=pubmed

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised in EU food additive framework for E322 (including hydrolysed lecithins).
- **Latest evaluations found:**
  - **2017** re-evaluation (EFSA Journal 15(4):e04742; DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4742).
  - **2020** follow-up for infants below 16 weeks and unresolved points (EFSA Journal 18(11):e06266; DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2020.6266).
- **ADI:** EFSA 2017 concluded there was **no need for a numerical ADI** for lecithins (E322).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No safety concern for general population (>1 year) at refined exposure estimates.
  - No safety concern for infants (12 weeks to 11 months) and for infants/young children consuming FSMP under assessed uses.
  - 2020 follow-up: use in infant formula/FSMP for infants below 16 weeks did not raise safety concerns up to MPL.
- URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33204307/?format=pubmed ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010002/

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA 2017):** no concern with respect to genotoxicity; no numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at refined exposure estimates for evaluated groups. URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625454/?format=pubmed ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010002/
- **Infant-focused follow-up (EFSA 2020):** addressed potential neurodevelopment concern indirectly via choline exposure context; concluded no safety concerns up to MPL for infants below 16 weeks in FC 13.1.1/13.1.5.1. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33204307/?format=pubmed
- **Allergenicity signal (EFSA discussion + PubMed):** residual soy/egg proteins may trigger reactions in susceptible individuals; EFSA notes residual proteins should be reduced as much as possible. One pediatric soy-lecithin case report exists, but controlled pediatric challenge data (non-IgE soy allergy) showed low reaction rate at 1.5 g/day with no clear excess versus placebo. URLs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010002/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31603281/?format=pubmed ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35023195/?format=pubmed ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10024228/?format=pubmed
- **Recent broader literature:** a 2024 cardiovascular review reports mixed literature and uncertainty regarding potential adverse effects (sporadic GI symptoms noted), without establishing a new regulatory hazard signal for food-additive lecithin. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39001966/?format=pubmed
- **Epidemiological data:** no strong population-level epidemiologic harm signal specific to E322(ii) identified in the reviewed regulatory and PubMed sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2017 refined scenarios (E322 overall):**
  - Brand-loyal scenario mean: 7 to 82 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile: 15 to 187 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Non-brand-loyal scenario mean: 3 to 22 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile: 6 to 62 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Highest relevant refined estimate highlighted: toddlers up to 175 mg/kg bw/day (95th percentile, brand loyal).
  - Infants (12 weeks to 11 months) up to 163 mg/kg bw/day (95th percentile, brand loyal).
  - With FSMP scenario, highest reported was 232 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers (95th percentile, brand loyal).
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA set no numerical ADI for E322, so standard "ADI exceedance" framing is not directly applicable.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants using formula/FSMP (handled explicitly in EFSA 2020, no safety concern up to MPL).
  - Soy/egg-allergic individuals due to residual proteins in lecithin ingredients.
- URLs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010002/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33204307/?format=pubmed

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA concluded no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at assessed uses; lecithin is a naturally derived phospholipid class and hydrolyses to normal dietary components including choline.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: documented residual-allergen issue (soy/egg proteins) and isolated clinical reaction reports; specific partially hydrolyzed JECFA entry does not itself display a clear ADI statement.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EU authorised with 2017/2020 positive conclusions; US Part 184 GRAS framework with GMP conditions; JECFA parent lecithin ADI not limited; no IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: some uncertainty remains around highly sensitive allergic subgroups and around direct extrapolation from lecithin-group data to all modified subtypes.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: plausible concern in susceptible allergic individuals; EFSA 2020 noted prior neurodevelopment-related concern required follow-up in very young infants.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: EFSA follow-up resolved infant concern at MPL; no consistent serious toxicological signal from regulators.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: none found in reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC records.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: no major bans identified, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B assignment found for lecithin, and major regulators continue to permit use.

### 2. Rationale
Key drivers are the consistency of regulatory conclusions (EFSA 2017 + 2020; CFR Part 184 GRAS framework; JECFA lecithin ADI not limited) and absence of major carcinogenic/banning signals. The main non-trivial caveat is allergenicity from residual proteins in sensitive individuals.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
