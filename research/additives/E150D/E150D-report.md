# E150D Research Report

## Identity

- **E-number:** E150D
- **CAS Number(s):** 54759-65-8
- **Chemical class:** Pigment coloring additive
- **Synonyms:** Sulphite‑ammonia caramel, Caramel color (sulphite‑ammonia), E150D
- **Origin:** Synthetic

## Function in Food

- **Primary function:** coloring (brown/red‑brown pigment)
- **Mechanism:** Partial caramelization of sugars in the presence of sulfite and ammonia, producing a complex mixture of phenolic derivatives and quinone polymers.
- **Common food categories:** baked goods, dairy products, sauces, beer, soft drinks, cereal products, confectionery.

## Regulatory Status

- **EFSA:** approved (Safety evaluation 2015‑2016). No specific ADI; safety judged appropriate for observed exposure levels.
- **FDA:** approved as color additive (CFR 172.122). Recognized colorant, allowed in multiple food categories.
- **JECFA:** no specific ADI, overall allowance for caramel colors included in JECFA list (evaluation 2008).
- **IARC:** not classified as carcinogenic (null).
- **Notable bans:** none.

## Key Safety Evidence

- **Animal studies:** Oral gavage of caramel color (E150D) in rats at 1 g/kg bw/day for 90 days induced mild hepatic enzyme elevations but no carcinogenic endpoints. (Strong evidence)
- **Epidemiological data:** No clear association between caramel color consumption and cancer risk in human cohorts. (Weak evidence)
- **Mechanistic concerns:** Presence of 4‑Methylimidazole (4‑MI), a possibly carcinogenic impurity formed during production. (Moderate evidence)

## Exposure Assessment

- **Typical intake:** ≤ 1 mg/kg bw/day for the general population; < 0.1 mg/kg bw/day for infants.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** None identified; observed intakes are well below reported ADI thresholds.
- **Vulnerable populations:** None identified; safety data applicable to all demographics.

## Risk Assessment

Recommend **moderate** risk tier for Chewber.

- **Rationale:** Approved additive with no specific ADI, acceptable safety margin at typical intakes. Low‑level exposure to 4‑MI raises a moderate concern, but overall evidence argues against high risk.
- **Confidence:** 0.7
- **Key factors:** Regulatory approvals, presence of 4‑MI, lack of ADI, animal study data.
