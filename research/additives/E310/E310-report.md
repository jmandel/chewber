# E310 (Propyl gallate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E310 (INS 310). [S1][S5]
- **Name:** Propyl gallate (propyl 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate). [S2][S5]
- **CAS number:** `121-79-9`. [S2][S5]
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic phenolic antioxidant (alkyl gallate ester). [S2][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Propyl ester of gallic acid; n-propyl ester of 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoic acid; propyl 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate. [S2][S5]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** U.S. CFR text states natural occurrence has not been reported and that commercial material is prepared by esterification of gallic acid with propyl alcohol. [S2]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Antioxidant in foods (limits oxidative deterioration in fat/oil-containing products). [S2][S3]
- **Regulatory function statement:** 21 CFR 184.1660(c) identifies use as an antioxidant. [S2]
- **Common food categories (EFSA exposure categories):** fats/oils and fat-containing categories including fine bakery wares, processed nuts, soups/broths, sauces, chewing gum, and food supplements. [S1]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Opinion identified:** EFSA ANS Panel re-evaluation of propyl gallate (E310), published **2014**. [S1]
  - **ADI:** **0.5 mg/kg bw/day**, based on a NOAEL of 50 mg/kg bw/day from a two-generation reproductive toxicity study in rats (UF 100). [S1]
  - **Context:** This replaced an earlier temporary ADI of 1.4 mg/kg bw/day. [S1]
  - **Key conclusion:** EFSA reported potential ADI exceedance in conservative exposure scenarios for some population groups, but concluded current reported uses/use levels were not a safety concern. [S1]
  - **Practical access note:** direct EFSA->Wiley full text was blocked in this environment; extraction relied on EFSA publication metadata/search record plus EFSA publication page metadata. [S1][S4]
- **FDA/eCFR (US):**

  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1660 (Propyl gallate)**. [S2]
  - **Conditions of use:** as antioxidant under GMP; maximum total antioxidant content **0.02% of fat or oil content** (including essential oils). [S2]
  - **Related feed citation:** 21 CFR 582.3660 includes the same 0.02% tolerance framework for food/feed contexts under GMP/feeding practice. [S2]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology anchor (EFSA 2014):** ADI set at 0.5 mg/kg bw/day from reproductive study NOAEL; panel concluded no safety concern at reported use levels despite conservative-scenario exceedance signals in some groups. [S1]
- **Recent animal/mechanistic signal (2024, Food Chem Toxicol, PMID 38325633):** mouse 2-cell embryo study reported impaired development with oxidative stress, DNA damage, mitochondrial/lysosomal dysfunction, autophagy, and epigenetic changes under experimental exposure. [S10]
- **Recent translational toxicology signal (2025, ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci, PMID 40370985):** nephrotoxicity observed in beagles with enteric-coated PG tablet context; study also found route/formulation dependence (no renal toxicity signal in a non-enteric capsule follow-up in dogs). [S11]
- **Recent review (2024, Food Sci Nutr, PMID 39479655):** summarizes cytotoxic/genotoxic concerns for synthetic phenolic antioxidants including PG, largely at high-dose/in vitro or non-dietary settings; useful as signal synthesis but lower evidentiary weight than targeted dose-response risk assessment. [S12]
- **Epidemiology:** no robust human population-level causal evidence specific to normal dietary E310 intake was identified in the recent PubMed search performed for this task. [S9]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure framing (2014):** EFSA reported both MPL-based and refined scenarios; conservative scenarios can reach or exceed the ADI in some groups, while panel judgment remained that reported use levels were not a safety concern. [S1]
- **Typical intake interpretation:** dietary risk is generally tied to high consumers of fat/oil-containing processed foods and additive-dense patterns rather than average intake in the general population. [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** possible in higher-exposure subgroups under conservative assumptions; uncertainty remains about real-world brand loyalty and actual additive concentration distributions. [S1]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** children/high consumers in conservative scenarios (per EFSA exposure modeling context), and potentially individuals exposed to non-standard pharmaceutical/formulation uses where kinetics differ from standard food exposure. [S1][S11]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: approved by major regulators (EFSA/FDA/JECFA); no IARC carcinogen classification found. [S1][S2][S5][S6]
  - Evidence arguing against: EFSA set a numerical ADI and identified potential exceedance scenarios; recent animal/mechanistic studies report toxicity signals at experimental exposures. [S1][S10][S11]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: CFR affirms GRAS with explicit GMP limit (0.02% of fat/oil), JECFA ADI exists, EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported uses. [S1][S2][S5]
  - Evidence arguing against: EFSA lowered ADI vs older temporary value and did not frame the additive as risk-free; newer studies raise mechanistic concerns under certain conditions. [S1][S10][S11]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: approved but with clear caveats (numerical ADI, modeled exceedance risk in some groups, and credible animal/mechanistic toxicity literature). [S1][S10][S11]
  - Evidence arguing against: no major ban, no IARC classification, and core regulators continue to allow use under limits. [S2][S5][S6]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: recent nephrotoxicity and embryotoxicity signals at non-standard/high experimental exposures could indicate hazard potential in specific contexts. [S10][S11]
  - Evidence arguing against: no major jurisdictional ban, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification found, and no EFSA conclusion that current food uses are unsafe. [S1][S2][S6]

### 2. Rationale
The total evidence fits an **approved-but-caveated** profile: strong regulatory acceptance under limits, but with an established ADI and exposure/exceedance considerations plus recent mechanistic toxicology signals that prevent a risk-free interpretation.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
