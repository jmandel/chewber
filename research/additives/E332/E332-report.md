# E332 (Potassium citrates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E332.
- **Primary names:** Potassium citrates; includes **potassium dihydrogen citrate** (INS 332(i)) and **tripotassium citrate** (INS 332(ii)) [S5, S6].
- **CAS numbers found in regulatory records:**
  - 921-53-9 (potassium dihydrogen citrate; INS 332(i), JECFA) [S5]
  - 866-84-2 (tripotassium citrate anhydrous; CFR/JECFA) [S4, S6]
  - 6100-05-6 (tripotassium citrate monohydrate; CFR) [S4]
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salts of citric acid (citrate salts; buffering/chelating salts) [S4, S5, S6].
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium citrate, tripotassium citrate, potassium dihydrogen citrate, INS 332(i), INS 332(ii) [S5, S6].
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Industrially manufactured by neutralizing citric acid with potassium hydroxide or potassium carbonate (food additive manufacturing route), i.e., **semi-synthetic/processed** from naturally occurring citric acid feedstock [S4].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Buffers acidity (pH control),
  - Chelates/sequesters metal ions,
  - Supports stabilization/emulsification in formulations [S4].
- **Common food-use roles:** Sequestrant, pH control agent, stabilizer/emulsifier in foods under GMP [S4].
- **Common food categories (practical):** Broad use where acidity buffering and sequestration are needed; US CFR listing is not category-limited and allows use in food under cGMP [S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA / EU
- **Status:** Authorized in the EU food additive database entry for E332 (Potassium citrates), with **ADI: Not specified** [S1].
- **Evaluation trail shown in the EU entry:** SCF (1990), SCF (2003 opinion on citric acid and salts), ANS (2014 re-evaluation citation), and a 2018 refined exposure report citation [S1].
- **Key conclusion (from accessible EU database data):** non-numerical ADI (**not specified**) is retained in the E332 entry [S1].
- **Access note:** direct EFSA publication URLs linked from the EU entry redirected to Wiley and were not fully accessible from this environment (rate-limit/anti-bot), so conclusions were taken from the official EU database entry and EFSA citation metadata shown there [S1, S2, S3].

### FDA / eCFR
- **Status:** **GRAS affirmed** as a direct human food ingredient in **21 CFR 184.1625** [S4].
- **Conditions of use:** Use in food with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice; functions include sequestrant, pH control agent, stabilizer, and emulsifier [S4].

### JECFA / WHO
- **INS 332(i) Potassium dihydrogen citrate:** ADI **“Not limited”**, publication year **1979** [S5].
- **INS 332(ii) Potassium citrate:** ADI **“Not limited”**, publication year **1973** [S6].

### IARC
- No explicit IARC monograph classification entry found for potassium citrate/potassium citrates in the current classification dataset; no Group 1/2A/2B assignment identified for this additive name [S7, S8].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Human mechanistic RCT (2026, CKD + healthy):** 40 mmol oral potassium citrate (and potassium chloride comparator) increased plasma potassium more in CKD; authors report increased hyperkalaemia risk context in CKD [S9].
- **Human RCT (2023, healthy):** acute potassium citrate 40 mmol produced plasma potassium rise similar to potassium chloride, with higher kaliuresis/intracellular uptake metrics for citrate; no acute disproportionate plasma potassium spike versus chloride in healthy participants [S10].
- **Observational cohort (2019/2020 publication context, stone formers):** potassium citrate improved urinary anti-lithogenic profile; no unfavorable short-term changes in fasting glucose, HbA1c, cholesterol, BMI reported [S11].
- **Carcinogenicity context:** no direct IARC classification entry found for potassium citrate/potassium citrates [S7, S8].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Numerical ADI framework:** EFSA/EU entry uses **ADI not specified**; JECFA uses **ADI not limited** [S1, S5, S6].
- **Typical intake data:** a numeric pan-population dietary intake value for E332 was not extractable from accessible pages in this run.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** classical ADI exceedance is not directly applicable due non-numerical ADI; practical risk focus shifts to **potassium load susceptibility** (e.g., CKD, RAAS-inhibitor users) at high supplemental/pharmacologic intakes [S9, S10].
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** chronic kidney disease, people with impaired potassium excretion, and users of medications that raise hyperkalemia risk [S9, S10].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports:** EU/FDA/JECFA allow use; EFSA/EU ADI not specified and JECFA ADI not limited; citrate is a common endogenous metabolite and potassium citrate is a simple salt [S1, S4, S5, S6].
  - **Against:** controlled clinical evidence shows higher plasma potassium/hyperkalemia susceptibility in CKD under oral potassium loading, so harm is plausible in vulnerable groups at higher intakes [S9].
- **limited**
  - **Supports:** Strong regulatory acceptance (EU authorized, FDA GRAS, JECFA ADI not limited), with concerns concentrated in high-dose/vulnerable settings rather than general dietary exposure [S1, S4, S5, S6, S9].
  - **Against:** limited additive-specific, modern long-term epidemiology directly isolating E332 exposure in general populations.
- **moderate**
  - **Supports:** credible renal-risk caveat (hyperkalemia susceptibility) in CKD from randomized data under oral potassium loads [S9].
  - **Against:** no evidence here of widespread regulatory restriction, ADI tightening, or population-level harm at normal food-additive exposure [S1, S4, S5, S6].
- **high**
  - **Supports:** none found.
  - **Against:** no major bans identified; no IARC carcinogenic group assignment found for potassium citrate; broad approval status remains [S1, S4, S7, S8].

### 2. Rationale
The additive has broad regulatory acceptance with non-numerical ADI designations (EFSA/EU “not specified”, JECFA “not limited”), which supports low concern at typical use. The main credible concern is **renal/potassium-handling risk** (hyperkalemia susceptibility) in vulnerable groups at higher oral potassium loads.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
