# E231 (Orthophenyl phenol) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E231
- **Primary name:** Orthophenyl phenol (also written o-phenylphenol; 2-phenylphenol)
- **CAS number(s):** 90-43-7 (E231). Related salt in the same regulatory family: sodium o-phenylphenate, CAS 132-27-4 (E232).
- **Chemical class:** Phenolic aromatic compound (hydroxybiphenyl; biphenyl-2-ol).
- **Common synonyms:** o-Phenylphenol, 2-phenylphenol, Biphenyl-2-ol, (1,1'-Biphenyl)-2-ol, 2-Hydroxydiphenyl, OPP.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **synthetic** in food/agricultural use.

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Preservative / post-harvest antimicrobial (fungistatic) for treated produce surfaces.
- **Mechanism of action (regulatory/toxicology context):** Phenolic antimicrobial action against microbial contamination on treated surfaces; in residue evaluation, EFSA/JMPR focus on parent compound and metabolite handling in risk assessment.
- **Common food categories in sources reviewed:** Citrus and pears in EFSA pesticide-residue documents; food-contact sanitizing contexts in US CFR.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and regulatory toxicology
- EFSA peer-review evidence files contain discussion of urinary-bladder effects/tumor findings in high-dose rodent studies, and this toxicology debate is reflected in ADI-setting discussions.
- EFSA PRAPeR record text in accessible evidence files indicates final meeting outcome in that workflow: ADI adjusted to 0.4 mg/kg bw/day; ARfD not set.

### Epidemiology / human population signal
- A recent population-based study (NHANES-linked analysis) reported associations between urinary OPP (with other chlorophenols) and diabetes/prediabetes outcomes and mortality endpoints; this is associative, not causal proof.

### Mechanistic / developmental studies
- Recent zebrafish studies (2023, 2026) reported developmental and cardiac toxicity signals (ROS/oxidative stress, migration/cardiac-function pathways) at experimental exposures.
- These studies are biologically relevant for hazard identification but are not direct human dietary-dose confirmation.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA dossier text (peer-review addendum evidence file) reports TMDI calculations with a **maximum ~10 µg/kg bw/day**, around **5% of ADI**, and states no ARfD/ADI exceedance in those modeled commodity scenarios.
- In the same materials, short-term and long-term intake conclusions were described as not indicating public-health concern for the assessed scenarios.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Children generally show higher relative intake in many residue models; in the recovered EFSA text, modeled percentages were still below toxicological thresholds in the scenarios shown.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA “no safety concern at current levels of intake” statement; modeled intakes in EFSA documentation below ADI.
  - Evidence against: Not an endogenous nutrient; animal high-dose toxicology concerns exist; metabolite uncertainty was explicitly flagged in EFSA MRL workflow.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Clear ADI values from EFSA/JECFA frameworks; modeled dietary exposures in accessible EFSA material below thresholds; no IARC carcinogenic classification for E231 itself (Group 3).
  - Evidence against: Toxicology caveats and data-gap language in EFSA MRL workflow; newer mechanistic/animal model studies continue to report adverse effects at experimental doses.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Credible animal toxicology signal history (urinary-bladder findings at high dose), EFSA caveats on metabolite toxicology, and emerging mechanistic/ecotoxicology studies.
  - Evidence against: Available regulatory intake modeling in retrieved EFSA/JECFA materials does not show threshold exceedance for assessed uses.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Related compound (E232 sodium salt) has IARC Group 2B.
  - Evidence against: E231 itself is IARC Group 3; no direct evidence from retrieved primary regulatory sources of a broad current ban based on confirmed human dietary harm.

### 2. Rationale
- The core regulatory picture is “allowed/restricted use with ADI-based management,” not a clear prohibition signal for E231 itself.
- However, uncertainty remains around certain metabolite toxicology points in EFSA residue workflow and newer mechanistic data add caution.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
