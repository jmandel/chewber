# E954 (Saccharin) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E954 (saccharins: saccharin and its Na/K/Ca salts; EFSA 2024 re-evaluation scope covers E954 forms) [S1]
- **Name:** Saccharin (and salts used as food additives) [S1][S7]
- **CAS number(s):** 81-07-2 (saccharin, JECFA chemical entry) [S7]
- **Chemical class:** Benzoisothiazolone dioxide high-intensity sweetener (non-nutritive sweetener) [S1][S7]
- **Common synonyms:** 1,2-benzisothiazol-3(2H)-one-1,1-dioxide; 3-oxo-2,3-dihydrobenzo[d]isothiazol-1,1-dioxide; benzoic sulfimide (common) [S1][S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic (EFSA: "chemically manufactured compound") [S1]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** High-intensity non-nutritive sweetener used at low concentrations to replace nutritive sweeteners while preserving sweetness [S4][S7]
- **Common food categories (US CFR conditions):**
  - Beverages/fruit drinks/bases or mixes (up to 12 mg as saccharin per fluid ounce prepared for consumption)
  - Table/cooking sugar substitutes (up to 20 mg per teaspoon sugar equivalency)
  - Processed foods (up to 30 mg per serving)
  - Additional technological uses: chewable vitamin/mineral tablets, chewing gum, flavor chips for nonstandardized bakery products [S4]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion found:** EFSA FAF Panel re-evaluation published in 2024 (EFSA Journal e9044). [S1]
- **Approval status:** Approved (with updated ADI/specification recommendations). [S1]
- **ADI:** **9 mg/kg bw/day** (expressed as free imide). [S1]
- **Evaluation year:** 2024. [S1]
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern identified for saccharins produced by the Remsen-Fahlberg process.
  - Potential genotoxicity concern identified for potential impurities associated with Maumee-process manufacturing.
  - Refined brand-loyal chronic P95 exposure estimates were below the ADI in the re-evaluation opinion.
  - EFSA recommended revising EU specifications. [S1]
- **EFSA exposure-support report (2024):** mean exposure below ADI in all groups; at P95, adolescents and toddlers could exceed ADI in scenarios including food supplements and at MPLs (up to ~1.2-fold and ~2-fold, respectively). [S2]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not GRAS-listed in Title 21 Part 182 or Part 184 searches for `saccharin`; instead regulated as a food additive under **21 CFR 180.37**. [S5][S6][S4]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 180.37** ("Saccharin, ammonium saccharin, calcium saccharin, and sodium saccharin"). [S4]
- **Conditions of use (key points):**
  - May be safely used as sweetening agents under specified conditions.
  - Use tied to valid special dietary substitution for nutritive sweeteners or authorized technological purpose other than calorie reduction.
  - Quantitative limits: 12 mg/fl oz beverages; 20 mg per teaspoon sugar equivalency for table/cooking products; 30 mg per serving in processed foods.
  - Labeling and concentration declaration requirements are specified. [S4]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-5 mg/kg bw** (group ADI for saccharin and calcium/potassium/sodium salts). [S7]
- **Last evaluation year:** **1993** (JECFA Meeting 41 in database entry). [S7]

### IARC
- **Classification:** **Group 3** ("Saccharin and its salts"). [S9]
- **Evaluation metadata in IARC dataset:** listed with year 1999 and evaluation year 1998. [S9]

### Notable bans
- No major current jurisdiction-wide ban identified in reviewed EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC sources. [S1][S4][S7][S9]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2024 re-evaluation: new data did not raise genotoxicity concern for the additive (Remsen-Fahlberg route), but indicated concern for potential Maumee-route impurities. [S1]
- Historical animal bladder-tumor findings in rats remain part of saccharin's legacy evidence base; modern reviews argue the rat mode of action is not human-relevant, but these reviews include industry-linked authorship and should be interpreted cautiously. [S12][S13]

### Human and epidemiological evidence
- A 2022 randomized controlled trial in 120 healthy adults gave saccharin/sucralose/aspartame/stevia for 2 weeks (doses below ADI); saccharin and sucralose groups showed significant glycemic-response impairment and microbiome shifts. [S10]
- A 2026 review concluded evidence for metabolic benefit/harm of non-nutritive sweeteners remains mixed; it specifically notes saccharin- and sucralose-associated microbiome effects and calls for longer-term RCTs. [S11]
- 2025 reviews of carcinogenicity/cancer epidemiology reported no consistent association between nonsugar sweeteners (including saccharin) and cancer risk, but both papers disclose industry-related conflicts of interest. [S12][S13]

### Mechanistic concerns currently under discussion
- Gut microbiome-mediated and person-specific glycemic effects are biologically plausible and supported by short-term human interventional evidence, but long-term clinical significance remains unresolved. [S10][S11]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark:** EFSA ADI 9 mg/kg bw/day; JECFA ADI 0-5 mg/kg bw. [S1][S7]
- **Typical/refined intake (EFSA re-evaluation scenario):** P95 refined brand-loyal estimates below ADI. [S1]
- **Conservative/high-end scenario:** EFSA supporting exposure report found possible P95 exceedance in adolescents/toddlers under MPL and supplements scenarios (up to about 1.2x and 2x ADI). [S2]
- **Vulnerable populations:** toddlers/children with high intake of multiple sweetener-containing products; heavy users of tabletop sweeteners and supplement-containing dietary patterns. [S2][S4]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:** IARC Group 3; EFSA/JECFA retain ADI-based approvals; EFSA found no genotoxic concern for evaluated additive stream. [S1][S7][S9]
- **Evidence against:** Numerical ADIs still required; EFSA reported scenario-based ADI exceedance in some high-exposure young groups; human short-term glycemic/microbiome signals exist. [S2][S10][S11]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:** Approved in EU/US/JECFA with explicit ADIs and use limits; no major current bans found. [S1][S4][S7]
- **Evidence against:** EFSA identified impurity-related concern for one manufacturing route and high-percentile exceedance in specific exposure scenarios. [S1][S2]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:** Regulatory caveats exist (manufacturing-impurity concern), and ADI exceedance is possible in toddlers/adolescents in conservative scenarios; human RCT evidence suggests potential metabolic effects at doses below ADI in the short term. [S1][S2][S10]
- **Evidence against:** Core refined EFSA scenario remained below ADI at P95; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for saccharin; no broad regulatory withdrawal. [S1][S9]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:** None strong from current major regulators.
- **Evidence against:** Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions; IARC classification is Group 3; approvals remain in place with ADI-based controls. [S1][S4][S7][S9]

### 2. Rationale
The additive remains legally approved across EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks, but "no-concern" is not absolute. EFSA's 2024 work raised process-dependent impurity concerns and exposure scenarios where high-percentile young consumers may exceed the ADI, while recent human intervention data suggest possible microbiome/glycemic effects. The evidence does not support "high" risk, but it is stronger than a purely "limited/no caveat" profile.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
