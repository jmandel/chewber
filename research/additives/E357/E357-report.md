# E357 (Potassium adipate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E357.
- **Name:** Potassium adipate.
- **CAS number(s):** 19147-16-1 (listed in PubChem synonym data for potassium adipate / dipotassium adipate). [18][19]
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of adipic acid (an aliphatic dicarboxylate/adipate salt). [18][11]
- **Common synonyms:** Dipotassium adipate; dipotassium hexanedioate; adipic acid, dipotassium salt. [18][19]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** for food use (inferred from adipic-acid industrial production route in CFR and salt chemistry; not identified as a naturally extracted additive source in retrieved regulatory records). [6][7]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Acidity regulator. [11]
- **Mechanism of action:** As an adipate salt, it acts as an acidulant/buffering component in formulations to control pH and sourness profile (mechanistic inference from chemical identity + adipic-acid regulatory functional roles). [6][7][11]
- **Common food categories:** A direct current category list for E357 was not identified in the sources retrieved. Closest explicit U.S. category examples are for **adipic acid** (same adipate family): baked goods, nonalcoholic beverages, condiments/relishes, dairy analogs, fats/oils, frozen dairy desserts, gelatin/puddings, gravies, meat products, snack foods, and others under GMP. [6]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Historical toxicology basis (group):** JECFA notes two-year rat findings (diarrhoea at higher dietary levels; no-effect level 1% diet on older assessment basis) supporting the historical group ADI for adipic acid and salts including potassium adipate. [12]
- **General toxicology context (adipic acid, indirect for E357):** PubMed review (2002) reports low acute oral toxicity, no clear genotoxic signal in tested systems, and no developmental toxicity in tested species. [17]
- **Recent direct E357 literature:** PubMed direct query for `"potassium adipate"[Title/Abstract]` returned **0 results**. [15]
- **Recent related literature pattern:** Broader adipic-acid food-additive safety query returns mixed/indirect literature, with limited direct oral safety studies specific to potassium adipate. [16]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Direct E357 exposure datasets:** Not identified in retrieved sources.
- **Best available group-level signal:** JECFA 2025 adipate-group exposure note states conservative exposure estimates can greatly exceed the current group ADI (0-5 mg/kg bw/day), and potential exceedances may occur with normal daily consumption patterns. [12]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** **Potentially yes** at group level (adipic acid + salts); direct E357-only intake quantification was not found.
- **Vulnerable populations:** JECFA excerpt does not provide an E357-only subgroup breakdown; practical concern is higher consumers in food patterns driving adipate-group exposure. [12]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence for: Longstanding authorization history in EU table; no direct IARC classification for potassium adipate/adipic acid in checked dataset. [4][13][14]
  - Evidence against: Numerical group ADI exists (not “ADI not specified”), and recent JECFA group-level exposure commentary indicates frequent potential exceedance and need for tox re-evaluation. [10][12]
- **limited**
  - Evidence for: Group ADI established and longstanding regulatory familiarity; no major bans found in retrieved EU/U.S. records. [4][6][10]
  - Evidence against: EFSA re-evaluation row for E357 appears incomplete in official state-of-play table; JECFA group-level exposure concerns go beyond minor high-dose-only caveats. [4][12]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence for: Approved/legacy-permitted context with caveats: unresolved EFSA row details, old group ADI basis, and JECFA 2025 indication that current adipate-group exposure can exceed ADI with recommendation for toxicology re-evaluation. [4][10][12]
  - Evidence against: No direct IARC carcinogenic classification; no direct strong new human-harm signal specifically for E357 found. [13][14][15]
- **high**
  - Evidence for: Limited. One concern is Codex-provision withdrawal note (CCFA47), which signals regulatory caution in standards setting. [10]
  - Evidence against: No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for potassium adipate; no broad ban in major jurisdictions identified in retrieved sources. [4][6][13]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest risk driver is **uncertainty plus exposure margin pressure at the adipate-group level**, not confirmed severe intrinsic hazard specific to potassium adipate. E357 remains legacy-permitted in EU state-of-play data, but EFSA re-evaluation fields are incomplete in the retrieved table and JECFA group-level comments indicate potential ADI exceedance and need for updated toxicology.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
