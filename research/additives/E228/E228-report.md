# E228 (Potassium hydrogen sulfite) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E228
- **Primary name:** Potassium hydrogen sulfite
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium bisulfite; Potassium hydrogen sulphite; Potassium acid sulfite
- **CAS number(s):** 7773-03-7 (primary CAS used in eCFR and major databases)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfite salt (hydrogen sulfite/bisulfite)
- **Origin:** Synthetic (manufactured sulfite preservative)

Evidence:
- JECFA chemical record for INS 228 lists the additive as "Potassium hydrogen sulfite" with synonym "Potassium bisulfite".
- eCFR §182.3862 identifies potassium bisulfite with **CAS Reg. No. 7773-03-7**.
- PubChem (CID 23663620) identifies potassium hydrogen sulfite / potassium bisulfite (formula HKO3S).

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Preservative (FDA wording) and antioxidant (sulfite class function).
- **Mechanism of action:** Releases sulfite/bisulfite (SO2-equivalent species) that suppress oxidation and inhibits spoilage reactions; this is the basis for preservative/antioxidant use in sulfited foods.
- **Common food categories (recent EFSA occurrence/exposure work):** wine and wine products, preserved tomatoes, mixed alcoholic beverages, dried vegetables, dried fruit.

Evidence:
- eCFR states potassium bisulfite may be used "as a preservative" under GMP with restrictions.
- EFSA 2025 supporting publication reports highest measured concentrations in the food categories listed above.
- Recent PubMed review describes sulfites as widely used preservatives/antioxidants in foods (e.g., wines, dried fruits, processed products).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in the EU as part of sulfites/sulfur dioxide (E220-E228), but under significant safety concern and restrictive risk-management context.
- **Latest key scientific opinion:** 2022 EFSA follow-up (E220-E228) concluded **no ADI could be established** and MOE analysis indicated safety concerns for high consumers in all groups and for mean consumers in infants/toddlers/children.
- **Earlier EFSA re-evaluation (2016):** Group ADI was **0.7 mg/kg bw/day (as SO2 equivalents)**; EFSA already reported ADI exceedance in several population groups and excluded sulfite-sensitive people from ADI applicability.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **Status:** GRAS-listed substance in Title 21 framework.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3862** (Potassium bisulfite).
- **Conditions of use:** Use as preservative under GMP; not for meats that are a source of vitamin B1; not in foods recognized as a source of vitamin B1; not in fruits/vegetables intended to be served raw or marketed fresh.

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **0-0.7 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as sulfur dioxide, covering sulfur dioxide and related sulfites including potassium hydrogen sulfite).
- **Evaluation timing:** JECFA sulfur dioxide page shows toxicity evaluation entries up to 1998 and intake evaluation in 2008; additive-specific INS 228 record shows earlier safety evaluations (1965, 1976, 1977).

### IARC
- IARC list does **not** show a separate "potassium hydrogen sulfite" entry.
- IARC currently lists **"Sulfites" as Group 3** and **"Sulfur dioxide" as Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity in humans).

### Notable bans
- No complete ban in major jurisdictions was identified in this search set.
- Major regimes instead show authorization with restrictions and exposure-management concerns.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal toxicology
- EFSA 2016 identified increased incidence of **gastric hyperplasia in a 2-year rat study** as the critical effect used for deriving the former group ADI.

### Human data and susceptible groups
- EFSA 2016 explicitly notes the ADI did not cover **sulfite-sensitive individuals**.
- Recent clinical review literature describes sulfite intolerance/hypersensitivity (especially among asthmatic individuals), with reactions ranging from respiratory symptoms to rare severe reactions.

### Mechanistic and newer data
- A 2024 PubMed-indexed study reported sulfites (sodium sulfite and sodium bisulfite) significantly increased DNA damage in human TK6 cells in the presence of mouse fecal supernatants, suggesting **microbiome-modulated genotoxic potential**.
- A 2025 review reports continuing uncertainty but highlights concern for sensitive individuals and ongoing need for improved exposure/safety characterization.

### Carcinogenicity
- IARC Group 3 listings for sulfites/sulfur dioxide do **not** support classification as carcinogenic to humans.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA supporting publication (2025) reports chronic dietary exposure estimates (SO2 equivalents) of approximately:
  - **Adults:** 0.2-2.6 mg/kg bw/day
  - **Infants:** 0.4-5.8 mg/kg bw/day
- Highest concentration contributors differed by subgroup (wine products in adults/adolescents/elderly; preserved tomatoes in toddlers/children; dried vegetables/fruits in infants).
- EFSA 2016 already reported former ADI exceedance at mean/high percentiles for infants/toddlers/children (and high percentiles in adolescents/adults).
- EFSA 2022 follow-up reported MOE-based safety concern for high consumers across all age groups.

### Vulnerable populations
- Infants, toddlers, children (higher bodyweight-adjusted exposure).
- Sulfite-sensitive individuals (especially some asthmatics), for whom ADI-based protection is not sufficient.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting: Historical JECFA group ADI exists; IARC sulfites/sulfur dioxide are Group 3 (not classifiable).
- Evidence against: EFSA 2022 could not establish an ADI and found safety concern at current exposure scenarios; exposure exceedance risk remains in younger groups.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting: FDA Title 21 GRAS listing with defined conditions; additive remains authorized in major jurisdictions.
- Evidence against: EFSA concerns are stronger than "minor concerns only at high dose" because concern includes high consumers in all groups and mean consumers in some child groups.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting: Additive remains approved but with major caveats, exposure exceedance patterns in children, and identified hypersensitivity concerns.
- Evidence against: EFSA's inability to establish an ADI is more severe than a standard "reduced ADI" scenario.

**high**
- Evidence supporting: EFSA follow-up opinion (2022) concluded no ADI could be established and MOE indicated safety concerns; EFSA 2025 exposure work continues to indicate concern under current use/exposure assumptions; sensitive subpopulations are recognized.
- Evidence against: No broad legal ban was found; FDA/JECFA positions are less restrictive than EFSA's latest position.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest current regulatory-scientific signal is EFSA's 2022 conclusion that no ADI could be set for E220-E228 and that MOE suggests a concern at realistic exposure levels (especially high consumers and children). This is reinforced by recent occurrence/exposure analyses and by persistent hypersensitivity concerns in a susceptible subgroup.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: high**
