# E575 (Glucono-delta-lactone) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E575 (INS 575)
- **Primary name:** Glucono-delta-lactone (GDL)
- **CAS number(s):** 90-80-2
- **Chemical class:** Lactone (cyclic ester) of D-gluconic acid; organic acidity regulator/acid precursor
- **Common synonyms:** Gluconolactone, delta-gluconolactone, D-glucono-1,5-lactone, D-gluconic acid delta-lactone
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **industrial (synthetic/biotechnological)** production for food use, typically via oxidation of D-glucose to gluconic acid then crystallization to the lactone (per 21 CFR 184.1318).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** GDL hydrolyzes in water to gluconic acid, producing gradual acidification rather than an immediate pH drop.
- **Technological roles:** Acid, acidity regulator, raising agent, sequestrant.
- **Common food categories:** Bakery/leavened products, tofu/coagulated soy products, cured/pickled foods, and products requiring gradual pH reduction.
- **US functional uses (explicitly codified):** Curing and pickling agent, leavening agent, pH control agent, sequestrant (21 CFR 184.1318(c)(1)).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:**
  - JECFA assigns a group ADI of "not specified," which generally reflects low toxicological concern at uses consistent with good manufacturing practice.
  - US FDA affirms GRAS direct use (21 CFR 184.1318).

- **Recent PubMed evidence (limited direct oral additive-focused literature):**
  - **PMID 33389968 (2020):** Young-rat 21-day high-dose study of taurine, glucuronolactone, and gluconolactone reported neurobehavioral/neurotransmitter changes, strongest in combination/high-dose groups. This is a signal but confounded by combination dosing and study context (energy-drink-style additive mix).
  - **PMID 37242438 (2023):** In vitro dermocosmetic formulation study reported biosafety in tested skin-related models (non-oral context).
  - **PMID 39970231 (2025):** Experimental lupus-related work reported immunomodulatory effects of GDL; this is mechanistic/therapeutic context rather than food-additive chronic safety testing.

- **Human epidemiology:**
  - No strong additive-specific human epidemiological harm signal was identified in the retrieved recent PubMed set.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA group ADI "not specified"; long-standing FDA GRAS status with GMP limits; chemistry suggests conversion to gluconic acid.
  - Evidence against: Direct contemporary EFSA final re-evaluation text was not retrievable in this run; at least one animal study reports high-dose mixture-associated neurobehavioral effects.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Clear FDA GRAS framework and defined functional uses; JECFA low-concern ADI basis; limited and non-definitive recent harm signals.
  - Evidence against: Sparse modern chronic oral human data specifically on food-additive exposure to GDL alone.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Could be argued due to data gaps (limited modern dedicated oral safety studies in humans).
  - Evidence against: No major regulatory withdrawals/bans found; core international regulatory posture remains permissive.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None substantial in the retrieved dataset.
  - Evidence against: No IARC carcinogenic classification for GDL found; no major-jurisdiction bans identified; JECFA/FDA positions do not indicate high concern.

2. **Rationale**
- The weight of evidence favors low concern under authorized/GMP uses, but the absence of a retrievable modern EFSA final E575 re-evaluation in the accessible EFSA pages and limited contemporary oral-human evidence argues against an unconditional "risk_free" designation.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
