# E635 (Disodium 5'-ribonucleotides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E635 (INS 635) [S4]
- **Name:** Disodium 5'-ribonucleotides [S4]
- **CAS number(s):** No single CAS for the mixed additive was identified in the WHO/JECFA E635 entry; component CAS numbers are disodium inosinate **4691-65-0** and disodium guanylate **5550-12-9**. [S4][S14][S15]
- **Chemical class:** Mixture of purine nucleotide salts (disodium inosine-5'-monophosphate + disodium guanosine-5'-monophosphate). [S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium 5'-ribonucleotides; sodium ribonucleotides; disodium ribonucleotide(s). [S4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** The component nucleotides occur naturally in foods and metabolism, but E635 sold as an additive is industrially manufactured/purified for flavor use. [S4][S14][S15]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Flavour enhancer (umami enhancer). [S4][S5][S6]
- **Mechanism of action:** IMP/GMP (the two components of E635) synergistically potentiate glutamate/umami perception and can increase perceived saltiness at equal sodium concentration. [S12][S13]
- **Common food categories:**
  - General flavor-enhancer use in foods (U.S. CFR text for the component salts). [S5][S6][S7]
  - Specific U.S. standard-of-identity food categories reference disodium inosinate/guanylate (e.g., canned vegetables/mushrooms). [S7]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Historical toxicology package (JECFA/WHO Food Additives Series 6):**
  - Long-term rat and dog studies reported no significant adverse findings at tested dietary levels.
  - Reproduction/teratology studies reported no significant fetal effects at tested doses.
  - Human volunteer note: very high intake increased serum/urinary uric acid; no adverse effects reported in that small study.
  - JECFA commentary flagged relevance for people with gouty diathesis and those on uric-acid-retaining diuretics. [S8]
- **Old PubMed toxicology index entry specific to disodium inosinate/guanylate/ribonucleotide:** Toxicology 1974 review article exists and aligns with JECFA-era evidence base. [S9]
- **Recent PubMed literature:** Recent papers found for E635/IMP/GMP were mostly sensory/taste-modulation and food-formulation studies rather than new chronic toxicity studies. [S12][S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory exposure framing:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not specified" (no numeric mg/kg bw/day cap established).
  - FDA component-salt CFR language uses functional/GMP-style limits (reasonably required level), not a single numeric cap in 172.530 text. [S4][S7]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Classical numeric ADI exceedance assessment is limited due ADI-not-specified status.
  - Main vulnerable-group concern is purine/uric-acid metabolism at high intakes rather than a broad-population toxicology signal at normal flavor-use levels. [S8]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Individuals with hyperuricemia/gout or on uric-acid-retaining diuretics. [S8]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

**risk_free**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not specified." [S4][S8]
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification entry identified. [S10][S11]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - JECFA explicitly notes high-intake uric-acid increases and special caution for gout/uric-acid-retaining diuretic contexts, so "no credible concern in any context" is too strong. [S8]

**limited**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - U.S. regulatory framework permits component salts as food additives with explicit use conditions. [S7]
  - JECFA ADI-not-specified conclusion and long-term toxicology package did not identify major general-population hazard at intended uses. [S4][S8]
  - No IARC listing and no major-jurisdiction ban identified. [S10][S11]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Limited modern, additive-specific human safety studies in recent PubMed literature. [S12][S13]

**moderate**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Credible mechanism for uric-acid increase at high purine loads and vulnerable subgroups highlighted by JECFA commentary. [S8]
  - EFSA re-evaluation context appears unresolved in accessible sources (archived/unavailable call page; no final dedicated EFSA E635 opinion located in this run). [S1][S2][S3]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Current core regulatory signals remain permissive, with no strong new harm signal at typical food-use levels. [S4][S7][S8]

**high**
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified in retrieved regulatory/carcinogenicity sources.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major bans identified; no IARC classification entry; longstanding JECFA and U.S. regulatory allowance at intended use conditions. [S4][S7][S10][S11]

### 2. Rationale
The best available evidence supports **low-to-limited** risk at normal flavor-enhancer use conditions: longstanding JECFA ADI-not-specified status, permissive U.S. CFR component approvals, and absence of IARC listing. The main caveat is a biologically plausible high-purine/uric-acid concern in susceptible individuals, plus the lack of modern dedicated chronic safety studies specifically for E635.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
