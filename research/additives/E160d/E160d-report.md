# E160d (Lycopene) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E160d
- **Name**: Lycopene
- **CAS number(s)**: 502-65-8 (primary); additional registry numbers are listed in PubChem cross-references (e.g., 101468-86-4).
- **Chemical class**: Carotenoid (acyclic tetraterpene; C40H56 polyene pigment).
- **Common synonyms**: all-trans-lycopene, psi,psi-carotene, tomato lycopene, lycopene from *Blakeslea trispora*.
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Both occur in commerce. E160d includes tomato-derived lycopene extract and can also include fermentation/synthetic manufacturing routes (source-dependent regulatory entries).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Lycopene is a highly conjugated carotenoid; the conjugated double-bond system absorbs visible light, producing red/orange coloration in foods.
- **Primary function**: Food color additive.
- **Common food categories**: Beverages, dairy analogues/desserts, confectionery, bakery, sauces/seasonings, and other color-standardized foods (jurisdiction- and category-specific).

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Approved food colour (E160d), with ADI-based risk management.
- **ADI**: **0.5 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **Evaluation timeline**:
- 2008 EFSA opinion established ADI 0.5 mg/kg bw/day and cited no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern in available data.
- 2017 EFSA opinion on extension of use concluded no safety concern under refined/non-brand-loyal scenarios, but potential ADI exceedance in conservative high-exposure scenarios.
- **Key conclusions**:
- ADI remained 0.5 mg/kg bw/day.
- Highest conservative 95th-percentile exposure estimates exceeded ADI in toddlers/children and in some adult scenarios at maximum proposed levels.
- Refined scenarios indicated no concern.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status in CFR**: Approved as a **color additive exempt from certification** (not a GRAS listing for lycopene itself).
- **Citation**: **21 CFR 73.585** (Tomato lycopene extract; tomato lycopene concentrate).
- **Conditions of use**:
- May be used for coloring foods generally in amounts consistent with good manufacturing practice.
- Not for foods with standards of identity unless added color is authorized by that standard.
- Exempt from batch certification.
- **GRAS status check**:
- eCFR Parts **182** and **184** (GRAS listings) were checked; lycopene was not found in those parts in this search.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Source-dependent ADI entries**:
- Lycopene extract from tomato (JECFA DB Chemical 5880): 2009 evaluation shows group ADI **“NOT SPECIFIED”**.
- Lycopene from *Blakeslea trispora* (Chemical 5573): 2006 group ADI **0–0.5 mg/kg bw**; 2023 entry retains 0–0.5 mg/kg bw and notes specification-focused review context.
- **Last evaluation year identified**: 2023 (for *Blakeslea trispora* entry).

### IARC
- **Classification**: No lycopene listing identified in IARC “List of Classifications” dataset (latest update on page data: 2026-02-25).
- **Interpretation**: No IARC monograph group classification found for lycopene in the accessed dataset.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified in the accessed regulatory sources.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal studies
- EFSA (2008) ADI derivation references a 1-year rat study with NOAEL context (50 mg/kg bw/day; liver enzyme/histopathology changes at higher dose levels in the cited dataset).
- A 90-day rat study (PubMed PMID: 12758219) on lycopene from *Blakeslea trispora* reported no observed toxicity up to the highest tested dietary level (NOEL at top tested dose).

### Epidemiological / human interventional evidence
- Recent human evidence is mostly supplementation/intervention rather than additive-specific population toxicology.
- A 2024 phase I oncology trial using synthetic lycopene with docetaxel reported generally low toxicity profile in that treatment context, with one dose-limiting event and modest docetaxel PK increases.
- A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of tomato/lycopene intervention studies in UV-related skin outcomes did not report a strong new dietary-safety signal at studied intakes.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Main practical concern in regulatory dossiers is not intrinsic carcinogenicity/genotoxicity signal, but **exposure margin** versus ADI in high-consumption scenarios and special contexts (e.g., high-dose supplement co-use with medications).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2017 conservative scenarios at proposed maximum levels showed 95th-percentile exposures that can exceed ADI:
- Toddlers: about **0.70–0.75 mg/kg bw/day**.
- Children: about **0.59–0.69 mg/kg bw/day**.
- Adults (high): up to about **0.46 mg/kg bw/day** in the cited high scenario, with exceedance possible in combined-source assumptions.
- EFSA refined scenarios (using non-brand-loyal assumptions and realistic use levels) did not indicate safety concern.
- **ADI exceedance risk**: possible in high-consuming children/toddlers under conservative assumptions; lower in refined exposure scenarios.
- **Vulnerable populations**: children/toddlers with high intake of colored foods; potential high-dose supplement users.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
- Evidence supporting: Lycopene is a naturally occurring dietary carotenoid; EFSA/JECFA evaluations did not identify major genotoxic/carcinogenic concern in reviewed datasets.
- Evidence against: A formal ADI exists (EFSA 0.5 mg/kg bw/day), and conservative exposure scenarios can exceed it in children/toddlers.
- **limited**
- Evidence supporting: Approved in EU/US, ADI established, no strong serious safety signal at normal use levels; FDA CFR allows GMP use with certification exemption.
- Evidence against: Exposure exceedance is plausible in some high-consumption populations/scenarios; this is a caveat beyond a purely “minor concern only” profile.
- **moderate**
- Evidence supporting: Exactly matches the “approved with caveats” pattern, including ADI exceedance risk in some child high-intake scenarios and source-dependent ADI complexity in JECFA records.
- Evidence against: Refined EFSA scenarios found no safety concern, and toxicology evidence does not indicate strong severe-hazard signal at typical dietary levels.
- **high**
- Evidence supporting: None substantial from accessed regulators (no ban pattern; no IARC Group 2A/2B or higher listing found).
- Evidence against: Major regulators continue to permit use with restrictions and ADI framework; no strong corroborated high-hazard regulatory action found.

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factor is **exposure caveat** rather than intrinsic severe hazard: E160d remains authorized and generally supported toxicologically at regulated use, but child high-intake conservative scenarios can exceed ADI, preventing a “risk_free” or fully uncomplicated “limited” assignment.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
