# E219 Research Report — Sodium methyl p-hydroxybenzoate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E219. [S5]
- **Primary name:** Sodium methyl p-hydroxybenzoate. [S5]
- **CAS number:** 35285-69-9. [S5]
- **Chemical class:** Paraben preservative (p-hydroxybenzoic acid ester/salt family). [S1][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium methylparaben; sodium methyl p-oxybenzoate; p-carboxylic acid methyl ester sodium salt. [S5]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic** in food use contexts (industrial preservative; paraben class used as antimicrobial additives). [S1][S3]

## Function in Food
- **Technological function:** Preservative/antimicrobial agent that inhibits growth of microorganisms. [S1][S3]
- **Mechanistic summary:** As a paraben preservative, it suppresses microbial growth; U.S. CFR classifies methylparaben uses as antimicrobial with GMP-limited concentrations. [S3]
- **Common food categories (historical EFSA context for parabens E214–E219):** surface treatment of dried meat products (quantum satis), jelly coatings of meat products (1 g/kg), confectionery excluding chocolate (0.3 g/kg), and liquid dietary food supplements (2 g/kg). [S1]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - Latest EFSA scientific opinion found in this search track is AFC panel work published in **2004** on para-hydroxybenzoates (E214–E219). [S1][S2]
  - EFSA concluded a **group ADI of 0–10 mg/kg bw/day** for **methyl and ethyl parabens and their sodium salts**; propyl paraben excluded from the group ADI. [S1]
  - Key conclusion: EFSA considered methyl/ethyl parabens (including sodium salts) compatible with the group ADI; no newer EFSA re-evaluation page for E219 was located during this run. [S1]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis:** EFSA (2004) retained a group ADI for methyl/ethyl parabens and sodium salts and reported no developmental toxicity signal for methyl/ethyl parabens in reviewed datasets; concern in that opinion centered on propyl paraben. [S1]
- **Recent literature signals (PubMed):**
  - A 2025 scoping review of dietary paraben exposure reported EU food concentrations generally within limits but warned cumulative multi-source intake could exceed ADI in some contexts. [S9]
  - A 2026 review reported weak estrogenic activity relative to endogenous hormones but highlighted cumulative/repeated exposure as a continuing risk-assessment issue. [S11]
  - A 2026 zebrafish study reported reproductive/developmental effects after maternal methylparaben exposure (environmental model; non-human). [S12]
- **Epidemiological data (human):**
  - Direct high-quality human epidemiology specific to **E219 in food** remains limited in the sources retrieved here; many recent studies concern mixed environmental/paraben exposures rather than E219-only dietary exposure. [S9][S10]
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - Endocrine activity remains the dominant mechanistic concern in modern literature, generally characterized as weak potency but potentially relevant under cumulative exposure scenarios. [S9][S11]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake context:**
  - Recent global review data indicate measurable parabens in multiple food groups and substantial between-country variability. [S9]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Source evidence indicates potential for **cumulative** intake to exceed ADI under some patterns of combined exposure. [S9]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children/high-consumption groups are plausibly more vulnerable on a mg/kg bw basis (inference from ADI structure plus cumulative-exposure findings). [S9]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: long-standing regulatory ADI framework exists for methyl/ethyl parabens; EFSA/JECFA did not characterize methyl paraben as high-hazard at typical regulated uses. [S1][S6]
  - Evidence against: ADI is finite (not "ADI not specified"), and recent literature highlights cumulative-exposure and endocrine-related concerns. [S9][S11]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: additive class has established ADI and longstanding regulatory handling; FDA methylparaben entry is under GRAS framework with GMP limits. [S3][S4][S6]
  - Evidence against: newer literature includes credible non-human reproductive/developmental and endocrine concern signals, and possible cumulative ADI exceedance scenarios. [S9][S11][S12]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: approved/accepted history exists but with caveats (group-ADI boundaries, cumulative exposure concerns, and mechanistic endocrine debate). This aligns with the "approved but caveats" definition. [S1][S6][S9][S11]
  - Evidence against: major regulatory bodies in sources retrieved here have not issued a high-severity carcinogenic classification or explicit inability to characterize safe use for methylparaben-group members at regulated levels. [S1][S6][S8]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: some modern studies report toxicological signals in model systems. [S12]
  - Evidence against: no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification found, no clear major-jurisdiction ban evidence retrieved, and regulatory ADI frameworks remain in place. [S1][S6][S8]

### 2. Rationale
The best-supported placement is not "risk_free" because ADI is bounded and cumulative-exposure concerns are documented. It is stronger than "limited" because recent evidence includes credible endocrine/reproductive concern signals in non-human systems and potential ADI exceedance scenarios in multi-source dietary contexts. It does not meet "high" due lack of IARC carcinogenic classification and absence of clear major-jurisdiction ban evidence in retrieved sources.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
