# E1401 Research Report: Acid-treated starch

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1401
- **Name:** Acid-treated starch
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-84-9 (listed in JECFA database as "STARCH, SOLUBLE")
- **Chemical class:** Modified starch (acid-hydrolyzed polysaccharide)
- **Common synonyms:** Starch, acid-treated; food starch-modified (acid-modified)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from natural starch, chemically modified with acid treatment)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Texture modifier/thickener
- **Mechanism of action:** Partial acid hydrolysis reduces average starch polymer chain length and viscosity, changing gelatinization/thickening behavior and process performance.
- **Functional classes reported by JECFA database:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener.
- **Common food categories (EU FAD):** Authorized across many categories (e.g., fermented dairy, cream analogues, confectionery, bakery, meat preparations, soups/sauces, flavorings, beverages), typically at **quantum satis** levels.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **Status:** **Approved food additive** (not listed as a GRAS section entry in the retrieved starch-related sections; appears in food additive Part 172).
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.892 (Food starch-modified)**
- **Conditions of use (relevant to acid-treated starch):**
  - Food starch-modified may be safely used in food.
  - For acid-modified starch, treatment is with **hydrochloric acid and/or sulfuric acid**.
  - Quantities of modifying substances must be no more than reasonably required and within listed limits.
  - Container labeling must bear "food starch-modified".

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and experimental evidence
- JECFA toxic monograph (WHO Food Additives Series 17) reports:
  - High digestibility in rats (acid-modified wheat starch diet study).
  - Pig studies with biochemical/weight observations but no clear signal of systemic toxic hazard at tested dietary uses.
  - Acute gastric rupture only at extremely large intragastric paste volumes (mechanical/bolus context, low relevance to normal dietary exposure).
- Committee comment in the monograph: acid-treated starches considered normal food constituents; ADI not specified.

### Epidemiological evidence
- No human epidemiological signal specific to E1401 was identified in the sources retrieved.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Direct toxic mechanism specific to E1401 was not established in the reviewed regulatory material.
- Main uncertainty is **data age and specificity** (many toxicology data are older, with limited modern E1401-specific studies).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not specified".
  - JECFA toxic monograph frames acid-treated starches as normal food constituents.
  - No IARC listing/classification found.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No clearly identified dedicated modern EFSA scientific opinion for E1401 in accessed EFSA content.
  - Limited recent E1401-specific human safety literature.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Authorized in EU additive database and FDA 21 CFR 172.892 framework.
  - JECFA ADI not specified and no major contemporary regulatory hazard signal found.
  - Recent PubMed safety-specific queries for "acid-treated starch" returned no direct adverse-signal literature.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Evidence base is partly older and not rich in modern targeted toxicology.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Data gaps (limited modern targeted epidemiology/toxicology for E1401).
  - Broad potential exposure footprint due broad category authorization.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No indication of ADI exceedance pattern, no bans, no IARC carcinogenic classification, and no strong mechanistic harm signal in consulted regulatory sources.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Not banned in consulted major frameworks.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification identified for this additive.
  - JECFA ADI remains not specified.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest drivers are: longstanding authorization status (EU/FDA), JECFA ADI "not specified," and lack of a major carcinogenic/toxic regulatory signal. The main constraint is evidence recency/specificity (limited modern E1401-focused studies and no clearly retrieved dedicated EFSA scientific opinion).

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
