# E941 (Nitrogen) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E941
- **Name:** Nitrogen
- **CAS number:** 7727-37-9
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic diatomic gas (inert atmospheric gas)
- **Common synonyms:** nitrogen gas, dinitrogen, N2, INS 941
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Natural** (major atmospheric gas); food-grade material is commercially obtained by fractionation of liquid air.

Evidence:
- JECFA chemical entry lists CAS 7727-37-9, INS 941, and name "Nitrogen".
- 21 CFR 184.1540 describes nitrogen (N2) and states it is produced commercially by fractionation of liquid air.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Inert gas that displaces oxygen/air, reducing oxidation and helping maintain texture/stability in specific applications.
- **Regulatory functional classes:**
  - JECFA: **FREEZING_AGENT**, **PROPELLANT**.
  - FDA/eCFR: **propellant, aerating agent, and gas** (21 CFR 184.1540(c)(1)).
- **Common food categories/use contexts:**
  - foods using gas-propellant/aeration systems,
  - cryogenic/freezing uses,
  - air-displacement atmospheres in some standardized dressings (e.g., mayonnaise/salad dressing standards reference CO2 or nitrogen atmosphere packing).

## Key Safety Evidence
- No strong evidence of chronic toxicological harm from normal food-additive use as an inert gas was identified in the gathered regulatory sources.
- Recent PubMed literature located for "liquid nitrogen" is mainly **acute injury case literature** from improper handling/consumption contexts:
  - 2022 pediatric emergency report on gastric perforation after liquid nitrogen application at point of sale.
  - 2025 case report on gastric perforation after liquid nitrogen cocktail ingestion.
- These reports indicate **physical cryogenic injury risk from misuse**, not a conventional chronic toxicity profile of E941 as an inert packaging/propellant gas.

## Exposure Assessment
- Quantitative dietary intake benchmarks for E941 are limited in the sources gathered; JECFA's "NO ADI NECESSARY" indicates no numeric health-based guidance value was required for typical food-additive exposure contexts.
- ADI exceedance analysis is therefore not applicable in the usual numeric sense.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:**
  - consumers exposed to improperly prepared liquid-nitrogen foods/beverages,
  - handlers/workers in poorly ventilated environments (asphyxiation/handling hazard context),
  - children in accidental-exposure scenarios.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI outcome is "NO ADI NECESSARY".
  - Nitrogen is a naturally occurring atmospheric gas and inert in typical food-use contexts.
  - FDA affirms GRAS use under GMP in 21 CFR 184.1540.
  - No elemental-nitrogen carcinogenic classification found in current IARC classification dataset.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EFSA re-evaluation is still ongoing (no finalized EFSA opinion yet).
  - PubMed case reports show severe acute injury can occur when liquid nitrogen is misused.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/GRAS regulatory status in major jurisdictions.
  - Safety concerns are mostly linked to misuse scenarios, not typical additive exposure.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - JECFA outcome is stronger than a standard numeric ADI-limited profile ("NO ADI NECESSARY"), which leans closer to risk_free logic.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA has not completed re-evaluation yet.
  - Serious acute injury outcomes are documented in misuse contexts.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No clear chronic toxicology signal in gathered regulatory evaluations for normal additive use.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for elemental nitrogen.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Severe injury can occur if liquid nitrogen is improperly served/ingested.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No ban in major jurisdictions from gathered sources.
  - No supportive evidence of high chronic hazard classification for elemental nitrogen as a food additive.

### 2. Rationale
Core regulatory evidence supports very low toxicological concern for typical E941 food-additive use (inert gas; JECFA "NO ADI NECESSARY"; FDA GRAS under GMP). The main risk signal identified is acute physical injury from improper liquid nitrogen handling/consumption scenarios, which is a use/misuse control issue rather than a chronic additive-toxicity pattern.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `risk_free`**
