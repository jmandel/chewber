# E939 (Helium) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E939
- **Name:** Helium
- **CAS number:** 7440-59-7
- **Chemical class:** Noble gas (inert monoatomic gas)
- **Common synonyms:** helium gas, He, INS 939
- **Origin (food additive supply):** Primarily isolated from natural gas streams and purified (not synthesized as an organic chemical)

Evidence: 21 CFR 184.1355 describes helium as an inert gas produced by liquefaction and purification of natural gas; EFSA 2024 also describes helium extraction/purification from natural gas and reports CAS 7440-59-7 (EFSA, 2024; eCFR, 2026) [S1, S2].

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Inert gas used to displace oxygen/air and reduce oxidative or microbial deterioration in processing/packaging contexts.
- **Regulatory functional framing:**
  - EU: authorised food additive at *quantum satis* (QS), including packaging-related uses (EFSA, 2024) [S1].
  - US: GRAS direct food ingredient used as a **processing aid** under GMP (21 CFR 184.1355) [S2].
  - JECFA database: helium listed with food additive/processing aid classification [S3, S4].
- **Mechanistic basis:** Chemical inertness and very low solubility in food systems support low expected dietary carryover from packaging gas uses (EFSA, 2024) [S1].

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion identified:** *Re-evaluation of argon (E938) and helium (E939) as food additives* (EFSA Journal 2024;22(10):e9048, DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9048) [S1, S5].
- **Approval status:** Authorised in EU; EFSA concluded current use of E939 "does not raise a safety concern" [S1, S5].
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI established in the 2024 opinion (the panel used a low-toxicological-concern approach for inert noble gases; historical SCF context notes ADI unnecessary for argon and EFSA applied analogous reasoning to helium) [S1, S5].
- **Evaluation year:** 2024.
- **Key caveats in EFSA opinion:** Data gaps remained on manufacturing details, impurities, and actual use levels; minimum EU purity specification (>=99.0%) noted [S1, S5].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS as a direct human food ingredient.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1355 (Helium)**.
- **Conditions of use:**
  - Purity suitable for intended use.
  - Use under current good manufacturing practice.
  - Use as processing aid; level not to exceed GMP.

Source: eCFR API content for 21 CFR Part 184, section 184.1355 [S2].

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal studies
- No dedicated oral toxicology dataset for helium as a conventional ingestible additive was identified in the reviewed regulatory materials; EFSA explicitly notes limited submitted toxicological/manufacturing/use data for E939 [S1, S5].

### Epidemiological data
- No human epidemiology linking dietary E939 exposure to chronic adverse outcomes was identified in this evidence sweep [S1, S8].

### Mechanistic concerns
- **Supports low concern:** Helium is chemically inert, monoatomic, and has low food solubility; EFSA considered low toxicological concern and no food-use safety concern [S1, S5].
- **Residual concern:** Inhalation asphyxiation risk exists at high ambient concentrations by oxygen displacement, but EFSA considered consumer inhalation exposure from food use extremely low and not relevant to food-additive risk characterization [S1].
- **Data-quality caveat:** EFSA highlighted uncertainty around potential manufacturing impurities due limited submitted data [S1, S5].

### PubMed check (recent studies)
- A targeted PubMed query for helium with food/additive terms yields many non-risk-assessment hits (instrumentation, plasma processing, etc.) [S8].
- A specific food-additive-focused query identified the 2024 EFSA re-evaluation (PMID: 39469433) as the key current record [S9].
- Practical interpretation: recent literature directly informing chronic dietary toxicity of E939 remains sparse; regulatory conclusions rely mainly on physicochemical inertness and low exposure plausibility [S1, S8, S9].

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA did **not** calculate a quantitative dietary exposure estimate for E939 due lack of submitted use/use-level data [S1].
- EFSA judged exposure likely low when used as packaging gas because helium solubility is low (reported around 1.6 mg/L in water at STP in the opinion context) [S1].
- **ADI exceedance assessment:** Not applicable in the standard numeric sense because no numeric ADI was set; available evidence does not indicate likely dietary overexposure risk under GMP/QS food uses [S1, S2, S4].
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:**
  - Consumers: no specific dietary subgroup exceedance signal identified.
  - Occupational/accidental inhalation context (non-dietary): oxygen displacement risk in confined spaces remains a separate hazard domain [S1].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Inert noble gas with low intrinsic reactivity.
  - EFSA 2024: no safety concern for E939 food-additive use.
  - FDA: GRAS under 21 CFR 184.1355 with GMP.
  - JECFA database lists no numeric ADI and long-standing use context.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA flagged uncertainty from limited submitted data on manufacturing impurities and actual use levels.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Clear approvals/GRAS exist with use conditions and purity expectations.
  - Some uncertainty persists due data gaps.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No credible dietary harm signal was identified, and the substance is chemically inert with very low expected intake from packaging gas use.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Could be argued only from uncertainty (incomplete use/manufacturing data), not from demonstrated toxicity.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No EFSA/JECFA/FDA indication of unresolved food-use toxicity requiring restrictive ADI management; no epidemiological or mechanistic dietary harm signal found.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None found (no bans, no IARC carcinogenic classification for helium, no major corroborating harm evidence).
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Regulatory consensus across EFSA/FDA/JECFA is consistent with low concern under intended food uses.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of current evidence supports very low toxicological concern for dietary E939 exposure: chemical inertness, low likely food carryover, EFSA 2024 no-safety-concern conclusion, and FDA GRAS/GMP status. The main limitations are evidence completeness (impurity/use data), not positive toxicity findings.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`risk_free`** (with moderate confidence, due remaining data gaps on real-world use levels/impurity characterization).
