# E202 (Potassium sorbate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E202
- **Name:** Potassium sorbate
- **CAS number(s):** 24634-61-5 (primary), 590-00-1 (listed synonym entry)
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of sorbic acid (unsaturated fatty-acid preservative salt)
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium sorbate; potassium salt of trans,trans-2,4-hexadienoic acid; potassium (E,E)-2,4-hexadienoate; INS 202
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic (industrial manufacture)**. PubChem/HSDB manufacturing text states sorbic acid is reacted with equimolar KOH and potassium sorbate is crystallized from aqueous ethanol.

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Preservative / antimicrobial (WHO JECFA functional class: "PRESERVATIVE").
- **Mechanism of action:** Weak-acid preservative effect is **pH-dependent**; efficacy is higher at lower pH (reported in food microbiology literature for sorbic acid/weak acids).
- **Common food categories:** Acidified foods and products vulnerable to yeast/mold spoilage; U.S. CFR examples include sliced/cut cheese standards (e.g., Colby) where sorbic acid/sorbates are optional mold inhibitors.

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved food additive in the EU (E202), with group ADI set by EFSA panels.
- **Latest follow-up evaluation:** **2019** (EFSA Journal 17(3):5625).
- **ADI:** **11 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as sorbic acid, for sorbic acid E200 + potassium sorbate E202).
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA used EOGRTS data (BMDL 1,110 mg/kg bw/day), replaced the earlier temporary ADI, and set a group ADI of 11 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Important prior context (2015):** EFSA previously set a **temporary** group ADI of **3 mg/kg bw/day** and noted exceedance in some child groups under higher exposure scenarios.

**Access note:** `efsa.europa.eu` journal pages redirected to Wiley and were not directly machine-readable from this environment; data were extracted via DOI metadata/PubMed records for the same EFSA opinions.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** **GRAS**
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3640 (Potassium sorbate)**
- **Conditions of use:** "Generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice."
- **Additional CFR context:** Product-specific standards can set limits (example in cheese standard language: optional sorbate preservative up to 0.3% calculated as sorbic acid in certain sliced/cut cheeses).

### JECFA / WHO
- **Substance entry:** Potassium sorbate (INS 202)
- **ADI:** **0-25 mg/kg bw** (group ADI as the sum of sorbic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium sorbates, expressed as sorbic acid)
- **Last evaluation year shown:** **1973**

### IARC
- **Result:** No potassium sorbate (or sorbic acid) listing found in IARC Monographs "List of Classifications / Agents Classified" data.
- **Related note:** "Parasorbic acid" appears (Group 3), but this is a different substance.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the reviewed regulatory sources.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Regulatory toxicology findings
- EFSA 2015/2019 opinions: no evidence of genotoxic activity for sorbic acid or potassium sorbate in the assessed data; ADI revised from temporary 3 to 11 mg/kg bw/day after new reproductive-toxicity evidence.

### Recent experimental studies (PubMed)
- **Mice (2024, Food Chem Toxicol):** 10-week oral potassium sorbate exposure changed gut microbiota and inflammatory markers (including IL-1beta, liver inflammatory infiltration); 5-week washout improved these effects.
- **Drosophila (2024, Insects):** Reduced lifespan/fecundity with ROS accumulation, apoptosis, and gut differentiation changes under exposure.
- **Rats (2025, BioMed Res Int; mixture study):** Potassium sorbate with sodium benzoate/citric acid at tested doses associated with reduced sperm indices, testosterone decreases, and pro-apoptotic testicular changes.
- **In vitro/mechanistic (2025, Sci Rep):** Network toxicology + cell work suggested pathways related to hepatocyte injury/tumor biology; hypothesis-generating but not equivalent to human causal proof.

### Human epidemiological signal
- **NutriNet-Sante cohort (2026, Nat Commun):** Observational association reported between higher intake of several preservatives (including potassium sorbate) and type 2 diabetes incidence; residual confounding/causality limits apply.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2015 context:** Temporary ADI exceedance concern was identified in toddlers/children at higher exposure scenarios.
- **EFSA 2019 update:** Higher group ADI (11 mg/kg bw/day) reduced, but did not eliminate, concern for high consumers in all settings.
- **Recent field data (Algeria, 2024):** Mean exposures in modeled scenarios were below ADI (17-48% for potassium sorbate), but **95th percentile** exposure exceeded ADI in some scenarios.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Children/adolescents with high intake of preservative-containing beverages/dairy products; high cumulative additive consumers.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Supports this tier:** Longstanding regulatory acceptance (EFSA/FDA/JECFA); no IARC carcinogen classification for potassium sorbate.
- **Argues against this tier:** EFSA previously reduced temporary ADI and identified child exposure exceedance scenarios; newer mechanistic/animal and epidemiological signals exist.

#### `limited`
- **Supports this tier:** GRAS in US (GMP use), EFSA-approved with defined ADI, JECFA group ADI established.
- **Argues against this tier:** Multiple post-2019 publications report biologically plausible adverse effects (microbiome/inflammation/reproductive markers) at experimental conditions; high-percentile intake can exceed ADI in some populations.

#### `moderate`
- **Supports this tier:** Matches rule criteria: approved additive **with caveats**, historical ADI tightening/revision, exceedance risk in children/high consumers, and credible animal/mechanistic signals.
- **Argues against this tier:** Human causal evidence remains limited and mixed; many concerning studies are high-dose, mixed-exposure, or non-human models.

#### `high`
- **Supports this tier:** Some recent studies raise mechanistic concerns.
- **Argues against this tier:** No major-jurisdiction ban identified; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for potassium sorbate; EFSA/JECFA continue to maintain ADI-based authorization.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight is from regulatory consensus (authorized with ADI) combined with nontrivial caveats: past EFSA concern at lower temporary ADI, evidence that high consumers (especially younger groups) may approach/exceed ADI in some settings, and emerging but not definitive toxicity signals.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
