# E252 (Potassium Nitrate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E252 (EU food additive code) [S1]
- **Name:** Potassium nitrate [S6]
- **CAS number:** 7757-79-1 [S6][S7]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic nitrate salt (KNO3) [S7]
- **Common synonyms:** Saltpetre, Saltpeter, Nitre [S6][S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Nitrate occurs naturally in foods (notably vegetables) and as an environmental contaminant, but food-additive E252 is an intentionally added manufactured preservative/curing agent [S1][S6]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative / curing agent; color-retention function in cured products [S1][S6][S8][S9]
- **Mechanism of action (food context):** Added nitrate can act as a nitrite source in curing systems, helping inhibit microbial growth (including botulism risk control) and supporting cured-meat color/flavor characteristics [S1][S9]
- **Common food categories:**
  - EU context: meat, fish, and some cheese products (E249-E252 group context) [S1]
  - US specific codified use: cod roe curing (max 200 ppm in finished roe) [S8]
  - US prior-sanctioned use context: cured red meat and cured poultry products (as nitrite source) [S9]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - EFSA re-evaluation for sodium nitrate (E251) and potassium nitrate (E252) was published in **2017** (linked as EFSA Journal output 4787). EFSA communication states existing safe levels remain protective at permitted additive uses [S1][S2]
  - **ADI:** 3.7 mg/kg bw/day (expressed as nitrate ion); EFSA communication also notes the nitrite ADI update separately [S1]
  - **Key EFSA conclusions:**
    - Additive-related exposure generally within safe levels, with possible slight exceedance in highly exposed children [S1]
    - Considering all dietary nitrate sources (additive + natural + contaminants), ADI may be exceeded in medium/high exposure groups across ages [S1]
- **FDA / CFR (Title 21):**
  - **Status:** approved/prior-sanctioned uses (not a modern GRAS listing in Part 184 for potassium nitrate) [S8][S9][S10]
  - **CFR citations and conditions:**
    - **21 CFR 172.160**: potassium nitrate may be used as a curing agent in cod roe, up to **200 ppm** in finished roe [S8]
    - **21 CFR 181.33**: sodium nitrate and potassium nitrate are under USDA prior sanctions for cured red meat and poultry (as nitrite sources, with/without nitrites) [S9]
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - Potassium nitrate record: **ADI 0-3.7 mg/kg bw** (as nitrate ion), evaluation year **1995**, ADI not applying to infants under 3 months [S6]
  - Nitrate record: **2002** entry retaining ADI 0-3.7 mg/kg bw (as nitrate ion; 0-5 mg/kg bw as sodium nitrate equivalent) [S11]
- **IARC:**
  - IARC classifies **"Nitrate or nitrite (ingested) under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation"** as **Group 2A** (probably carcinogenic to humans), Volume 94, publication year 2010 [S4][S5]
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major-jurisdiction outright ban on potassium nitrate food-additive use was identified in this run; current status is mainly approval with use limits/restrictions [S1][S8][S9]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** EFSA and JECFA keep/retain a nitrate ADI framework at 3.7 mg/kg bw/day (as nitrate ion), indicating accepted use under exposure control [S1][S6][S11]
- **Mechanistic concern (nitrosation pathway):** EFSA notes nitrite-related endogenous nitrosamine formation as a concern driver; IARC separately classifies ingested nitrate/nitrite with endogenous nitrosation conditions as Group 2A [S1][S5]
- **Recent epidemiological signal (human cohort):**
  - 2026 BMJ NutriNet-Sante cohort reported positive associations between higher **potassium nitrate** preservative intake and overall/breast cancer incidence (observational; not proof of causality) [S12]
  - 2026 Nature Communications NutriNet-Sante analysis linked several preservatives to higher type 2 diabetes incidence; nitrate-related preservative context is part of wider additive signal [S13]
- **Recent exposure and toxicology context:**
  - 2025-2026 publications show ongoing concerns around processed-meat nitrate/nitrite exposure, vulnerable groups (children/pregnancy), and experimental pathway findings (animal models) [S14][S15][S16][S17]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical contribution of additive nitrate:** EFSA states nitrate from food additive uses was estimated at **<5% of overall dietary nitrate exposure** [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - For nitrate additives alone, EFSA indicates exposure generally did not exceed ADI [S1]
  - For **all nitrate sources combined**, EFSA indicates potential ADI exceedance in medium/high exposure groups across ages [S1]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children with high intake of foods containing these additives (slight exceedance context) [S1]
  - Infants are a special toxicological caveat in JECFA ADI wording (ADI not applied to infants <3 months) [S6]
  - Additional literature flags pregnancy and high processed-meat consumers as risk-relevant populations requiring attention [S15][S16]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - EFSA and JECFA maintain established ADI frameworks and do not report outright prohibition at permitted uses [S1][S6][S11]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - IARC Group 2A classification for ingested nitrate/nitrite under endogenous nitrosation conditions [S5]
    - EFSA highlights exceedance risk when total dietary sources are included [S1]
    - New observational signals (e.g., NutriNet-Sante) suggest possible adverse associations [S12][S13]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - Regulatory approval with explicit limits/conditions (EU ADI framework; US CFR use limits) [S1][S8][S9]
    - Additive-only nitrate exposure often below ADI [S1]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - Exceedance can occur in some subgroups and when all sources are counted [S1]
    - Carcinogenicity concern pathway (nitrosation) remains active in hazard frameworks [S5]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - Approved status but with meaningful caveats (ADI exceedance scenarios, child/high exposure concerns) [S1]
    - Credible mechanistic concern (nitrosation/nitrosamines) and IARC 2A context for nitrate/nitrite ingestion conditions [S1][S5]
    - Emerging cohort evidence linking preservative exposure (including potassium nitrate signal) with chronic disease outcomes [S12][S13]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - Current risk assessments still conclude permitted additive uses can be safe when exposure is controlled [S1][S6]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - Presence of carcinogenicity concern pathway and high-profile hazard classification context [S5]
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - No major-jurisdiction ban identified; authorized uses remain in place with limits [S1][S8][S9]
    - EFSA does not conclude inability to establish safety at permitted use levels [S1]

### 2. Rationale
E252 does not fit `risk_free` because hazard concerns and exceedance scenarios are documented. It does not fit `high` because major authorities still authorize use with dose controls. The strongest fit is a conditional middle position: approved but with exposure-sensitive caveats, vulnerable groups, and ongoing mechanistic/epidemiological concern signals.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `moderate`**
