# E401 (Sodium alginate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E401. [R2][R3]
- **Name:** Sodium alginate. [R2][R3][R6]
- **CAS number:** 9005-38-3. [R3][R5]
- **Chemical class:** Alginic acid sodium salt; a high-molecular-weight anionic polysaccharide (polyuronide) derived from brown algae. [R5][R6]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium alginate, alginate sodium, INS 401. [R3][R6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic / natural-derived** (extracted alginic acid from brown algae, then neutralized to sodium salt). [R5]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological roles:** emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener, texturizer; also formulation/processing aid depending on application. [R3][R5]
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrophilic polymer that binds water, increases viscosity, and forms gels (especially in ionic environments), improving texture, suspension stability, and structure. [R5][R6]
- **Common food categories (US examples in regulation):** condiments/relishes, confectionery/frostings, gelatins/puddings, hard candy, processed fruits/juices, and other categories with specified maximum levels. [R5]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorized in EU food additive legislation (E400-E404 group). [R6]
- **Latest major re-evaluation:** 2017 EFSA ANS Panel opinion. [R6]
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** (equivalent regulatory interpretation: ADI “not specified” for this group). [R6]
- **Key conclusions:** No safety concern at refined exposure estimates for reported uses; however, available data were insufficient to adequately assess safety for infants/young children consuming certain foods for special medical purposes (FC 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2). [R6]

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** In **21 CFR Part 184** (“Direct food substances affirmed as generally recognized as safe”), including sodium alginate-specific entry. [R4][R5]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 184.1724**. [R5]
- **Conditions of use:** Ingredient-specific limits by food category (e.g., 0.3% in confections/frostings, 4.0% gelatins/puddings, 10.0% hard candy, etc.), and use within specified functional purposes. [R5]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED**. [R3]
- **Last evaluation year shown in WHO JECFA database entry:** **1992** (with prior evaluations listed). [R3]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No sodium alginate listing found in IARC “List of Classifications” dataset (latest dataset timestamp on fetched app bundle: 2026-02-25 16:34 CET). [R7][R8]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction bans identified in the consulted EFSA, eCFR/FDA, JECFA, and IARC sources. [R3][R4][R5][R6][R7]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Toxicology base (EFSA):** No concern for genotoxicity; no carcinogenic signal in available long-term rodent data; high-dose subchronic studies did not indicate adverse effects of toxicological concern (caecal enlargement interpreted as adaptive at high doses). [R6]
- **Human tolerability context (EFSA summary):** Adult oral sodium alginate at 175 mg/kg bw/day (7 days) then 200 mg/kg bw/day (16 days) reported as well tolerated; higher therapeutic pediatric doses for reflux (417-834 mg/kg bw/day) associated with GI side effects, including rare intragastric mass formation. [R6]
- **Vulnerable-use caveat (EFSA):** Data were not adequate for robust safety assessment in infants/young children consuming certain FSMP categories. [R6]
- **Specification-quality concerns (EFSA recommendation):** EFSA recommended revisiting toxic element limits (As, Cd, Pb, Hg) in EU specifications and noted protein-related hypersensitivity should be minimized via specs. [R6]
- **Recent PubMed signals (contextual, not core regulatory toxicology):**
  - 2025 multicenter RCT in PPI-refractory GERD found comparable adverse-event rates between generic vs original alginate formulations. [R9]
  - 2025 pilot LPRD treatment study reported no adverse effects in either treatment arm. [R10]
  - 2025 90-day rat study (dietary sodium alginate, different viscosities/doses) reported metabolic/microbiota modulation, not a classical toxicity signal. [R11]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary exposure (EFSA refined scenario):** up to **208 mg/kg bw/day** in infants (12 weeks to 11 months) under non-brand-loyal scenario for reported uses. [R6]
- **Special medical purpose foods (higher scenario):** p95 estimate up to **290.4 mg/kg bw/day** in toddlers (brand-loyal scenario, MPL-based for relevant categories). [R6]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Formal ADI exceedance is **not directly applicable** because ADI is “not specified”/no numerical ADI; risk management emphasis is on keeping infant/young-child exposure below therapeutic-dose ranges where GI side effects are reported. [R6]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** infants and young children (especially FSMP users), and individuals sensitive to GI effects at high intakes. [R6]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: ADI not specified/no numerical ADI needed in EFSA and JECFA frameworks; broad EU/US authorization; no genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in assessed toxicology package. [R3][R5][R6]
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA identified data gaps for infants/young children in specific FSMP categories; GI side effects reported at therapeutic pediatric doses; EFSA flagged specification concerns (toxic elements/protein impurities). [R6]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Authorized in EU and affirmed GRAS in US with defined use conditions; EFSA concluded no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses; recent clinical literature does not show major new safety signals. [R4][R5][R6][R9][R10]
  - Evidence against this tier: Infant FSMP data uncertainty means safety cannot be generalized to all high-vulnerability use cases. [R6]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Clear caveat for vulnerable infant FSMP populations, and side effects at higher therapeutic exposures. [R6]
  - Evidence against this tier: No reduced ADI, no broad-population regulatory reversal, and no strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal supporting a higher baseline concern for typical food-additive exposure. [R3][R5][R6]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: None identified in consulted regulatory/monograph sources (no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing, no major-jurisdiction bans found). [R7][R8]
  - Evidence against this tier: Continued authorization and favorable regulatory conclusions for general-use exposure scenarios. [R3][R5][R6]

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are strong regulatory acceptance (EU + US + JECFA), ADI not specified/no numerical ADI requirement, and lack of robust genotoxic/carcinogenic evidence in the evaluated package, balanced against a specific uncertainty in infant FSMP contexts and known GI effects at much higher therapeutic doses. [R3][R5][R6]

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
