# E552 (Calcium silicate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E552 (INS 552) [S12]
- **Name:** Calcium silicate [S4][S12]
- **CAS number(s):** 1344-95-2 (calcium silicate; food-additive synonym set), 13983-17-0 (wollastonite, related calcium silicate mineral used in IARC listings) [S12][S9]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic alkaline-earth metal silicate [S4][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium silicate, calcium monosilicate, silicic acid calcium salt, INS 552, E-552 [S12]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Food additive use includes **synthetic calcium silicate**; natural calcium silicate (wollastonite) also exists as a mineral [S4][S9]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Acts as an **anticaking agent** that improves powder flow by reducing caking in dry products [S4][S5]
- **Common food categories:** Table salt and baking powder are explicitly listed in US regulation; broader dry powdered foods may use it under permitted conditions [S4][S5]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest EFSA re-evaluation found:** 2018 (EFSA Journal 2018;16(3):5088) [S1][S2]
  - **Key conclusion:** EFSA stated it *could not assess safety* for calcium silicate/magnesium trisilicate because of toxicology data limitations and missing adequate studies [S2]
  - **ADI status:** EFSA reported the old SCF temporary group ADI of **0-50 mg/kg bw/day** (1990) as **obsolete** [S2]
  - **Approval status interpretation:** E552 remains authorized for use in EU additive legislation context, but EFSA's latest scientific re-evaluation did not confirm safety due data gaps [S1][S2]
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **GRAS status:** **21 CFR 182.2227** states calcium silicate is generally recognized as safe under specified use limits [S5]
  - **CFR conditions of use (GRAS):** up to **2% in table salt** and **5% in baking powder** [S5]
  - **Additional food-additive section:** **21 CFR 172.410** permits calcium silicate (including synthetic) as an anticaking agent with the same limits [S4]
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - WHO/JECFA database entry for calcium silicate reports **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED** [S6]
  - Same entry lists **Latest evaluation: 1990** and comments that a group ADI was established at the 29th JECFA (1985) [S6]
  - Historical JECFA monograph (17th meeting, 1973) reported ADI as **"not limited"** [S7]
- **IARC:**
  - No direct "calcium silicate" agent entry was identified in IARC's current online classified-agents dataset [S8][S9]
  - Related entries in the same IARC dataset: **"Silica, amorphous" Group 3** and **"Wollastonite" Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans) [S9]
- **Notable bans:** No major jurisdiction-wide ban for food additive E552 was identified in EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed [S1][S4][S5][S6][S8]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Direct regulatory/toxicology evidence for calcium silicate:**
  - JECFA monograph reports chronic feeding studies in rats (up to 5% in diet for 2 years) and dogs (2.5% in diet for 89 weeks) without adverse effects in the reported endpoints [S7]
  - 2018 EFSA re-evaluation identified major toxicology data gaps and therefore did not complete a positive safety conclusion [S2]
- **Recent PubMed evidence specific to E552:**
  - PubMed search for `calcium silicate` + `food additive` yields very limited directly relevant records (notably the EFSA re-evaluation paper; little modern direct oral safety literature for E552 itself) [S10]
- **Recent PubMed read-across evidence (mainly E551/synthetic amorphous silica, not E552-specific):**
  - 2025 mouse fertility/early embryonic oral study reported no adverse effects up to 1000 mg/kg/day [S11]
  - 2022 rat oral toxicokinetic/28-day study found low oral absorption (<4%), transient liver accumulation at high dose, and no major toxicological findings [S13]
  - 2023 rat oral nano-SiO2 study reported apoptosis/histopathology signals at high doses (300-900 mg/kg/day) [S14]
- **Epidemiological evidence:** No strong additive-specific human epidemiology for dietary E552 was identified in the reviewed sources [S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** I did not find a robust, recent quantitative intake distribution specific to E552 in the consulted EFSA/JECFA/FDA records [S1][S2][S6]
- **ADI exceedance framing:** There is no current numerical ADI from EFSA for E552 (old SCF temporary group ADI marked obsolete), and JECFA lists ADI as "not specified," so classic ADI exceedance calculations are limited [S2][S6]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Data gaps make subgroup risk characterization uncertain; conservative attention is warranted for high consumers of dry powdered foods/salt-containing products until newer additive-specific oral datasets are available [S2][S4][S5]

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: long-standing US permission (GRAS/food-additive conditions), JECFA ADI "not specified," and legacy animal studies without major adverse findings [S4][S5][S6][S7].
  - Evidence against: EFSA's latest re-evaluation could not confirm safety due insufficient toxicology data; obsolete historical ADI basis [S2].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: still permitted in major jurisdictions with explicit use limits; no strong modern human harm signal specific to E552 [S4][S5][S10].
  - Evidence against: unresolved EFSA data gaps are more serious than a simple "minor high-dose-only concern" profile [S2].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: additive is approved, but with meaningful unresolved uncertainty in the latest EFSA scientific opinion; modern direct E552 oral data are sparse [S2][S10].
  - Evidence against: lack of bans and supportive legacy toxicology reduce certainty for a higher risk tier [S4][S5][S6][S7].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA's inability to complete a safety assessment is a substantial regulatory caution signal [S2].
  - Evidence against: no IARC Group 1/2 classification for calcium silicate itself, no major bans, and no consistent direct evidence of severe harm at regulated food uses [S8][S9][S4][S5].

2. **Rationale**
- The decisive factor is the mismatch between long-standing regulatory use and the 2018 EFSA conclusion that available toxicology was insufficient to confirm safety. This indicates non-trivial uncertainty but not a strong, confirmed-harm pattern.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
