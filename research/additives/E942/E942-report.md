# E942 (Nitrous oxide) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E942 (INS 942).
- **Primary name:** Nitrous oxide.
- **CAS number(s):** 10024-97-2.
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic oxide gas (N2O), used as a food propellant/packaging gas.
- **Common synonyms:** dinitrogen monoxide, nitrogen oxide, laughing gas.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** for food-grade supply (US CFR manufacturing description: thermal decomposition of ammonium nitrate). 

Supporting data:
- WHO/JECFA entry for NITROUS OXIDE (INS 942) lists CAS 10024-97-2 and synonyms including dinitrogen monoxide/nitrogen oxide. [S5]
- 21 CFR 184.1545 describes nitrous oxide identity and manufacturing route from ammonium nitrate. [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Pressurized gas used to aerate/dispense foods (propellant/aerating gas). In packaging applications, it can act as a headspace gas.
- **Common food categories:** Aerated dairy product analogs and products using whipped/aerated dispensing systems (FDA GMP context), and packaging-gas contexts considered by Codex/JECFA. [S4, S5]

Supporting data:
- FDA specifies use as "propellant, aerating agent, and gas" under GMP and specifically references dairy product analogs. [S4]
- JECFA notes food use as propellant was acceptable and discusses requested evaluation for packaging gas use. [S5]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval/re-evaluation status:** E942 is listed by the European Commission among EU food additives (E-number list), and EFSA identifies E942 among the **remaining additives** in the re-evaluation programme (i.e., not yet finalized in EFSA's re-evaluation workflow). [S1, S2, S3]
- **ADI (EFSA):** No finalized EFSA ADI value for E942 was identified from a completed EFSA re-evaluation opinion in the sources checked. [S2, S3]
- **Evaluation year (EFSA):** No final EFSA re-evaluation year found for E942 as of the accessed pages; the public call for remaining additives (including E942) was published in 2023. [S2]
- **Key EFSA conclusion available now:** E942 remains in the group of additives for which EFSA requested additional technical data for completion of re-evaluation. [S2, S3]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS affirmation as a direct human food ingredient.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1545**.
- **Conditions of use:**
  - purity suitable for intended use,
  - no limitations other than current GMP,
  - use as propellant/aerating agent/gas,
  - dairy product analog use not exceeding GMP. [S4]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED**.
- **Last evaluation year:** **2000** (with history entries including 1985 and earlier).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 1985: food use as propellant acceptable.
  - 2000: request to evaluate additional packaging-gas use could not be completed due to lack of intake information. [S5]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No nitrous oxide entry was found in IARC's current "Agents classified by the IARC Monographs" dataset/app list checked (last update shown in app bundle: 2026-02-25). Therefore, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B/3 classification could be assigned from that list for E942. [S6, S7]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction food-additive ban for E942 was identified in the accessed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources. [S2, S4, S5, S6]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- A 2021 systematic review/dose-response analysis of animal reproductive/developmental studies on nitrous oxide used animal evidence to derive a recommended occupational exposure limit (20 mg/m3, 8-h TWA), indicating biologically relevant toxicity at sufficiently high inhalation exposure. [S10]
- A 2020 rat study reported neurobehavioral changes and biomarkers of oxidative stress/excitotoxicity after repeated nitrous oxide exposure (2 h/day for 60 days), supporting mechanistic neurotoxicity plausibility at sustained high exposure. [S11]

### Epidemiological / human data
- A 2025 US poison-center analysis (2003-2024) reported a large increase in intentional nitrous oxide abuse/misuse cases and substantial neurologic morbidity, showing real-world harm from non-dietary high-dose exposure patterns. [S9]

### Mechanistic concerns
- Multiple recent PubMed records and reviews describe functional vitamin B12 inactivation/hyperhomocysteinemia pathways and resulting neurologic complications in heavy exposure contexts; these mechanisms are well-established for nitrous oxide toxicology but are not evidence of harm at normal dietary additive exposures. [S8, S9, S11]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary exposure:** Quantitative dietary intake data specific to E942 in food were limited in the sources reviewed.
- **Key uncertainty:** JECFA explicitly reported inability (in 2000) to evaluate additional packaging-gas use due to missing intake information, showing a long-standing data gap for that use case. [S5]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not assessable in conventional numeric form because JECFA ADI is "NOT SPECIFIED" and no numeric EFSA ADI was identified in completed EFSA re-evaluation output for E942. [S3, S5]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - individuals with high non-food inhalation exposure (recreational/occupational),
  - people with marginal vitamin B12 status may be more susceptible to toxicodynamic effects at high exposure.
  These concerns are primarily outside normal food-additive dietary exposure scenarios. [S9, S10, S11]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI is "NOT SPECIFIED" and historical acceptance of food propellant use exists. [S5]
  - FDA GRAS affirmation with GMP conditions. [S4]
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Credible neurotoxicity/reproductive-toxicity signals exist at higher inhalation exposures (animal + epidemiology/mechanistic literature).
  - EFSA re-evaluation for E942 is still unfinished, so EU risk re-assessment is not fully closed. [S2, S3, S9, S10, S11]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/GRAS status in major frameworks (FDA; EU authorization context).
  - Serious safety signals are concentrated in high-dose non-dietary exposure settings, not typical food-additive consumption.
  - JECFA ADI not specified supports low concern for intended additive use. [S1, S4, S5, S9]
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Missing quantitative intake data for some use patterns (e.g., packaging gas) adds uncertainty.
  - EFSA has not yet issued a completed re-evaluation opinion for E942. [S2, S5]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Credible animal and mechanistic neurotoxicity findings exist.
  - Epidemiological severe outcomes from misuse are increasing and involve products marketed for culinary use. [S9, S10, S11]
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - These adverse outcomes are linked mainly to intentional/high inhalation exposure rather than expected dietary residual exposure from additive use.
  - No major regulatory ban for approved food-additive use identified. [S2, S4, S5]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - High-dose exposure can clearly cause clinically serious harm.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No IARC carcinogenic grouping found for nitrous oxide from the IARC monographs classification list checked.
  - No major-jurisdiction food-additive ban identified.
  - Core food-additive regulatory statuses remain permissive. [S4, S5, S6, S7]

### 2. Rationale
The evidence supports **low concern for intended food-additive use** but **not zero concern overall** because nitrous oxide has clear toxicological effects at sustained/high inhalation exposure. Regulatory status (FDA GRAS, JECFA ADI not specified) and absence of major food-additive bans argue against moderate/high classification for ordinary dietary use, while EFSA's still-pending re-evaluation and intake-data gaps prevent a strong risk_free conclusion.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
