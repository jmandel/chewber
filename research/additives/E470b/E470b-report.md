# E470b (Magnesium salts of fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E470b
- **Name:** Magnesium salts of fatty acids
- **Representative CAS number(s):**
  - **557-04-0** (magnesium stearate)
  - **91031-63-9** (fatty acids C16-C18 magnesium salts)
  - Additional component salts commonly referenced in chemical databases: magnesium palmitate (**2601-98-1**) and magnesium myristate (**4086-70-8**)
- **Chemical class:** Magnesium carboxylate salts of long-chain fatty acids (soap-like fatty acid salts; emulsifier/anticaking class)
- **Common synonyms:** Magnesium stearate, magnesium octadecanoate, magnesium distearate, fatty acids C16-C18 magnesium salts
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured by neutralizing/precipitating fatty acids from edible fat sources with magnesium salts)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic fatty-acid magnesium salts reduce surface/interfacial friction and improve powder flow, acting as anticaking/lubricating emulsifier-type agents.
- **Common technological functions:** Anticaking agent, emulsifier; US CFR text for salts of fatty acids also lists binder use, and magnesium stearate GRAS text lists lubricant/release agent and processing-aid/nutrient-supplement uses.
- **Common food categories:** EFSA exposure scenarios identified major contributors including fine bakery wares, confectionery (including chewing gum), and decorations/coatings/fillings; uses are broad but not all authorized categories reported actual use levels.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised in the EU for specified food categories; EFSA states E470a/E470b are currently authorised under Annex II and III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008.
- **Latest scientific re-evaluation found:** **2018** (EFSA Journal 16(3):5180).
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for E470a/E470b.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
  - No concern for mutagenicity based on available data.
  - Fatty-acid moieties from E470a/E470b contribute only a small fraction of total saturated fat intake (up to about 5% at mean estimates).
  - EFSA also evaluated infants >12 weeks and concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline (EFSA 2018):**
  - Salts are expected to dissociate in the GI tract into fatty-acid carboxylates plus cations.
  - EFSA reported no concern for mutagenicity from available data (including magnesium stearate data cited in the opinion).
  - EFSA noted important data gaps for dedicated subchronic/chronic/reproductive/developmental datasets for these salts, but still concluded no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Genotoxicity study (PubMed, 2017):**
  - A test battery (Ames, chromosome aberration, mouse micronucleus) reported no in vitro/in vivo genotoxic signal for magnesium stearate at tested conditions.
- **Allergen signal (PubMed, 2012):**
  - Single case report described urticarial reaction attributed to magnesium stearate; this is low-level evidence but indicates rare hypersensitivity is possible.
- **Human outcome signal outside food-use context (PubMed, 2020):**
  - Observational study in aspirin formulations reported higher cardiovascular composite events in patients receiving magnesium-stearate-containing aspirin formulations versus non-magnesium-stearate formulations; this primarily reflects pharmaceutical formulation/performance concerns, not direct food-additive toxicity.
- **Epidemiology specific to dietary E470b:**
  - No robust population-level epidemiological studies specific to dietary E470b adverse effects were identified in the searches performed.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA estimated broad exposure ranges across population groups; reported highest means/high percentiles in toddlers and children in refined scenarios.
- EFSA reported for all population groups (excluding infants/toddlers in specific scenarios) mean and high-level ranges up to roughly **0.03-212 mg/kg bw/day** and **0.14-301 mg/kg bw/day** respectively, with toddlers among highest exposed groups.
- For infants >12 weeks in the dedicated assessment, EFSA reported no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
- EFSA estimated the fatty-acid moiety contribution from E470a/E470b as a limited fraction of total saturated-fat intake (up to ~5% at mean estimates).
- Because EFSA and JECFA do not assign a numerical ADI ("no need for numerical ADI" / "ADI not specified"), classical ADI exceedance calculations are not central for this additive.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** high consumers of confectionery/bakery categories and medically-dieted infants >12 weeks in relevant categories; based on EFSA conclusions, these groups were still not identified as having a safety concern at reported use levels.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA concluded no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses; JECFA lists ADI not specified; substance dissociates to common dietary fatty-acid moieties plus magnesium.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: EFSA acknowledged limited dedicated long-term/reproductive datasets for salts; rare hypersensitivity case report exists.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting placement: Strong regulatory acceptance (EFSA-authorised, JECFA ADI not specified, FDA/CFR GRAS and GMP conditions), with no major carcinogenic/genotoxic regulatory signal.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: Data gaps and sparse modern human safety datasets specific to dietary E470b.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting placement: EFSA documented data gaps; isolated human allergy report; formulation-interaction concerns reported in non-food pharmaceutical contexts.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: No regulator currently indicates systemic toxicity concern at approved food-use levels; no IARC classification for E470b/magnesium stearate.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting placement: Minimal; no major regulatory trigger criteria met.
  - Evidence arguing against placement: No major bans, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for magnesium stearate/E470b, and EFSA/JECFA outcomes remain permissive.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence supports a low-concern profile under current food-use conditions, but not a pure "risk_free" categorization because long-term dedicated toxicology datasets are limited and rare hypersensitivity has been reported.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
