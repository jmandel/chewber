# E412 (Guar gum) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E412 (EFSA, 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625396/)
- **Name:** Guar gum (JECFA specification page, 2006, https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/scientific-advice/jecfa/jecfa-additives/detail/en/c/1204/)
- **CAS number:** 9000-30-0 (JECFA specification page, 2006, https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/scientific-advice/jecfa/jecfa-additives/detail/en/c/1204/)
- **Chemical class:** High-molecular-weight hydrocolloidal polysaccharide (galactomannan) (EFSA, 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38711804/; JECFA spec, 2006, https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/scientific-advice/jecfa/jecfa-additives/detail/en/c/1204/)
- **Common synonyms:** Guaran; gum cyamopsis; cluster bean gum (JECFA specification page, 2006, https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/scientific-advice/jecfa/jecfa-additives/detail/en/c/1204/)
- **Origin:** Natural (obtained from the seed endosperm of *Cyamopsis tetragonoloba*) (21 CFR 184.1339 text via eCFR API, 2026-02-19 issue date, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml)

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Thickener/stabilizer (21 CFR 184.1339 table and functions, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml)
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrophilic galactomannan that hydrates, increases viscosity, and stabilizes/emulsifies dispersed systems (JECFA spec + 21 CFR functional uses, https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/scientific-advice/jecfa/jecfa-additives/detail/en/c/1204/ ; https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml)
- **Common food categories (US CFR examples):** Baked goods, breakfast cereals, cheese, dairy analogs, fats/oils, gravies/sauces, jams/jellies, milk products, processed vegetables, soups, sweet sauces/toppings/syrups (21 CFR 184.1339, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml)

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - 2017 re-evaluation concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** and **no safety concern for the general population** at refined exposure estimates (EFSA Journal 2017; PMID 32625396: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625396/ ; full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/).
  - 2024 follow-up concluded submitted data were **not sufficient** to support safe use for infants/young children in FC 13.1.1, 13.1.5.1, 13.1.5.2, and recommended specification amendments (EFSA Journal 2024; PMID 38711804: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38711804/ ; full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11070944/).
  - **Evaluation years used:** 2017 and 2024.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **GRAS affirmed** as direct human food ingredient at **21 CFR 184.1339** (eCFR Title 21 XML, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml).
  - Conditions include listed maximum use levels by food category and functions.
  - For animal food, guar gum appears at **21 CFR 582.7339** with GRAS language for use under good manufacturing/feeding practice (same eCFR XML URL).
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - ADI = **“Not limited”** (practically equivalent to ADI not specified) and evaluation year shown as **1975** in the WHO/JECFA database (https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/863).
- **IARC:**
  - No dedicated guar gum agent entry found in IARC classifications dataset snapshot (IARC list page + JS data source checked):
    - https://monographs.iarc.who.int/list-of-classifications/
    - https://webapi.iarc.who.int/loc/loc.app.js
- **Notable bans/restrictions:**
  - No broad jurisdiction-wide ban identified for guar gum as a food additive in EU/US/JECFA sources above.
  - EU context includes concern about choking risk in jelly mini-cup type products for gel-forming additives and non-authorization of such use context (EFSA 2024 full text discussion, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11070944/).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2017: no adverse effects in available subchronic/carcinogenicity datasets at highest tested doses; no genotoxicity concern (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625396/).
- Reproductive/developmental benchmarks cited in EFSA full text include high NOAEL values (e.g., 5,200 mg/kg bw/day reproductive; 11,800 mg/kg bw/day developmental) in older studies (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/).
- Mouse model evidence with **partially hydrolyzed guar gum (PHGG)** showed attenuation of NAFLD-related outcomes through gut-liver axis modulation (PMID 34025071: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34025071/).

### Human evidence
- EFSA 2017 summarized adult oral intake tolerability, with abdominal discomfort considered undesirable (not adverse) in some individuals at around 15 g/day (~214 mg/kg bw/day in cited context), and GI discomfort reports in one pediatric intervention context (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/).
- 2026 RCT in post-GI-surgery patients: PHGG-containing enteral formula was non-inferior for diarrhea incidence and reported as well tolerated (PMID 41483486: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41483486/).
- 2025 RCT in adults with type 2 diabetes: guar gum lowered postprandial glucose without major acute safety signal highlighted in abstract (PMID 40410155: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40410155/).
- 2023 meta-analyses reported metabolic benefits (lipid/HbA1c effects), but evidence still not sufficient to set strong clinical guidelines (PMIDs 35837742, 36958432: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35837742/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36958432/).

### Specific concerns
- **Infants/special medical foods:** EFSA 2024 found submitted data insufficient to demonstrate safe use in FC 13.1.1/13.1.5.1/13.1.5.2; piglet study had high risk of bias and did not support deriving a robust reference point (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11070944/).
- **Hypersensitivity/allergenicity:** EFSA discussed case-report concerns linked to residual proteinaceous material and recommended minimizing such allergenic impurities (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11070944/).
- **Choking/esophageal obstruction context:** Historical severe events with dry bolus hydrocolloid products (including guar gum) are discussed in EFSA and reflected in US OTC warning context for hydrophilic gums (EFSA full text + 21 CFR 201.319 text available in eCFR title XML: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/ ; https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml).

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population (EFSA 2017 refined scenarios):**
  - Non-brand-loyal mean roughly 6–312 mg/kg bw/day across age groups; high-level values up to 610 mg/kg bw/day in infants in the summary table text.
  - Brand-loyal mean up to 449 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers), high-level up to 865 mg/kg bw/day (children) in EFSA summary text.
  - Source: EFSA full text (PMC7010168), exposure summary/table narrative (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/).
- **FSMP-focused infant/toddler scenario (EFSA 2017):**
  - Infants mean 325–609 mg/kg bw/day; P95 912–1,555 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Toddlers mean 120–457 mg/kg bw/day; P95 310–743 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Source: EFSA full text exposure section (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010168/).
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA/JECFA ADI is non-numerical (“no need for numerical ADI” / “not limited”), so classical ADI exceedance framing is not directly applicable.
  - Practical concern remains for vulnerable subpopulations (especially infants/young children consuming special medical foods) due insufficient safety dataset (EFSA 2024).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Longstanding authorization in major jurisdictions; EFSA 2017 and JECFA both non-numerical ADI conclusions; no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern in reviewed datasets.
  - Evidence against: EFSA 2024 could not confirm safe use for infant/special-medical categories; impurity/specification and vulnerable-population uncertainty remain.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: FDA GRAS affirmation with defined CFR conditions; EFSA 2017 no general-population safety concern at refined exposure; recent clinical literature generally shows tolerability in studied adult settings.
  - Evidence against: Infant/young-child medical-use uncertainty is more than a minor edge case in regulatory follow-up and prevents a fully low-concern classification.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved additive but with material caveats for specific populations (infants/young children in certain categories), plus allergenicity/impurity and choking-context concerns requiring controls.
  - Evidence against: No carcinogenic classification, no broad bans, and broad adult/general-population regulatory acceptance.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Some severe-event contexts (choking in specific dosage forms) and unresolved infant-category uncertainties.
  - Evidence against: No IARC Group 2A/2B signal for guar gum, no broad regulatory bans/withdrawals, and strong evidence of tolerability/approval in mainstream use.

### 2. Rationale
Overall evidence indicates low concern for general dietary use under approved conditions, but not “risk-free” because EFSA’s latest follow-up did not establish safety in specific infant/young-child categories and highlighted specification-related issues.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate** (driven by vulnerable-population uncertainty rather than general-population toxicity signal).
