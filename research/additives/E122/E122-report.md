# E122 (Azorubine/Carmoisine) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E122 / INS 122 (WHO JECFA chemical record, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081)
- **Primary name**: Azorubine (synonym: Carmoisine) (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1704)
- **CAS number**: 3567-69-9 (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081)
- **Chemical name**: Disodium 4-hydroxy-3-(4-sulfonato-1-naphthylazo)-1-naphthalenesulfonate (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081)
- **Synonyms**: Carmoisine; C.I. Food Red 3 (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081)
- **Chemical class**: Synthetic azo food color (inferred from azo chemical name + JECFA functional class "COLOUR") (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081)
- **Origin**: Synthetic.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function**: Food colour additive (WHO JECFA functional class, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081).
- **Mechanism of action (technological)**: The azo chromophore absorbs visible light and imparts red coloration (inferred from chemical structure/class and use as colour).
- **Common food categories**: EFSA reports it is authorised in many categories (50 categories cited in EFSA refined exposure work), indicating broad use across coloured processed foods/drinks (EFSA Journal, 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061619/).

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - 2009 EFSA re-evaluation (E 122) reported ADI **4 mg/kg bw/day** and concluded available data did not justify revision downward (EFSA Journal entry, 2009 page/snippet, https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1332).
  - 2015 EFSA refined exposure: 2009 scenarios showed possible exceedance in children at high percentile under MPL/use-level assumptions; refined scenarios did **not exceed ADI** at mean or high exposure in any group (EFSA Journal, 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061619/).
  - **Approval status**: Approved/authorised in EU with ADI-based risk management (EFSA Journal, 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061619/).
- **FDA / eCFR (U.S.)**:
  - Azorubine/Carmoisine is **not listed by name** in Title 21 color-additive food listing structures (Part 74 list output and part text checks) (eCFR API, 2026 access, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/versions/title-21.json?chapter=I&subchapter=A&part=74).
  - General CFR framework states unlisted uses are treated as not listed color-additive uses for those contexts (21 CFR 70.5) (eCFR API, 2026 access, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/2026-02-25/title-21?part=70&section=70.5).
  - **GRAS status**: Not GRAS/approved for food color use under the specific E122/azorubine identity in Title 21 searches performed.
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - ADI: **0-4 mg/kg bw** (WHO JECFA chemical page, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081).
  - Evaluation year shown on record: **1983** (WHO JECFA, 2026 access, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1081).
  - Historical progression shown: temporary lower ADIs in older meetings before 0-4 mg/kg bw allocation (same source URL).
- **IARC**:
  - IARC classifications dataset entry lists **Carmoisine = Group 3**, evaluation year 1987 (IARC monographs classification dataset, 2026 access, https://webapi.iarc.who.int/loc/loc.app.js; classification page container: https://monographs.iarc.who.int/list-of-classifications/).
  - InChem/IARC summary page also indicates **Group 3** for azorubine/carmoisine (IARC summary, https://www.inchem.org/documents/iarc/suppl7/azorubine.html).
- **Notable bans/restrictions**:
  - U.S. food-use approval not identified for azorubine/carmoisine in Title 21 searches (eCFR API URLs above).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Genotoxicity (recent in vitro)**: A 2025 human lymphocyte study reported genotoxic signals mainly at high concentrations; low concentrations showed less/no effect in their test battery (Toxicology Research, 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40059972/).
- **Developmental toxicity (co-exposure model)**: 2024 zebrafish study reported stronger developmental toxicity for azorubine when co-exposed with BPA versus single-agent exposures (Food and Chemical Toxicology, 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38992409/).
- **Mechanistic/cell model evidence**: 2025 albumin-adduct study found concentration-dependent cytotoxicity and conformational effects in model systems (J Biomol Struct Dyn, 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38047623/).
- **Oxidative-stress model evidence**: 2024 Drosophila study reported oxidative stress/mitochondrial markers at tested doses (Mol Biol Rep, 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38796672/).
- **Neurobehavioral context**: The Southampton trial (mixtures of artificial colors plus sodium benzoate) found small but significant hyperactivity effects in children; this is not azorubine-only evidence but remains relevant to azo color risk context (Lancet, 2007, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17825405/).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2009 assessment scenario (high percentile) indicated potential ADI exceedance in children under conservative assumptions (EFSA 2015 abstract recounting 2009 conclusion, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061619/).
- EFSA 2015 refined assessment with updated use/analytical data found no ADI exceedance at mean or higher exposure levels in any population group (EFSA Journal, 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061619/).
- **Vulnerable populations**: toddlers/children remain the key focus due higher intake per kg bw and behavioral sensitivity discussions in the artificial-color literature (EFSA 2015 + Lancet 2007, URLs above).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: IARC Group 3 (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity) and modern EFSA refined exposure not exceeding ADI under refined scenarios.
  - Evidence against: Established finite ADI (not "ADI not specified"), repeated mechanistic/toxicology signals in newer studies, and historical high-percentile child exceedance in conservative EFSA scenarios.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA/JECFA approvals with ADIs (EFSA 4 mg/kg bw/day; JECFA 0-4 mg/kg bw), and no clear major-jurisdiction carcinogenic classification.
  - Evidence against: Child-focused caveats, mixture-related neurobehavioral concerns, and recent in vitro/in vivo mechanistic studies showing adverse endpoints at higher exposures.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Matches criteria of "approved but with caveats"; EFSA historical exceedance concerns in children under MPL assumptions and growing mechanistic evidence base.
  - Evidence against: Refined EFSA exposure no longer exceeding ADI in assessed populations, and many recent findings come from non-human/high-dose or co-exposure models.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Some recent studies report developmental/oxidative/genotoxic concerns under experimental conditions.
  - Evidence against: No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification for carmoisine; EFSA and JECFA still maintain ADI-based acceptability; no clear global consensus ban pattern demonstrated in retrieved primary regulatory sources.

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are: (a) regulatory acceptance with ADI, (b) child-intake caveat history and mixture-related behavioral concerns, and (c) ongoing mechanistic toxicity signals that are credible but not yet sufficient to overturn ADI-based regulatory conclusions.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: moderate**.
