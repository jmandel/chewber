# E263 (Calcium acetate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E263
- **Name:** Calcium acetate
- **CAS:** 62-54-4
- **Chemical class:** Calcium salt of acetic acid (organic acetate salt)
- **Common synonyms:** Acetate of lime; vinegar salts; calcium ethanoate
- **Origin (food additive supply chain):** Primarily **synthetic/manufactured** (produced by neutralizing acetic acid with calcium hydroxide) [FDA 21 CFR 184.1185].

## Function in Food
- **Technological roles (regulatory):**
  - FDA: firming agent, pH control agent, processing aid, sequestrant, stabilizer/thickener, texturizer [21 CFR 184.1185].
  - WHO/JECFA entry: acidity regulator, preservative, stabilizer.
- **Mechanistic interpretation:**
  - Acetate contributes acidification/pH buffering and preservative effect (reduced microbial growth at lower pH).
  - Calcium ion contributes firming/textural effects and can support stabilization/sequestration functions.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and historical toxicology evidence
- JECFA monograph (WHO Food Additives Series 5) reports limited specific toxicity data for calcium acetate itself; historical data cited include acetic acid in rat drinking water:
  - 0.25%: no toxic symptoms
  - 0.5%: slight growth retardation
- JECFA conclusion in that monograph stream: ADI “Not limited.”

### Human/clinical evidence relevant to hazard signals
- Most modern human studies mentioning calcium acetate are in **CKD phosphate-binder therapy** (therapeutic-dose context, not normal food additive intake).
- 2025 Cochrane review (PubMed 40576086): calcium-based binders show more hypercalcaemia than non-calcium binders in dialysis comparisons (low-certainty evidence), indicating a high-dose calcium-load risk context.
- 2022 pilot microbiome study (PubMed 34923600): 5-month calcium acetate vs sucroferric oxyhydroxide in hemodialysis did not show major microbiome-diversity differences.
- 2021 pediatric CKD RCT comparator data (PubMed 33106892): calcium acetate arm had high AE incidence typical for this medically complex population; findings are not directly transferable to low food-additive exposure.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Primary plausible concern is **excess calcium load** at pharmacologic intakes (renal/cardiovascular context), not acetate-specific genotoxic/carcinogenic hazard at food-additive levels.
- No IARC carcinogenic classification was identified for calcium acetate.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory exposure controls:** FDA sets low maximum as-served concentrations by food category under CGMP.
- **EU quantitative exposure estimate for E263:** Not identified in the EFSA re-evaluation summary table because no published EFSA re-evaluation opinion entry was found there.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA ADI is “Not limited,” reducing concern for a strict numerical exceedance framework.
  - Practical risk may still rise in special populations with high total calcium burden (e.g., CKD patients on calcium-containing therapeutics), but that is mainly medicinal-dose context.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Individuals with impaired calcium/phosphate handling (e.g., advanced CKD) if total calcium intake from all sources is high.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free placement:**
  - JECFA ADI is “Not limited.”
  - Calcium and acetate are physiologic/nutritional moieties.
  - No IARC listing/classification found for calcium acetate.
- **Evidence against risk_free placement:**
  - EFSA food-additive re-evaluation entry for E263 was not found as completed in the current EU summary table.
  - Modern dedicated oral food-additive toxicology datasets are limited.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited placement:**
  - FDA GRAS with specific use limits in 21 CFR 184.1185.
  - No strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal identified from checked sources.
  - JECFA “Not limited” ADI and long history of use.
- **Evidence against limited placement:**
  - Historical toxicology base is old and sparse for calcium acetate specifically.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate placement:**
  - Therapeutic calcium-based binder literature shows hypercalcaemia risk at high doses (medical setting).
- **Evidence against moderate placement:**
  - This signal is from dialysis pharmacotherapy, not normal food-additive exposure.
  - No evidence found of widespread ADI exceedance in general consumers for E263.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high placement:**
  - None identified (no IARC Group 1/2A/2B assignment for calcium acetate; no major bans found).
- **Evidence against high placement:**
  - Existing regulatory frameworks (FDA GRAS, JECFA ADI not limited, EU permitted listing) do not indicate high hazard under authorized use.

### 2. Rationale
Calcium acetate is an authorized/GRAS additive with long-standing use and no clear carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in the checked regulatory datasets. JECFA’s ADI is not limited, and U.S. use is constrained by category-level maxima. The main cautionary signal (hypercalcaemia) comes from therapeutic calcium-binder use in CKD, which is materially higher exposure than standard food-additive intake.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
