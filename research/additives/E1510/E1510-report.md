# E1510 (Ethanol) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1510
- **Name:** Ethanol (ethyl alcohol)
- **CAS number(s):** 64-17-5
- **Chemical class:** Simple aliphatic alcohol (primary alcohol)
- **Common synonyms:** Ethyl alcohol, alcohol, spirit alcohol
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Both naturally formed (fermentation/endogenous in some foods) and industrially produced; food-use ethanol can come from fermentation or synthetic routes.

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Carrier solvent / extraction solvent.
- **Other roles:** Flavouring-related solvent use; specific antimicrobial use in US regulation.
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Solubilises flavouring substances and other ingredients.
  - Acts as a volatile carrier that can aid dispersion and processing.
  - At sufficient concentration has antimicrobial activity (membrane/protein disruption in microbes).
- **Common food categories (from retrieved evidence):**
  - Flavouring systems and solvent uses (JECFA functional class: flavouring agent, carrier solvent, extraction solvent).
  - Foods where endogenous/processing ethanol can occur (e.g., non-alcoholic beer, fruit juices/drinks, baked goods, bananas, baby foods) in exposure studies.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Carcinogenicity / epidemiology:**
  - IARC classifies ethanol in alcoholic beverages as Group 1.
  - A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis found alcohol use associated with higher early-onset colorectal cancer risk (pooled OR 1.39; dose-response OR 1.02 per 10 g/day ethanol).
- **Low-dose food exposure evidence:**
  - 2023 Food and Chemical Toxicology study (1260 food samples) reported that acute concern was uncommon, but chronic exposure scenarios could exceed derived HBGV in several cases; most analysed foods did not produce high-risk doses.
  - 2023 study on jarred fruit purees estimated ethanol exposure in children at 6.57–8.76 mg/kg bw/day under study assumptions, above that paper’s cited allowable value.
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - At sufficient dose, ethanol has known systemic toxicity and carcinogenic relevance in alcohol-consumption contexts; uncertainty remains for extrapolating high-dose alcohol-harm evidence to low-dose additive/trace-food exposure contexts.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake context:** Ethanol can be present as an intentional carrier/solvent and also as endogenous fermentation byproduct in multiple foods.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - No numeric EFSA ADI retrieved in this run.
  - JECFA uses “limited by GMP,” so classical ADI-exceedance comparison is not numeric.
  - Study-level evidence suggests possible exceedance of low-dose health-based thresholds in some child/high-consumption scenarios.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants/young children with high consumption of certain products (e.g., fruit purees in specific scenarios).
  - Individuals with medical, developmental, or behavioral sensitivity to ethanol exposure.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Ethanol is endogenous/naturally occurring in foods; JECFA did not assign a numeric ADI and used GMP-limited framing.
  - Evidence against: IARC Group 1 classification context and multiple epidemiological signals at higher exposures argue against a blanket “risk-free” classification.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: FDA GRAS listing with specific conditions; JECFA “limited by GMP” and 1996 no-safety-concern statement for flavouring-agent context.
  - Evidence against: Chronic/aggregate low-dose exposure concerns in some studies and established carcinogenicity context for alcohol consumption suggest more than only minor concerns.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved/allowed uses exist, but with caveats (GMP limitation, use-specific CFR condition) and credible evidence that some populations can approach or exceed low-dose risk benchmarks in certain scenarios.
  - Evidence against: Not all low-dose studies show broad risk; many sampled foods produced low acute concern.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: IARC Group 1 context for ethanol in alcoholic beverages indicates serious hazard potential under relevant exposure conditions.
  - Evidence against: No retrieved major-jurisdiction ban for additive ethanol; regulatory frameworks still permit use under conditions, indicating risk management rather than prohibition.

### 2. Rationale
The evidence base is mixed: regulatory systems allow use (FDA/JECFA) with constraints, but ethanol has established carcinogenic hazard context and some low-dose food-exposure studies highlight potential chronic exceedance in high-consumption scenarios, especially for children.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
