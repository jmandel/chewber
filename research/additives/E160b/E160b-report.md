# E160b (Annatto) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E160b
- **Primary name:** Annatto (annatto extracts; annatto E)
- **CAS number(s):**
  - **1393-63-1** (annatto extract; INS 160b)
  - **6983-79-5** (bixin)
  - **542-40-5** (norbixin)
- **Chemical class:** Natural carotenoid colorants (apocarotenoids), primarily bixin (lipophilic) and norbixin (water-dispersible salts).
- **Common synonyms:** Rocou, Orlean, Terre Orellana, L. Orange; bixin/norbixin-based annatto extracts.
- **Origin:** **Natural** (extract from seeds of *Bixa orellana* L., with permitted processing/extraction steps).

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Color additive (orange-yellow to orange-red hue).
- **Mechanism of action:** Bixin/norbixin pigments absorb visible light and impart color; form-dependent solubility (oil/aqueous processed) determines food-system compatibility.
- **Common food categories:** Broad food-color use where permitted; in U.S. CFR text, annatto extract is allowed for coloring foods generally under GMP (with standards-of-identity caveat).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in EU as E160b, with EFSA re-evaluation and subsequent annatto E opinion.
- **Latest evaluation/opinion used:** 2019 EFSA Journal opinion (building on 2016/2017 EFSA work).
- **ADI:**
  - **6 mg/kg bw/day (as bixin)**
  - **0.3 mg/kg bw/day (as norbixin)**
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - Annatto E did **not raise concern for genotoxicity**.
  - ADIs established for bixin/norbixin can be applied to annatto E.
  - Bixin exposure estimates did not exceed ADI; norbixin reached/exceeded ADI at high-percentile scenarios in limited cases, but EFSA concluded no health concern considering uncertainty/overestimation.

### FDA / U.S. CFR (Title 21)
- **Status in CFR:** **Approved color additive** (not a GRAS-only pathway for this color use), listed under **21 CFR §73.30 (Annatto extract)**.
- **CFR citations and conditions:**
  - **21 CFR §73.30**: identity/specifications, solvent and residue controls, labeling, and use restriction.
  - **Use condition:** "may be safely used for coloring foods generally, in amounts consistent with good manufacturing practice," with standards-of-identity limitations.
  - **Certification:** §73.30(e) states exemption from batch certification.
- **GRAS status note:** In the fetched current Title 21 XML, annatto for color use appears under color additive regulations (§73.30) rather than an explicit GRAS listing for this use.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Database entry:** Annatto extracts (INS 160b), chemical page shows bixin/norbixin identities and evaluations.
- **Last evaluation year shown:** **2006**
- **ADI(s) shown:**
  - **Bixin:** 0-12 mg/kg bw
  - **Norbixin + sodium/potassium salts:** 0-0.6 mg/kg bw (group ADI, expressed as norbixin)
- **Key JECFA notes:** ADIs are extract-form dependent; evaluation explicitly notes inability to apply the same conclusion to oil-processed bixin when data were insufficient.

### IARC
- Checked IARC Monographs classification resources. No entry/classification found for "annatto", "bixin", or "norbixin" in the current published list dataset.

### Notable Bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and toxicology evidence
- EFSA (2019, with 2016 basis) considered available toxicological data sufficient to set ADIs for bixin and norbixin and found no genotoxicity concern for annatto E.
- JECFA indicates ADI assignment depends on extract composition/form and notes data gaps for oil-processed bixin in that evaluation context.

### Epidemiological / human data
- No strong population-level epidemiology signal was identified in the reviewed sources.
- Human evidence is mainly **case-based hypersensitivity** literature.

### Mechanistic / clinical concerns
- **Allergenicity:** Case reports and oral challenge literature show that annatto can trigger IgE-mediated reactions in susceptible individuals, including severe reactions (rare, low-certainty population prevalence).
- **Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity:** EFSA found no genotoxicity concern for annatto E; no IARC classification entry located for annatto/bixin/norbixin.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure context:**
  - Bixin exposures below ADI across assessed groups/scenarios.
  - Norbixin high-percentile exposure reached/exceeded ADI in limited child/toddler scenarios (including one-country refined scenario), but EFSA judged overall health concern unlikely given conservative assumptions.
- **JECFA intake context:**
  - JECFA notes low modeled exposure relative to ADI in one scenario, while historical data also documented possible ADI exceedance in high annatto-seed consumers in Brazil.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - High-intake toddlers/children in upper-percentile scenarios.
  - Individuals with dye/spice hypersensitivity risk.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Supports:** Natural plant origin; no IARC classification found; EFSA no genotoxicity concern.
- **Against:** Established ADIs (not "ADI not specified"); exposure caveats at high percentiles; documented allergy cases.

#### limited
- **Supports:** Approved by EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks; ADIs established; broad food use under GMP; no strong carcinogenic regulatory signal.
- **Against:** Not completely concern-free due exposure edge cases and confirmed hypersensitivity reports.

#### moderate
- **Supports:** EFSA/JECFA exposure caveats in some high consumers (especially young populations/high-percentile scenarios); clinical allergy/anaphylaxis case literature exists.
- **Against:** Signals are limited and not broad population harm; regulators still conclude acceptable safety under current conditions.

#### high
- **Supports:** Very limited support.
- **Against:** No major-jurisdiction ban, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment identified for annatto itself, and no regulatory conclusion that safety cannot be established under authorized conditions.

### 2. Rationale
Annatto (E160b) is regulated and usable with defined ADIs and GMP conditions, but it is not risk-free: there are reproducible hypersensitivity case reports and some high-percentile exposure scenarios (especially for norbixin/toddlers) that approach or exceed ADI assumptions. Regulatory bodies nonetheless consider authorized use acceptable when uncertainties and conservative exposure modeling are accounted for.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
