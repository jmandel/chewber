# E1402 Research Report (Alkaline-treated starch)

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1402
- **Name:** Alkaline treated starch
- **INS:** 1402
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-84-9 (listed as "Starch, soluble" in the WHO/JECFA database entry for alkaline treated starch)
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (polysaccharide derivative)
- **Common synonyms:** "Starch, Alkaline treated"
- **Origin:** **semi-synthetic** (derived from native starch, chemically modified with alkali)

Evidence:
- WHO/JECFA chemical profile for `Home/Chemical/2358` lists chemical name, synonym, CAS, INS, and functional classes.[S1]
- JECFA toxic monograph states modification is done with up to 1% alkali hydroxide and mainly causes depolymerization.[S2]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Thickener/stabilizer/emulsifier (WHO/JECFA functional class listing).[S1]
- **Mechanism of action:** Alkali treatment (up to 1% hydroxide) modifies starch physicochemical behavior mainly via depolymerization; this changes viscosity/texture behavior in food systems.[S2]
- **Common food categories:** General processed foods using modified starch functionality; FDA also regulates “food starch-modified” for direct food use under conditions in 21 CFR 172.892.[S3]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **What was found on EFSA/related EU sources:**
  - EFSA 2017 re-evaluation opinion covered **other modified starches** (E1404, E1410, E1412, E1413, E1414, E1420, E1422, E1440, E1442, E1450, E1451) and concluded **no safety concern** for those uses.[S4]
  - In the Commission summary table of permitted additives and EFSA re-evaluation status (status dated **18 February 2026**), entries begin at E1404 in this group; no E1402 entry was identified.[S5][S6]
- **E1402-specific EFSA ADI:** No dedicated EFSA ADI identified for E1402 from the accessed EFSA opinion/re-evaluation sources.
- **Evaluation year (E1402-specific):** Not identified.
- **Key conclusion:** No directly identified EFSA scientific opinion specifically re-evaluating E1402 in the accessed EFSA/EU status sources; related modified starches were considered of no safety concern in 2017.[S4][S5][S6]

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status (E1402 name specifically):** No standalone GRAS entry for "alkaline treated starch" was identified in the checked Title 21 sections.
- **Primary CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.892 (Food starch-modified)** — states food starch-modified may be safely used in food under specified limits/conditions, including process-agent limits and labeling as "food starch-modified".[S3]
- **Conditions of use relevant to alkali treatment:** Section includes sodium hydroxide treatment limit (**not to exceed 1%**) among permitted modification treatments.[S3]
- **Related GRAS context:** **21 CFR 184.1277 (Dextrin)** is GRAS and describes preparation by drying acid- or alkali-treated starch; this is related context but not identical to E1402 as a separate named additive.[S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- The JECFA toxicological monograph for alkali-treated starches reports that treatment does not result in appreciable chemical difference from normal food constituents and gives an ADI conclusion of **Not limited** in that historical evaluation context.[S2]

### Epidemiological data
- No direct human epidemiology specifically for E1402 was identified in the accessed regulatory sources and targeted PubMed searches.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Alkali treatment can alter starch structure/digestibility characteristics (e.g., increased in vitro enzymatic breakdown reported in a pea starch alkali-treatment study), but this paper is functional/physicochemical, not a direct toxicology hazard signal study.[S9][S10]

### Recent PubMed safety-study signal check
- Targeted PubMed searches for alkali-treated starch plus toxicity/safety terms returned very limited direct hits and no robust recent toxicology dataset specifically on food additive E1402 in humans.
- Example query outputs were low and mostly non-clinical functional/material studies.[S9][S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake:** No E1402-specific contemporary population intake estimate was identified in the accessed EFSA/JECFA/FDA sources.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** JECFA ADI status is NOT SPECIFIED, so classical numerical ADI exceedance calculation is not applicable.[S1]
- **Vulnerable populations:** No dedicated E1402 subgroup risk quantification found. For related modified starches, EFSA (2017) reported high exposure scenarios in infants for authorized modified starches but still concluded no safety concern for that assessed group.[S4]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - JECFA/WHO ADI status is NOT SPECIFIED (historically “Not limited”), and older toxicological commentary indicates little meaningful chemical divergence from normal starch constituents.[S1][S2]
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified for this additive.[S7][S8]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No clearly identified modern, E1402-specific EFSA re-evaluation opinion in accessed sources.
  - Recent direct toxicology literature specific to E1402 appears sparse.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - FDA framework permits relevant modified starch use in food with explicit process limits and conditions (21 CFR 172.892).[S3]
  - JECFA keeps ADI as NOT SPECIFIED with later evaluations focused on specification updates.[S1]
  - No strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal was identified in the required-source sweep.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Evidence base for recent E1402-specific toxicology is thin.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Data-gap argument: limited contemporary E1402-specific toxicology and exposure datasets.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No regulatory red flags equivalent to reduced ADI, confirmed exceedance concern, or major hazard findings were identified.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None identified from the required-source set.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B entry for E1402 identified; no major-jurisdiction ban identified; JECFA ADI status remains NOT SPECIFIED.[S1][S7][S8]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest concrete signals are longstanding JECFA acceptance with ADI not specified and FDA permission of relevant modified-starch use with controls, while direct modern E1402-specific toxicology depth appears limited. That combination supports a low-concern but not “fully resolved” evidence posture.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
