# E304 (Ascorbyl palmitate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E304(i) (ascorbyl palmitate; E304(ii) is ascorbyl stearate)
- **Primary substance researched:** Ascorbyl palmitate
- **CAS:** 137-66-6
- **Chemical class:** Fatty-acid ester of ascorbic acid (lipophilic vitamin C derivative); food additive class: antioxidant
- **Common synonyms:** L-ascorbyl palmitate; 6-palmitoyl-3-keto-L-gulofuranolactone; 2,3-dehydro-L-threo-hexono-1,4-lactone-6-palmitate; vitamin C palmitate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Typically produced by esterification (best described as **semi-synthetic** for food additive use)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antioxidant, especially in lipid-containing matrices.
- **Mechanistic rationale:** EFSA/JECFA discussions indicate ascorbyl palmitate is rapidly hydrolyzed pre-systemically to ascorbic acid and palmitate; antioxidant effect is linked to the ascorbate moiety and lipid solubility.
- **Common food use contexts:** Fat-containing products and formula contexts where oxidative stability is relevant; in EU assessments, notable regulated infant categories are FC 13.1.1 and FC 13.1.5.1.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised in EU food additive framework; latest targeted follow-up opinion identified was **2020** (infants below 16 weeks + follow-up issues from earlier re-evaluation).
- **ADI:** In the 2020 EFSA opinion text, EFSA states the prior ANS panel considered toxicology too limited to establish an ADI for ascorbyl palmitate specifically. The same opinion also records that JECFA had previously set **1.25 mg/kg bw/day** (historical) for ascorbyl palmitate/stearate.
- **Key conclusions (2020):**
  - Ascorbyl palmitate is assumed to hydrolyse pre-systemically to ascorbic acid + palmitate.
  - At MPLs for infant formula (FC 13.1.1) and infant FSMP (FC 13.1.5.1), intake of the metabolites **does not raise safety concerns**.
  - EFSA recommended specification updates (e.g., lower limits for some toxic elements and adding solvent limits).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** **GRAS** in CFR listings.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.3149** (human food): generally recognized as safe when used per good manufacturing practice.
- **Related:** **21 CFR 582.3149** (animal feed): GRAS when used per good manufacturing or feeding practice.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Latest evaluation found:** **2025** (WHO/JECFA database, chemical page for ASCORBYL PALMITATE).
- **Current ADI:** **Not specified** (group ADI not specified for ascorbyl palmitate/ascorbyl stearate or the sum).
- **Important update:** JECFA states it **withdrew the previous ADI of 0-1.25 mg/kg bw/day** based on newer hydrolysis/exposure interpretation.

### IARC
- No IARC monograph classification entry found for ascorbyl palmitate in IARC's current published classification dataset/list.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified from EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Mechanistic evidence (regulatory):** EFSA (2020) and JECFA (2025) both emphasize rapid pre-systemic hydrolysis to ascorbic acid and palmitate, reducing concern for systemic exposure to intact ascorbyl palmitate.
- **Animal/toxicology context:** EFSA 2020 discussion references older JECFA toxicology basis (historical rat chronic study) and also states the EFSA ANS panel considered the specific toxicological package too limited to set a stand-alone ADI for ascorbyl palmitate.
- **Human/clinical evidence:** EFSA 2020 reports infant-formula studies were not designed to isolate ascorbyl palmitate safety and had methodological limitations; evidence considered limited but with no remarkable growth/development deviations reported.
- **Recent PubMed records:**
  - 2025 EFSA FEEDAP renewal assessment (includes ascorbyl palmitate among vitamin C-source additives) concluded continued safety for target species/consumers/environment.
  - 2024 Food Chemistry liposome study reported non-toxicity in its in vitro profile, but this is formulation-specific and not a definitive oral-food-additive safety study for general populations.
- **Epidemiology:** No robust population epidemiology signal specific to oral E304 intake was identified in the reviewed sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Infants below 16 weeks (EFSA 2020):**
  - FC 13.1.1 infant formula: **2 mg/kg bw/day (mean)**, **2.6 mg/kg bw/day (high)**.
  - FC 13.1.5.1 infant FSMP: **20 mg/kg bw/day (mean)**, **26 mg/kg bw/day (high)**.
- **General-population context from EFSA 2020 discussion:** cites prior ANS brand-loyal P95 exposure for E304 up to **9.9 mg/kg bw/day in children**.
- **ADI exceedance interpretation:**
  - Against the **historical** 0-1.25 mg/kg bw/day benchmark, some high-intake scenarios would exceed.
  - Current JECFA position is ADI not specified, and EFSA 2020 concluded no safety concern at evaluated infant MPL scenarios.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Infants consuming FSMP at high intakes are the most exposure-intensive subgroup in the reviewed EFSA data.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Hydrolysis to common nutrients (ascorbic acid + palmitate); EFSA 2020 and JECFA 2025 no-safety-concern framing under assessed conditions.
  - Evidence against: Regulatory discussions still note limitations in additive-specific toxicology and evidence gaps.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved in EU and US; GRAS listing in 21 CFR; latest JECFA evaluation gives ADI not specified; no IARC classification signal found.
  - Evidence against: Exposure can be high in infant FSMP scenarios; dataset for additive-specific human safety endpoints is not robust.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Historical ADI benchmark (0-1.25) can be exceeded in some modeled groups; EFSA documented data limitations.
  - Evidence against: Most recent EFSA/JECFA conclusions do not indicate active safety concern at regulated uses.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Very limited.
  - Evidence against: No major bans identified, no IARC carcinogenic classification found, and major regulators continue to permit use.

### 2. Rationale
Primary drivers are the convergence of modern EFSA/JECFA hydrolysis-based interpretation and continued regulatory acceptance, offset by limited additive-specific toxicology depth and high-exposure infant scenarios that require ongoing specification and exposure management.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
