# E551 (Silicon dioxide) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E551 (INS 551)
- **Name:** Silicon dioxide (food additive grade; typically synthetic amorphous silica, SAS)
- **CAS number(s):** 7631-86-9; 112945-52-5
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic silicon oxide; anti-caking carrier/filler class
- **Common synonyms:** Silicon dioxide, silica, synthetic amorphous silica, colloidal silicon dioxide
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** for food additive use (manufactured forms in 21 CFR 172.480 include vapor-phase hydrolysis products and precipitation from sodium silicate)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Fine silica particles adsorb moisture/oils and reduce interparticle adhesion, which improves powder flow and prevents caking.
- **Common food categories:** Powdered seasonings, powdered mixes, powdered sweeteners, supplements, and other dry foods requiring free-flowing properties.
- **Regulatory function language (US):** Used as an anti-caking agent under 21 CFR 172.480.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **2018 re-evaluation (E551):** EFSA concluded E551 was *not of safety concern at reported uses and use levels* and that there was **no need for a numerical ADI**.
  - **2024 follow-up (nano fraction):** EFSA assessed nano-sized particles from E551 and reported **significant concern** for large-bowel effects in certain populations/food categories; EFSA stated **no safe levels were identified from available data** in that assessment context.
  - **Approval status:** E551 remains authorized in the EU, but with updated safety concern context from the 2024 particle-focused assessment.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Direct food use:** **21 CFR 172.480** permits silicon dioxide as an anti-caking agent, limited to **not more than 2% by weight of the food**.
  - **GRAS context:** **21 CFR 182.90** lists "silicon dioxides" among substances migrating to food from paper/paperboard that are generally recognized as safe for intended use.
  - **Interpretation:** In Title 21, E551 has explicit approved direct-use conditions (172.480) and a separate GRAS context for packaging migration (182.90).
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - JECFA database entry for Silicon dioxide (INS 551) reports **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED** (historically established for silicon dioxide and certain silicates at the 29th JECFA, 1985).
  - Database also indicates **Last evaluated: 2017** (specification-focused update context).
- **IARC:**
  - IARC classification resources identify **crystalline silica dust (inhaled in occupational settings)** as carcinogenic (Group 1).
  - No separate IARC carcinogenic classification was identified for **food-grade synthetic amorphous silica (E551) by oral dietary exposure**.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban specific to E551 identified in the checked EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline (EFSA/JECFA):**
  - EFSA 2018 found no safety concern at then-reported uses/use levels and no need for a numerical ADI.
  - JECFA maintained ADI "not specified" for the silicon dioxide/silicates group.
- **Recent concern signal (EFSA 2024):**
  - Particle-focused EFSA 2024 assessment found concern for some exposure scenarios/populations and noted inability to identify safe levels from available data for that endpoint framework.
- **Animal/mechanistic evidence (recent PubMed):**
  - 2024 mouse study (Environmental Health Perspectives) reported chronic oral food-grade SiO2 exposure disrupted oral tolerance markers and worsened gluten-related immunopathology in a susceptible model.
  - 2025 mouse reproductive/developmental oral toxicity study (up to 1000 mg/kg/day) reported no significant adverse fertility or early embryonic-development effects.
- **Cell/in vitro evidence:**
  - 2025 in vitro digestive-cell study on food-grade E551 reported no acute cytotoxicity under tested conditions but did report sub-cytotoxic morphological/secretory changes.
  - 2025 in vitro digestion/Caco-2 study of confectionary-derived TiO2/SiO2 reported limited nanoparticle bioaccessible fractions and cytotoxic effects mainly attributed to matrix components rather than particles.
- **Epidemiology:**
  - No strong, additive-specific prospective human epidemiological signal was identified in the reviewed PubMed set.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory exposure concern framing:**
  - EFSA 2024 flagged concern in infants, toddlers, children, and some high-consuming groups for specific food categories in the particle-focused assessment.
- **Typical concentration context from published study:**
  - A PubMed-indexed food survey study reported measurable silica content in sampled commercial foods (microgram-per-gram range), confirming dietary presence.
- **Absorption/bioaccessibility context:**
  - A 2025 in vitro digestion study reported low measured bioaccessible fractions (~5%) for particle types tested in intestinal extracts, though this does not replace in vivo human exposure assessment.
- **ADI exceedance risk statement:**
  - There is **no numerical ADI** from EFSA for E551 and JECFA uses "ADI not specified"; however, EFSA 2024 identified endpoint-specific concern in some population/intake scenarios, so practical exceedance risk is better framed as **possible MOE-based concern in vulnerable/high-exposure groups** rather than classic ADI exceedance.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants, toddlers, children, and high consumers of relevant food categories, per EFSA 2024 concern language.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: longstanding approvals; JECFA ADI "not specified"; EFSA 2018 no safety concern at reported uses.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA 2024 reported significant concern in some scenarios and inability to identify safe levels from available data for assessed endpoints; recent animal immunology findings raise mechanistic caution.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: U.S. and EU authorization remains in place; clear U.S. use limit (2%); no consistent severe toxicity signal across all recent studies; one modern oral mouse study found no reproductive/developmental toxicity up to 1000 mg/kg/day.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA 2024 concern signal is stronger than a "minor high-dose only" caveat and indicates uncertainty for some real-world exposure patterns.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: approved additive with important caveats; EFSA 2024 concern in younger populations/high consumers; credible animal evidence of immune-tolerance disruption in chronic oral exposure model; mixed mechanistic in vitro findings.
  - Evidence against this tier: absence of broad bans, lack of IARC oral-food-additive carcinogenic classification, and presence of studies showing no major toxicity endpoints under certain designs.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA 2024 inability to derive safe levels for assessed particle-based endpoints suggests non-trivial uncertainty.
  - Evidence against this tier: no major jurisdictional ban, no direct IARC high-risk classification for food-grade oral E551, and heterogeneous evidence with several no-major-effect findings.

2. **Rationale**
- E551 has a mixed profile: established regulatory acceptance and long use history, but newer particle-focused evidence (especially EFSA 2024 and some immune/mechanistic studies) introduces population-specific concern that is stronger than "minimal concern" yet not strong enough to support a "high" tier.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
