# E403 (Ammonium alginate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E403 (INS 403).
- **Name:** Ammonium alginate.
- **CAS number:** 9005-34-9.
- **Chemical class:** Ammonium salt of alginic acid; hydrocolloid polysaccharide (alginate).
- **Common synonyms:** ammonium alginate; ammonium salt of alginic acid; INS 403; E403.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (alginic acid is extracted from brown seaweeds; ammonium alginate is produced by neutralizing alginic acid with ammonium/pH-control agents).

**Evidence:** EFSA identity table and description (2017), JECFA chemical record (WHO database), and 21 CFR section 184.1133.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** High-viscosity hydrocolloid behavior; water binding and thickening; stabilizes emulsions; forms gels/structured matrices.
- **Functional classes:** Emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener (JECFA).
- **Common food categories:**
  - EU: broadly authorized food additive categories (E400-E404 group; EFSA re-evaluation context).
  - US (21 CFR section 184.1133 specific limits): confections/frostings, fats and oils, gelatins/puddings, gravies/sauces, jams/jellies, sweet sauces, and low-level use in other foods.

**Evidence:** EFSA physicochemical/function narrative; JECFA functional class; eCFR use table.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Re-evaluated in **2017** as part of E400-E404; concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** and **no safety concern** at refined exposure for reported uses. EFSA also stated data were insufficient to adequately assess safety for infants/young children consuming FSMP categories 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2.
- **FDA/CFR (US):** Listed in **21 CFR 184.1133** under substances affirmed as GRAS in Part 184 (see 21 CFR 184.1). Use is limited by food category and maximum percentage (e.g., 0.4-0.5% in named categories; 0.1% in all other categories for specified functions).
- **JECFA/WHO:** WHO JECFA database entry for ammonium alginate shows **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED**, **evaluation year: 1992**.
- **IARC:** Not listed as an IARC-classified agent in the current IARC "Agents classified by the IARC Monographs" dataset (inference from no match for "alginate/alginic/9005-34-9").
- **Notable bans/restrictions:** No broad jurisdiction-wide ban identified for E403 itself. EU decision history includes suspension of jelly mini-cups containing certain gel-forming additives (including E403) due choking risk (not a total ban on E403 across foods).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology (EFSA-reviewed):** No adverse effects in subchronic rodent studies up to 13,500 mg/kg bw/day sodium alginate; no carcinogenic concern in long-term studies up to 37,500 mg/kg bw/day sodium alginate in mice.
- **Genotoxicity (EFSA):** No concern for genotoxicity for E400-E404; read-across applied to ammonium alginate.
- **Human data (EFSA-reviewed):** Oral sodium alginate at 175-200 mg/kg bw/day was well tolerated in healthy adults in short-term studies; therapeutic infant regimens around 417-834 mg/kg bw/day reported GI adverse effects (constipation, diarrhea, obstruction, bezoar) in medicinal context.
- **Recent PubMed signal check:** Direct modern toxicology specifically for ammonium alginate as a food additive is limited; recent "ammonium alginate" literature is largely materials/aerogel engineering. A 2021 mouse study reported sodium-binding/salt-excretion effects for ammonium and calcium alginate (not a classic chronic-toxicity study).

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population refined estimate (EFSA):** up to **208 mg/kg bw/day** (high-end infant estimate in non-brand-loyal scenario for E400-E404).
- **Special medical purpose foods (EFSA):** up to **290.4 mg/kg bw/day** (p95, toddlers, brand-loyal scenario) for specific FSMP categories.
- **ADI exceedance framing:** EFSA/JECFA ADI is non-numerical ("no numerical ADI" / "not specified"), so classic numeric exceedance is not applicable; risk management concern is mainly vulnerable infants/young children in FSMP contexts and GI effects at therapeutic-dose ranges.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** infants/young children using FSMP products, especially with underlying GI/medical conditions.

**Evidence URL:** https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7010146/fullTextXML

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA and JECFA both concluded non-numerical ADI ("no need for numerical ADI" / "not specified"); no genotoxicity/carcinogenic concern in reviewed data; substance class derived from natural alginic acid.
  - Evidence against: EFSA identified a clear data gap for certain infant FSMP uses and highlighted side-effect context at therapeutic infant doses; specification recommendations (toxic elements/formaldehyde/protein considerations) indicate non-zero risk-management needs.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: US GRAS framework with specific CFR use limits; EFSA positive conclusion for reported uses; JECFA ADI not specified; no strong modern signal of severe harm at food-use levels.
  - Evidence against: Limited contemporary additive-specific human safety studies for ammonium alginate itself.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Presence of caveats for vulnerable infant FSMP populations and limited dedicated modern toxicology for E403 specifically.
  - Evidence against: No major regulator has withdrawn/ban-classified E403 broadly; no IARC carcinogenic classification entry; EFSA overall conclusion for general use remains no safety concern.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: none identified from EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC evidence set.
  - Evidence against: Not banned across major jurisdictions; no IARC 2A/2B/1 listing for this agent; positive regulatory evaluations persist.

### 2. Rationale
The main weight of evidence supports a low-to-limited concern profile at authorized food-use levels, with the key caution concentrated in specific infant medical-use contexts and quality/specification control.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
