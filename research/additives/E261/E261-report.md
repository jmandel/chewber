# E261 (Potassium acetate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E261 (specifically INS/EU form commonly shown as **E261(i)**) [S1][S7]
- **Name:** Potassium acetate [S1][S7]
- **CAS number(s):** 127-08-2 [S1][S7]
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of acetic acid (short-chain carboxylate salt) [S1][S7]
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium acetate; E 261(i); INS 261(i) [S1][S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercial food additive use is primarily **synthetic/manufactured** (regulatory additive form), while acetate itself is a normal endogenous/metabolic anion. This origin classification is an inference from regulatory context and naming conventions. [S1][S2][S7]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological roles:** Acidity regulator and preservative (JECFA functional class). [S7]
- **Additional role in US CFR context:** Listed among synthetic flavoring substances/adjuvants under 21 CFR 172.515. [S4]
- **Mechanism of action:** Weak-acid/acetate preservation effect (pH and undissociated acid antimicrobial effects) and formulation acidification/flavor adjustment; supported by food microbiology studies using potassium acetate blends. [S10][S11][S12]
- **Common food categories observed in sources:**
  - Processed meats (e.g., bologna slices)
  - Poultry/chicken products
  - Broader flavoring/adjuvant use under 21 CFR 172.515 [S4][S10][S11]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - Open EFSA lists **"Re-evaluation of E261 Potassium acetate" (EFSA-Q-2011-00593)** as **Ongoing Risk Assessment** (internal status: Planned), with last modified date 2026-01-17. [S2][S3]
  - EFSA comment states the additive is **already authorised in the EU** and re-evaluation is delayed due to workload; one grouped output is foreseen with E260/E262/E263-related questions. [S3]
  - **ADI (EFSA):** No final EFSA ADI value published in this ongoing re-evaluation entry. [S3]
  - **Evaluation year:** No final EFSA opinion year available yet for this question (mandate initiated in 2011; still ongoing as of 2026-01-17). [S2][S3]

- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - Potassium acetate appears in **21 CFR 172.515** (Synthetic flavoring substances and adjuvants). [S4]
  - Conditions include minimum quantity needed for intended effect and compliance with GMP. [S4]
  - In the reviewed Title 21 text, potassium acetate was **not found as a dedicated GRAS listing** in parts 182 or 184; this indicates CFR treatment here is as an approved use under 172.515 rather than a specific GRAS affirmation entry for potassium acetate. (Inference from direct text search results.) [S4][S5][S6]

  - Chemical record: **POTASSIUM ACETATE**, ID 3218. [S7][S8]

  - **Evaluation year:** 1973 (with maintenance note in 1997). [S7]

  - In IARC's classifications infrastructure and agent list data, no direct classification entry for **potassium acetate** was found. [S8][S9]

- **Notable bans:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** JECFA assigned a group ADI of "not limited" for acetic acid and potassium/sodium salts and maintained this position (1997), supporting low concern at normal food use levels. [S7]
- **Recent PubMed evidence profile:** Recent indexed studies involving potassium acetate are mainly food preservation efficacy studies (pathogen control/shelf-life) rather than chronic human toxicity studies.
  - 2023 bologna study: blends with potassium acetate/sodium diacetate controlled Listeria/Salmonella during storage. [S10]
  - 2022 chicken study: organic acid salt blend including potassium acetate/diacetate improved microbial quality/shelf-life metrics. [S11]
  - 2016 electro-activation study: potassium acetate solution showed antibacterial activity against E. coli O157:H7. [S12]
- **Epidemiological data specific to E261 as consumed food additive:** Not identified in this search set.
- **Mechanistic concerns:** No strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal specific to potassium acetate from the reviewed regulatory sources; IARC has no dedicated classification entry for this substance. [S8][S9]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** A current dedicated EFSA exposure estimate for E261 was not located in a completed EFSA re-evaluation output because the re-evaluation entry remains ongoing. [S2][S3]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA uses "not limited" for this acetate group, so a numeric ADI exceedance calculation is not applicable from that framework. [S7]
  - Practical exposure risk is generally interpreted as low at normal use levels, but high total potassium intake may still matter in potassium-sensitive populations (clinical caution inference, not a direct E261 epidemiology finding).
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Individuals requiring potassium intake restriction (e.g., severe renal impairment) may merit extra caution in high-potassium dietary contexts.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: JECFA group ADI is "not limited"; no IARC listing for potassium acetate; acetate is a simple endogenous metabolite-related ion. [S7][S9]
  - Evidence against: EFSA's dedicated E261 re-evaluation is still ongoing, so an updated EU risk conclusion is pending. [S2][S3]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Regulatory acceptance exists (EU authorised status per EFSA question comment; US listing in 21 CFR 172.515; JECFA "not limited"). No strong hazard signal emerged in reviewed sources. [S3][S4][S7]
  - Evidence against: Modern, additive-specific chronic human exposure datasets are limited in the retrieved literature.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Data-gap uncertainty (ongoing EFSA process; limited direct epidemiology) could justify caution.
  - Evidence against: No evidence in reviewed sources of major regulatory downgrades, bans, or carcinogenic classification for potassium acetate itself. [S3][S9]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None identified from the reviewed regulatory/scientific set.
  - Evidence against: Not banned in reviewed jurisdictions; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification entry specific to potassium acetate; JECFA ADI not limited. [S7][S9]

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest anchors are JECFA's "not limited" group ADI and absence of IARC classification signal for potassium acetate, balanced against incomplete/ongoing EFSA re-evaluation and limited recent additive-specific long-term human data.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
