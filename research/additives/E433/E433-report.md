# E433 (Polysorbate 80) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E433 (INS 433)
- **Name:** Polysorbate 80
- **CAS number:** 9005-65-6
- **Chemical class:** Non-ionic surfactant; polyoxyethylene sorbitan fatty-acid ester
- **Common synonyms:** Polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monooleate; polyoxyethylene sorbitan monooleate; Polysorbate 80
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** (manufactured from sorbitol/sorbitan and oleic acid, then ethoxylated with ethylene oxide)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Emulsifier.
- **Mechanism:** Amphiphilic surfactant that lowers oil-water interfacial tension, improving emulsion formation/stability and dispersion of lipophilic ingredients.
- **Common food categories (from CFR conditions of use):**
  - Ice cream/frozen desserts
  - Pickles/pickle products
  - Vitamin/mineral preparations
  - Shortenings and edible oils
  - Whipped edible oil toppings
  - Gelatin desserts and mixes

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Approved food additive (E433) under the EFSA re-evaluation of polysorbates (E432-E436). EFSA set a **group ADI of 25 mg/kg bw/day** and reported refined exposure estimates below this ADI for assessed population groups at reported uses. **Evaluation year: 2015 (EFSA Journal opinion).**
- **FDA/CFR (US):** **Approved** as a direct food additive in **21 CFR § 172.840 (Polysorbate 80)** with detailed compositional specs and specific use limits (e.g., 0.1% in some frozen desserts; ppm and mg/day limits in several applications).
  - **GRAS status:** Not listed as GRAS in Part 182/184 checks from the eCFR title-21 API snapshot used here; regulated as a permitted food additive under Part 172.
- **JECFA/WHO:** ADI **0-25 mg/kg bw** (as total polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan esters); **evaluation year: 1973** (meeting 17).
- **IARC:** No monograph classification identified for polysorbate 80 in IARC agent listings.
- **Notable bans:** No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the checked EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal/mechanistic concerns (gut/metabolic):**
  - Mouse work has associated dietary emulsifiers including polysorbate 80 with microbiota shifts, mucus-layer effects, and pro-inflammatory/metabolic phenotypes.
  - Recent studies (2023-2025) report barrier impairment, microbiome-mediated effects, and metabolic/senescence-related pathways in preclinical models.
- **Human evidence:**
  - A 2024 IBD cohort analysis reported higher total emulsifier exposure in IBD vs controls, but **recorded P80 exposure was absent** in that dataset, indicating limited direct human intake evidence for P80-specific harm in that cohort.
- **Carcinogenicity classification:**
  - No IARC classification identified for polysorbate 80.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark:** EFSA group ADI 25 mg/kg bw/day; JECFA ADI 0-25 mg/kg bw.
- **Population intake vs ADI:** EFSA re-evaluation summary indicates refined dietary exposures did not exceed ADI in assessed groups under reported use levels.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Currently appears low in general population scenarios from EFSA refined estimates, but uncertainty remains for high processed-food patterns and cumulative emulsifier effects.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** Individuals with pre-existing gut inflammatory conditions, and populations with high ultra-processed food intake, based on mechanistic literature rather than confirmed causal human evidence.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Supports: Regulatory approvals exist; no IARC classification.
  - Against: Multiple preclinical studies show plausible biological effects (microbiome/barrier/inflammatory/metabolic pathways), so "no credible evidence of harm" is too strong.
- **limited**
  - Supports: EFSA/JECFA ADIs exist; EFSA refined exposure reported below ADI; FDA allows uses with strict conditions.
  - Against: Newer mechanistic and animal literature raises non-trivial concern beyond minor high-dose artifacts.
- **moderate**
  - Supports: Approved additive but with caveat profile: substantial mechanistic/animal signal on gut and metabolism; evidence uncertainty in humans; no ban but persistent concern.
  - Against: Lack of robust causal human evidence and no major regulator withdrawal.
- **high**
  - Supports: Some preclinical findings are concerning.
  - Against: No major bans, no IARC 2A/2B classification, and regulators still permit use with ADI/conditions.

2. **Rationale**
- Regulatory frameworks continue to permit E433 with quantitative intake limits, but the mechanistic literature (especially gut microbiome/barrier endpoints) is strong enough to justify caution beyond a "limited concern only" framing.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
