# E463 (Hydroxypropyl Cellulose) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E463
- **Name:** Hydroxypropyl cellulose
- **CAS number:** 9004-64-2
- **Chemical class:** Modified cellulose (cellulose ether)
- **Common synonyms:** Hydroxypropyl ether of cellulose; Cellulose hydroxypropyl ether; Modified cellulose; low-substituted hydroxypropyl cellulose (specific grade in CFR)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (cellulose backbone chemically modified with hydroxypropyl substitution; described in CFR as a cellulose ether with propylene glycol groups)

Evidence:
- JECFA chemical entry lists name, synonyms, CAS, INS 463, and functional class (WHO/JECFA database, 2026 access: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/609)
- CFR identity language for hydroxypropyl substitution is in 21 CFR 172.870 (CFR XML mirror: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title21-vol3/xml/CFR-2024-title21-vol3.xml)

## Function in Food
Hydroxypropyl cellulose is used as a **thickener/stabilizer-type hydrocolloid**. Its cellulose-ether structure allows water binding, viscosity increase, suspension/emulsion stabilization, and film formation.

Common use roles:
- Emulsifier
- Stabilizer
- Thickener
- Glazing/film-forming functions
- Binder for dietary supplements (US)

Evidence:
- JECFA functional class includes emulsifier, glazing agent, stabilizer, thickener (WHO/JECFA database: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/609)
- US CFR conditions specify use as emulsifier, film former, protective colloid, stabilizer, suspending agent, thickener, and binder/disintegrator in supplement tablets/wafers (21 CFR 172.870 text in govinfo XML and Cornell e-CFR mirror: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title21-vol3/xml/CFR-2024-title21-vol3.xml ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/172.870)

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and toxicology evidence
- EFSA re-evaluation (2018) for celluloses including E463 reports low acute toxicity, no genotoxic concern via read-across and available studies, and no carcinogenic properties signal in reviewed chronic studies.
- Reported chronic-study NOAEL values in the EFSA review reached up to ~9,000 mg/kg bw/day for reviewed celluloses.

Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7009359/

### Human/clinical and mechanistic observations
- JECFA monograph notes GI effects consistent with bulking fiber behavior (laxation/constipation variability at high intakes), with ADI remaining “not specified”.
- A PubMed case report (2021) describes fixed drug eruption associated with hydroxypropyl cellulose in a drug-product context (rare hypersensitivity signal; case-level evidence).
- Recent PubMed records include non-food contexts (vaccine adjuvant/mice; pharmaceutical excipient systems), which are informative but not direct dietary-risk evidence.

Sources:
- http://www.inchem.org/documents/jecfa/jecmono/v26je08.htm

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41327585/

### Epidemiological data
- No strong population-level epidemiological harm signal specific to dietary E463 was identified in reviewed regulatory sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA’s cellulose-group assessment (including E463) estimated high refined exposure around **506 mg/kg bw/day** at the 95th percentile in toddlers (brand-loyal scenario) and still concluded no need for a numerical ADI.
- EFSA also cited an indicative total exposure range where conclusions remained valid (~660–900 mg/kg bw/day for microcrystalline/powdered/modified celluloses).
- In infants/toddlers FSMP scenarios, high exposures were largely tied to **E466** assumptions in specific categories, not clearly attributable to E463 alone.

Implication for E463:
- Numerical ADI exceedance cannot be computed because ADI is “not specified/no numerical ADI”.
- Practical concern is mainly GI tolerance at high bolus intakes and rare hypersensitivity reports.

Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7009359/

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free placement:**
  - EFSA: no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses.
  - JECFA: ADI not specified.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified.
- **Evidence against risk_free placement:**
  - Hydroxypropyl cellulose is a chemically modified polymer (not an endogenous molecule).
  - Some high-intake GI effects (laxation potential) are acknowledged in JECFA context.
  - Rare case-report hypersensitivity signal exists.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited placement:**
  - Regulatory approvals in EU/US and JECFA with favorable toxicology profile.
  - Conditions of use largely GMP/quantum-satis based, without major red-flag restrictions.
  - Safety concerns are minor and mostly at high intake or idiosyncratic (rare allergy case reports).
- **Evidence against limited placement:**
  - Limited modern, E463-specific long-term human epidemiology.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate placement:**
  - Some uncertainty in subgroup exposure modeling and read-across dependence in EFSA review.
  - High-intake GI effects are biologically plausible.
- **Evidence against moderate placement:**
  - No EFSA ADI tightening for E463 specifically.
  - No consistent genotoxic/carcinogenic signal and no regulatory downgrading.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high placement:**
  - None identified in reviewed sources.
- **Evidence against high placement:**
  - No major-jurisdiction bans found.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification found for this agent.
  - EFSA/JECFA conclusions are overall favorable.

### 2) Rationale
The weight of evidence is strongly regulatory-favorable (EFSA/JECFA/FDA-CFR) with no convincing carcinogenic or genotoxic signal and with ADI frameworks indicating low concern. Remaining issues are mostly non-severe: possible laxation at high intakes and rare hypersensitivity case reports.

### 3) Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
