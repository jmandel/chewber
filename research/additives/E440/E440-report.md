# E440 (Pectin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E440 (E440i pectin; E440ii amidated pectin).
- **Primary substance name:** Pectin / pectins.
- **CAS numbers identified in regulatory texts:**
  - 9000-69-5 (pectins)
  - 9000-59-8 (sodium pectate/salt form referenced in CFR text)
- **Chemical class:** Complex high-molecular-weight plant polysaccharides, chiefly partially methylated polygalacturonic acid units and related salts/amides.
- **Common synonyms:** pectins, amidated pectin, pectinic acids, pectinates, INS 440.
- **Origin:** Natural (commercially extracted from citrus peel, apple pomace, or beet pulp; then purified/precipitated).

**Sources:**
- 21 CFR text (GovInfo XML, 2025 edition): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol3/xml/CFR-2025-title21-vol3-sec184-1588.xml
- WHO/JECFA chemical record (PECTINS): https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3043

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrated pectin polysaccharide chains increase viscosity and form gel-like structures, enabling texture setting and stabilization in aqueous foods.
- **Regulatory functional uses:** Emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener.
- **Common food categories:** Jams/jellies/fruit preparations, acidified dairy/fruit beverages, confectionery gels, and infant formula thickening systems.

**Sources:**
- WHO/JECFA functional class listing: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3043

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved for food additive use in the general population; follow-up assessment identified infant-specific risk management needs.
- **ADI:** "Not specified" / no numerical ADI needed for general population (2017 re-evaluation).
- **Evaluation years used:** 2017 (major re-evaluation), 2021 (infants <16 weeks follow-up).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2017: no safety concern for general population; no need for numerical ADI.
  - 2021 follow-up: for infants below 16 weeks, margin of safety was below 1 in some scenarios at MPLs; methanol formation at MPLs could cause adverse effects; EFSA recommended reducing MPLs in infant food categories 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2017 abstract reports:
  - no indication of genotoxicity (data limited),
  - no adverse effects in chronic rat study up to 5,000 mg/kg bw/day,
  - no treatment-related effects in one-generation rat reproductive study (pAOS) up to 6,200 mg/kg bw/day.
- A neonatal pig study (Regul Toxicol Pharmacol, 2020) found 0.2% dietary pectin (~704 mg/kg bw/day) was safe and well tolerated over 3 weeks, while 1% reduced growth/feed intake/digestibility.

### Human and clinical evidence
- EFSA 2017 abstract cites human intake of 36 g/day (~515 mg/kg bw/day) for 6 weeks without adverse effects.
- Infant anti-regurgitation studies (Acta Paediatr, 2020; pooled trial summary) reported normal growth and safety across pectin-containing thickened formulas.

### Epidemiological data
- No strong population-level epidemiological signal for carcinogenicity or systemic toxicity identified in the sources reviewed.

### Mechanistic concerns
- EFSA 2021 and JECFA infant-focused assessments indicate risk concern at high infant formula use levels due to exposure margins and methanol generated from pectin demethylation, especially in infants <16 weeks.

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population:** EFSA 2017 reported exposure estimates up to **442 mg/kg bw/day** (95th percentile toddlers, brand-loyal scenario) without concluding general-population safety concern.
- **Infant-specific high-risk scenarios:**
  - JECFA 2014 considered 0.5% use in infant formula of concern.
  - EFSA 2021 found margin of safety below 1 in some infant scenarios at MPL and flagged methanol-dose concern.
- **ADI exceedance framing:** Because EFSA/JECFA use "ADI not specified" for general use, classic numerical ADI exceedance is not the main concern; risk management is instead concentrated on infant formula use levels and specifications.
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants below 16 weeks; potentially high-consumption infants on specialized formulas.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: natural dietary polysaccharide; EFSA/JECFA ADI "not specified"; no major genotoxic/chronic toxicity signal in reviewed studies; FDA GRAS with GMP conditions.
  - Evidence against: infant-specific scenarios (EFSA 2021/JECFA 2014) show concern at high permitted formula levels; methanol-related concern and MOE <1 scenarios for infants.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: broad regulatory acceptance (EU/FDA/JECFA), GRAS conditions, no general-population safety concern, and tolerability at typical additive ranges.
  - Evidence against: concerns are stronger than "minor only" for some infant scenarios because both EFSA and JECFA flagged potential adverse-effect conditions at higher infant use levels.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: approved additive but with important caveats in vulnerable population (infants <16 weeks), including low MOE scenarios and recommendation to reduce MPL; dose-dependent adverse findings at higher pectin in neonatal pig model.
  - Evidence against: these concerns are population- and exposure-scenario-specific, not broad general-population harm.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: infant high-exposure concern exists.
  - Evidence against: no IARC carcinogenic classification found, no major-jurisdiction ban, and no regulatory conclusion of overall unsafety for general use.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest current evidence supports overall safety at regulated general-use levels, but not an unconditional "risk-free" classification because infant-specific high-exposure scenarios have triggered formal concern and risk-management recommendations (EFSA 2021; JECFA 2014/2016 context).

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate** (driven by infant-specific caveats despite favorable general-population profile).
