# E162 (Beetroot red / Betanin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E162 (EU), INS 162 (JECFA/WHO).
- **Primary names**: Beetroot red, beet red, betanin.
- **CAS numbers**: 57917-55-2; 7659-95-2 (betanine/betanin).
- **Chemical class**: Betalain pigment (primarily betacyanins; principal compound betanin).
- **Origin**: Natural (extracted from edible beetroot; Beta vulgaris).

Evidence:
- WHO/JECFA chemical entries list INS 162, names/synonyms, CAS numbers, and identify betanine as principal coloring compound (JECFA database, 1987 evaluation history):
  - https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/877
  - https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/878

## Function in Food
- **Primary function**: Color additive (red-purple coloring).
- **Mechanism**: Betanin/betacyanins absorb visible light and impart red-violet hue in aqueous food systems; used as a natural color.
- **Common food categories**:
  - EU context: EFSA considered exposure across food categories and an extension request for hard candy at 300 mg/kg.
  - US context: permitted for coloring foods generally (subject to GMP and standards-of-identity restrictions).

Evidence:
- EFSA extension context for hard candy at 300 mg/kg: https://open.efsa.europa.eu/study-inventory/EFSA-Q-2014-00557
- US use condition in 21 CFR §73.40 from eCFR XML: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Approved/authorised as E162 in EU food additive framework; EFSA re-evaluated safety.
- **Latest core re-evaluation**: 2015 (EFSA Journal, Question EFSA-Q-2011-00807).
- **ADI**: EFSA concluded **no numerical ADI could be established** for beetroot red (E162), and concluded no safety concern under refined exposure assumptions.
- **Exposure context from EFSA re-evaluation summary**: refined exposure up to about **15.3 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers** at high level.
- **Additional EFSA support publication (2016)**: extension-of-use assessment for hard candy concluded **no safety concern** at requested levels.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status**: Not handled as a GRAS determination for color use; it is regulated as a **color additive exempt from certification**.
- **CFR citation**: **21 CFR §73.40 — Dehydrated beets (beet powder)**.
- **Conditions of use** (from current eCFR XML, 2026-02-25):
  - may be safely used for coloring foods generally in amounts consistent with GMP;
  - not for foods with standards of identity unless added color is authorized by those standards;
  - certification not necessary (exempt from certification under section 721(c)).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology evidence
- **EFSA re-evaluation (2015)**: no numerical ADI established; conclusion indicates no safety concern for current refined exposure scenario.
- **EFSA extension assessment (2016)**: no safety concern for proposed hard-candy use.
- **JECFA (1987)**: ADI not specified with explicit nitrate-specification caveat.

### PubMed recent evidence (safety-relevant)
- Recent literature is weighted toward pharmacology/functional-health endpoints, not classical long-term additive toxicology programs.
- A recent narrative review reports betalain consumption as generally safe and without major adverse effects/allergic reactions reported (review-level evidence).
- A randomized controlled trial using a betalain-rich food matrix (keywords include E162) reports vascular outcomes and does not flag specific safety signals in abstract-level reporting.

Evidence:
- Narrative review (2024): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39682981/
- Randomized crossover trial (2022; keyword includes E162): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35265960/

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate context**: refined high-level exposure reported up to ~15.3 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers in re-evaluation summaries.
- **ADI exceedance framing**: numeric ADI is not established (EFSA no numerical ADI; JECFA “not specified”), so conventional exceedance ratio cannot be calculated.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - toddlers/high consumers (higher modeled color-intake scenario);
  - infants/young children in relation to nitrate-specification caveat from JECFA.

Evidence:
- EFSA re-evaluation summary page: https://open.efsa.europa.eu/study-inventory/EFSA-Q-2011-00807
- JECFA comment and ADI page: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/877

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: natural pigment source; JECFA ADI “not specified”; no IARC listing identified; no major bans found.
  - Evidence against: EFSA did not set a numerical ADI; JECFA specifically flags nitrate-specification management (infant/young-child relevance).

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved/authorised in EU and US regulatory frameworks; FDA/eCFR has defined GMP-based conditions; EFSA and JECFA conclusions do not indicate current-use safety crisis.
  - Evidence against: modern long-term, additive-specific human toxicology evidence is limited; much recent literature is efficacy-focused rather than dedicated safety testing.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA did not derive a numerical ADI; higher modeled intake in toddlers; nitrate caveat from JECFA may require tighter manufacturing/specification control.
  - Evidence against: regulators still conclude no safety concern at current/refined uses; no corroborating IARC classification/bans.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: none substantial from reviewed regulatory sources.
  - Evidence against: no IARC Group 1/2A/2B entry identified for this additive, no major-jurisdiction ban found, and no EFSA/JECFA conclusion indicating inability to assure safety at current conditions.

### 2. Rationale
Key drivers are: (a) continued authorization in major frameworks (EU/US), (b) EFSA/JECFA non-alarm conclusions, and (c) caveats around lack of numerical ADI and nitrate-specification control, especially for young-population dietary contexts.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**.
