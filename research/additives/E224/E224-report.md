# E224 (Potassium metabisulfite) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E224.
- **Primary name:** Potassium metabisulfite.
- **CAS number(s):** 16731-55-8.
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfiting agent (metabisulfite/disulfite salt).
- **Common synonyms:** potassium metabisulphite, potassium disulfite, dipotassium pyrosulfite, INS 224.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** (manufactured additive salt).

Evidence: PubChem CID 28019 lists the name, CAS, formula and synonyms ([S1]).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative (antimicrobial/anti-browning sulfiting agent).
- **Secondary function:** Antioxidant/reducing agent in susceptible products.
- **Mechanism of action:** In aqueous/acid food systems, sulfiting agents generate sulfite/bisulfite/sulfur dioxide species that reduce oxidation and inhibit browning and some microbes (regulatory usage context for sulfites/E220-228) ([S2], [S3], [S4]).
- **Common food categories (from dietary exposure contributors and legal restrictions):** dried fruit, wine, some nonalcoholic beverages/fruit juices/soft drinks, sausages, processed potatoes; restricted uses in the U.S. (not for meats in this GRAS section, not on fruits/vegetables intended to be served/sold raw) ([S5], [S6], [S7]).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** **Restricted / under safety concern follow-up** (E224 is within sulfur dioxide-sulfites group E220-228, reassessed by EFSA).
- **Latest EFSA scientific follow-up year:** **2022**.
- **ADI outcome:** EFSA follow-up concluded the available toxicity database was inadequate to derive an ADI and **withdrew the temporary group ADI**; EFSA used an MOE approach and concluded this raised a safety concern under assessed scenarios ([S2], [S8]).
- **Historical ADI context:** 2016 EFSA re-evaluation had set a **temporary group ADI of 0.7 mg SO2 equivalents/kg bw/day** pending data ([S3], [S8]).
- **Key conclusion:** Data gaps remained unresolved; MOE findings indicated concern in several populations and scenarios ([S2], [S8]).

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** **GRAS** under specific conditions.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 182.3637** (potassium metabisulfite).
- **Conditions of use (food):** GRAS with GMP, **except** not used in meats; not used in foods recognized as a source of vitamin B1; not used on fruits/vegetables intended to be served or sold raw/fresh ([S6]).
- **Related citation:** **21 CFR 582.3637** (animal food/feed context, similar restrictions) ([S7]).
- **Sulfite labeling threshold context:** sulfiting agents at detectable levels (10 ppm or more sulfite in finished standardized food) must be declared (21 CFR 130.9) ([S9]).

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-0.7 mg/kg bw/day** for sulfites (group basis, including metabisulfites) ([S5], [S10]).
- **Evaluation timeline:** WHO/JECFA records show ADI evaluation entries (including 1998 in the WHO database) and subsequent intake/exposure discussion in 2008 ([S5], [S10]).
- **Last evaluation year (database listing):** **2008** (intake/exposure discussion), with ADI value shown from prior evaluation record ([S5]).

### IARC
- **Classification:** No separate potassium-metabisulfite-only classification found; IARC lists **“Sulfur dioxide and some sulfites, bisulfites and metabisulfites” as Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans) in classification listings ([S11]).

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No complete cross-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed.
- Notable restrictions include U.S. CFR use limitations for potassium metabisulfite in foods and sulfite declaration requirements ([S6], [S9]).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/in vitro toxicology
- A genotoxicity/cytotoxicity study (human lymphocytes + rat bone marrow model; high-dose conditions) reported dose-dependent chromosomal effects and micronuclei with potassium metabisulfite exposure ([S14]).
- EFSA 2022 used a lower reference point (BMDL 38 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day, prolonged visual evoked potential latency endpoint) and reported MOE-based concerns in multiple populations/scenarios ([S8]).

### Epidemiological/human evidence
- A large prospective cohort study (NutriNet-Sante; France) reported associations between several preservatives (including potassium metabisulfite) and higher type 2 diabetes incidence; observational design does not establish causality ([S12]).
- Clinical allergy literature shows sulfite hypersensitivity can occur in susceptible individuals, including a DBPC challenge-confirmed case report with potassium metabisulfite-triggered anaphylaxis ([S16]).
- Conversely, one CIU challenge study found additive sensitivity (including potassium metabisulfite among tested additives) was rare (<1% in that cohort) ([S15]).

### Mechanistic/other concerns
- Concern domains include hypersensitivity in susceptible individuals and unresolved systemic toxicity uncertainties highlighted by EFSA (insufficient new toxicology to maintain ADI) ([S8], [S16]).

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2016:** estimated dietary exposures exceeded the temporary ADI in multiple populations at mean and high-percentile intake scenarios ([S3]).
- **EFSA 2022 follow-up:** under refined and MPL-based scenarios, many population groups had MOEs below the assessment factor threshold, raising safety concern ([S8]).
- **JECFA 2008 intake context:** high consumers (e.g., high wine consumers at 97.5 percentile) could exceed ADI; children/teenagers may receive notable exposure from beverages and certain processed foods ([S5]).

### Vulnerable populations
- Sulfite-sensitive/asthmatic individuals (hypersensitivity risk context).
- Children/toddlers in high-exposure dietary patterns.
- High regular consumers of sulfite-containing products (e.g., certain beverages/dried fruit categories) ([S5], [S8], [S16]).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting: long-standing regulated use; IARC Group 3 (no classifiable human carcinogenicity signal for sulfites/metabisulfites group) ([S11]).
- Evidence against: EFSA could not maintain an ADI in 2022 and identified MOE-based safety concern; historical intake exceedance concerns ([S3], [S8]).

**limited**
- Evidence supporting: FDA GRAS status with explicit GMP and use restrictions; JECFA group ADI framework exists ([S5], [S6]).
- Evidence against: EFSA follow-up concluded data gaps remained and ADI was withdrawn; concern persisted in assessed exposure scenarios ([S8]).

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting: additive remains permitted in major markets but with significant caveats/restrictions; exposure exceedance risk documented for some populations and scenarios; hypersensitivity evidence exists ([S5], [S6], [S8], [S16]).
- Evidence against: EFSA’s inability to derive/retain ADI and explicit safety concern can justify elevation beyond moderate under stricter criteria ([S8]).

**high**
- Evidence supporting: EFSA follow-up explicitly states inadequate toxicity database for ADI derivation and reports MOE-based safety concern; repeated exposure concern signals in high consumers/population subgroups ([S5], [S8]).
- Evidence against: no complete major-jurisdiction ban found; IARC classification is Group 3, not 2A/2B/1; some human challenge data suggest low prevalence of additive sensitivity in certain cohorts ([S11], [S15]).

### 2. Rationale
The pivotal factor is EFSA’s 2022 follow-up outcome: unresolved data gaps, withdrawal of temporary ADI, and MOE findings indicating concern in multiple population groups/scenarios. This outweighs the reassurance implied by historical approvals when applying the provided tier rules strictly.

### 3. Recommended tier
**high** (with moderate confidence, because regulatory approvals/restrictions still coexist with EFSA concern-based uncertainty).
