# E466 (Carboxymethyl cellulose / Sodium carboxymethylcellulose) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E466
- **Primary name:** Sodium carboxymethylcellulose (carboxymethyl cellulose, cellulose gum)
- **CAS number(s):** 9004-32-4
- **Chemical class:** Modified cellulose hydrocolloid (anionic cellulose ether)
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium cellulose glycolate, Na CMC, CMC, cellulose gum, carboxymethyl cellulose
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (cellulose-derived; sodium salt of carboxymethyl ether of cellulose). This is inferred from regulatory identity text.

**Evidence:** WHO/JECFA chemical record for INS 466 lists chemical name/synonyms/CAS (1989 evaluation entry) and defines the substance as sodium salt of carboxymethyl ether of cellulose.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrophilic polymer that increases viscosity and stabilizes dispersed systems; used as a texturizer/structure modifier.
- **Common functional categories:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener.
- **Common food categories:** Broad processed-food uses; EFSA follow-up specifically discusses dietary foods for infants/young children in FC 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2.

**Evidence:** WHO/JECFA functional class for INS 466 is emulsifier/stabilizer/thickener; EFSA 2022 follow-up abstract names FC 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Approved, with unresolved data gaps for some infant/young-child medical-diet uses.
  - **Latest EFSA E466-focused follow-up:** 2022 (EFSA Journal DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2022.7665; PMID 36514369).
  - **ADI:** EFSA 2018 cellulose-group re-evaluation reported **no need for a numerical ADI** (consistent with ADI not specified concept for these celluloses) and no safety concern at reported uses/use levels for E460-E466/E468/E469.
  - **Key conclusions:**
    - 2018: No genotoxic concern; no carcinogenic signal in available studies; no need for numerical ADI for the cellulose group at reported uses/use levels.
    - 2022 E466 follow-up: Business operators reported E466 not used in foods for infants below 16 weeks/FC 13.1.5.1; due lack of data, no assessment for that FC/age group; insufficient data remained for adequate safety assessment in FC 13.1.5.2.
  - **Access note:** Direct EFSA journal URLs were visited but blocked in this environment (robots/anti-bot); EFSA findings were extracted from the EFSA records indexed in PubMed (official DOI-linked entries).

- **FDA / CFR (U.S.):** **GRAS** under Part 182 listing.
  - **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 182.1745** (Sodium carboxymethylcellulose), within **21 CFR Part 182—Substances Generally Recognized as Safe**.
  - **Conditions of use:** “This substance is generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice.”

- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **Not specified** (group ADI context for modified celluloses).
  - **Last evaluation year shown in INS 466 record:** **1989** (meeting 35), with comment that ADI is group-based across modified celluloses including sodium carboxymethylcellulose.

- **IARC:**
  - No E466/carboxymethylcellulose entry identified in IARC Monographs classification dataset searched from the official classification app script.

- **Notable bans:**
  - No major-jurisdiction outright ban identified from sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline (EFSA 2018):**
  - No genotoxic concern.
  - No carcinogenic properties detected in available cellulose studies reviewed.
  - No need for numerical ADI for the cellulose group at reported use levels.

- **Human intervention evidence:**
  - 2022 randomized controlled feeding study (healthy adults; n=16 total, 7 on CMC arm): 15 g/day CMC for 11 days was associated with reduced microbiota diversity, reduced fecal short-chain fatty acids/free amino acids, and modestly higher postprandial discomfort.

- **Human/ex vivo mechanistic evidence:**
  - 2021 ex vivo human microbiota model study reported CMC among emulsifiers with lasting detrimental microbiota compositional/functional changes and increased pro-inflammatory potential markers in that model.

- **Epidemiology:**
  - 2023 prospective NutriNet-Sante cohort (n=95,442) reported positive associations between higher E466 intake and CVD/CHD risk (HR for +1 SD intake: CVD 1.03, CHD 1.04).
  - Interpretation limitation: observational, residual confounding possible.

- **Recent animal/mechanistic signal:**
  - 2025 mouse study reported that 1% CMC exposure aggravated experimental acute pancreatitis, with gut dysbiosis/SCFA changes and monocyte pathway findings; translational relevance to routine human intake remains uncertain.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA quantitative exposure context (cellulose group):**
  - 2018 EFSA abstract reports 95th percentile refined exposure up to **506 mg/kg bw/day** for combined celluloses and an indicative total exposure around **660-900 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **ADI exceedance framing:**
  - EFSA/JECFA do not use a numerical ADI for this cellulose group in current conclusions; classical ADI exceedance calculations are therefore limited.
- **Vulnerable populations / uncertainty zones:**
  - Infants and young children consuming foods for special medical purposes (especially FC 13.1.5.2) remain a regulatory uncertainty area in EFSA follow-up.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA/JECFA non-numerical ADI framework and no major carcinogenic/genotoxic regulatory signal.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA follow-up identifies unresolved evidence gaps for some infant/young-child medical-diet uses; newer human/mechanistic studies suggest possible gut effects.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EU/US/WHO regulatory acceptance remains in place; FDA GRAS condition is cGMP-based; EFSA 2018 found no safety concern at reported use levels.
  - Evidence against this tier: Multiple post-2018 studies raise biologically plausible concerns (microbiome/inflammation signals) and one large cohort reports small positive cardiovascular associations.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Approved but with caveats (EFSA uncertainty for pediatric medical-food contexts) plus credible human intervention and epidemiologic signals that justify caution rather than dismissal.
  - Evidence against this tier: Effect sizes in observational work are modest, causality is not established, and regulators have not withdrawn approval.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: None from current regulatory status (no major bans; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing found for E466).
  - Evidence against this tier: Existing approvals and ADI-not-specified style regulatory conclusions are not consistent with a high-risk classification.

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest anchor remains regulatory acceptance (EFSA/FDA/JECFA), but a pure low-risk framing is weakened by:
  - EFSA follow-up uncertainty in vulnerable infant/young-child medical-food categories.
  - Human trial microbiome/metabolome changes at high controlled intake.
  - Prospective cohort association signals for cardiovascular outcomes.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **moderate**
