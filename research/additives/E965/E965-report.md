# E965 (Maltitol) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E965, split as **E965(i) maltitol** and **E965(ii) maltitol syrup** in EFSA calls. [S2][S3]
- **Primary name:** Maltitol.
- **CAS number(s):** **585-88-6** (JECFA entry for maltitol). [S6]
- **Chemical class:** Polyol (sugar alcohol), produced by hydrogenation of starch-derived carbohydrates. [S4][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Maltitol, maltitol syrup, hydrogenated starch hydrolysate products, E965(i), E965(ii). [S2][S4][S5]
- **Natural vs synthetic origin:** **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured by catalytic hydrogenation of starch hydrolysates/maltose feedstocks). [S4][S5]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Provides sweet taste with lower available energy than sucrose; incomplete absorption can lead to colonic fermentation/osmotic effects at high intakes. [S10][S13]
- **Typical technological functions:** Sweetener, humectant, texturizer, and formulation aid under U.S. CFR conditions. [S4]
- **Common food categories:** Sugar-free confectionery/chocolate/gum and reduced-sugar bakery/dairy products (reviewed food applications). [S10]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Authorized food additive in EU framework; EFSA documents reference maltitol/maltitol syrup as authorized additives undergoing re-evaluation data collection. [S2][S3]
- **ADI:** EFSA 2006 opinion states the Scientific Committee for Food (SCF) had allocated an ADI **"not specified"** to maltitol and maltitol syrup (non-numeric). [S1]
- **Evaluation year:** Latest EFSA scientific opinion specific to maltitol syrup amendment identified here is **2006**. [S1]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2006 EFSA opinion: no safety concern for the proposed amendment to maltitol syrup specifications. [S1]
  - 2023 EFSA call: FAF Panel stated available data were insufficient to assess potential genotoxicity for read-across to sorbitol and requested new technical/toxicological data for E965(i)/E965(ii) re-evaluation. [S2]

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** **GRAS (affirmed)** for maltitol syrup as a direct human food ingredient in 21 CFR Part 184.
- **Primary citation:** **21 CFR 184.1924** (Maltitol syrup).
- **Conditions of use:** May be safely used in food per prescribed conditions; function includes sweetener/humectant/texturizer/formulation aid, and use must be consistent with good manufacturing practice (GMP). [S4]
- **Related citation:** **21 CFR 172.841** (Food starch-hydrolyzate products), describing safe use conditions and GMP for hydrogenated starch hydrolyzate products. [S5]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **Not limited** (non-numeric). [S6]
- **Last evaluation year:** **1985** (JECFA database entry shown for maltitol). [S6]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No maltitol-specific listing was found in IARC monographs search/dataset checks performed.
  - IARC WordPress search API query for `maltitol` returned empty results.
  - IARC list-of-classifications application dataset (updated 2026-02-25) did not include a maltitol entry. [S8][S9]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified from the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal studies:**
  - Rat study (PMID 22585934): no chromosome aberration signal and no teratogenic finding, but decreased fetal weight/growth retardation reported at the highest intraperitoneal dose. [S11]
  - Mouse high-fat diet model (PMID 26061453): reported metabolic/liver benefits rather than toxicity signal under study conditions. [S15]
- **Human/clinical evidence:**
  - Healthy volunteer study (PMID 18797153): osmotic diarrhea occurred in many subjects at individualized bolus thresholds within 15-45 g; fiber co-administration reduced symptoms. [S13]
- **Mechanistic / genotoxic concerns:**
  - In vitro lymphocyte study (PMID 16777705): reported non-dose-dependent micronucleus increase with conclusion of weak genotoxic potential.
  - EFSA 2023 call indicates remaining data gaps for genotoxicity assessment in ongoing re-evaluation context. [S2][S12]
- **Recent literature context:**
  - 2023 ex vivo human microbiota study (PMID 37537786) found compound-specific effects across low/no-calorie sweeteners, with maltitol showing gradual fermentation behavior in that model. [S14]
  - 2020 review (PMID 32698373) concludes lower-dose use appears low-risk but high-dose intake is associated with GI adverse effects. [S10]
- **Epidemiological data:** No robust long-term prospective epidemiology specific to maltitol and major chronic disease endpoints was identified in this search set.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark context:** EFSA/SCF and JECFA report non-numeric ADI descriptors ("not specified" / "not limited"), so classical numeric ADI exceedance calculations are not straightforward. [S1][S6]
- **Practical intake concern:** Acute GI tolerance appears to be the main limiting factor; human bolus dosing study shows diarrhea risk emerging across roughly 15-45 g individual thresholds. [S13]
- **Population groups with higher practical risk:**
  - High consumers of sugar-free/polyol-rich foods
  - Children consuming large portions of sugar-free confectionery
  - People with GI sensitivity (e.g., IBS-like susceptibility)
- **EFSA re-evaluation context:** EFSA call text explicitly notes high potential exposure context and requests updated data for reassessment. [S2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Evidence supporting:** Longstanding authorization; JECFA non-numeric ADI descriptor; no IARC listing found.
  - **Evidence against:** Documented dose-related GI intolerance in humans and unresolved EFSA genotoxicity data gap language in current re-evaluation workflow. [S2][S6][S8][S9][S13]

- **limited**
  - **Evidence supporting:** Approved/authorized with favorable historical regulatory conclusions (EFSA 2006 no concern for amended specs; FDA GRAS + GMP conditions; JECFA ADI not limited).
  - **Evidence against:** Practical GI adverse effects occur at higher intakes; older mechanistic/genotoxic literature is mixed and not fully closed out by current EFSA call process. [S1][S2][S4][S6][S10][S12][S13]

- **moderate**
  - **Evidence supporting:** EFSA explicitly requests new genotoxicity-relevant data and states insufficiency for part of read-across reasoning; clinically relevant GI effects are reproducible at high intake.
  - **Evidence against:** No clear regulatory move to restrict/withdraw authorization; available evidence remains more consistent with dose-dependent tolerance limits than severe intrinsic toxicity at typical use. [S1][S2][S4][S6][S13]

- **high**
  - **Evidence supporting:** None strong from reviewed sources.
  - **Evidence against:** No major bans found, no IARC classification found, and core regulatory frameworks remain permissive under GMP/use conditions. [S4][S6][S8][S9]

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence supports a generally acceptable regulatory profile with the main confirmed human risk being high-dose GI intolerance rather than severe systemic toxicity at normal use levels. However, unresolved EFSA re-evaluation genotoxicity-data language prevents a "risk_free" classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
