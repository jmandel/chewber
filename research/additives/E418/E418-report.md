# E418 (Gellan gum) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E418 (INS 418) [S7]
- **Name:** Gellan gum [S7]
- **CAS number(s):** 71010-52-1 [S7]
- **Chemical class:** High-molecular-weight microbial exopolysaccharide (tetrasaccharide-repeat gum) [S4][S7]
- **Common synonyms:** Gellan gum; INS 418; low-acyl clarified gellan gum (specific form evaluated by JECFA) [S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Natural (fermentation-derived)** polysaccharide, produced by pure-culture fermentation (FDA CFR text describes production by *Pseudomonas elodea*) [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrocolloid that forms/strengthens gel-viscosity networks in aqueous foods, improving texture stability and suspension behavior; used as stabilizer/thickener/gelling agent [S4][S7]
- **Common food categories:** Broad direct-food use under GMP in the US (where standards of identity do not preclude use), and evaluated in infant special medical purpose formulas in JECFA/EFSA risk work [S2][S4][S7]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorized in EU food additive framework; EFSA re-evaluation concluded safety at reported uses/levels [S1][S2]
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation year:** 2018 [S1][S2]
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** [S2]
- **Key conclusions (2018):**
  - Adequate toxicity/exposure data were available [S2]
  - Refined exposure up to 72.4 mg/kg bw/day at 95th percentile in toddlers [S2]
  - No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity; no adverse effects in chronic animal studies at highest tested doses [S2]
  - No safety concern for reported uses/use levels; EFSA also recommended tighter specification detail (e.g., viable source cells/PHB/protein/residual enzymatic activities) [S2]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** No GRAS affirmation for gellan gum found in 21 CFR Part 184 search; instead it is listed as an approved direct food additive [S5]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.665** [S4]
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 172.665):**
  - Produced by pure culture fermentation
  - Must meet CFR identity/specification requirements
  - Used under current GMP as a stabilizer and thickener
  - May be used where standards of identity do not preclude use [S4]
- **Access note:** Direct `ecfr.gov` pages were accessed but blocked by CAPTCHA/request-access controls in this environment; alternative queries including the API route timed out, so section text was taken from the Cornell LII eCFR mirror [S3][S4]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED** [S6][S7]
- **Last evaluation year (database):** **2025** [S7]
- **Latest conclusions captured in database:**
  - 2025: considered for specifications only; ADI remained not specified [S7]
  - 2019: infant FSMP low-acyl clarified gellan gum use judged not of safety concern [S7]
  - 1990: potential laxative effect at high intakes should be considered [S7]

### IARC
- Checked IARC monographs classification resources and underlying list feed; **no gellan gum entry/classification found** [S8][S9]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in EFSA/FDA-CFR/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed [S1][S2][S4][S7][S8]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA abstract reports no adverse effects in chronic rodent studies at highest tested doses (mice 3,627 mg/kg bw/day; rats 1,460 mg/kg bw/day) [S2]
- 2023 mouse study reports beneficial gut-liver effects (NAFLD model) rather than toxicity signals [S10]

### Human evidence
- EFSA abstract reports repeated oral intake up to 200 mg/kg bw/day for 3 weeks had no adverse effects in humans [S2]
- 1988 controlled human dietary study (23 days, 175-200 mg/kg bw/day) found no adverse dietary/physiological effects; GI/fecal bulking and bile-acid changes were observed [S11]

### Mechanistic/other concerns
- JECFA historical note: potential laxative effect at high intakes [S7]
- EFSA 2018: recommendation to tighten specifications (microbial/source-related impurities and residual components) [S2]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA re-evaluation reported refined high-end exposure up to **72.4 mg/kg bw/day** (toddlers, 95th percentile) [S2]
- EFSA and JECFA both use non-numeric ADI outcomes (no numerical ADI / ADI not specified), so classic ADI exceedance calculations are not directly applicable [S2][S7]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** infants/young children in special medical purpose nutrition contexts and individuals sensitive to GI-laxative effects at high intake [S7]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at refined exposure [S2]
  - JECFA: ADI not specified [S7]
  - No IARC classification found [S8][S9]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - JECFA notes potential laxative effect at high intake [S7]
  - EFSA specification caveats indicate quality-control dependencies [S2]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved in EU and US framework (as direct additive), with JECFA ADI not specified [S1][S2][S4][S7]
  - No serious toxicology signal in EFSA summary; human high-intake studies did not show overt adverse effects [S2][S11]
  - Main concern is GI/laxative effect at high doses rather than severe systemic toxicity [S7]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Infant-specialized uses still require careful specification and exposure framing [S2][S7]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Could be argued from infant-focused caution and high-intake GI effects [S7]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No ADI reduction, no broad population safety failure, and no severe hazard classification [S2][S7][S8]

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None identified from reviewed authorities
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions; no IARC 2A/2B/1 signal; EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain permissive [S2][S4][S7][S8]

### 2. Rationale
The authoritative pattern is consistent: regulatory acceptance, non-numeric ADI outcomes, and absence of strong carcinogenic/genotoxic/systemic toxicity signals. Remaining concerns are mainly high-intake GI/laxative effects and specification-control details.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
