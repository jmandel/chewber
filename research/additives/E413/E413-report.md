# E413 (Tragacanth gum) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E413 (INS 413) [S2][S6]
- **Primary name:** Tragacanth gum [S2][S6]
- **CAS number(s):** 9000-65-1 [S2][S6]
- **Chemical class:** Natural exudate **acidic polysaccharide hydrocolloid (plant gum)** from *Astragalus* species [S2][S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Tragacanth; Gum tragacanth; INS 413 [S2][S6]
- **Origin:** Natural (plant exudate) [S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrocolloid thickener/stabilizer-emulsifier; EFSA reports it is unlikely to be absorbed intact and is partially fermented by intestinal microbiota [S2][S6].
- **Regulatory functional classes:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [S4][S6].
- **Common food categories (US CFR maximum-use table):** baked goods and mixes, condiments/relishes, fats and oils, gravies/sauces, meat products, processed fruits/fruit juices, and other food categories [S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorized food additive in EU (E413), with latest EFSA re-evaluation in 2017 [S1][S2].
- **ADI:** **No numerical ADI required** (equivalent to ADI not specified approach) [S2].
- **Evaluation year:** 2017 [S2].
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA states no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern for the general population at refined exposure, at reported uses/use levels [S2].
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA publication URL was reached but blocked by anti-bot/challenge in this environment; conclusions were extracted from the linked PubMed record for the same EFSA opinion (DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4789) [S1][S2].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS framework under **21 CFR Part 184** (Direct food substances affirmed as generally recognized as safe) with specific listing at **21 CFR 184.1351** [S3][S4].
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 184.1351):** Direct food ingredient with maximum permitted percentages by category (e.g., baked goods 0.2%, condiments/relishes 0.7%, fats/oils 1.3%, gravies/sauces 0.8%, meat products 0.2%, processed fruits/juices 0.2%, other foods 0.1%) [S4].

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED** [S6].
- **Last evaluation year:** 1985 (toxicological evaluation year shown in database entry) [S6].
- **Functional class:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [S6].

### IARC
- **Classification:** No IARC Monographs classification entry was identified for tragacanth / gum tragacanth / CAS 9000-65-1 in the current IARC classification dataset checked [S7][S8].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in reviewed EU/US/JECFA/IARC sources [S2][S3][S4][S6][S7][S8].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA summary indicates no adverse effects in carcinogenicity studies at highest tested dose and no genotoxicity concern [S2].
- Rat electron microscopy study (1984) reported no detectable pathological abnormalities in heart/liver at tested gum tragacanth dietary concentrations [S11].

### Human data
- Human volunteer study (1984): 9.9 g/day for 21 days was reported as well tolerated, with no adverse clinical/biochemical toxicological findings (stool/transit changes noted but interpreted as non-adverse) [S10].
- EFSA abstract includes this high-intake human tolerance evidence (~141 mg/kg bw/day equivalent) as part of its safety basis [S2].

### Allergy / hypersensitivity signals
- Historical case reports/letters describe hypersensitivity or suspected allergic reactions where tragacanth was implicated (e.g., food/tablet additive contexts) [S13][S14][S15].
- Immunogenicity study of exudate gums (including tragacanth) showed systemic immune responses in animal model, suggesting a plausible allergy mechanism in susceptible individuals [S12].

### Epidemiological evidence
- No robust modern epidemiological cohort evidence directly linking dietary E413 exposure to chronic disease risk was identified in this search set; available direct safety literature is mostly older and incorporated into EFSA/JECFA reviews [S2][S6][S9].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** EFSA concludes no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses/use levels, but the full EFSA exposure tables were not directly retrievable from EFSA site due access blocking in this environment [S1][S2].
- **Historical contextual intake estimate:** older human literature cited very low expected additive intake in normal diet versus high-dose tolerance tests [S10].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not directly applicable because EFSA concluded no need for a numerical ADI and JECFA ADI is not specified [S2][S6].
- **Vulnerable populations:** Individuals with pre-existing gum hypersensitivity/allergic susceptibility may represent a higher-risk subgroup for idiosyncratic reactions [S12][S13][S14][S15].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Natural plant gum with long regulatory history.
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed, no general-population safety concern at refined exposure.
  - JECFA: ADI not specified [S2][S6].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Credible hypersensitivity case reports exist.
  - Immunogenic potential has been demonstrated in experimental settings [S12][S13][S14][S15].

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved in EU; GRAS framework in US CFR; JECFA ADI not specified.
  - No convincing genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in regulator summaries.
  - Human high-dose short-term tolerance data did not show toxicological adverse effects [S2][S3][S4][S6][S10][S11].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Allergy/hypersensitivity concerns, while apparently uncommon, are non-zero [S12][S13][S14][S15].

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Presence of credible allergenicity signals could justify caution for susceptible individuals [S12][S13][S14][S15].
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No regulatory ADI tightening, no evidence of broad-population exposure exceedance concern, no major contemporary toxicology signal [S2][S6][S9].

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Essentially none from current regulatory landscape.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Not banned in major jurisdictions reviewed.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification identified.
  - EFSA and JECFA conclusions remain permissive [S2][S6][S7][S8].

### 2. Rationale
Regulatory authorities (EFSA/JECFA/FDA-CFR framework) consistently indicate low toxicological concern for general dietary exposure, including ADI-not-specified/no-numerical-ADI conclusions. The main counterweight is a small body of hypersensitivity evidence, which prevents classification as fully risk-free.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
