# E251 (Sodium nitrate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E251 (INS 251). [S6]
- **Name:** Sodium nitrate. [S6]
- **CAS number:** 7631-99-4. [S6]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic nitrate salt (alkali metal nitrate). [S6][S2]
- **Common synonyms:** Chile saltpetre; cubic/soda nitre. [S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Used as an intentionally added food additive (industrial ingredient), but nitrate also occurs naturally in vegetables/water. [S1]

## Function in Food
- **Primary functions:** Preservative and colour fixative (especially in cured products). [S2]
- **Mechanism of action:** Inhibits microbial growth (including anti-botulism role in cured products context) and helps maintain cured-meat color/flavour profile. [S1]
- **Common food categories:**
  - Smoked/cured fish products (e.g., sablefish, salmon, shad) with legal residual limits. [S2]
  - Home meat-curing preparations with use directions limiting final concentrations. [S2]
  - Broader EFSA context: meat, fish and cheese categories where nitrite/nitrate salts are authorized. [S1]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised (as part of E249-E252 framework). [S1]
- **Latest key evaluation used here:** 2017 re-evaluation communication linked to EFSA scientific opinion (`EFSA Journal 2017;15(6):4787`). [S1]
- **ADI:** 3.7 mg/kg bw/day (nitrate, as nitrate ion). [S1]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - Existing safe levels for nitrites/nitrates added to food were considered sufficiently protective.
  - Exposure from additives alone was generally within safe levels.
  - Considering **all** dietary sources, ADI exceedance may occur in medium/high consumers; slight exceedance in some children with high additive-containing diets was noted. [S1]

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** Approved as a regulated food additive with conditions; also listed under prior sanctions for specific cured-meat/poultry uses (not GRAS-affirmed in 182/184/186 listings for sodium nitrate in queried text). [S2][S3]
- **CFR citations and conditions:**
  - **21 CFR 172.170 (Sodium nitrate):** allowed in specified foods with limits (e.g., max 500 ppm sodium nitrate and 200 ppm sodium nitrite in specified finished cured fish/meat contexts), plus labeling/directions requirements and household warning statement. [S2]
  - **21 CFR 181.33:** sodium nitrate/potassium nitrate subject to USDA prior sanctions as nitrite sources in cured red meat and poultry products. [S2]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** 0-3.7 mg/kg bw (expressed as nitrate ion). [S6]
- **Special note:** ADI does not apply to infants below 3 months. [S6]
- **Last evaluation year shown in WHO database:** 1995 (Meeting 44; links to TRS 859 / tox monograph). [S6]

### IARC
- **Direct sodium nitrate listing:** no direct individual sodium nitrate Group entry identified in the IARC classification dataset extract.
- **Relevant IARC classification:** "Nitrate or nitrite (ingested) under conditions that result in endogenous nitrosation" is **Group 2A** (probably carcinogenic to humans), Volume 94, publication year 2010 (evaluation year 2006 in dataset). [S8][S9][S10]

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide outright ban on sodium nitrate as a food additive was identified in the sources reviewed here; instead, use is controlled by concentration and food-category limits. [S1][S2]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Epidemiological (cancer):** NutriNet-Sante cohort reported positive associations for additive-derived nitrites/nitrates with certain site-specific cancers (e.g., nitrite additive exposure with prostate cancer; additive nitrate exposure with breast cancer), while natural-source exposures showed no association in that study. [S11]
- **Epidemiological (metabolic):** NutriNet-Sante cohort found higher type 2 diabetes risk with higher nitrite exposure (including additive-origin nitrites, specifically sodium nitrite/E250), but not with nitrates. Observational design limits causal inference. [S12]
- **Epidemiological (renal):** Agricultural Health Study analysis linked processed-meat-derived nitrate/nitrite exposure patterns to higher end-stage renal disease risk signals; total water nitrate and total dietary nitrate/nitrite were not uniformly associated. [S13]
- **Mechanistic concern context:** Nitrate/nitrite can contribute to endogenous nitrosation and nitrosamine formation under certain conditions; this underpins IARC's condition-specific Group 2A entry and remains a central risk discussion point. [S8][S10][S14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical contribution from additive use:** EFSA indicates nitrate from food additives is a small share (reported as <5%) of overall nitrate dietary exposure. [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Additive-only nitrate exposure generally within ADI in EFSA re-evaluation context.
  - All-source nitrate exposure (additives + natural occurrence + contaminants) may exceed ADI in medium/high consumers across age groups.
  - Children with high intake of additive-containing foods were highlighted as a group with potential slight exceedance in EFSA communication. [S1]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants <3 months (JECFA ADI non-applicability statement).
  - High-consuming children and high-exposure consumers overall when total dietary sources are counted. [S1][S6]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: long-standing regulatory approvals; established ADIs by EFSA/JECFA.
  - Evidence against: IARC condition-specific Group 2A classification for ingested nitrate/nitrite with endogenous nitrosation; cohort signals for some adverse outcomes; EFSA notes possible total-diet ADI exceedance in some groups. [S1][S6][S10][S11][S12]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved with quantitative limits in EU/US; additive-only exposure often within ADI; strong regulatory frameworks for concentrations/labeling. [S1][S2]
  - Evidence against: credible modern epidemiological concern signals and non-negligible mechanistic concern pathway (nitrosation/nitrosamines). [S10][S11][S12][S13]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: approved but with caveats (strict use limits, source-dependent risk context, ADI exceedance possibility in subpopulations from total dietary exposure, and condition-specific carcinogenicity concern). [S1][S2][S10]
  - Evidence against: no broad regulatory withdrawal/ban; additive-only nitrate share often low relative to total nitrate exposure. [S1][S2]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: IARC Group 2A condition-specific classification is a serious hazard signal. [S10]
  - Evidence against: no major broad bans/withdrawals identified; EFSA did not conclude inability to establish safe use at permitted additive levels; JECFA ADI remains established. [S1][S6]

### 2. Rationale
Sodium nitrate (E251) has clear regulatory acceptance but not a "no-concern" profile. The main uncertainty/risk driver is not acute toxicity at permitted additive levels, but chronic risk context tied to nitrosation pathways, diet pattern, and cumulative total-source exposure. Regulatory authorities maintain ADIs and use limits, while cohort literature reports selected risk associations.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
