# E1520 (Propylene Glycol) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1520. (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)
- **Chemical name:** Propane-1,2-diol (propylene glycol). (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)
- **CAS number:** 57-55-6. (Source: 21 CFR 184.1666, accessed via eCFR API XML, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)
- **Common synonyms:** 1,2-propanediol; 1,2-dihydroxypropane; methyl glycol; propanediol. (Source: WHO/JECFA database entry for PROPYLENE GLYCOL, 2002 evaluation entry, https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/2698)
- **Chemical class:** Low-molecular-weight diol/polyol (alcohol). (Source: 21 CFR 184.1666 formula C3H8O2, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Reported as not occurring in nature in U.S. regulation; manufactured industrially. (Source: 21 CFR 184.1666(a), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Carrier/solvent and humectant (retains moisture, helps dissolve and distribute flavors/additives). (Sources: JECFA Functional Class "CARRIER_SOLVENT, HUMECTANT", https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/2698; 21 CFR 184.1666(c), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)
- **Other technological functions:** Anticaking agent, antioxidant aid, emulsifier, flavor agent, formulation aid, processing aid, solvent/vehicle, stabilizer/thickener, surface-active agent, texturizer. (Source: 21 CFR 184.1666(c), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)
- **Common food categories in U.S. conditions of use:** Alcoholic beverages, confections/frostings, frozen dairy products, seasonings/flavorings, nuts/nut products, and other foods under cGMP-based limits. (Source: 21 CFR 184.1666(d), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=184)

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal toxicology
- EFSA re-evaluation reports no treatment-related effects in subchronic studies; in chronic studies, a dog study showed hematological effects at 5,000 mg/kg bw/day, while a rat 2-year study up to 2,500 mg/kg bw/day reported no adverse effects and supported the ADI derivation. (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)
- A 2021 mouse study (oral dosing) reported nephrotoxicity at high subacute doses (1 and 5 g/kg/day for 28 days) and oral LD50 values in the tens of g/kg range (22.81-26.62 g/kg, depending on enantiomeric form). (Source: Han et al., 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34461418/)

### Human/clinical and epidemiological evidence
- A 2024 case report described severe toxicity in an adolescent with extreme chronic cornstarch ingestion, a non-typical exposure scenario far above normal dietary additive intake. (Source: Hashim et al., 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38756767/)
- A 2023 review on skin sensitization/contact dermatitis concluded propylene glycol can behave as a weak allergen under certain conditions, mainly relevant to dermal exposure contexts. (Source: Kimber et al., 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36702195/)

### Mechanistic/other concerns
- A 2023 in vitro study found that food additive solvents including propylene glycol can increase ZnO nanoparticle dispersion/solubility and associated cytotoxicity in intestinal cell models (co-exposure context). (Source: Lee et al., 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37764602/)
- EFSA identified no genotoxicity concern from the evaluated toxicology package and considered reproductive/developmental datasets without adverse findings at tested levels. (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)

## Exposure Assessment
- **EU re-evaluation estimate:** EFSA’s refined brand-loyal scenario reported highest P95 at **23.3 mg/kg bw/day (children)**, below the ADI of 25 mg/kg bw/day. (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)
- **Uncertainty direction:** EFSA states exposure likely overestimated for categories with available data and notes non-mandatory labeling can limit completeness of market-screening data. (Source: EFSA ANS Panel, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)
- **Recent beverage-focused assessment:** A 2025 tiered intake assessment in US/UK surrogate markets reported refined intake estimates below a proposed safe intake of 62.5 mg/kg bw/day, including high consumers. (Source: Arnold et al., 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39819376/)
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Young children have higher body-weight-normalized intake potential; separate medicine-excipient guidance cited by EFSA indicates much lower safe thresholds in neonates/young children for medicinal exposure contexts. (Source: EFSA discussion citing EMA, 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009459/)

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - Long regulatory history and broad approvals (EFSA/FDA/JECFA).
  - EFSA retained ADI and reported refined P95 below ADI.
  - No genotoxicity concern identified in EFSA re-evaluation.
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - A finite ADI exists (not "ADI not specified").
  - High-dose animal effects (hematological changes; nephrotoxicity at gram/kg-level dosing) and rare severe toxicity in extreme human exposure scenarios indicate hazard exists at sufficiently high doses.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - Clear approvals with established ADI and cGMP-linked use limits.
  - EFSA risk characterization found no ADI exceedance in refined scenarios.
  - Core toxicology database did not trigger ADI reduction in latest EFSA re-evaluation.
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - Evidence of toxicity at very high or atypical exposures, and weak allergenicity concerns in dermal contexts.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - Presence of documented adverse effects in specific high-dose contexts.
  - Exposure uncertainty remains (combined exposure from metabolites of other additives not fully quantified by EFSA).
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - No EFSA ADI reduction and no refined-scenario ADI exceedance reported.
  - No major jurisdictional withdrawal/ban and no IARC carcinogenic grouping identified.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Severe toxicity can occur in overdose/extreme exposure settings (case reports).
- **Evidence against high:**
  - No broad food-use bans in major reviewed jurisdictions.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for propylene glycol identified.
  - Regulatory assessments support current food-use safety within established limits.

### 2) Rationale
Primary decision drivers are: stable ADI across major regulators, EFSA refined exposure below ADI, and absence of strong carcinogenic/genotoxic regulatory signals. Counter-signals are mainly high-dose or non-standard exposures and specific-context sensitization literature, which do not currently overturn food-use approvals at regulated levels.

### 3) Recommended tier
**limited**
