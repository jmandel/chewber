# E625 (Magnesium diglutamate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E625
- **Primary name:** Magnesium diglutamate (WHO/JECFA: **MAGNESIUM DI-L-GLUTAMATE**)
- **CAS number(s):** 18543-68-5 (WHO/JECFA record)
- **Chemical class:** Magnesium salt of L-glutamic acid (glutamate flavour-enhancer salt; amino-acid salt)
- **Common synonyms:** Monomagnesium di-L-glutamate tetrahydrate; magnesium glutamate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Glutamate is naturally present in foods and human metabolism, while E625 is used as a manufactured additive salt form in food systems [S3][S11].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Provides free glutamate ions that activate umami taste pathways and enhance savoury intensity; also supports sodium-reduction strategies by partly replacing saltiness/palatability losses [S11].
- **Common functional classes (JECFA):** Flavour enhancer; salt substitute [S3].
- **Common food categories:** Savoury processed foods such as soups/broths, sauces, condiments/seasonings, and meat-containing prepared foods (group glutamate use context in EU risk-management documents) [S1][S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest re-evaluation identified:** EFSA ANS Panel re-evaluation of glutamates (E620-E625), published in 2017 (EFSA Journal 15(7):4910) [S1][S2].
- **ADI:** Group ADI **30 mg/kg bw/day**, expressed as glutamic acid [S1][S4].
- **Key conclusions:** EFSA concluded that estimated exposure could exceed the ADI in parts of the population (especially younger groups/high consumers in the original assessment scenarios), and recommended revisiting maximum limits [S1][S4].
- **Approval status interpretation:** E625 remains authorized in the EU glutamates group, but subsequent EU amendments tightened conditions of use/maxima (restricted-use profile rather than withdrawal) [S4].
- **Access note:** Direct opening of the EFSA Journal page was blocked in this environment (anti-bot/CAPTCHA on the journal host), so EFSA press content and EU legal text citing EFSA were used [S2].

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- JECFA historical toxicology context treats glutamate salts as a group with ADI "not specified" under intended use conditions [S3].
- A recent PubMed-indexed **in vitro** study in human lymphocytes (2020) reported concentration-dependent cytotoxic/genotoxic findings for magnesium diglutamate, with clearer effects at higher test concentrations [S13].
- A 2024 molecular-docking paper explored interactions between selected additives (including magnesium diglutamate) and genotoxicity-relevant targets; this is mechanistic/hypothesis-generating rather than direct in vivo hazard confirmation [S14].
- Older non-mammalian cytogenetic work (Allium cepa model, 2013 publication) also reported chromosomal-abnormality signals for glutamate flavour enhancers including magnesium diglutamate, but external validity to human dietary exposure is limited [S15].

### Epidemiological / human data
- No robust, additive-specific prospective human epidemiology for E625 was identified in the PubMed search used here; recent direct E625 safety literature is sparse [S13][S14].

### Mechanistic concerns summary
- Current concern signals are mainly **high-concentration in vitro** and mechanistic modelling; direct human-causal evidence specific to E625 remains limited [S13][S14].

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA's 2017 glutamates group assessment reported ADI exceedance in parts of the population in then-current/proposed use scenarios, with younger groups and high consumers most relevant for risk management [S1][S4].
- The ADI applies to the **combined glutamic acid/glutamate additive group (E620-E625)**, so E625-specific standalone intake values are typically not separated in publicly summarized regulatory texts [S1][S3][S4].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** plausible at group level where glutamate additive exposure is high; particularly relevant for toddlers/children/high consumers in EFSA's cited scenarios [S1][S4].
- **Vulnerable populations:** toddlers, children, and other high-consumption groups within the glutamate additive exposure framework [S1][S4].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - JECFA lists ADI as "not specified" for the glutamate-salt group under intended uses [S3].
  - Glutamate is a normal dietary/endogenous molecule family context [S11].
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - EFSA established a finite group ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day) and identified exceedance scenarios [S1][S4].
  - In vitro genotoxicity findings exist for magnesium diglutamate at higher concentrations [S13].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - Regulatory acceptance persists (EU authorized with controls; no major-jurisdiction ban identified) [S1][S4].
  - IARC has no specific carcinogenic classification entry for this additive [S10][S12].
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - EFSA-driven EU risk-management tightening indicates meaningful exposure caveats [S4].
  - Direct E625 human long-term safety data remain thin [S13][S14].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - Approved additive, but with explicit ADI framework and documented exceedance risk in some populations [S1][S4].
  - Credible but limited hazard signals from in vitro work specific to magnesium diglutamate [S13].
  - Regulatory trajectory is "authorized with tighter limits," not unconstrained approval [S4].
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - JECFA historical group ADI-not-specified position and lack of strong direct human harm evidence for E625 [S3][S13].

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Some experimental studies indicate genotoxic/cytotoxic potential at high test concentrations [S13][S15].
- **Evidence against high:**
  - No major bans identified; no IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found for E625 [S10][S12].
  - EFSA did not conclude withdrawal; instead, it set an ADI and exposure-control direction [S1][S4].

### 2. Rationale
The strongest defensible classification is a regulated-caution profile: E625 remains accepted in major frameworks, but EFSA's glutamates re-evaluation established a finite group ADI and documented exceedance risk in certain populations, while direct additive-specific human safety evidence is sparse and in vitro signals remain a non-zero concern.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
