# E948 (Oxygen) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS**: E948 / INS 948. [S1, S3]
- **Name**: Oxygen (molecular oxygen, dioxygen; O2). [S1, S3]
- **CAS number(s)**: **7782-44-7** (current correct CAS used by EFSA/PubMed metadata for E948). EFSA also notes an older/mistyped CAS entry (**7727-44-7**) in legacy specification context and recommends correction to 7782-44-7. [S1, S2]
- **Chemical class**: Inorganic diatomic gas (elemental oxygen). [S1]
- **Common synonyms**: Oxygen, dioxygen, molecular oxygen, O2, INS 948, E948. [S1, S3]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Naturally present in air and endogenous biology; food-grade additive material is industrially produced (e.g., air separation) to specification. [S1, S7]

## Function in Food
- **Technological role**: Packaging gas and (historically for E948) propellant. [S1, S2]
- **Mechanism of action**: Used to control headspace composition in packaging/protective atmosphere systems and, for propellant applications, to expel product from containers. [S1, S2]
- **Common food categories (reported/label-observed)**: Flavoured waters; fresh cheese and cream cheese; broader protective-atmosphere packaged foods (not always visible in ingredient lists because protective atmosphere can be declared without naming gas). [S1]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status**: Approved (currently authorized in the EU at quantum satis in all food categories, including foods for infants and young children). [S1]
- **Latest re-evaluation**: EFSA FAF opinion published in **2025** (EFSA Journal 23(8):e9595). [S1, S3]
- **ADI**: No numerical ADI established; EFSA references historical SCF conclusion that ADI establishment was unnecessary for atmospheric packaging gases/propellants and concludes no safety concern for E948 use. [S1]
- **Key conclusions**:
  - E948/E949 are of low toxicological concern at food-additive use.
  - Dietary exposure expected to be very low; quantitative exposure estimate not considered relevant.
  - No safety concern identified for current food-additive use.
  - EFSA recommends specification updates (including CAS correction). [S1]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- eCFR Title 21 query for "oxygen" returned non-food entries (mainly device/medical-gas sections such as 21 CFR 868 series), not a direct food-additive GRAS oxygen section. [S4]
- In Title 21 Part 184 food-gas entries, nearby gas listings include carbon dioxide, helium, nitrogen, nitrous oxide, and ozone; no dedicated "Oxygen" section was identified in the queried current Part 184 output. [S5, S6]
- **Interpretation for this report**: No explicit Title 21 Part 184 oxygen food-additive GRAS codification was identified in the queried eCFR data. [S4, S5, S6]

### JECFA / WHO
- WHO JECFA chemical record exists for **OXYGEN (INS 948)** with functional class food additive/propellant. [S8, S9]
- EFSA cites that JECFA set **specifications** for oxygen at the 53rd meeting (**1999**) and that no toxicological monograph was prepared. [S1]
- **ADI**: No numerical JECFA ADI value was identified in the queried current WHO/JECFA oxygen outputs; EFSA's summary indicates specification-only JECFA history for oxygen. [S1, S8]
- **Last evaluation year (best available from sources)**: 1999 (JECFA specification meeting context). [S1]

### IARC
- Oxygen (name) and CAS 7782-44-7 were not found as classified IARC agents in the current IARC classifications dataset/list checked. [S10, S11]
- **IARC group for E948**: No listing identified. [S10, S11]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban signal was identified in the reviewed EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC sources. [S1, S4, S8, S10]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Core modern regulatory evidence**: EFSA 2025 re-evaluation concluded E948 use as a food additive does not raise a safety concern and characterized toxicological concern as low at intended use. [S1]
- **Historical ADI context**: SCF (as reported in EFSA) considered ADI unnecessary for atmospheric packaging gases/propellants due minimal contribution relative to background exposure. [S1]
- **High-exposure mechanistic caveat**: EFSA discusses oxygen toxicity concerns in clinical high-FiO2 scenarios (especially premature/newborn contexts), but separates these from realistic food-additive exposure scenarios. [S1]
- **Data-gap caveat**: EFSA notes missing operator submissions on manufacturing details/use levels and residual uncertainty regarding impurity coverage, though this did not change the no-safety-concern conclusion. [S1]
- **Recent human-literature context**: A systematic review of oxygenated water studies reported no significant additional health effect signal versus mineral water in healthy populations (not specific to additive-grade E948 regulation, but relevant oral-exposure context). [S12]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA considered dietary exposure from E948 food-additive uses to be low enough that quantitative exposure modeling was not relevant for the 2025 opinion. [S1]
- Label database findings captured limited explicit E948 labels; EFSA notes packaging-gas use is often declared as "packaged in a protective atmosphere," which can undercapture specific gas identification in ingredient-style databases. [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk**: No numeric ADI exists; based on EFSA's low-exposure/low-concern conclusions, exceedance-type risk is not a practical concern at intended food uses. [S1]
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**: Concern is primarily tied to non-dietary high-concentration inhalation scenarios (e.g., clinical oxygen toxicity settings), not normal food-additive ingestion pathways. [S1]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - EFSA 2025: no safety concern for E948 food-additive use. [S1]
  - ADI considered unnecessary historically for atmospheric packaging gases (SCF context reported by EFSA). [S1]
  - Oxygen is an endogenous/naturally prevalent molecule; food-additive incremental exposure assessed as very low. [S1]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - EFSA noted unresolved data gaps (manufacturing-route details and impurity-coverage uncertainty). [S1]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - EU authorization with quantum satis conditions and favorable EFSA re-evaluation. [S1]
  - No IARC carcinogenic listing identified. [S10, S11]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - The total evidence profile (regulatory conclusion + endogenous substance + low exposure) leans closer to risk_free than to a cautionary limited tier.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Biological plausibility of harm exists at high oxygen inhalation concentrations (medical/high-FiO2 contexts). [S1]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - Those hazards are exposure-regime mismatches versus food-additive use; EFSA still concludes no food-additive safety concern. [S1]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - No direct supporting evidence found in reviewed regulatory/carcinogenicity sources.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier**:
  - No major bans identified, no IARC classification signal for oxygen, and EFSA concluded no safety concern for food-additive use. [S1, S10, S11]

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factors are EFSA's 2025 no-safety-concern conclusion, very low expected dietary exposure at food-additive uses, and the substance being physiologically ubiquitous. The main uncertainty is not a demonstrated hazard at intended use, but incomplete manufacturing/impurity submissions.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `risk_free`**
