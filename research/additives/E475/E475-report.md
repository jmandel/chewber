# E475 (Polyglycerol esters of fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E475 (INS 475)
- **Name:** Polyglycerol esters of fatty acids (PEFA)
- **CAS number(s):** 503590-90-7 (reported in PubMed keyword metadata for the 2022 EFSA follow-up record)
- **Chemical class:** Mixed polyglycerol fatty-acid esters (non-ionic emulsifier class)
- **Common synonyms:** PEFA; polyglyceryl fatty acid esters; glycerin fatty acid esters
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured by esterifying polymerized glycerol with edible fats/oils/fatty acids)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Amphiphilic esters reduce oil-water interfacial tension, stabilizing emulsions and modifying crystal/cloud behavior in fats and oils.
- **Common food functions/categories (documented regulatory uses):**
  - Emulsifier in food (general use under GMP / minimum effective amount)
  - Cloud inhibitor in vegetable and salad oils
  - Emulsifier in dry whipped topping base
- **JECFA functional class:** Food additive; emulsifier.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Approval status:** Approved/authorized (E475 remains authorized; EFSA reevaluation and follow-up focused on specifications).
  - **ADI:** EFSA concluded in the 2017 reevaluation that there was **no need for a numerical ADI** for E475 at reported uses/use levels.
  - **Evaluation year(s):** 2017 reevaluation; **latest follow-up 2022**.
  - **Key conclusions:**
    - 2017: Not of safety concern at reported uses and use levels; no need for a numerical ADI.
    - 2022 follow-up: Recommended tighter impurity/specification controls (toxic elements, erucic acid, 3-MCPD, glycidyl esters) and definition updates.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **GRAS status:** Not identified as a Part 184 GRAS listing in the eCFR Title 21 Part 184 query used here.
  - **Regulatory status:** **Approved direct food additive** under **21 CFR 172.854**.
  - **Conditions of use (21 CFR 172.854):**
    - Prepared from specified edible fats/oils or permitted fatty-acid sources.
    - Used as emulsifiers at levels not greater than needed for intended technical effect.
    - Specific allowance as cloud inhibitor in vegetable/salad oils.
    - Specific allowance in dry whipped topping base with other approved emulsifiers.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **0-25 mg/kg bw/day**.
  - **Last evaluation year in WHO JECFA database page:** **2024** (ADI reaffirmed; page notes no new toxicological information and re-affirmation of 0-25 mg/kg bw).
- **IARC:**
  - No IARC monograph classification found for E475 / polyglycerol esters of fatty acids in the IARC classification resources checked.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources checked.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology (regulatory summaries):**
  - EFSA 2017 reported no adverse effects in short-term, subchronic, or chronic studies at tested levels; NOAELs reported as 9,000 mg/kg bw/day (subchronic) and 2,500 mg/kg bw/day (chronic).
- **Human data:**
  - EFSA 2017 reported a limited clinical study (37 volunteers, up to 300 mg/kg bw/day for 3 weeks) with no adverse findings in reported clinical chemistry/urinalysis.
- **Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity:**
  - EFSA 2017 reported no genotoxic potential from available data and no carcinogenic signal in available chronic/carcinogenicity studies.
- **Mechanistic/other concerns:**
  - EFSA 2022 follow-up highlighted specification-related impurity concerns and recommended stricter limits (toxic elements, erucic acid, 3-MCPD, glycidyl esters).
  - A PubMed-indexed in vitro study (2020) on PGFA excipients reported macrophage functional effects above 1 mg/mL; concentrations up to 1 mg/mL were described as uncritical in that test context.
  - Older in vitro work (2000) reported down-regulation of neutrophil respiratory burst at high concentrations of decaglycerol monooleate.
- **Epidemiological evidence:**
  - No robust recent E475-specific prospective epidemiology was identified in the PubMed queries performed.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate (2017 reevaluation):**
  - Highest non-brand-loyal exposure reported as **2.6 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **6.4 mg/kg bw/day (95th percentile)** in children.
  - EFSA stated this scenario was below the previously endorsed 25 mg/kg bw/day benchmark and concluded no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Uncertainty context from EFSA:**
  - Data were limited to a subset of authorized food categories and included niche-product use levels; EFSA discussed both under- and over-estimation drivers.
- **Additional intake modeling context:**
  - FACET project publication (2013) included E475 among 13 additives and reported that potential theoretical ADI exceedance in children was seen for some additives, but not E475.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Based on EFSA non-brand-loyal estimates, exceedance risk appears low for general populations under reported use levels.
  - Residual uncertainty remains for high processed-food patterns and for impurity-driven exposure concerns addressed in EFSA 2022.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - High consumers of foods where E475 is used, and populations with higher cumulative additive exposure patterns (uncertainty-based, not a confirmed E475-specific harm signal).

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA found no safety concern at reported uses; no genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in available regulatory summaries; no IARC classification found.
  - Evidence against: E475 is a manufactured additive with impurity/specification caveats (EFSA 2022), so "risk_free" is too strong.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved in EU/US; JECFA ADI 0-25 mg/kg bw/day; EFSA 2017 found no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses; exposure estimates in EFSA non-brand-loyal scenario below historical ADI benchmark.
  - Evidence against: Some in vitro high-concentration functional effects and ongoing specification tightening for impurities indicate residual caution.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA follow-up identified non-trivial specification improvements needed; evidence base has data gaps and limited recent human direct evidence.
  - Evidence against: No major bans, no IARC carcinogenic classification, and core regulatory assessments remain favorable.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None compelling from the reviewed evidence.
  - Evidence against: No major jurisdictional bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 signal for E475, and supportive regulatory safety conclusions at authorized uses.

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest evidence is still regulatory: EFSA and JECFA conclude acceptable use with either no numerical ADI needed (EFSA, 2017 opinion) or ADI 0-25 mg/kg bw (JECFA). FDA permits use in 21 CFR 172.854 with explicit conditions. The main caveat is impurity/specification control (EFSA 2022), not a newly established intrinsic toxicity signal at normal dietary use.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
