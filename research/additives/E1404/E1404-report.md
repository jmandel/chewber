# E1404 (Oxidized starch) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1404
- **Name:** Oxidized starch (also written oxidised starch)
- **CAS number(s):** 65996-62-5 (PubChem substance synonym record for "Starch, oxidized"); JECFA's broader "modified starches" entry lists CAS numbers for related modified starches (e.g., starch acetate, acetylated distarch adipate, hydroxypropyl starch, hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate) rather than a single CAS exclusively for E1404.
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified polysaccharide (modified starch); carbohydrate polymer derivative.
- **Common synonyms:** "Starch, oxidized", "OXIDIZED STARCH".
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from natural starch and chemically oxidized).

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Thickener/texturizer; also stabilizer/emulsifier in modified-starch use classes.
- **Mechanism:** Oxidation introduces carbonyl/carboxyl functionalities in starch chains, changing viscosity, gel behavior, and processing performance (e.g., easier handling, film/texture functionality).
- **Common food categories (typical modified-starch uses):** Processed foods needing texture/viscosity control, including sauces/soups, bakery systems/batters, and formulated foods using modified starch functionality.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation (E1404 within a 12-modified-starch group):** 2017 EFSA Journal opinion (DOI: `10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4911`).
- **Approval status:** EFSA opinion states these modified starches (including E1404) are authorised in the EU framework and concludes **no safety concern at reported uses/use levels**.
- **ADI:** **No numerical ADI allocated/needed** (consistent with "ADI not specified" history and EFSA conclusion that no numerical ADI is needed).
- **Key conclusion:** No genotoxic concern identified; no treatment-related adverse effects relevant to human risk assessment at high tested doses; overall no safety concern at reported uses.
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA journal URL access from this environment returned blocking/challenge pages (robots/CAPTCHA), so data were extracted from the PubMed record of the same EFSA paper (same DOI).

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **Status in Title 21:** Regulated as an **approved food additive use** via **food starch-modified** under **21 CFR 172.892** (not a standalone GRAS section for "oxidized starch").
- **Citation:** `21 CFR 172.892` (Food starch-modified).
- **Conditions of use (relevant to oxidized starch):**
  - Food starch-modified may be safely used in food under prescribed limits.
  - For oxidation specifically: food starch may be oxidized using sodium hypochlorite with a maximum limit (`0.055 pound chlorine per pound dry starch`).
  - Quantity of modification reagents must not exceed what is reasonably required for the technical effect.
  - Additive container labeling must include "food starch-modified".
- **GRAS check:** No direct "oxidized starch" GRAS listing was found in the queried Part 184 text; regulatory placement for this use is under Part 172 food additive provisions.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis (strongest evidence):**
  - EFSA 2017 re-evaluation (group including E1404): no safety concern at reported uses; no need for numerical ADI; no genotoxic concern by in silico read-across; high-dose animal data and human tolerance information considered.
  - JECFA: ADI "not specified" in historical toxicological evaluations; recent updates specification-focused.
- **Recent literature signal (non-E1404-specific, weaker for causal attribution):**
  - Large observational NutriNet-Sante analyses report associations between certain **mixtures** of additives (including modified starches in one mixture profile) and type 2 diabetes incidence; these studies explicitly note residual confounding and inability to infer causality from a single observational design.
- **Mechanistic/non-dietary literature:**
  - Much recent "oxidized starch" PubMed output concerns biomaterials/packaging/drug-delivery modifications, which is not directly equivalent to dietary exposure to food-grade E1404.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA group exposure estimate:** The EFSA abstract reports combined exposure for E1404-E1451 at high-percentile refined scenarios up to **3,053 mg/kg bw/day** (group estimate, not E1404-only).
- **ADI exceedance framing:** Because EFSA/JECFA use a non-numerical ADI approach ("not specified" / no numerical ADI needed), classical "ADI exceedance" interpretation is limited.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - No E1404-specific vulnerable subgroup hazard was identified in the reviewed regulatory conclusions.
  - Mixture-based epidemiology suggests additive-combination effects remain an area for continued surveillance/research.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA conclusions are generally favorable; ADI not specified/no numerical ADI needed; no genotoxic concern in the EFSA evaluation framework.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - E1404 is chemically modified (not simply unmodified endogenous starch).
  - Recent epidemiology raises mixture-level concerns for additive combinations (not specific proof for E1404 alone).

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Clear regulatory acceptance (EU EFSA positive conclusion; U.S. CFR authorization; JECFA ADI not specified).
  - No strong direct signal of serious harm at authorized uses in the reviewed regulatory toxicology.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Modern human data specific to E1404 alone are sparse; much newer literature is not directly dietary toxicology.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Observational mixture studies (not E1404-specific) suggest potential chronic-risk associations when additive mixtures are considered.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No EFSA/JECFA downgrading or safety-withdrawal signal for E1404.
  - No evidence here of confirmed E1404-specific ADI exceedance concern causing regulatory restriction.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None identified from EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC checks.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found for oxidized starch.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence is dominated by established regulatory re-evaluations (EFSA/JECFA) and current authorization status (including specific CFR conditions). These support low concern at authorized uses. The main uncertainty is that newer epidemiology increasingly examines additive mixtures rather than E1404 in isolation.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
