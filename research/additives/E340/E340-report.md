# E340 (Potassium phosphates) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E340 (subtypes: E340(i), E340(ii), E340(iii))
- **Name:** Potassium phosphates
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic phosphate salts (potassium salts of phosphoric acid)
- **Components and CAS numbers (JECFA):**
  - **Monopotassium phosphate (E340(i))** - CAS **7778-77-0**
  - **Dipotassium phosphate (E340(ii))** - CAS **7758-11-4**
  - **Tripotassium phosphate (E340(iii))** - CAS **7778-53-2**
- **Common synonyms:** potassium dihydrogen phosphate, dipotassium hydrogen phosphate, tribasic/tripotassium phosphate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **manufactured (synthetic industrial production)** for food use.

## Function in Food
Potassium phosphates are used to control acidity and stabilize food systems, and can also act as sequestrants and water-retention agents.

- **Mechanistic roles (JECFA functional classes):** acidity regulation, raising agent, sequestrant, stabilizer, water-retention
- **Common food categories (EFSA high contributors to phosphate exposure):** fine bakery wares, meat products, processed cheese, sauces, and soft/energy drinks

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved food additive group (phosphates: E338-341, E343, E450-452), re-evaluated by EFSA in **2019**.
- **ADI:** EFSA set a **group ADI of 40 mg/kg bw/day, expressed as phosphorus**.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - Mean/high dietary exposure exceeded the ADI in **infants, toddlers, and children**; high exposure exceeded ADI in **adolescents**.
  - EFSA noted uncertainty for **infants below 16 weeks** and concern for people with **impaired kidney function**.
- **Access note:** The EFSA Journal page URL was reachable in search results but direct retrieval was blocked (HTTP 403 via Wiley/Cloudflare), so EFSA's public communication and indexed abstract text were used.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** **GRAS** for dipotassium phosphate in direct human food use.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 182.6285** (Dipotassium phosphate) - "generally recognized as safe" when used according to **good manufacturing practice (GMP)**.
- **Additional condition example in Title 21:** **21 CFR 160.110(b)** permits monopotassium phosphate in frozen eggs up to **0.5% by weight** (with related labeling conditions).
- **Access note:** Browser eCFR pages were CAPTCHA-blocked; data were pulled from official eCFR API content.

### JECFA/WHO
- **Status/ADI:** For monopotassium, dipotassium, and tripotassium phosphate entries, JECFA lists **"ADI not specified"**.
- **Last evaluation year:** **1982** for these food additive entries.

### IARC
- **Classification:** No specific IARC monograph classification found for potassium phosphates.
- IARC "List of Classifications" dataset (last update observed: **2026-02-25**) did not include monopotassium/dipotassium/tripotassium phosphate entries.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified for E340 potassium phosphates.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Human/intervention evidence
1. **Clin J Am Soc Nephrol (2025, PMID 40359505)** - Feeding study in adults with and without CKD:
   - Lowering phosphorus additive intake reduced 24 h urinary phosphorus (~30% in both groups).
   - In CKD, low-additive diet lowered FGF23 (~25%) and PTH (~20%).
   - Supports biologic sensitivity of mineral metabolism to additive phosphorus exposure.

2. **Eur J Nutr (2024, PMID 38175251)** - Single phosphate-enriched meal (700 mg phosphorus) in healthy adults:
   - No significant adverse change in postprandial inflammatory markers versus placebo.
   - Suggests no clear acute inflammatory signal from one meal in healthy participants.

### Exposure/food-supply evidence
3. **Am J Clin Nutr (2025, PMID 40180501)** - US packaged food survey:
   - Phosphate additives present in **56%** of products from top manufacturers.
   - Indicates broad population exposure potential.

### Mechanistic and observational concerns
4. **Nutrients (2023, PMID 37630701)** - Review:
   - Discusses fast absorption of inorganic phosphate additives and potential cardiorenal pathways (FGF23/PTH, vascular effects).

5. **Brain Behav Immun Health (2025, PMID 41050506)** - Animal model:
   - High-phosphate diet in male mice associated with anxiety-like behavior and neuroimmune changes.
   - Relevance to humans remains uncertain.

6. **Nutrients (2023, PMID 37686766)** - Observational breast cancer analysis:
   - Reported elevated risk estimate at high phosphorus intake but limited by sample size and nonsignificant p-value.
   - Evidence is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark:** EFSA group ADI = **40 mg/kg bw/day as phosphorus**.
- **Exceedance risk:** EFSA identified likely exceedance in children and adolescents at higher exposures.
- **Dietary context:** Recent US packaged-food data indicate high prevalence of phosphate additives in marketed products.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children (higher intake per kg body weight)
  - Individuals with CKD or reduced renal function
  - Potentially other high-processed-food consumers

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - Longstanding regulatory approvals.
  - JECFA entries list "ADI not specified" for the potassium phosphate additives.
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - EFSA established a numeric group ADI and found exceedance in younger populations.
  - CKD-related vulnerability and endocrine/mineral metabolism responses are documented.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - Approved/GRAS status in major jurisdictions.
  - FDA GRAS condition under GMP for dipotassium phosphate.
  - Human acute meal data do not show strong immediate inflammatory harm.
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - EFSA exceedance findings in multiple child age groups.
  - Chronic exposure concerns and CKD-related endocrine changes suggest more than minor high-dose-only concern.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - EFSA set/reduced group ADI framework and identified exceedance in children/adolescents.
  - Human feeding evidence indicates additive phosphorus meaningfully shifts FGF23/PTH and urinary phosphorus, especially relevant to CKD.
  - Broad food-supply prevalence supports realistic high-exposure scenarios.
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - Some studies are review/observational or mechanistic; causal long-term harm from E340 alone is not definitively established in general healthy populations.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Mechanistic plausibility and vulnerable-population concerns exist.
- **Evidence against high:**
  - No broad regulatory bans for E340.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification found for potassium phosphates.
  - Regulators still permit use with ADI/GMP frameworks.

### 2. Rationale
E340 is not risk-free in practice because exposure can exceed EFSA's ADI in younger groups and because CKD/vulnerable populations show biologically relevant effects from additive-phosphorus reduction. At the same time, current evidence does not justify a "high" tier given continuing approvals and lack of carcinogenic classification by IARC.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
