# E951 (Aspartame) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E951
- **Name:** Aspartame
- **CAS number(s):** 22839-47-0
- **Chemical class:** High-intensity sweetener; methyl ester of the dipeptide L-aspartyl-L-phenylalanine
- **Common synonyms:** Aspartame, L-aspartyl-L-phenylalanine methyl ester, E 951, INS 951
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic (industrially produced high-intensity sweetener)

Evidence: PubChem compound record and synonyms [S14].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Aspartame is a high-intensity sweetener (around 200x sweeter than sugar), so very small quantities provide sweetness [S6].
- **Common food categories:** Under US 21 CFR 172.804, use is permitted in specific categories including breakfast cereals, chewing gum, dry beverage mixes, fillings, fruit juices, gelatins/puddings, hard candy, milk products, processed fruits/juices, and soft candy with category-specific maximum percentages [S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation/opinion:** 2013 full risk assessment/re-evaluation (EFSA ANS Panel, EFSA Journal 2013;11(12):3496) [S1][S2][S3].
- **Approval status:** Authorized food additive in the EU [S1].
- **ADI:** 40 mg/kg bw/day [S1][S2][S3].
- **Key EFSA conclusions (2013):**
  - No safety concern for consumers at current exposure levels [S2].
  - Aspartame and its breakdown products were not considered a safety concern at current exposure [S2].
  - No concern for genotoxicity and carcinogenicity based on available evidence [S2].
  - ADI of 40 mg/kg bw/day was retained; not applicable to people with phenylketonuria (PKU) [S2].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not listed as a GRAS-affirmed direct food substance in 21 CFR Part 184 search; regulated as an approved food additive in Part 172 [S4][S5].
- **CFR citation:** 21 CFR 172.804 (Aspartame) [S4].
- **Conditions of use:** Section 172.804 specifies identity/purity conditions, category-specific maximum use levels, and required labeling statement for phenylketonurics due to phenylalanine content [S4].
- **FDA ADI context:** FDA lists an ADI of 50 mg/kg bw/day for aspartame in US guidance content [S6].

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** 0-40 mg/kg bw/day [S7][S8].
- **Latest evaluation year:** 2023 (JECFA re-evaluated at its 96th meeting and maintained ADI) [S8][S10].
- **Additional database context:** WHO/JECFA database entry shows historical evaluations, including older entries [S7].

### IARC
- **Classification:** Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic to humans), announced in 2023 [S9][S10].
- **IARC evidence framing:** IARC reported limited evidence in humans and experimental animals for cancer in relation to aspartame [S10].

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in reviewed EFSA, FDA/eCFR, WHO/JECFA, and IARC sources [S1][S4][S8][S10].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- IARC (2023) classified aspartame in Group 2B and described limited evidence in humans and experimental animals [S10].
- EFSA (2013) concluded the overall dataset did not indicate genotoxic or carcinogenic concern at assessed exposure levels [S2][S3].

### Epidemiological evidence
- **Cancer cohort data (2022, NutriNet-Sante):** Higher intake of artificial sweeteners was associated with higher overall cancer risk; for aspartame, hazard ratio for total cancer was reported at 1.15 (95% CI 1.03-1.28) [S11].
- **Cardiovascular cohort data (2022, NutriNet-Sante):** Higher artificial sweetener intake was associated with CVD risk; aspartame was associated with cerebrovascular events (HR 1.17; 95% CI 1.03-1.33) [S12].
- **Interpretation review (2023):** A review focused on aspartame and cancer judged evidence as suggestive but limited/inconclusive due potential biases and confounding [S13].

### Sensitive-population concern
- People with phenylketonuria (PKU) require avoidance/strict management because aspartame is a source of phenylalanine; US labeling requirements reflect this [S4][S6].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EU regulatory benchmark:** EFSA ADI = 40 mg/kg bw/day [S2][S3].
- **EU intake conclusion:** EFSA reported exposure estimates below ADI for assessed population groups in the 2013 re-evaluation context [S2].
- **WHO practical intake framing:** WHO notes a 70 kg adult would generally need to consume roughly more than 9-14 cans of diet soft drink in a day (assuming no other intake sources) to exceed ADI [S8].
- **Vulnerable populations for monitoring:**
  - People with PKU (phenylalanine sensitivity) [S4][S6]
  - High consumers of multiple aspartame-containing products, especially lower-body-weight consumers (children/adolescents) [S8]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA both maintain ADI-based approvals and did not conclude population-level harm at current dietary exposure [S2][S8].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - IARC classified aspartame as Group 2B in 2023 [S10].
  - Recent cohort studies report statistically significant risk associations for some outcomes [S11][S12].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved by EFSA/FDA/JECFA with defined ADI and use conditions [S2][S4][S8].
  - EFSA and JECFA did not reduce or withdraw authorization after re-evaluation [S2][S8].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - IARC Group 2B classification and contemporary observational signals are more than "minor concerns only at high doses" [S10][S11][S12].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved additive but with important caveats: IARC Group 2B classification, ongoing epidemiologic associations, and a clear vulnerable subgroup (PKU) [S4][S10][S11][S12].
  - Evidence base includes credible human observational signals even though causality is unsettled [S11][S12][S13].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No major regulator has withdrawn authorization; EFSA/JECFA retained ADI-based safety conclusions [S2][S8].
  - Intake estimates in core regulatory assessments are generally below ADI [S2][S8].

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - IARC Group 2B classification can be considered a hazard signal [S10].
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No major bans or withdrawals were identified in primary regulatory sources [S1][S4][S8].
  - Regulatory ADIs remain in place and were reaffirmed by JECFA in 2023 [S8].
  - IARC hazard classification has not been accompanied by broad regulatory prohibition [S10].

### 2. Rationale
Aspartame has broad regulatory authorization with explicit ADI/use constraints and no major-jurisdiction ban. However, it no longer fits a simple "limited concern" profile because IARC classified it as Group 2B in 2023 and recent large prospective cohort studies report associations with cancer/cerebrovascular outcomes. The evidence does not currently justify a high tier because EFSA/JECFA maintained ADI-based safety conclusions and did not impose broad withdrawal.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
