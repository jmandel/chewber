# E201 (Sodium sorbate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E201.
- **Primary name**: Sodium sorbate.
- **CAS number**: **7757-81-5**.
- **Chemical class**: Sorbic acid salt; unsaturated aliphatic carboxylate preservative (sodium (2E,4E)-hexa-2,4-dienoate).
- **Common synonyms**: Sorbic acid, sodium salt; sodium (E,E)-2,4-hexadienoate; INS 201.
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Commercial food-additive sodium sorbate is used as a manufactured salt form; JECFA and PubChem list it as a defined additive chemical (INS 201) rather than a natural extract.

**Evidence**: JECFA chemical entry for INS 201 (CAS, class) and PubChem compound record/synonyms.

## Function in Food
- **Technological function**: Preservative (JECFA functional class).
- **Mechanistic role in foods**: Sorbate preservatives are used for antimicrobial control (especially spoilage yeasts/moulds; additional antifungal data are shown in modern food-model studies with potassium sorbate).
- **Common food categories (from EFSA exposure contributors for sorbates and measured beverage studies)**: bread and rolls, fine bakery wares, flavoured drinks, sauces, processed cheese, and beverage products.

**Evidence**: JECFA functional class; EFSA 2015/2019 exposure contributor categories; beverage monitoring studies with sorbate preservatives.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA follow-up opinion year**: **2019** (FAF Panel follow-up to 2015 re-evaluation).
- **Key EFSA conclusion in 2015 re-evaluation context**: EFSA re-evaluation of E200/E202/E203 reported that available data indicated possible genotoxicity concern for **sodium sorbate**, and it should not be included in the group ADI in that opinion.
- **ADI context**:
  - 2015 opinion: temporary group ADI **3 mg/kg bw/day** for **sorbic acid (E200) + potassium sorbate (E202)**.
  - 2019 follow-up opinion: new group ADI **11 mg/kg bw/day** for **E200 + E202** (based on BMDL 1,110 mg/kg bw/day and UF 100).
- **For E201 specifically**: no separate current EFSA ADI value identified for sodium sorbate in the accessible EFSA opinion set; EFSA ADI values above apply to E200/E202.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status**: **GRAS**.
- **Citation**: **21 CFR 182.3795**.
- **Condition of use**: “generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice.”

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA ADI**: **0-25 mg/kg bw** (group ADI, expressed as sorbic acid, covering sorbic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium sorbates).
- **Evaluation year (latest shown in JECFA entry)**: **1985**.

### IARC
- Sodium sorbate was **not identified as an IARC-classified agent** in the current IARC “List of Classifications” dataset endpoint used by the Monographs site.

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No direct major-jurisdiction ban identified for **E201** in collected sources.
- Related sorbate note: **E203 (calcium sorbate)** was withdrawn from EU additive lists (referenced within EFSA 2019 follow-up discussion).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and experimental toxicology
- EFSA considered reproductive/developmental signals in prior rat data relevant enough to lower the 2015 temporary group ADI for E200/E202 to 3 mg/kg bw/day, then revised to 11 mg/kg bw/day in 2019 after new EOGRTS and BMD analysis.
- Older genotoxicity literature shows mixed findings:
  - Negative/mostly negative findings for sorbic acid and potassium sorbate in several systems.
  - Weak clastogenic findings reported for **stored sodium sorbate** in some assays.
  - A 2012 in-vitro human lymphocyte study reported genotoxic effects for sodium sorbate at higher test concentrations.

### Human epidemiological signal
- A large 2026 NutriNet-Santé prospective analysis reported association between **potassium sorbate exposure** and higher type 2 diabetes incidence (observational association, not proof of causality).

### Mechanistic concerns
- EFSA 2015/2019 context discusses potential formation of reactive degradation/reaction products of sorbates in model systems (e.g., interactions with nitrites or ascorbic acid + iron), while emphasizing limited real-food occurrence/toxicity data under realistic processing/storage.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2019 follow-up reports (using 2015 exposure estimates) that the non-brand-loyal high estimate in children was **3.7 mg/kg bw/day** for sorbic acid/sorbates, with conclusion that the **new 11 mg/kg bw/day ADI** for E200/E202 would not be exceeded.
- Under the earlier temporary ADI framework (3 mg/kg bw/day), exceedance was possible in toddlers/children in some scenarios.
- For E201 specifically, a dedicated EFSA intake-vs-ADI conclusion was not identified because EFSA ADI setting in the accessible opinions is framed for E200/E202.

### Vulnerable populations
- Higher-consuming children/toddlers remain the most sensitive exposure subgroup in EFSA sorbate scenarios.
- High consumption of preservative-rich ultra-processed foods/beverages may increase cumulative preservative exposure.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Supports**: Longstanding approvals in major systems (FDA GRAS; JECFA group ADI).
- **Argues against**: EFSA documented concern signal for sodium sorbate genotoxicity in the 2015 re-evaluation context; mixed genotoxicity literature and ongoing mechanistic uncertainty.

#### `limited`
- **Supports**: Clear FDA GRAS status with GMP condition; JECFA group ADI exists; no IARC carcinogen classification found.
- **Argues against**: EFSA ADI updates were applied to E200/E202, not clearly to E201; some in-vitro and stored-sample genotoxicity signals for sodium sorbate; newer epidemiology on preservative mixtures/sorbate exposure raises caution.

#### `moderate`
- **Supports**: Presence of caveats and uncertainty signals (EFSA sodium sorbate concern statement in re-evaluation context, mixed genotoxic findings, subgroup exposure concerns, emerging observational epidemiology).
- **Argues against**: Regulatory acceptance remains in place (FDA/JECFA), and high-quality evidence for severe harm at typical permitted use levels is not conclusive.

#### `high`
- **Supports**: Could be argued only if strong convergent human + regulatory hazard action existed.
- **Argues against**: No major-jurisdiction blanket ban identified for E201; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification identified for sodium sorbate; regulatory frameworks still permit use.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest data support a **cautionary but not high-hazard** position: formal approvals remain, but EFSA’s sodium-sorbate-specific concern language (within the 2015 re-evaluation context), mixed genotoxic literature, and newer epidemiologic association signals argue against classifying as risk-free/low-concern without qualification.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
