# E322 (Lecithin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E322 (lecithins).
- **Primary CAS number**: **8002-43-5** (lecithins) [S6, S3].
- **Additional CAS number used in EFSA context**: **85711-58-6** (hydrolysed lecithins; listed in EFSA full-text keywords) [S3].
- **Chemical class**: Complex mixture of phosphatides/phospholipids, mainly phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, and phosphatidylinositol [S6, S3].
- **Common synonyms**: Lecithins, phosphatides, phospholipids, soy lecithin [S6, S3].
- **Natural vs synthetic**: Primarily **natural-origin** (isolated from edible oils such as soy/corn/safflower; can be bleached and partly hydrolysed during processing) [S5, S3].

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role**: Emulsifier (amphiphilic phospholipids reduce interfacial tension and stabilize oil-water systems).
- **Secondary roles**: Antioxidant and processing aid/texture aid in several categories [S6, S5].
- **Common food categories**: Bakery, confectionery/chocolate, fat-containing foods, instant mixes, and infant formula/foods for special medical purposes [S3, S4].

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Approval status**: Authorised food additive in EU (Annex II/III framework referenced by EFSA) [S3, S4].
  - **ADI**: EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for lecithins (E322) [S3].
  - **Latest evaluation year**: **2020** (infants below 16 weeks follow-up opinion) [S4].
  - **Key conclusions**:
    - 2017 re-evaluation: no safety concern for general population (>1 year) at refined exposure estimates [S3].
    - 2020 follow-up (infants <16 weeks): intake from infant formula/FSMP did not raise safety concerns up to MPL; EFSA also supported tightening impurity specs (toxic elements) [S4].
- **FDA / eCFR (US)**:
  - **Status**: **GRAS** direct human food substance under **21 CFR 184.1400 (Lecithin)** [S5].
  - **Conditions of use**: “used in food with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice” (via §184.1(b)(1) reference in the lecithin section) [S5].
- **JECFA / WHO**:
  - **ADI**: **NOT LIMITED** [S6].
  - **Evaluation year**: **1973** (with later specification updates listed separately) [S6].
- **IARC**:
  - Lecithin/phosphatidylcholine not found in IARC’s current classification dataset (Volumes 1–140, last update shown 2026-02-25) [S9].
  - Therefore no IARC carcinogenic group assignment identified for lecithin in that listing.
- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology/regulatory package (EFSA 2017)**:
  - Low acute toxicity; no genotoxicity concern; no carcinogenic effects in chronic studies at tested doses in EFSA summary [S3].
  - EFSA noted limited-relevance neurodevelopmental signals at high dietary concentrations in some older studies, and recommended caution for very young infants (later specifically addressed in 2020) [S3, S4].
- **Human/intervention evidence (recent PubMed)**:
  - 2025 placebo-controlled randomized trial of five emulsifiers (including soy lecithin) reported microbiome/SCFA changes with emulsifier exposure but no significant intestinal/systemic inflammation or metabolic endpoint changes versus placebo over intervention period [S10].
- **Allergenicity evidence**:
  - 2020 randomized double-blind crossover trial in children with non-IgE-mediated soy allergy found low reaction rate to soy lecithin challenge and suggested most can tolerate it, while noting occasional individual reactions [S11].
  - 2022 FDA-affiliated in vitro study found polysorbate-80 had stronger tight-junction/allergen transport effects than lecithin in Caco-2 model [S12].
  - 2024 in vitro work suggested lecithin could reduce beta-conglycinin immunoreactivity during digestion under tested conditions [S13].
- **Mechanistic cardiometabolic concern (older but influential)**:
  - NEJM 2013 study linked microbial metabolism of phosphatidylcholine to TMAO and cardiovascular risk associations; this is mechanistically relevant but not a direct regulatory finding that E322 at permitted food-additive exposure is unsafe [S14].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (2017)**:
  - Brand-loyal scenario: mean 7-82 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile 15-187 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Non-brand-loyal scenario: mean 3-22 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile 6-62 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Average dietary intake from regular diet estimated around 4-71 mg/kg bw/day across age groups [S3].
- **Infants/young children**:
  - EFSA 2020 specifically concluded no safety concern up to MPL in infant formula/FSMP use scenarios for infants below 16 weeks [S4].
- **ADI exceedance framing**:
  - No numerical ADI is set by EFSA; JECFA ADI is “not limited,” so classical ADI exceedance calculation is not applicable [S3, S6].
- **Potentially more exposed groups**:
  - Brand-loyal consumers of certain products (infant formula, FSMP, some toddlers) can have higher mg/kg exposures, but EFSA conclusions remained “no safety concern” in evaluated scenarios [S3, S4].

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Supports: Naturally occurring phospholipid mixture; EFSA no numerical ADI needed; JECFA ADI not limited; no regulatory carcinogenic classification found; long history of food use [S3, S6, S9].
  - Against: Some mechanistic and high-dose signal uncertainty (neurodevelopment notes in older animal studies; TMAO pathway concerns), so evidence is not completely "zero concern" [S3, S14].
- **limited**
  - Supports: Approved/GRAS in major jurisdictions, no serious safety signal at authorized uses, and recent human evidence does not show clear inflammatory/metabolic harm in short-term exposure studies [S5, S10].
  - Against: Remaining uncertainty for specific sensitive subpopulations and long-term mechanistic interpretation.
- **moderate**
  - Supports: Existence of mechanistic cardiovascular hypotheses (TMAO), and limited developmental concern flags in older datasets [S3, S14].
  - Against: Regulators did not reduce to numeric low ADI or withdraw authorization; EFSA/JECFA conclusions remain favorable.
- **high**
  - Supports: Very limited support; no major regulatory ban or IARC 2A/2B assignment specific to lecithin identified [S9].
  - Against: Regulatory consensus is continued approval with no numeric ADI and no identified safety concern at current use levels [S3, S4, S5, S6].

2. **Rationale**
- Weight of evidence is predominantly reassuring at approved food-additive uses (EFSA/JECFA/FDA alignment), but there are minor residual concerns from mechanistic literature and specific allergy-sensitive individuals.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**.
