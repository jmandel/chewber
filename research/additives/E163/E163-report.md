# E163 (Anthocyanins) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E163
- **Name:** Anthocyanins
- **CAS number(s):** **7084-24-4** (as listed in JECFA specification monograph for anthocyanins) [S6]
- **Chemical class:** Water-soluble **flavonoid pigments** (red/blue/violet plant pigments) [S6]
- **Common synonyms:** Food Colours, Natural Red 2, Enocianina, INS No. 163, C.I. Natural Red 26 [S6]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **natural**; extracted from edible fruits/vegetables such as grape skins, blackcurrant, elderberry, red cabbage [S6][S1]

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological function:** Color additive (red to purple/blue shades depending on matrix/pH) [S6]
- **Mechanism of action:** Anthocyanins are chromophoric flavonoids; as water-soluble pigments they absorb visible light and impart color, with hue influenced by food matrix conditions (including pH) [S6][S8]
- **Common food categories:** Beverages and beverage bases, fruit drinks, candies, and other foods where natural red/purple coloring is desired. In U.S. CFR text for grape skin extract (anthocyanin-rich), examples include soft drinks, alcoholic beverages, dairy drinks, and confections [S3]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Status:** Approved/re-evaluated food color (E163) in EU context [S1][S2][S11]
  - **Latest EFSA re-evaluation:** EFSA Journal **2013**;11(4):3145 [S1]
  - **ADI:** EFSA ANS Panel stated composition data were inadequate for a full definition of E163, but **temporarily accepted the previous ADI of 2.5 mg/kg bw/day** and concluded no toxicological concern for anthocyanins extracted from edible fruits/vegetables at reported uses [S1]
  - **Key conclusion:** No toxicological concern at reported uses, but characterization/specification gaps remained [S1]
- **FDA / eCFR (U.S.):**
  - **GRAS status:** No standalone generic "anthocyanins" GRAS listing identified in Title 21; U.S. use is handled through color additive listings (exempt from certification) such as grape skin extract [S3]
  - **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 73.170 (Grape skin extract)** [S3]
  - **Conditions of use:** May be safely used for coloring specified foods/beverages, at levels consistent with GMP, and not in standardized foods unless explicitly permitted [S3]
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **ADI:** **0-2.5 mg/kg bw** [S5]
  - **Last evaluation year in JECFA summary page:** **1982** [S5]
- **IARC:**
  - No anthocyanins-specific IARC monograph classification entry was found in the IARC list of classifications [S7]
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in the reviewed regulatory sources [S1][S3][S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis (EFSA/JECFA):**
  - EFSA (2013) found no toxicological concern for anthocyanins from edible sources at reported uses, but also noted insufficient compositional definition and temporary reliance on the previous ADI [S1]
  - JECFA set ADI 0-2.5 mg/kg bw and this remains the value shown in the WHO/JECFA summary entry [S5]
- **Recent animal/genotoxicity evidence:**
  - A 2025 repeated-dose and genotoxicity package for yellow pea flower anthocyanin-rich extract reported no mutagenicity/clastogenicity and no treatment-related adverse effects up to 5% in diet in rats over 90 days [S8]
- **Human data signals:**
  - A 2024 randomized clinical trial of blackcurrant anthocyanin supplementation reported no serious adverse events; only mild GI symptoms were observed [S9]
- **Mechanistic concerns:**
  - A 2021 review noted low-dose oral anthocyanins are generally considered safe, while high-dose/pro-oxidant effects and DNA-damage signals are mainly from in vitro contexts at elevated concentrations [S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA additive exposure estimates (2013):**
  - Refined mean exposure: **0.01 to 0.71 mg/kg bw/day**
  - Refined high (95th percentile) exposure: **0.03 to 4.2 mg/kg bw/day** [S1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Compared with temporary ADI 2.5 mg/kg bw/day, **high-level children** could exceed ADI in EFSA scenarios [S1]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Children with high intake of colored beverages/confectionery are the main group flagged in EFSA exposure modeling [S1]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: naturally occurring plant pigments; EFSA found no toxicological concern at reported uses; no IARC carcinogenic classification located [S1][S7]
  - Evidence against: EFSA reported insufficient compositional/specification clarity and only temporary acceptance of prior ADI; exposure exceedance possible in high-consuming children [S1]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved in EU/U.S. regulatory frameworks; ADI exists from EFSA/JECFA; recent toxicology and human studies show no major safety signal at tested doses [S1][S3][S5][S8][S9]
  - Evidence against: ADI exceedance at high-end child intake and specification uncertainty weaken a purely low-concern classification [S1]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: matches criteria of "approved but with caveats" because EFSA highlighted data gaps and potential ADI exceedance in some child scenarios [S1]
  - Evidence against: absence of strong carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in vivo and no major regulatory withdrawal/bans [S1][S7][S8]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: limited; only theoretical high-dose mechanistic concerns from non-regulatory experimental contexts [S10]
  - Evidence against: no IARC 2A/2B classification found, no major bans, and no clear corroborating regulatory action indicating high hazard [S3][S5][S7]

### 2. Rationale
The most decision-relevant factors are: (a) continued regulatory acceptance, (b) temporary ADI framework with explicit EFSA caveats on characterization, and (c) modeled ADI exceedance potential in high-consuming children. This pattern indicates neither "risk_free" nor "high," but an intermediate profile driven by exposure-context and data-quality caveats.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **moderate**
