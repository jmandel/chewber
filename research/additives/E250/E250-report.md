# E250 (Sodium nitrite) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E250 (INS 250). [S12]
- **Name:** Sodium nitrite. [S11][S12]
- **CAS number(s):** 7632-00-0. [S12]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic nitrite salt (nitrous acid sodium salt). [S11][S12]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium nitrite, nitrous acid sodium salt, INS 250, E250. [S12]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly synthetic as a food additive ingredient (industrial salt), though nitrite can also arise endogenously/from nitrate reduction in the body and foods. [S1][S8][S12]

## Function in Food
Sodium nitrite is used as a **preservative** and **colour fixative** in cured products. In U.S. rules it is explicitly authorized to inhibit *Clostridium botulinum* outgrowth/toxin formation (e.g., smoked fish contexts) and to act as a colour fixative/preservative in curing. [S3][S4]

Common regulated categories include smoked/cured fish products and certain meat-curing preparations with defined ppm limits. [S3][S4]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies and toxicology
- JECFA reviewed chronic animal data and set ADI 0-0.07 mg/kg bw as nitrite ion; the monograph notes nitrite was not carcinogenic in rats or male mice in referenced 2-year studies, with equivocal evidence in female mice forestomach findings. [S8]

### Epidemiological data
- **Type 2 diabetes (NutriNet-Sante, 2023):** higher additives-originated nitrites and specifically sodium nitrite (E250) were associated with higher T2D incidence (HR about 1.54 in higher consumers vs non-consumers in abstract). [S13]
- **Hypertension/CVD (NutriNet-Sante, 2022):** higher additives-originated nitrites (especially sodium nitrite E250) were associated with higher hypertension risk (HR about 1.19), while no clear association was found for CVD outcomes in that analysis. [S14]
- **Cancer incidence (NutriNet-Sante, 2026):** higher sodium nitrite intake was associated with higher prostate cancer incidence in this observational cohort analysis (HR about 1.32). [S15]
- **Additional 2026 diabetes evidence:** preservative-focused cohort analysis also reported sodium nitrite among preservatives associated with higher T2D incidence. [S16]

### Mechanistic concerns
- Endogenous nitrosation and potential formation of carcinogenic N-nitroso compounds remain central concerns across JECFA/IARC/EFSA contexts. [S1][S8][S10][S17]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2017 re-evaluation context:** ADI exceedance risk is mainly in higher-exposure groups (notably toddlers/children in some scenarios); EFSA also highlighted that when combining multiple sources and endogenous nitrate-to-nitrite conversion, ADI exceedance may occur in worst-case assumptions. [S1][S2]
- **JECFA vulnerability note:** ADI not applicable below 3 months of age. [S7]
- **Likely vulnerable populations:**
  - infants under 3 months,
  - toddlers/children with high intake of nitrite-preserved processed foods,
  - high consumers of processed/cured meats,
  - populations with higher endogenous nitrosation conditions/risk factors. [S1][S7][S8][S10]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Longstanding authorization by EFSA/FDA/JECFA with explicit ADI or legal conditions. [S1][S3][S7]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - IARC Group 2A context for ingested nitrate/nitrite under endogenous nitrosation conditions.
  - EFSA/JECFA both highlight caveats (nitrosation, high-exposure groups, infant caveat).
  - Recent cohort studies report associations with T2D/hypertension/prostate cancer for additive nitrites/sodium nitrite. [S1][S7][S10][S13][S14][S15]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Clear regulatory authorization framework with ADI 0.07 mg/kg bw/day (EFSA/JECFA) and CFR use limits in the U.S. [S1][S3][S7]
  - Some toxicology datasets did not show clear carcinogenicity signals in all animal models. [S8]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Exposure exceedance concerns in children/toddlers at high intake scenarios.
  - Mechanistic nitrosation concern and Group 2A context are not minor-only caveats.
  - Emerging epidemiology for additive sodium nitrite associations is stronger than “minor concern only.” [S1][S10][S13][S14][S15]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved additive with ADI and strict use limits, but with meaningful caveats.
  - EFSA highlights potential exceedance in younger/high consumers and broader nitrosation concern.
  - Credible large-cohort epidemiology links additives-originated nitrites/sodium nitrite with higher T2D/hypertension and prostate cancer incidence signals.
  - IARC 2A context (conditional on endogenous nitrosation). [S1][S10][S13][S14][S15]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No outright major-jurisdiction ban specific to E250 in visited core regulatory sources.
  - Hazard evidence is not equivalent to definitive causal proof at all regulated intake levels. [S3][S4][S7]

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Carcinogenic concern context exists (IARC 2A conditional listing) plus recent positive epidemiology signals. [S10][S15]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - E250 remains legally permitted in major jurisdictions with ADI/use limits.
  - Regulators have not concluded a categorical inability to establish safe use under controlled conditions. [S1][S3][S7]

### 2. Rationale
E250 does not fit `risk_free` or `limited` because safety is conditional on strict exposure control and because multiple authoritative lines of evidence (IARC conditional Group 2A context, EFSA caveats, and recent large-cohort associations) indicate non-trivial concern. It does not cleanly fit `high` because outright bans and definitive regulatory withdrawal are absent in the visited primary frameworks.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
