# E150b (Caustic sulphite caramel) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS:** E150b / INS 150b.
- **Primary name:** Caustic sulfite caramel (also written caustic sulphite caramel).
- **CAS number(s):** 8028-89-5.
- **Chemical class:** Class II caramel colour (complex process mixture from heat-treated carbohydrates).
- **Common synonyms:** Caramel colour, Class II; Caustic sulfite caramel.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic (process-derived)** for regulatory classification purposes.

Evidence:
- WHO/JECFA chemical entry for caustic sulfite caramel lists synonym, CAS 8028-89-5, INS 150b, and colour function (WHO JECFA DB, evaluation page, URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1389).
- JECFA summary describes Class II caramels as produced with sulfite compounds and without ammonium compounds (Inchem/JECFA summary, URL: https://inchem.org/documents/jecfa/jecmono/v46je06.htm).

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Brown food colour additive.
- **Mechanism:** Produced by controlled thermal treatment of carbohydrates, generating complex coloured polymers/chromophores that absorb visible light and darken foods.
- **Common food categories:** Foods generally under GMP (unless a standard of identity disallows added colour); in dietary datasets, caramel colours are commonly associated with beverages and seasonings.

Evidence:
- 21 CFR 73.85 defines caramel identity and allows use for colouring foods generally at GMP levels, with standards-of-identity caveat (eCFR API Title 21 Part 73, URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml?part=73).
- Recent U.S. and China exposure papers discuss caramel-colour-containing food categories (PubMed PMIDs 41542868 and 31100047; URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41542868/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31100047/).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (re-evaluated in EU food additive programme).
- **Latest scientific opinion located:** 2011 EFSA ANS re-evaluation of caramel colours E150a,b,c,d.
- **ADI:** Group ADI **300 mg/kg bw/day** for caramel colours; EFSA also set an individual ADI for Class III only (100 mg/kg bw/day).
- **E150b-specific conclusion in EFSA opinion:** Estimated exposure to **Class II (E150b) was below ADI**.

Key EFSA conclusions captured:
- Group ADI 300 mg/kg bw/day established from toxicology database consistency.
- Exposure estimates for Class II below ADI.
- THI/4-MEI/SO2 exposure estimates not of concern in that assessment.

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** Approved as a color additive for food use under 21 CFR; additionally, caramel is listed in GRAS part 182.
- **CFR citations:**

  - **21 CFR 182.1235 (Caramel):** GRAS when used in accordance with GMP.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology signal
- EFSA (2011) and JECFA identify a numerical ADI framework for caramel classes and report no Class II exposure concern at estimated intakes in that EFSA assessment.

### Animal/mechanistic evidence (mostly related contaminant evidence)
- Recent PubMed literature focuses heavily on **4-methylimidazole (4-MEI)** rather than E150b specifically.
- Recent examples:
  - 2026 acute oral mouse study: LD50 estimate 310 mg/kg bw; rapid neurotoxic signs at high doses (PMID 41702566).
  - 2025 review: summarizes hepatotoxic/neuro/reproductive concern signals for 4-MEI and regulatory divergence (PMID 41106562).
  - 2024 mouse study: reduced sperm motility and blood-testis barrier effects under 4-MEI exposure (PMID 39083984).
  - 2025 reproductive/developmental risk assessment estimated MOEs (735-1489) above 100 and concluded low concern for U.S. dietary exposure levels (PMID 40412543).

Interpretation boundary:
- These studies are informative for impurity/process-contaminant risk context, but they are **not equivalent to direct toxicity evidence for E150b itself at regulated food-use exposure**.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA class-specific finding:** Class II exposure estimates were below ADI in the 2011 EFSA caramel-colour opinion.
- **JECFA benchmark:** ADI 0-160 mg/kg bw/day (Class II).
- **Recent 4-MEI exposure context (not Class II-specific):**
  - Updated U.S. dietary assessment (2026) reported lower added-source 4-MEI exposure versus earlier FDA-era estimates (0.016-0.017 mg/day vs prior ~0.043-0.046 mg/day) (PMID 41542868).
  - China dietary intake assessment (2019) found caramel-colour and by-product exposures considered low concern in that dataset (PMID 31100047).
- **ADI exceedance risk for E150b:** Not indicated by EFSA’s class-II exposure conclusion; current uncertainty is mainly lack of very recent class-II-specific intake updates.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of heavily coloured foods (general principle), plus individuals sensitive to sulfites (contextual caution), although EFSA reported SO2 exposure from caramel constituents not of concern in its 2011 analysis.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA/JECFA have established safety frameworks and no direct IARC classification for E150b.
  - EFSA class-II exposure estimate was below ADI.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - E150b does not meet the stronger "ADI not specified / very high-end no-concern" profile.
  - It is a manufactured process mixture, and related-process contaminant debates (e.g., 4-MEI) exist.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved in EU/U.S. and listed in JECFA with numerical ADIs.
  - U.S. CFR allows use at GMP with explicit restrictions.
  - EFSA indicates Class II exposure below ADI and no concern for key caramel constituents in that assessment.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Contemporary toxicology concern literature on 4-MEI keeps a non-zero uncertainty signal in the broader caramel-colour domain.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Credible animal/mechanistic studies exist for 4-MEI; IARC Group 2B classification for 4-MEI raises caution in the broader topic area.
  - Exposure uncertainty persists when extrapolating across caramel classes and jurisdictions.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Signals are largely contaminant/class-context driven and not strong direct evidence that regulated E150b uses are unsafe.
  - EFSA/JECFA class-II conclusions and ADI frameworks do not currently indicate class-II exceedance concerns.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Related compound (4-MEI) has IARC Group 2B classification.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - E150b itself is not IARC 2A/2B, not banned in major jurisdictions, and EFSA/JECFA do not indicate inability to confirm safety for regulated use.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest direct evidence for E150b is regulatory re-evaluation and ADI-based control, with EFSA explicitly reporting Class II exposure below ADI in its major re-evaluation context. Concern signals in recent literature are mostly about 4-MEI toxicology and are important for monitoring but are not sufficient to override the class-specific regulatory safety conclusion for E150b at current authorized use.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
