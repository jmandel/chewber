# E213 (Calcium benzoate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E213
- **Name:** Calcium benzoate (IUPAC: calcium dibenzoate)
- **CAS number(s):** 2090-05-3 (primary); additional registry numbers returned in PubChem xrefs include 5743-30-6. [S12][S13]
- **Chemical class:** Benzoate salt (aromatic carboxylate preservative)
- **Common synonyms:** calcium dibenzoate; calcium salt of benzoic acid [S12]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercial calcium benzoate is a manufactured salt (synthetic route). Benzoic acid itself also occurs naturally in some foods; this is relevant because benzoate exposure includes natural background sources. [S7][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative / antimicrobial (benzoate class). JECFA lists calcium benzoate functional class as preservative. [S4]
- **Mechanism of action:** Inferred from benzoate chemistry and regulatory function assignments: benzoates act as weak-acid preservatives, with antimicrobial effect in acidic foods via benzoic acid/benzoate equilibrium. (Inference based on benzoate-class evidence, not a calcium-benzoate-specific mechanistic trial in these regulatory pages.) [S4][S8][S9]
- **Common food categories (from retrieved regulatory/exposure records):**
  - Standardized margarine/oleomargarine preservatives (as benzoic acid and sodium/potassium/calcium salts) [S8]
  - Acidic/flavored drink scenarios highlighted by EFSA exposure modeling (brand-loyal and non-brand-loyal consumers) [S1]
  - Beverage preservative occurrence studies (market surveillance) [S14]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion/re-evaluation found:** EFSA ANS Panel scientific opinion on benzoic acid/benzoates (E210-E213), published in 2016. [S1]
- **ADI:** **5 mg/kg bw/day**, expressed as benzoic acid (SCF basis retained by EFSA). [S1]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No safety concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity.
  - Exposure scenarios indicated ADI exceedance in some groups (notably toddlers/children in certain scenarios, and infants/toddlers/children in brand-loyal flavoured-drink scenarios).
  - EFSA also noted conservative assumptions may overestimate risk. [S1]
- **Approval status interpretation:** Authorized additive group re-evaluated by EFSA with retained ADI and exposure caveats (not withdrawn by this opinion). [S1]

### FDA / CFR (US, Title 21)
- **Direct GRAS status for “calcium benzoate” as a standalone entry:** Not found in Part 184 in the retrieved up-to-date Title 21 XML search; benzoic acid and sodium benzoate are explicitly listed in Part 184 GRAS sections. [S6][S9][S10]
- **Specific CFR citations found for calcium benzoate name/use:**
  - **21 CFR 178.2010** (Antioxidants and/or stabilizers for polymers): “Calcium benzoate” appears in the permitted substances table for food-contact polymers. [S11]
  - **21 CFR 166.110(b)(5)** (Margarine): permits benzoic acid and sodium/potassium/calcium salts as preservatives at stated limits. [S8]
- **Conditions of use extracted:**
  - In margarine: benzoic/sorbic systems individually 0.1% or combined 0.2% (as acids). [S8]
  - In 178.2010 context: use in food-contact polymer applications under section provisions (amount reasonably required; additional row-specific limits where listed). [S11]

### JECFA / WHO
- **Calcium benzoate page (chemical entry):** ADI shown as **0-5 mg/kg bw**, evaluated in 1996 (older entry context). [S3]
- **Benzoates group entry (newer):** ADI updated to **0-20 mg/kg bw** in 2021 (91st meeting), with previous 0-5 withdrawn, applying to free/conjugated benzoic acid from benzoates and naturally occurring benzoic acid. [S2]
- **Interpretation:** Latest JECFA group evaluation is less conservative than older calcium-specific listing. [S2][S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline:** EFSA found no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern for E210-E213 at established ADI framework. [S1]
- **Exposure-driven concern (children):** EFSA identified scenario-based ADI exceedance in infants/toddlers/children, especially flavor-drink brand-loyal patterns. [S1]
- **Recent analytical/exposure study (beverages):** 2026 J Appl Toxicol study reported sodium benzoate up to 500 mg/L in sampled beverages; cumulative HI remained <1 but child exposure signals were higher than adults. [S14]
- **Recent mechanistic/toxicological signals (non-clinical, mostly sodium benzoate):**
  - 2026 Biochimie: insulin resistance/atrophy signals in C2C12 myotubes at experimental exposure levels. [S16]
  - 2026 Regul Toxicol Pharmacol: rat ovarian/reproductive toxicity markers under sub-chronic sodium benzoate dosing. [S17]
  - 2025 systematic review (mammalian cell studies): frequent cytogenotoxic findings reported across preservatives including sodium benzoate. [S15]
- **High-risk subgroup hypothesis:** 2025 Nutrients review raises theoretical concern about benzoic acid + vitamin C benzene formation in impaired renal clearance settings (hypothesis-generating, not definitive causal proof). [S18]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory ADI reference point used by EFSA:** 5 mg/kg bw/day (as benzoic acid) with exceedance in some child scenarios. [S1]
- **JECFA updated group ADI:** 0-20 mg/kg bw (2021), which may reduce exceedance probability relative to older benchmarks, but jurisdictional risk management may still rely on local limits. [S2]
- **Observed market variability:** beverage data show product-level variability and occasional limit exceedances; child-weight normalization increases estimated exposure burden. [S14]
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants/toddlers/children (high intake/brand loyalty scenarios), and potentially people with impaired renal clearance in high benzoate + vitamin C contexts (hypothesis-based). [S1][S18]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA found no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern; no IARC listing/classification found; JECFA has a numeric ADI framework. [S1][S2][S5]
  - Evidence against: EFSA reported ADI exceedance scenarios in children; recent non-clinical literature reports biologically plausible adverse effects at tested doses. [S1][S15][S16][S17]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Additive class is regulated with ADIs and defined use contexts; eCFR contains allowed uses/conditions; many exposure assessments do not exceed aggregate risk thresholds (e.g., HI<1 in surveyed beverages). [S2][S8][S11][S14]
  - Evidence against: Child-focused exceedance/near-threshold findings and experimental toxicity signals prevent a fully low-concern placement. [S1][S14][S15]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA identifies population subgroups with potential ADI exceedance; credible recent mechanistic/animal/cell evidence indicates adverse effects under some exposure conditions; exposure heterogeneity and product non-compliance can elevate risk locally. [S1][S14][S15][S16][S17]
  - Evidence against: Major regulators maintain additive frameworks with ADIs and no carcinogenic classification by IARC for this agent. [S1][S2][S5]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: Presence of recent reproductive/metabolic/cytogenotoxic signals in non-clinical studies. [S15][S16][S17]
  - Evidence against: No major-jurisdiction ban identified; no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for calcium benzoate; EFSA/JECFA did not conclude inability to establish safety. [S1][S2][S5]

### 2. Rationale
The evidence does not support “risk_free” because child exposure exceedance scenarios are repeatedly flagged by EFSA and recent literature adds plausible hazard signals (mostly from sodium benzoate/benzoate models). It also does not meet “high” because regulatory consensus remains permissive with quantitative ADIs and no IARC carcinogenic classification for this specific additive.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
