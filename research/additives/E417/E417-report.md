# E417 Research Report: Tara gum

## Identity
- **E-number:** E417
- **Name:** Tara gum
- **CAS number(s):** 39300-88-4
- **Chemical class:** Galactomannan polysaccharide hydrocolloid (plant gum)
- **Common synonyms:** Tara gum, Peruvian carob, INS 417
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Natural (extracted from endosperm of *Caesalpinia spinosa* seeds)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrophilic galactomannan that increases viscosity, stabilizes dispersions, and contributes to texture/gel-thickening behavior in processed foods.
- **Common food categories:** Bakery and confectionery systems, dairy desserts, sauces, and other products using hydrocolloid thickeners/stabilizers (EU Group I authorizations under quantum satis contexts).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorised in the EU (Annex II/III uses; EFSA re-evaluation completed).
- **Latest scientific opinion found:** EFSA Journal 2017, "Re-evaluation of tara gum (E 417) as a food additive" (DOI: 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4863).
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI (EFSA concluded "no need for a numerical ADI").
- **Evaluation year:** 2017.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No concern for genotoxicity.
  - Not carcinogenic in rats/mice in evaluated chronic datasets.
  - No safety concern for the general population at refined exposure for reported uses.
  - EFSA also recommended tighter contaminant/microbiological specification controls (e.g., toxic elements, microbiological criteria).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS/CFR status from searched records:** No tara gum entry identified in searched eCFR Title 21 Part 172, 182, or 184 text.
- **Specific CFR citation outcome:** No direct 21 CFR section for tara gum identified in searched parts; a related botanical mention appears in **21 CFR 184.1097** (tannic acid source includes tara seed pods), but this is not a tara gum authorization.
- **GRAS notices context:** FDA GRAS Notice Inventory search for exact term "tara gum" returned **0 records** on the visited search result page.
- **Conditions of use in CFR:** No tara gum-specific conditions found in searched 21 CFR parts.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Database entry:** TARA GUM (Chemical ID 4115).
- **ADI:** NOT SPECIFIED.
- **Last evaluation year shown:** 1986 (earlier temporary ADI history listed for 1981/1984).

### IARC
- **Classification:** No IARC monograph classification entry for tara gum identified in the checked IARC classification pages/dataset resources.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified for tara gum itself in the reviewed EFSA/JECFA/FDA/IARC sources.
- Historical EU risk-management context exists for gel-forming additives (including tara gum) in jelly mini-cups due choking hazard scenario; this is a product-format restriction context, not a global additive ban.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA review (2017) and supporting chronic rodent studies found no carcinogenicity signal for tara gum at tested dietary concentrations.
- NTP-linked chronic/carcinogenicity publications in rats/mice reported no treatment-related tumor increase attributable to tara gum.
- EFSA summary indicates high-dose NOAELs in subchronic/chronic/reproductive datasets and no genotoxicity concern.

### Epidemiological / human data
- Direct modern epidemiological evidence specifically on purified E417 is limited in accessible literature.
- PubMed-indexed recent hazard discussion (2024) concerns tara seed germ flour by-product and accidental illness hypotheses; this is related to *tara-derived ingredients* but not direct evidence of harm from purified food additive-grade tara gum.

### Mechanistic concerns
- EFSA notes tara gum is unlikely to be absorbed intact and is expected to be fermented by intestinal microbiota into SCFAs; this was not considered a toxicological concern at reported uses.
- EFSA recommended stricter contaminant/microbiological specs for gums (including tara gum), indicating quality-control relevance rather than intrinsic additive toxicity at authorized use.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined intake estimates (reported-use scenario):** up to **70 mg/kg bw/day** in children (3-9 years, non-brand-loyal scenario) for evaluated categories.
- **Food supplements consumer-only scenario:** approximately **10 mg/kg bw/day** (adolescents) to **51 mg/kg bw/day** (children).
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Classical exceedance comparison is not applicable because EFSA and JECFA use "not specified" / no numerical ADI conclusions.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Children/high consumers (higher per-body-weight exposure).
  - Consumers of concentrated supplement products.
  - Populations potentially affected by contaminant/specification failures in raw gum supply chains (quality control issue).
  - Choking-risk contexts for certain gel confectionery formats (historical mini-cup restriction context).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA both indicate non-numerical ADI ("not specified").
  - No genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern in core toxicology evaluations.
  - Natural plant-derived polysaccharide with low systemic absorption expectation.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA highlighted specification/contaminant-control needs.
  - Modern direct human safety datasets for purified E417 are relatively limited.
  - US CFR codification/GRAS-notice evidence for "tara gum" is not explicit in searched records.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EU-authorised with completed EFSA re-evaluation and no safety concern at assessed exposures.
  - JECFA ADI "NOT SPECIFIED" with long-standing evaluation history.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Regulatory/textual ambiguity in US CFR listings for tara gum by name.
  - Evidence base is weighted toward older animal studies, with fewer modern dedicated toxicology trials.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Could be argued due data-age limitations, specification concerns, and external tara-derived ingredient hazard discussions.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No reduced ADI, no EFSA unsafe conclusion, no major ban pattern, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 designation.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Minimal; no strong regulatory-toxicological trigger for this level in reviewed sources.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA safety conclusions at reported uses and ADI-not-specified stance.
  - No major-jurisdiction broad ban for tara gum itself.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence supports a low-to-limited risk profile for additive-grade tara gum under reported authorized uses, with key caveats centered on specification/quality controls and the relative scarcity of modern human-focused toxicology.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
