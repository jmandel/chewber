# E226 (Calcium sulfite) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E226
- **Name:** Calcium sulfite
- **CAS number(s):** 10257-55-3; 15091-91-5
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfite salt (calcium salt of sulfurous acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium sulphite; Sulfurous acid, calcium salt (1:1); INS 226
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic** as a manufactured food additive.

## Function in Food
- Sulfites are used as **preservatives/antioxidant reducing agents** to slow discoloration/browning and oxidation in foods and beverages.
- Reported major sulfite-contributing food categories include **dried fruit, sausages/processed meats, non-alcoholic beverages/soft drinks, fruit juices, processed potatoes, beer, and wine** (country-dependent).
- FDA also describes sulfites as additives used during food preparation/processing to preserve color and stability.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Authorized as part of the sulfur dioxide-sulfites group (E220-E228), but currently under significant safety concern.
- **Key opinion year:** 2022 (follow-up opinion); **latest exposure update:** 2025.
- **ADI status:** EFSA reported that the 2016 temporary group ADI of **0.7 mg/kg bw/day (as SO2 equivalents)** was **withdrawn** in 2022 because the toxicity database remained inadequate.
- **Key conclusions (2022):**
  - Could not derive an ADI from available toxicity data.
  - Used MOE approach; MOEs were below the target value for many populations.
  - Concluded this raises a **safety concern**.
- **2025 update (EFSA supporting publication):**
  - Reported exceedance of levels considered safe in portions of children and adults in surveyed populations.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- Direct additive listing for **calcium sulfite** was not found in Title 21 snapshot searched via eCFR API.
- eCFR Part 182 Subpart D lists related sulfiting GRAS substances (e.g., potassium bisulfite, sodium bisulfite, sodium metabisulfite, sodium sulfite, sulfur dioxide), but not calcium sulfite.
- **CFR citation used for sulfite GRAS framework and conditions:** 21 CFR 182.3798 (sodium sulfite), 21 CFR 182.3862 (sulfur dioxide), and neighboring sulfite entries in 21 CFR Part 182 Subpart D.
- **Conditions of use (for listed sulfites):** not for meats, foods recognized as vitamin B1 sources, or raw fruits/vegetables presented as fresh.
- **FDA public guidance:** sulfites are not allowed on fruits/vegetables intended to be sold or served raw to consumers (except potatoes) and are associated with sensitivity reactions in susceptible individuals.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Calcium sulfite entry:** group ADI **0-0.7 mg/kg bw** (as sulfur dioxide), **evaluation year 1998**.
- **Related sulfites entry:** intake re-considered in **2008**; high regular wine consumers at high percentile exposure could exceed the ADI.

### IARC
- IARC Monographs (Vol. 54) classify **sulfur dioxide and some sulfites/bisulfites/metabisulfites** as **Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans).

### Notable bans/restrictions
- US restrictions include prohibitions on certain direct sulfite uses (e.g., raw produce for direct consumer sale/serving, with limited exceptions).
- No major jurisdiction-wide total ban on E226 specifically was identified in the retrieved sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- Recent rodent studies with sodium metabisulfite/sulfite exposures report:
  - Oxidative stress biomarkers in brain tissue.
  - Behavioral/neurofunctional changes at higher repeated doses (e.g., anxiety-/depression-like behaviors).
- These findings support biological plausibility for adverse effects at sufficiently high exposures, but they are not direct human causal proof for typical dietary E226 exposure levels.

### Human/allergenicity evidence
- Sulfites are recognized triggers in susceptible people (especially some asthmatics); FDA documents this concern.
- 2024 dermatology review (ACDS Allergen of the Year) highlights sulfites as a relevant allergen source across foods, drugs, and personal-care products.

### Carcinogenicity
- IARC Group 3 does **not** support classifying sulfites as known/probable/possible human carcinogens.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2022:** MOE-based assessment indicated safety concern in multiple populations under refined and MPL scenarios.
- **EFSA 2025 update:** reported that in pooled survey scenarios, exceedance of levels considered safe was present in both children and adults in nontrivial fractions of surveys.
- **JECFA 2008:** high wine consumers (97.5th percentile) may exceed the 0-0.7 mg/kg bw ADI; children/teen contributions can come from beverages, processed potatoes, dried fruit, and sausages.
- **Vulnerable populations:** sulfite-sensitive individuals (especially asthmatics), high consumers of sulfited foods/beverages, and some child cohorts.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting:** Historical international use; JECFA established a numerical group ADI.
- **Evidence against:** EFSA could not confirm safety in follow-up, withdrew temporary ADI, and identified population-level concern.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting:** Sulfite group remains regulated with explicit use conditions; IARC carcinogenic evidence is not strong (Group 3).
- **Evidence against:** Calcium sulfite itself is not directly GRAS-listed in searched US CFR text; EFSA/JECFA exposure analyses show possible ADI-related concern in high consumers.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting:** Approved/regulated context with caveats, exposure exceedance signals, and supporting animal/mechanistic concern.
- **Evidence against:** EFSA finding goes beyond routine caveats by concluding the database is inadequate to derive ADI and that current exposure scenarios raise safety concern.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting:** EFSA 2022 follow-up explicitly withdrew temporary ADI and concluded safety concern under assessed exposure scenarios; 2025 EFSA exposure update still indicates exceedance in parts of populations.
- **Evidence against:** No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification; no clear evidence of major jurisdiction-wide outright ban of E226.

### 2. Rationale
- The strongest driver is the **EFSA follow-up outcome** (inability to derive ADI + MOE-based safety concern), reinforced by updated exposure signals and known sulfite sensitivity in susceptible groups.
- Offsetting factors (IARC Group 3; retained JECFA group ADI) prevent a maximal-confidence severe conclusion but do not negate EFSA's current concern.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `high`**
