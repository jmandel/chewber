# E414 Research Report: Acacia gum (Gum arabic)

## Identity
- **E-number:** E414
- **Name:** Acacia gum (gum arabic)
- **CAS number:** 9000-01-5
- **Chemical class:** Natural, high-molecular-weight plant exudate polysaccharide (mainly arabinogalactan-type polysaccharides with minor protein fraction).
- **Common synonyms:** Gum arabic, acacia gum, gum arabic (Acacia senegal), gum arabic (Acacia seyal).
- **Origin:** Natural (dried exudate from stems/branches of Acacia spp.).

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological roles:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener (JECFA and CFR functional classes).
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Forms protective colloids and interfacial films, improving oil-in-water emulsion stability.
  - Provides modest viscosity and texture control in aqueous systems.
  - Functions as soluble dietary fiber in some applications.
- **Common food categories (from CFR limits/examples):** beverages, chewing gum, confectionery (hard/soft candy), dairy analogs, fillings/puddings, snack foods, soups, grain-based bars, and other categories under GMP/maximum-use constraints.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised (E414 listed as authorised food additive under Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008; EFSA re-evaluated safety).
- **Latest evaluations identified:**
  - **2017 ANS Panel re-evaluation** (general population): concluded no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses.
  - **2019 FAF Panel follow-up** (infants below 16 weeks): again considered no need for numerical ADI and found no safety concern under assessed uses/exposure scenarios.
- **ADI:** Effectively “not specified / numerical ADI not required”.
- **Key conclusion:** No safety concern at reported use levels; recommendations focused on impurity limits/specification updates.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:**
  - **21 CFR Part 184** is titled “Direct Food Substances Affirmed as Generally Recognized as Safe”.
  - **21 CFR 184.1330** specifically covers acacia (gum arabic) with listed food-category maximum levels.
  - **21 CFR 582.7330** lists gum arabic as GRAS for animal feed use under good manufacturing/feeding practice.
- **Additional food additive listing:** **21 CFR 172.780** allows acacia (gum arabic) in food with specified conditions and maximum use levels (including certain named categories).
- **Conditions of use (examples from CFR text):**
  - 184.1330 sets category-specific maximum percentages (e.g., beverage bases, chewing gum, confectionery, etc.).
  - 172.780 includes category-specific limits and references GMP and FCC specifications.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **90-day oral rat study (Food Chem Toxicol, 2006):** no treatment-related toxicological findings up to 5% dietary inclusion; reported NOAEL at highest tested level (~3.1–3.3 g/kg bw/day depending on sex).

### Human studies
- **Healthy adults crossover trial (Nutrients, 2021):** 20 g and 40 g doses in a meal context; gum acacia described as well tolerated, with increased bloating/flatulence/rumbling at 40 g but overall low GI symptom scores.
- **Sickle cell cohort supplementation study (Blood Research, 2019):** 30 g/day for 12 weeks; no major safety signal reported in the abstracted clinical chemistry outcomes.

### Regulatory toxicology conclusions
- **EFSA 2017 re-evaluation:** no concern for genotoxicity; no need for numerical ADI; no safety concern at reported uses.
- **EFSA FEEDAP 2025 (animal feed additive):** no genotoxicity concern; safe for target animal species at assessed feed levels; user-safety caveat for dermal/respiratory sensitisation.

### Allergenicity and case signals
- PubMed includes occupational allergy case reports (e.g., allergic rhinitis/contact urticaria in candy-factory exposure contexts), suggesting sensitisation risk in high-exposure occupational settings rather than typical dietary consumer exposure.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2017 exposure estimates (refined scenarios):**
  - Mean exposure ranged from <1 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) up to 291 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers).
  - 95th percentile ranged from 3 mg/kg bw/day (elderly) up to 758 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers).
  - Supplement-consumer scenario reached higher values (reported up to ~1,582 mg/kg bw/day in children).
- **EFSA 2019 infant (<16 weeks) scenarios:** mean around 8.7 mg/kg bw/day and high-level scenarios around ~20.5–24.5 mg/kg bw/day (depending on scenario assumptions).
- **ADI exceedance framing:** EFSA/JECFA use “ADI not specified / no numerical ADI required”, so classical exceedance-to-ADI comparison is not applicable.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:**
  - High consumers (especially toddlers/children) due to greater intake per body weight.
  - Infants consuming specific formula/medical-purpose products (assessed by EFSA with no safety concern under evaluated assumptions).
  - Workers with inhalation/dermal exposure (sensitisation concern).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA both conclude no need for numerical ADI (ADI not specified / not required).
  - Natural food gum with long food-use history.
  - No genotoxicity concern in major regulatory assessments reviewed.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Occupational sensitisation/allergy cases exist.
  - High-dose GI symptoms (bloating/flatulence) are reported in human studies.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved in EU and affirmed GRAS/allowed in US CFR framework.
  - Regulatory conclusions show no serious systemic toxicity signal at intended use levels.
  - Human and animal studies generally support tolerability/safety at relevant intake ranges.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - User-safety caveats (respiratory/dermal sensitisation potential) and rare allergy cases prevent labeling as essentially risk-neutral in all contexts.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - High per-kg exposures can occur in toddlers/high consumers.
  - Credible sensitisation/allergy signals exist in occupational settings.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No EFSA/JECFA numerical ADI restriction was set.
  - EFSA did not identify consumer safety concern at assessed dietary exposures.
  - No major jurisdictional bans or withdrawals identified.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Rare severe allergy case reports exist (limited-case evidence).
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - No broad regulatory ban/withdrawal.
  - Core regulatory assessments consistently support safety at intended uses.

### 2. Rationale
The total evidence is most consistent with an additive that is broadly permitted and well supported by regulatory review, while still having limited caveats (notably occupational sensitisation and mild GI intolerance at higher doses).

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
