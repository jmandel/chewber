# E514 (Sodium sulfates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E514 (split into E514(i) and E514(ii))
- **Primary names:**
  - **E514(i):** sodium sulfate (also called Glauber's salt in decahydrate form)
  - **E514(ii):** sodium hydrogen sulfate (sodium bisulfate)
- **CAS numbers:**
  - **7757-82-6** (sodium sulfate; listed in 21 CFR 186.1797)
  - **7681-38-1** (sodium bisulfate / sodium hydrogen sulfate)
- **Chemical class:** inorganic sulfate salts (sodium salts of sulfuric acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Glauber's salt, sodium sulphate/sulfate, sodium acid sulfate, sodium bisulfate, nitre cake
- **Natural vs synthetic:** occurs naturally (e.g., mineral forms) but food additive-grade material is commonly manufactured industrially (e.g., neutralization process described in CFR)

## Function in Food
- **Main technological functions:** acidification/pH control and related processing roles; JECFA also lists INS 514(i) as a colour-retention agent and INS 514(ii) as an acid.
- **Mechanistic basis:** dissociates to sulfate/sodium/hydrogen ions; can lower pH (especially E514(ii)) and modify ionic environment.
- **Common food categories (EFSA exposure contribution):** bread and rolls, fine bakery wares, and (in conservative scenarios) meat products.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Re-evaluation published 2019 (EFSA Journal 17(10):5868). EFSA concluded exposure does **not** raise a safety concern at reported uses/use levels and there is **no need for a numerical ADI** (effectively ADI not specified). The additives are authorized in EU food additive legislation (Annex II/III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008).
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 186.1797** (Sodium sulfate)
  - **Status context:** In Part 186 (indirect food substances affirmed as GRAS), sodium sulfate is affirmed for indirect food-contact uses.
  - **Conditions of use:** constituent of paper/paperboard food packaging and cotton/cotton fabric for dry food packaging; use at levels not exceeding good manufacturing practice (per 186.1797(c) and 186.1(b)(1)).
  - **Scope note:** this citation is for **indirect** food-contact use, not broad direct-addition use.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **INS 514(i) Glauber's salt:** ADI **NOT SPECIFIED**, evaluation year shown as **2001**.
  - **INS 514(ii) Sodium hydrogen sulfate:** ADI **NOT SPECIFIED**, evaluation year shown as **2009**.
- **IARC:** no specific listing found for sodium sulfate/sodium hydrogen sulfate in the IARC Monographs “List of Classifications” dataset (no Group 1/2A/2B/3 assignment identified for E514 itself).
- **Notable bans:** none identified in major jurisdictions reviewed here.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA 2019):**
  - Sulfates considered low in acute oral toxicity.
  - No concern identified for genotoxicity and carcinogenicity.
  - Subchronic study: diarrhea noted at high dose; EFSA considered **2,025 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** as NOAEL (highest tested in that study context).
  - Chronic/carcinogenicity data (ammonium sulfate) indicated no carcinogenicity, NOAEL **1,090 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** (highest tested).
  - Data gap: no reproductive/developmental studies aligned with current OECD expectations were available.
- **Human-effect threshold context (EFSA):** laxative effects in humans reported at **300 mg sodium sulfate/kg bw** (about **207 mg sulfate/kg bw**), far above EFSA’s refined non-brand-loyal exposure estimates.
- **Recent PubMed safety signals (not food-additive-dose studies, but oral sulfate exposure context):**
  - 2023 RCT and 2024 trial (colonoscopy prep): generally good safety/tolerability and no clinically significant lab abnormalities in trial settings.
  - 2025 RCT: no severe adverse events reported with sulfate solution prep.
  - 2025 retrospective cohort: higher rates of erosive gastritis/ulcer findings with oral sulfate tablet vs PEG in one center; hypothesis-generating and not definitive for food-additive-level exposure.
- **Specification/impurity concern (EFSA):** recommendation to lower toxic-element limits (arsenic/lead/mercury) in specifications to minimize contribution from additive impurities.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal scenario (most realistic for risk characterization):**
  - Mean: **0.4 to 35 mg sulfate/kg bw/day**
  - High percentile: **3 to 68 mg sulfate/kg bw/day**
- **EFSA refined brand-loyal scenario:** up to **129 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** at high percentile in children.
- **EFSA maximum-level conservative scenario:** high percentile up to **217 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** in children; EFSA explicitly characterizes this framework as conservative and likely overestimating typical long-term exposure.
- **ADI exceedance interpretation:** no numerical ADI was set by EFSA/JECFA; comparison is therefore to effect thresholds (not ADI exceedance). Typical refined exposure remains well below the human laxative-effect level.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** toddlers/children (highest modeled intake), people with GI sensitivity, and scenarios with very high short-term sulfate bolus intake.

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA/JECFA “no numerical ADI/ADI not specified”; no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern; sulfate is a normal biological constituent.
  - Evidence against this tier: high-dose GI/laxative effects are well recognized; data gaps remain for modern reproductive/developmental packages; impurity-spec concerns exist.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: approved by EFSA/JECFA and affirmed GRAS (indirect use in US CFR); refined exposure estimates are below known effect thresholds; serious hazard signals are weak at food-additive exposure ranges.
  - Evidence against this tier: some uncertainty from incomplete toxicology domains and occasional clinical sulfate-prep GI findings at much higher short-term doses.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: conservative high-end modeled exposure can approach/exceed laxative threshold; retrospective clinical signal for erosive gastritis with sulfate bowel-prep tablets.
  - Evidence against this tier: those signals come from high-dose medical prep contexts and conservative exposure assumptions, not typical food additive intake; major regulators still conclude no safety concern at reported uses.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: no major supporting evidence found (no IARC classification for E514 itself, no major-jurisdiction ban identified, no EFSA inability-to-assess outcome).
  - Evidence against this tier: broad regulatory acceptance with non-numerical ADI conclusions and low concern at reported use levels.

### 2) Rationale
E514 salts have long-standing regulatory acceptance with convergent EFSA/JECFA conclusions of no need for a numerical ADI at authorized uses, and exposure estimates are generally below human effect thresholds. Remaining concerns are mainly high-dose GI effects, impurity control, and incomplete modern reproductive/developmental datasets.

### 3) Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
