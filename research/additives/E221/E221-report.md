# E221 (Sodium sulfite) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E221 [S1]
- **Name:** Sodium sulfite (disodium sulfite) [S1][S9]
- **CAS number(s):** 7757-83-7 (anhydrous sodium sulfite) [S1][S9]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfite salt (sulfurous acid salt) [S9]
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium sulphite, disodium sulfite, INS No. 221, E-221 [S9]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily a manufactured (synthetic) additive used as a preservative/antioxidant in processed foods; sulfite chemistry also occurs in biological/food systems. (Inference from regulatory/additive context and chemical identity) [S1][S9]

## Function in Food
Sodium sulfite is used mainly as a **chemical preservative/reducing antioxidant**. In food systems, sulfite/bisulfite species can suppress oxidation and inhibit undesirable chemical/enzymatic changes (including anti-browning effects in some applications), and help control spoilage processes. [S5][S10]

Common categories include sulfite-permitted processed foods and beverages (for example, sulfited beverages and preserved products) under jurisdiction-specific limits. [S1][S6]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- EFSA’s 2022 follow-up opinion on sulfur dioxide/sulfites (including E221) concluded that the toxicity database was **inadequate to derive an ADI** and therefore **withdrew the temporary group ADI** previously set in 2016. [S1][S2]
- The 2016 temporary group ADI had been **0.7 mg SO2 equivalents/kg bw/day**. [S1][S2]
- EFSA moved to a **MOE approach** and concluded that exposure scenarios raise a **safety concern** (MOEs below 80 for multiple population groups at high exposure). [S1][S2]
- Evaluation year used here: **2022** (follow-up opinion). [S1][S2]
- Authorization context: these sulfites were described as authorized in the EU, but with risk concerns from updated assessment. [S1]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status in regulation:** 21 CFR § 182.3798 (Sodium sulfite) describes sodium sulfite as generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with GMP, with explicit restrictions. [S5]
- **Conditions/restrictions in section text:** not used in meats; not in foods recognized as a source of vitamin B1; not on fruits/vegetables intended to be served or sold raw/fresh to consumers. [S5]
- Note: direct `ecfr.gov` page requests from this VM returned anti-bot/captcha “Request Access” pages, so section content was taken from the Cornell LII mirror of e-CFR text. [S4][S5]

### JECFA / WHO
- WHO/JECFA database entry for sodium sulfite/sulfites reports group ADI **0–0.7 mg/kg bw** (as sulfites/SO2 basis context) and includes evaluation history entries for **1998** and **2008**. [S6]
- The database text indicates potential for high consumers to exceed ADI in some intake scenarios. [S6]

### IARC
- IARC Monographs classification data list **“Sulfites” as Group 3** (Volume 54; evaluation year 1991, publication year 1992). [S7][S8]
- “Sulfur dioxide” is also listed as Group 3 in the same volume. [S7]
- No separate current IARC Group 1/2 classification specifically for sodium sulfite (E221) itself was identified in the visited IARC classification dataset. [S7]

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No major full-jurisdiction ban identified in the visited core sources; however, FDA CFR includes explicit use restrictions (notably raw produce/meat/B1-source constraints). [S5]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2022 follow-up considered new/old evidence and highlighted CNS-related concerns (including prolonged visual evoked potential latency basis for reference point), while maintaining no genotoxicity concern for sulfites as food additives. [S1][S2]
- In vitro/mechanistic studies report oxidative-stress-mediated toxicity signals (for example gastric mucosal cell injury model with sodium sulfite). [S10]
- Newer animal/mechanistic work (e.g., mouse osteoporosis/ferroptosis model) reports adverse outcomes at experimental exposures, supporting biological plausibility but not by itself proving risk at regulated dietary exposure levels. [S11]
- Oral microbiome experimental work reported short-term viability/composition changes after sulfite preservative exposure in saliva models. [S12]

### Epidemiological / human-oriented evidence
- EFSA 2022 is the main regulatory human-risk synthesis for E220–E228 and concluded safety concern under modeled dietary exposures. [S1][S2]
- A broader 2025 cohort on **food additive mixtures** found associations with type 2 diabetes incidence for some mixtures (not specific to E221 alone), indicating mixture-level uncertainty relevant to additive risk assessment contexts. [S14]
- A 2025 review on artificial additives and lung health flags sulfites as potential contributors to bronchial hyper-responsiveness/allergic reactions, especially in sensitive groups; this is supportive but lower-certainty evidence than targeted dose-response regulatory assessments. [S13]

### Overall evidence signal
- Strongest regulatory signal: EFSA follow-up (2022) withdrew temporary ADI and flagged safety concern via MOE approach. [S1][S2]
- Key uncertainty: translation of mechanistic/high-dose experimental findings to typical dietary intakes and specific contribution of E221 versus total sulfite mixture exposure. [S1][S10][S11]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA refined scenario (Data set D) reported mean dietary exposures from **<0.01 mg SO2 eq/kg bw/day (infants)** up to **0.32 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers)**; 95th percentile up to **1.17 mg/kg bw/day (adults)**. [S1]
- Under EFSA MOE framework (assessment factor 80), high-percentile MOEs were below target for most groups (except adolescents in one refined high-percentile range), and MPL-based scenarios also raised concern. [S1][S2]
- JECFA database also records potential ADI exceedance in high consumers depending on scenario/data inputs. [S6]
- Vulnerable populations highlighted across sources: high consumers, sulfite-sensitive individuals (especially asthma/allergy phenotypes), and potentially children depending on exposure pattern. [S1][S13]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Long regulatory history of permitted use in multiple jurisdictions. [S1][S5][S6]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA withdrew temporary ADI and identified safety concern under current exposure scenarios.
  - JECFA intake history indicates potential ADI exceedance in high consumers. [S1][S2][S6]

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - FDA CFR still treats sodium sulfite as GRAS with GMP and specific restrictions.
  - JECFA group ADI exists (0–0.7 mg/kg bw). [S5][S6]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA’s latest follow-up could not maintain ADI and raised safety concern at modeled exposures.
  - Mechanistic and animal evidence continues to suggest biologically plausible adverse pathways. [S1][S2][S10][S11]

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved/authorized but with major caveats (ADI withdrawal by EFSA, MOE-based concern, high-exposure risk).
  - Evidence of sensitivity/allergenicity and respiratory concerns in susceptible groups.
  - Multiple mechanistic toxicity signals exist, though dose relevance varies. [S1][S2][S10][S11][S13]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No IARC Group 1/2 carcinogenic classification; EFSA did not conclude genotoxic concern.
  - Not broadly banned across major jurisdictions. [S1][S7]

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA safety concern and ADI withdrawal are substantial risk-management flags. [S1][S2]
- Evidence against this tier:
  - IARC classification for sulfites is Group 3 (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity), not Group 2A/2B/1.
  - Regulatory systems still permit use with restrictions; no broad major-jurisdiction ban identified. [S5][S7]

### 2. Rationale
E221 does not fit risk_free/limited because the most recent EU risk reassessment withdrew the temporary ADI and found safety concern under realistic high-exposure scenarios. It does not clearly fit high because major jurisdictions still permit use with restrictions and IARC carcinogenic classification is Group 3. The balance of evidence best matches **moderate** risk: approved but with important caveats, exposure-driven concern, and susceptible-population issues. [S1][S2][S5][S6][S7]

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
