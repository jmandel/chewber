# E100 (Curcumin) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E100 (INS 100i).
- **Primary chemical:** Curcumin (major curcuminoid).
- **CAS number(s):** 458-37-7 (curcumin).
- **Chemical class:** Curcuminoid polyphenol colorant.
- **Common synonyms:** Turmeric Yellow, Diferuloylmethane, Kurkum, C.I. Natural Yellow 3.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **natural** origin in food regulation contexts (turmeric/oleoresin from *Curcuma longa*), though chemically identical curcumin can also be manufactured.

Evidence: WHO/JECFA chemical entry for CURCUMIN lists chemical names, synonyms, CAS 458-37-7, INS 100i, and functional class "COLOUR" (JECFA database, 2003 evaluation record, URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1355). US CFR identity for turmeric/turmeric oleoresin defines botanical source *Curcuma longa* (21 CFR 73.600, 73.615; URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=73).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Food color (yellow-orange hue).
- **Mechanism of action:** Color expression from curcuminoid chromophore (conjugated system) that absorbs visible light in the yellow/orange range (mechanistic chemistry inference from identity; regulatory role confirmed as color additive).
- **Common food categories:** Foods generally (GMP-limited use), except where standards of identity prohibit added color unless specifically authorized.

Evidence: 21 CFR 73.600 and 73.615 specify turmeric/turmeric oleoresin "may be safely used for the coloring of foods generally" with GMP limits and standards-of-identity caveat (URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=73). JECFA functional class: "Food Additives / COLOUR" (URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1355).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved food color (E100) with established ADI.
- **ADI:** **3 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **Evaluation year:** 2010 re-evaluation (EFSA ANS Panel), with a later EFSA refined exposure assessment in 2014.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2010 EFSA re-evaluation established ADI at 3 mg/kg bw/day and reported that intake could exceed ADI for children (1-10 years), especially in high-consumption scenarios.
  - 2014 EFSA refined exposure assessment for E100 reported exposure ranges that included high-consumer estimates above 3 mg/kg bw/day in some scenarios.

Evidence:
- EFSA publication URLs visited: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1679 and https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3876 (both redirect to Wiley DOI pages).
- EFSA 2010 conclusions recovered from PubMed index of the EFSA opinion (PMID 20873220, URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20873220/).
- 2014 title/year confirmed via Crossref DOI metadata (URL: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3876).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS context:** Turmeric appears in GRAS lists for spices/natural flavorings and for essential oils/oleoresins.
  - 21 CFR 182.10 (spices and natural seasonings/flavorings; includes turmeric).
  - 21 CFR 182.20 (essential oils, oleoresins, natural extractives; includes turmeric).
- **Color additive regulation:**
  - 21 CFR 73.600 (Turmeric) and 21 CFR 73.615 (Turmeric oleoresin): exempt from batch certification; use permitted for coloring foods generally at GMP levels; standards-of-identity caveat applies.
  - 21 CFR 73.615 also sets solvent extraction identity and solvent-residue specification linkage.

Evidence: eCFR API title 21 part 182 and part 73 XML (URLs: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=182 and https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-19/title-21.xml?part=73).

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-3 mg/kg bw**.
- **Last evaluation year (current ADI basis):** **2003**.
- **Key conclusion:** ADI allocated based on a rat multigeneration NOEL and 100-fold safety factor.

Evidence: JECFA chemical entry and JECFA search endpoint (URLs: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1355 and https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/api/SearchChemical/ByPartialName/curcumin).

### IARC
- **Classification:** No specific IARC group classification located for curcumin/turmeric as standalone agents in the current IARC classification app dataset checked.

Evidence: IARC classification pages and the live list app bundle were checked; "curcumin/turmeric" were not found in the agent list payload (URLs: https://monographs.iarc.who.int/agents-classified-by-the-iarc/, https://monographs.iarc.who.int/list-of-classifications/, https://webapi.iarc.who.int/loc/loc.app.js).

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified during this search for E100/curcumin as a food color additive under normal regulated use.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal / mechanistic
- EFSA/JECFA ADIs are based on toxicology datasets including rat multigeneration data (NOEL-based derivation) and safety factors.
- Recent formulation-specific genotoxicity work (solid lipid curcumin particles) reported negative mutagenicity/clastogenicity findings in standard in vitro and in vivo tests at tested levels, with subchronic NOAELs reported in rats.

Evidence: JECFA evaluation comments (URL: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/1355); PubMed PMID 36609910 (URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36609910/).

### Epidemiological / human signals
- Evidence for routine dietary-level harm is limited.
- Recent human case reports describe **rare liver injury** associated with turmeric/curcumin-containing dietary supplements (often concentrated products, sometimes with bioavailability enhancers like black pepper/piperine), which is a higher-dose/extract context than ordinary food-color use.

Evidence: PubMed case reports PMID 41255460 and PMID 41371411 (URLs: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41255460/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41371411/).

### Mechanistic concerns
- Potential risk context is mainly **dose/formulation-dependent** (supplement extracts, enhanced bioavailability products), not standard food-color exposure.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA ADI benchmark:** 3 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Population pattern:** EFSA reported possible ADI exceedance in children/high consumers in some dietary scenarios.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Children with high intake of brightly colored foods; users of high-dose turmeric/curcumin supplements (separate from normal additive exposure).

Evidence: EFSA re-evaluation abstract on PubMed (PMID 20873220, URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20873220/) and EFSA refined exposure assessment publication page (URL: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3876).

## Risk Assessment
- **Recommended Chewber tier:** **moderate**.
- **Rationale:**
  - Approved by EFSA/FDA/JECFA with a defined ADI (supports controlled use).
  - ADI exceedance is plausible in some high-consumption subgroups (notably children) in EFSA assessments.
  - Additional human safety signal exists for rare hepatotoxicity from concentrated supplement products (higher-dose exposure context).
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high (regulatory consistency is strong; uncertainty remains around real-world high-end cumulative exposure and supplement-vs-food extrapolation).
