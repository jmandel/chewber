# E334 (Tartaric acid) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E334 (tartaric acid) [S1][S2]
- **Primary chemical identity used for food additive E334 in EU/US records:** **L(+)-tartaric acid** (2,3-dihydroxysuccinic acid), formula C4H6O6 [S2][S6]
- **CAS number(s):** 87-69-4 (L(+)-tartaric acid; the form explicitly described for food-grade use in 21 CFR 184.1099 and JECFA entry for INS 334) [S2][S6]
- **Related stereoisomers (not equivalent for risk conclusions):** D(-), DL, and meso tartrates are discussed separately in EFSA and are not covered by the EFSA group ADI set for L(+)-forms [S1]
- **Chemical class:** alpha-hydroxy dicarboxylic acid (organic acid / tartrate family) [S2][S6]
- **Common synonyms:** L(+)-tartaric acid, L-2,3-dihydroxysuccinic acid, L-2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid [S6]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** Both natural/byproduct (wine manufacture) and chemical/microbiological production routes are described in regulatory material [S2][S1]

## Function in Food
- **Core technological role:** acidity regulator/acidulant; EFSA identifies E334-337/E354 as used as acidity regulators [S1]
- **Mechanistic role in formulations:** lowering pH and buffering acid systems; supporting sequestration/synergy functions consistent with tartrate chemistry and JECFA functional classes [S6]
- **Specific functions listed by FDA (21 CFR 184.1099):** firming agent, flavor enhancer, flavoring agent, humectant, pH control agent; use at levels not exceeding current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) [S2]
- **Common food categories (regulatory-function based):** acidic beverages/wine-related applications, confectionery/bakery, and other foods needing acidification/pH control or flavor-brightening (inference from FDA functional uses + EFSA exposure framework for broad food categories) [S1][S2]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Approved/re-evaluated as **L(+)-tartaric acid and tartrates (E334-337, E354)** with a **group ADI of 240 mg/kg bw/day (as tartaric acid)**; previous 30 mg/kg bw/day ADI withdrawn in 2020 opinion [S1][S3]
- **EFSA evaluation year:** 2020 (EFSA Journal 18(3):e06030; DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2020.6030) [S1][S3]
- **EFSA key conclusions:** no genotoxicity indication for tartaric acid and sodium/potassium salts; no carcinogenicity signal in chronic rat study at highest tested dose; refined exposure did not exceed ADI; ADI does **not** apply to D(-), DL, or meso tartrates [S1]
- **FDA/CFR (US):** Listed as **direct food substance affirmed as GRAS** under **21 CFR 184.1099 (Tartaric acid)**; only L-configuration food-grade tartaric acid is specified; use limited by cGMP [S2]
- **JECFA/WHO:** L(+)-tartaric acid (INS 334, CAS 87-69-4) has **ADI 0-30 mg/kg bw**, with latest listed evaluation year **2017** (84th meeting), maintaining group ADI and including metatartaric acid in that group context [S6]
- **IARC:** No tartaric acid entry found in the current IARC Monographs list-of-classifications dataset checked (last update shown in dataset: 2026-02-25), so no IARC group classification identified for tartaric acid [S7][S8]
- **Notable bans:** No major-jurisdiction ban found in the consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources [S1][S2][S6][S7]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology:** EFSA reports no carcinogenicity indication for monosodium L(+)-tartrate in chronic rat study up to 3,100 mg/kg bw/day (highest tested dose) [S1]
- **Genotoxicity:** EFSA reports no indication of genotoxic potential for tartaric acid and sodium/potassium salts [S1]
- **Developmental/reproductive:** available maternal/developmental studies did not show relevant effects; dedicated reproductive studies were not available in the reviewed dataset [S1]
- **Renal findings nuance:** EFSA states no toxic effects (including nephrotoxicity) for tested L(+)-form, but nephropathy findings were discussed for DL-related material at high doses in rats; this supports separating stereoisomer conclusions [S1]
- **Human/ADME relevance:** EFSA highlighted lower systemic availability in humans than rats and used this to justify a chemical-specific uncertainty approach [S1]
- **Epidemiology directly on E334:** limited recent human epidemiology specifically for E334 was found in PubMed searches; main direct modern evidence remains the EFSA/JECFA regulatory re-evaluations [S3][S9]
- **Related but indirect signal:** a 2024 cohort study reported associations for some emulsifiers including E472e (a tartaric-acid ester derivative), which is not equivalent to E334 but is relevant as adjacent evidence in tartaric-acid-derived additives [S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined scenario (E334-337/E354):** mean exposure up to 91 mg/kg bw/day and P95 up to 174 mg/kg bw/day (infants), below the group ADI of 240 mg/kg bw/day [S1]
- **Combined tartaric acid exposure (other additive sources + natural occurrence):** EFSA reports P95 up to 285 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers in a combined high-end scenario but judged this exceedance as likely overestimation and not indicating health concern [S1]
- **Population data point (France TDS):** measured tartaric acid concentrations up to 420 mg/kg in sampled foods; ADI exceedance was not identified for tartaric acid in that study [S11]
- **Vulnerable groups:** infants/toddlers and high consumers are the key groups to watch in upper-percentile combined-exposure scenarios [S1]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: naturally occurring in foods; broad international authorization (EFSA/FDA/JECFA); no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity signal for authorized L(+)-forms at reviewed doses [S1][S2][S6]
  - Evidence against this tier: numeric ADI is required (not “ADI not specified” in EFSA/JECFA for E334); stereoisomer-specific caveat (D/DL/meso not covered by EFSA L(+)-based ADI); high-end combined toddler scenario can exceed ADI estimate [S1][S6]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: clear regulatory approval/GRAS status with defined ADIs; refined-use exposure generally below ADI; no serious safety signal at authorized uses [S1][S2][S6]
  - Evidence against this tier: some data gaps remain (e.g., no dedicated reproductive dataset in EFSA summary), and EFSA recommended specification improvements [S1]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: stereoisomer caveat and high-end combined-exposure estimate above ADI in toddlers in modelled scenario; nephrotoxicity findings in DL-related studies indicate potential hazard outside L(+)-specific authorization context [S1]
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA explicitly concluded no safety concern for L(+)-tartrate additives at reported uses/levels and considered combined exceedance likely overestimated [S1]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: none from the consulted major regulators/classification systems [S1][S2][S6][S7]
  - Evidence against this tier: no major bans identified; no IARC carcinogen classification identified; consistent regulatory acceptance with ADI-based control [S1][S2][S6][S7]

### 2. Rationale
E334 fits a **regulated low-concern profile under normal authorized use**: established ADIs, GRAS affirmation, and no strong genotoxic/carcinogenic evidence for L(+)-forms. The main caveats are stereoisomer specificity (D/DL/meso not covered by EFSA’s L(+)-based ADI), specification quality controls, and upper-bound combined exposure scenarios in toddlers that EFSA considered likely overestimation rather than confirmed risk. [S1][S2][S6]

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
