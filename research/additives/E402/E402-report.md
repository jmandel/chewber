# E402 Research Report — Potassium Alginate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E402
- **Name:** Potassium alginate
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-36-1 (potassium alginate); related parent acid in EFSA context: alginic acid CAS 9005-32-7
- **Chemical class:** Alginate salt (anionic polysaccharide hydrocolloid)
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium salt of alginic acid, INS 402, E 402
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from alginic acid extracted from brown algae, then neutralized to potassium salt)

## Function in Food
- **Main technological functions:** thickener, stabilizer, gelling agent, emulsifier.
- **Mechanism of action:** as a high-molecular-weight alginate polymer, it binds water and increases viscosity; with suitable ions/conditions it contributes to gel network formation.
- **Common food categories:**
  - Confections/frostings
  - Gelatins/puddings
  - Processed fruits/fruit juices
  - Other foods at low residual use levels

(US category-specific maxima are codified in 21 CFR 184.1610(c), see Regulatory Status.)

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised as food additive in the EU (E400-E404 group context).
- **Key opinion:** EFSA ANS Panel re-evaluation (adopted **26 September 2017**, published **10 November 2017**, output ON-5049, DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.5049).
- **ADI:** **No numerical ADI required** (equivalent to ADI not specified).
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No safety concern at refined exposure estimates for reported uses.
  - Exposure in infants/young children should remain below therapeutic-dose ranges associated with GI side effects.
  - Available data were not sufficient for an adequate safety assessment in certain FSMP infant/young-child categories (13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2).

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** **Affirmed GRAS** under Part 184 framework.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 184.1610 (Potassium alginate)**; GRAS framework relationship clarified in **21 CFR 170.30(d), (i), (j)**.
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 184.1610(c)):**
  - Confections/frostings: **0.1%** (as served)
  - Gelatins/puddings: **0.7%**
  - Processed fruits and fruit juices: **0.25%**
  - All other food categories: **0.01%**
- **Functional use in CFR section:** stabilizer, thickener.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Status in WHO JECFA database entry (Potassium alginate):**
  - INS 402, CAS 9005-36-1
  - **ADI: NOT SPECIFIED**
  - Evaluation year shown: **1992**
- **Supporting JECFA monograph (FAS 30):** group ADI not specified for alginic acid and ammonium/calcium/potassium/sodium salts; laxative effects may occur at high intake.

### IARC
- No potassium alginate/alginic acid/alginate entry was found in IARC Monographs “List of Classifications” dataset (Volumes 1-140 listing, last-update stamp 2026-02-25 in the web app dataset).
- **Classification:** no IARC monograph classification identified for E402.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the reviewed regulatory sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology consensus (EFSA/JECFA):** no numerical ADI needed / ADI not specified; no genotoxicity concern via available data/read-across; no carcinogenicity concern in available long-term animal evidence.
- **GI effects at high intake:** both EFSA and JECFA note GI/laxative-type effects can occur at high intakes.
- **Infant-special-medical-food uncertainty:** EFSA identified insufficient data for specific infant/young-child FSMP categories.
- **Recent PubMed evidence (post-2020) is limited for direct E402 food-additive risk endpoints:**
  - 2021 review (algae-derived hydrocolloids) describes alginate as generally non-toxic relative to debated carrageenan concerns.
  - 2022 EFSA FEEDAP sodium alginate opinion (animal feed context) found intended uses safe for target species at specified levels, while noting user-handling irritation/sensitisation concerns for dust exposure.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal estimate (general-population context):** up to **208 mg/kg bw/day** in infants (12 weeks-11 months) in the reported-use scenario.
- **FSMP brand-loyal scenario:** up to **290.4 mg/kg bw/day** in toddlers (12-35 months) using MPL assumptions for special medical purpose categories.
- **Reference side-effect range noted by EFSA:** therapeutic daily doses around **417-834 mg/kg bw/day** (sodium alginate-equivalent context in infants/young children) linked to GI adverse effects.
- **ADI exceedance framing:** no numerical ADI exists, so conventional numeric exceedance is not applicable; risk management focus is on vulnerable subgroups and use contexts.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** infants/young children consuming FSMP categories with limited dedicated safety data.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - EFSA: no need for numerical ADI; no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses.
  - JECFA: ADI not specified for alginic acid salts group.
  - Natural-source origin from brown algae.
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - EFSA explicitly identified unresolved uncertainty for infant/young-child FSMP categories.
  - High-dose GI side effects are documented in therapeutic-use ranges.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - Approved in EU and US (affirmed GRAS with explicit CFR limits).
  - Longstanding evaluations without major carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in regulatory conclusions.
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - Infant FSMP uncertainty is stronger than a purely minor caveat.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - Approved, but with a meaningful subgroup caveat (insufficient safety evidence for specific infant/young-child medical-diet categories).
  - GI effects at higher intake are credible and repeatedly acknowledged.
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - No IARC signal, no major bans, and broad regulatory acceptance with ADI-not-specified status in core evaluations.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - No strong support from reviewed evidence.
- **Evidence against high:**
  - Not banned in major jurisdictions reviewed.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - EFSA/JECFA do not indicate generalized unacceptable risk at approved use conditions.

### 2. Rationale
The additive has strong baseline regulatory acceptance and no major carcinogenic/genotoxic signal in current core assessments. However, EFSA retained a non-trivial uncertainty specifically for infants/young children in special medical food categories and highlighted GI side effects at higher therapeutic exposures. This moves the profile above purely minimal-concern classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
