# E400 (Alginic acid) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E400
- **Name:** Alginic acid
- **CAS number(s):** 9005-32-7
- **Chemical class:** Natural hydrocolloid polysaccharide (alginate; mannuronic/guluronic acid polymer)
- **Common synonyms:** Algin, INS 400, alginic acid
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Natural (extracted from brown algae by alkaline extraction)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Water-binding hydrocolloid; increases viscosity and contributes to gel/structure formation (especially with divalent cations), supporting thickening/stabilizing/emulsifying functions.
- **Common functional categories:** Thickener, stabilizer, emulsifier/emulsifier salt, formulation aid.
- **Common food categories:**
  - EU/WHO descriptions include broad use in thickened/stabilized foods (e.g., jams/jellies, ice cream, instant desserts, beverages).
  - U.S. direct GRAS listing for alginic acid is specifically constrained to **soups and soup mixes** at cGMP (21 CFR 184.1011).

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Approved.
  - **Latest re-evaluation:** 2017 (EFSA ANS Panel, EFSA Journal 15(7):5049).
  - **ADI:** No numerical ADI considered necessary (effectively ADI not specified).
  - **Key conclusions:** No safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses; however, available data were insufficient for an adequate safety assessment in infants/young children consuming foods for special medical purposes (FC 13.1.5.1 and 13.1.5.2), and exposure in these groups should remain below therapeutic-dose ranges.
- **FDA / CFR (U.S.):** GRAS affirmed in 21 CFR Part 184.
  - **CFR citation:** 21 CFR 184.1011 (Alginic acid), within the GRAS framework at 21 CFR 184.1.
  - **Conditions of use in 184.1011(c):** Food use limited to soup and soup mixes; maximum level is **not to exceed current good manufacturing practice**; functional uses listed as emulsifier/emulsifier salt, formulation aid, stabilizer/thickener.
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - WHO JECFA database entry for alginic acid lists **ADI: "Not limited"** and **evaluation year: 1993**.
  - FAO JECFA additive detail also reports ADI was allocated as **"not specified"** (35th JECFA, 1989), consistent with very low toxicological concern at food-use levels.
- **IARC:** No alginic acid entry identified in IARC Monographs “List of Classifications” dataset (no Group 1/2A/2B/3 classification located for alginic acid).
- **Notable bans:** None identified in major jurisdictions reviewed here.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline (EFSA/JECFA):** Both EFSA and JECFA outcomes are non-numerical ADI outcomes (EFSA: no need for numerical ADI; JECFA: not limited/not specified), indicating low concern at food-additive use levels.
- **Human evidence (older controlled feeding):** A 1991 human dietary study (Food Additives and Contaminants) using relatively high short-term sodium alginate intake reported fecal-bulking effects with no major systemic toxicity signal.
- **Recent PubMed evidence (selected):**
  - 2025 review emphasizes safety-assessment complexity for seaweed polysaccharides due structural heterogeneity and GI interactions, supporting ongoing specification/exposure vigilance.
  - 2025 rat 90-day study reports viscosity- and sex-dependent metabolic/microbiota effects of sodium alginate, without presenting a strong overt toxicity signal at tested doses.
  - 2024 in vitro colon fermentation work shows calcium-crosslinked alginate structures alter fermentability and SCFA-related gut processing, suggesting formulation-dependent GI interaction rather than clear hazard by itself.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake evidence available from retrieved sources:**
  - EFSA summary conclusion states refined exposure assessment did **not** indicate safety concern for reported uses.
  - A publicly accessible full-text extraction of exact EFSA exposure percentiles was limited in this environment due EFSA/Wiley access controls; therefore, this report anchors quantitative exposure interpretation to EFSA’s published conclusion rather than reconstructed secondary values.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - No numerical ADI is set by EFSA/JECFA for general population use, so classical “ADI exceedance” framing is limited.
  - **Higher-risk subgroup:** Infants/young children consuming foods for special medical purposes (FC 13.1.5.1/13.1.5.2), where EFSA reported insufficient data for adequate safety assessment.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Natural seaweed-derived polymer; EFSA and JECFA non-numerical ADI outcomes; no IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - Evidence against: EFSA highlighted unresolved safety assessment for certain infant medical-diet categories; formulation/viscosity can materially affect GI processing.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved in EU and U.S.; GRAS framework in FDA CFR; EFSA found no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses; JECFA ADI not limited/not specified.
  - Evidence against: U.S. alginic acid listing has specific use limitation (soups/soup mixes, cGMP); not a blanket no-conditions authorization.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Documented caveat in vulnerable pediatric medical-diet groups; recent literature indicates biologically meaningful gut/metabolic interactions dependent on physicochemical form and dose.
  - Evidence against: No major regulator concluded general-population harm at current food-additive uses; no major jurisdictional ban identified.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None found (no major bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing for alginic acid).
  - Evidence against: Regulatory consensus indicates low concern at food-use levels.

2. **Rationale**
- The dominant evidence is regulatory: EFSA and JECFA both support low concern at authorized use levels. The key uncertainty is not broad-population toxicity, but subgroup-specific evidence gaps (infants/young children on special medical diets) and formulation-dependent GI effects.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**
