# E570 (Stearic acid / Fatty acids) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E570
- **Primary name:** Stearic acid (within the EFSA E570 "fatty acids" group)
- **CAS number(s):**
  - **57-11-4** (stearic acid; listed in 21 CFR 184.1090)
- **Chemical class:** Long-chain fatty acid (saturated C18 fatty acid; octadecanoic acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Octadecanoic acid; octadecylic acid; fatty acids (E570 group context)
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Naturally occurring in animal/vegetable fats; commercially produced from edible tallow or fully hydrogenated vegetable oils (21 CFR 184.1090).

## Function in Food
- **Regulatory function (explicit):**
  - FDA/eCFR: used as a **flavoring agent and adjuvant** under GMP (21 CFR 184.1090(c)(1)).
  - JECFA record (chemical ID 690): functional class listed as **flavouring agent**.
- **Mechanism of action (inferred from chemistry + regulatory use):** Long-chain fatty acids are lipophilic components that can modify mouthfeel/lubricity and interact with fat phases; stearic acid is used at GMP levels to support flavor/adjuvant functions.
- **Common food categories:** eCFR does not enumerate fixed categories for stearic acid; use is broadly allowed in foods at levels not exceeding current good manufacturing practice.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Opinion identified:** EFSA Journal opinion on E570, DOI **10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4785**.
- **Evaluation year:** **2017** (published May 2017; panel adoption in 2017).
- **Approval status:** Remains authorised in EU food additive framework (EFSA re-evaluation was a safety reassessment, not a withdrawal).
- **ADI:** The EFSA E570 opinion reports prior SCF context and concludes **no need for a numerical ADI** in its risk framework (effectively consistent with "not specified"-type conclusion for use as evaluated).
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - E570 fatty acids are absorbed like normal dietary fatty acids.
  - No genotoxicity concern identified from available data.
  - No safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
  - Additive contribution estimated around **1%** of total saturated-fat exposure from diet.

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** **Affirmed as GRAS** direct human food ingredient.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1090 (Stearic acid)**.
- **Conditions of use:**
  - Use with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice.
  - Functional use as flavoring agent and adjuvant.
  - Ingredient identity includes CAS 57-11-4 and edible-source manufacturing description.

### JECFA / WHO
- **WHO JECFA database entry found:** Chemical page **ID 690 (STEARIC ACID / OCTADECANOIC ACID)**.
- **ADI statement on record:** **"No safety concern at current levels of intake when used as a flavouring agent."**
- **Last evaluation year shown on page:** **1997** (Meeting 49), with specification-history updates listed separately.
- **Additional JECFA context (as cited within EFSA E570 opinion):** JECFA had established an ADI **"not limited"** for long-chain fatty acids including stearic acid.

### IARC
- **Result:** No standalone IARC Group classification found for stearic acid (CAS 57-11-4) in the current IARC list/classification dataset reviewed.
- **Note:** Related compounds such as **cis-9,10-epoxystearic acid** and **glycidyl stearate** appear as Group 3 entries; these are not stearic acid itself.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide bans identified in EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (EFSA 2017 E570):**
  - Low acute toxicity profile noted.
  - Limited subchronic data; no toxic effects reported up to high dietary doses in available studies.
  - No genotoxicity concern from reviewed evidence.
  - Chronic/reproductive/developmental data were limited.
- **Recent PubMed human evidence (cardiometabolic-focused):**
  - **2025 RCT (AJCN, PMID 40975239):** palmitic-rich vs stearic-rich interesterified fats at realistic intakes for 6 weeks showed no significant difference in primary lipid risk marker (total:HDL ratio); no broad adverse signal attributable to stearic-rich arm.
  - **2021 RCT (Clinical Nutrition, PMID 32900520):** stearic vs palmitic intake lowered LDL and HDL; some inflammatory markers increased in stearic phase, while key cholesterol-efflux endpoint did not differ.
  - **2024 systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 37632423):** higher dietary stearic acid intake was modestly associated with higher coronary-event risk in pooled observational/RCT evidence, but effect sizes were modest and confounding from overall dietary pattern remains relevant.
  - **2020 systematic review (PMID 32111040):** substituting palmitic with stearic generally lowers LDL-C; effects on non-lipid cardiometabolic endpoints remain less certain.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA estimate:** E570 use contributed approximately **1%** of total saturated-fat intake from all dietary sources (regular diet + additive use).
- **ADI exceedance context:** No numerical ADI established/needed in EFSA conclusion framework; JECFA entry for stearic acid uses "no safety concern" wording for flavouring use.
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - No additive-specific vulnerable subgroup with demonstrated ADI exceedance was identified in the reviewed regulatory sources.
  - Broader cardiometabolic risk management may still depend on total saturated fat intake patterns, not E570 alone.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: endogenous/dietary nature of fatty acids; EFSA no-safety-concern conclusion at reported uses; no numerical ADI required; FDA GRAS.
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA identified limited chronic/reproductive/developmental dataset depth; some recent human literature shows mixed cardiometabolic signals.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: consistent regulatory acceptance (EFSA/FDA/JECFA), no IARC carcinogenic classification for stearic acid, and low additive-attributable exposure fraction in EFSA assessment.
  - Evidence against this tier: some epidemiologic/meta-analytic signals associate higher stearic acid intake with modestly higher coronary-event risk; not all inflammatory biomarkers are neutral across studies.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: data gaps in long-term dedicated additive toxicology and mixed human biomarker outcomes could justify caution.
  - Evidence against this tier: no regulator has moved to restriction/withdrawal for E570 on these grounds; current conclusions remain no safety concern at reported uses.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: essentially none from reviewed authorities.
  - Evidence against this tier: no major bans, no IARC Group 1/2 classification for stearic acid, no EFSA inability-to-confirm-safety outcome.

### 2. Rationale
Weight-of-evidence supports low concern under authorised use conditions, but not a true "risk_free" designation because long-term endpoint data are limited and recent human literature is not uniformly neutral for all cardiometabolic markers.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
