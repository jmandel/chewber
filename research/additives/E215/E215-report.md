# E215 Research Report: Sodium ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E215
- **Primary name:** Sodium ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate (sodium ethylparaben)
- **CAS number(s):**
  - 35285-68-8 (sodium ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate; sodium ethylparaben)
  - 120-47-8 (ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate; parent ethylparaben)
- **Chemical class:** Paraben preservative (p-hydroxybenzoic acid ester/salt family)
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium ethylparaben; ethylparaben sodium salt; p-hydroxybenzoic acid ethyl ester sodium salt
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Used as a **synthetic** food preservative (paraben compounds can occur naturally at trace levels in some plants, but additive-grade E215 is manufactured)

## Function in Food
- **Technological function:** Preservative / antimicrobial
- **Mechanism of action:** Parabens inhibit growth of yeasts, molds, and some bacteria; antimicrobial effect is linked to disruption of microbial membrane/enzyme function and is generally stronger in acidic foods.
- **Common food categories (EFSA-described uses for parabens E214-219):** bakery products, snacks, confectionery, meat products.

## Key Safety Evidence

### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- **Reproductive toxicity signal at high doses:** Mouse study (PMID 34101200) reported impaired implantation at high oral doses of ethylparaben (not low-dose dietary-range conditions).
- **Metabolic/liver signal in high-fat-diet model:** Mouse work (PMID 38236135) reported worsened NAFLD endpoints with methyl/ethyl paraben exposure, with gut microbiome and arachidonic-acid-pathway findings.

### Human observational evidence
- **Associations with metabolic outcomes:** Korean national survey analysis (PMID 41380597) linked higher urinary ethylparaben with obesity/MASLD risk (observational, not causal proof).
- **Pregnancy endocrine associations:** Study in pregnant women (PMID 40517806) found high paraben detection rates and thyroid hormone associations.

### Broader reviews / exposure-risk context
- **Dietary exposure review:** Global scoping review (PMID 41172944) found EU measurements typically within limits but raised concern that cumulative exposure from multiple foods can exceed ADI in some settings; much higher concentrations were reported in some non-EU datasets.
- **Population risk estimate example:** Taiwan nationwide analysis (PMID 41164939) reported paraben hazard indices below 1 in that surveyed population.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake context:** Highly variable by geography and food pattern; processed foods and condiments can contribute substantially in some datasets.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - **Possible** for high cumulative consumers in higher-contamination settings (especially outside strict enforcement environments), per 2025 scoping review findings.
  - Not universal; some population-level probabilistic assessments report paraben hazard indices below concern thresholds.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Children/adolescents with high processed-food intake.
  - Pregnant women (because endocrine-sensitive developmental window).
  - Populations with higher preservative contamination burdens.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Longstanding regulatory use history for methyl/ethyl parabens.
  - JECFA numeric ADI exists and is not extremely low.
  - No direct IARC carcinogen classification found.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EFSA position used a **temporary** group ADI and explicit caveats on related parabens.
  - Recent animal and human observational studies continue to report endocrine/metabolic concern signals.
  - Some datasets suggest potential cumulative ADI exceedance.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA and EFSA frameworks provide a defined acceptable intake range for methyl/ethyl paraben group.
  - Some population risk analyses do not show exceedance-level risk for parabens.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Multiple newer publications report non-trivial endocrine/metabolic associations.
  - E215 is not directly listed in current US Title 21 eCFR by name.
  - Exposure heterogeneity is large, including high-concentration outlier findings.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/use-recognized framework exists but with caveats (temporary/group ADI, exclusion logic for related parabens).
  - Credible animal signals at high doses (reproductive/metabolic).
  - Human epidemiological literature shows associations that justify caution even without proving causality.
  - ADI exceedance is plausible in some high-exposure subgroups.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Not all populations show risk metrics above concern thresholds.
  - No major global carcinogenic classification/regulatory ban for E215 itself was confirmed.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Mechanistic and epidemiological concern signals exist.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No IARC Group 1/2 classification identified for this additive.
  - No clear major-jurisdiction food ban confirmed for E215 in this run.
  - Existing ADI frameworks still in use for methyl/ethyl parabens.

### 2. Rationale
Main drivers are: (1) presence of formal ADI-based regulatory frameworks, (2) ongoing but mostly non-definitive concern signals (high-dose animal and observational human), and (3) evidence that exposure may exceed guidance in some contexts but not consistently across all populations.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
