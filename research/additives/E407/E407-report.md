# E407 Research Report: Carrageenan

## Identity
- **E-number**: E407 (INS 407) [S7]
- **Name**: Carrageenan [S7]
- **CAS number(s)**: 9000-07-1 (native carrageenan) [S7, S9]
- **Chemical class**: Sulfated polysaccharide hydrocolloid (galactose/anhydrogalactose-rich red-seaweed extract) [S2, S3]
- **Common synonyms**: Carrageenin, Chondrus extract, Irish moss-derived carrageenan terms (e.g., Eucheuman/Iridophycan/Furcellaran) [S4, S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Natural** (aqueous extraction from red seaweeds) [S3]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Hydrophilic sulfated polysaccharide that builds viscosity/gel structure, enabling thickening, stabilization, and emulsifier support in aqueous food systems [S3, S7].
- **Common food categories**: Broadly used in foods as emulsifier/stabilizer/thickener; includes many processed foods and some specialized nutrition products depending on jurisdiction/rules [S2, S3, S7].
- **Regulatory functional classes**: Emulsifier, gelling agent, stabilizer, thickener [S7].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Authorised in the EU (E407), with 2018 EFSA re-evaluation confirming authorization context [S2].
- **ADI**: Existing group ADI of **75 mg/kg bw/day**, but EFSA (2018) concluded it should be treated as **temporary** pending new data [S2].
- **Evaluation year**: **2018** (EFSA Journal 16(4):5238) [S2].
- **Key conclusions**:
  - No concern for carcinogenicity/genotoxicity for food-grade carrageenan in the reviewed dataset [S2].
  - Important uncertainties remained, especially around low molecular-weight fractions and data quality/coverage [S2].
  - Refined brand-loyal exposure estimates could exceed the temporary ADI, sometimes by up to ~10-fold at high percentile levels [S2].

### FDA/CFR (US)
- **GRAS status**: Chondrus extract (carrageenin) is listed as **generally recognized as safe** under **21 CFR 182.7255** with GMP condition [S4].
- **Food additive section**: Carrageenan is also listed in **21 CFR 172.620** as a permitted direct food additive with composition and use conditions [S3].
- **Conditions of use (key points)**:
  - 21 CFR 172.620: use as emulsifier/stabilizer/thickener in amount necessary; composition and sulfate-range conditions; labeling requirement [S3].
  - 21 CFR 182.7255: GRAS chondrus extract when used in accordance with GMP [S4].

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI**: **NOT SPECIFIED** for carrageenan [S7].
- **Last evaluation year (current record)**: **2014** (Meeting 79 entry shown on WHO/JECFA record) [S7].
- **Additional note**: WHO/JECFA record states infant formula/special medical purpose formula use up to 1000 mg/L was “not of concern” in that evaluation context [S7].

### IARC
- **IARC classification for carrageenan relevant to E407**: **Carrageenan, native = Group 3** (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans) [S9].
- **Important distinction**: **Carrageenan, degraded (poligeenan) = Group 2B** in the same dataset, but this is distinct from food-grade native carrageenan [S9].

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC sources for native food-additive carrageenan (E407) [S2, S3, S4, S7, S9].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2018 review: no carcinogenicity/genotoxicity concern for food-grade carrageenan in reviewed evidence, but highlighted uncertainty around low molecular-weight fractions and representativeness of test materials [S2].
- Mouse dietary study (2024, food additive context): reported non-significant taxonomic microbiota shifts but increased fecal-supernatant cytotoxicity and immune/oxidative-stress changes, with authors recommending microbiome degradation-capacity consideration in risk assessment [S14].
- Rat study (2021, semi-refined carrageenan/E407a): oral exposure associated with increased leukocyte ROS and viability effects in vivo; highlights possible indirect inflammatory mechanisms [S16].

### Human data
- Randomized pilot crossover in quiescent ulcerative colitis (2023, n=7): no significant worsening in SCCAI or biochemical inflammation markers versus placebo over short-term exposure; authors still call for larger studies [S11].
- Randomized double-blind crossover trial (2024, n=20 men): no overall insulin-sensitivity effect, but subgroup signal in overweight participants (reduced insulin sensitivity, higher inflammatory markers, increased intestinal permeability) [S12].

### Evidence synthesis/review
- 2023 scoping review (carrageenan/E407/E407a and CMC): mapped substantial literature and emphasized evidence gaps, especially need for realistic-dose, adequately powered human RCTs and clearer characterization of native food-grade carrageenan forms [S15].
- 2025 human-cell study in Crohn’s disease organoid-derived monolayers: reported pro-inflammatory cytokine and gene-expression changes with carrageenan, with no TEER permeability change [S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2018 reported refined brand-loyal exposure up to **758.6 mg/kg bw/day** for toddlers in modeled scenarios [S2].
- EFSA reported 95th percentile maximum exposure up to **49.4 mg/kg bw/day** for infants consuming special medical purpose foods/special formulae scenarios considered [S2].
- EFSA concluded high-level exposure estimates could exceed the temporary ADI (75 mg/kg bw/day), in some cases by up to ~10-fold [S2].
- **Vulnerable populations to monitor**:
  - High-consuming children/toddlers in brand-loyal patterns [S2]
  - Infants with specialized dietary products (context-specific modeling) [S2]
  - People with inflammatory bowel disease or overweight/metabolic vulnerability based on emerging but not definitive clinical/mechanistic signals [S11, S12, S13, S15]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Authorized in major systems (EU/US) and JECFA ADI not specified [S2, S3, S4, S7].
  - Native carrageenan is IARC Group 3, not Group 1/2A/2B [S9].
  - EFSA did not identify carcinogenicity/genotoxicity concern for food-grade carrageenan [S2].
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA retained only a temporary ADI and flagged major data uncertainties [S2].
  - Exposure modeling showed potential temporary-ADI exceedance in some populations [S2].
  - Newer mechanistic/clinical literature shows credible pro-inflammatory/metabolic signals in specific contexts [S12, S13, S14, S15, S16].

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Approved/GRAS with defined use conditions and no hard ban signal [S3, S4, S7].
  - Human evidence is mixed and not uniformly adverse; one short pilot RCT in UC was neutral on main endpoints [S11].
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA temporary ADI plus modeled exceedance up to ~10-fold is a stronger caveat than a typical low-concern profile [S2].
  - Multiple recent studies suggest plausible gut-inflammatory or metabolic concern pathways requiring follow-up [S12, S13, S14, S15].

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Approved but with explicit caveats: temporary ADI, data gaps, and modeled exceedance concerns (especially high consumers/children) [S2].
  - Credible mechanistic and early clinical signals exist for gut inflammation/metabolic effects in susceptible subgroups/settings [S12, S13, S14, S15, S16].
  - Distinction between native carrageenan and degraded poligeenan remains central; low-MW fraction uncertainty is unresolved in EFSA conclusions [S2, S9].
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - Regulatory agencies still permit use; no broad ban and no IARC high-carcinogenic classification for native carrageenan [S2, S3, S4, S7, S9].

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Degraded carrageenan/poligeenan has IARC Group 2B classification and is associated with adverse intestinal findings in historical toxicology context [S2, S9].
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - E407 refers to native food-grade carrageenan, not degraded poligeenan [S2, S9].
  - No major regulatory bans identified; EFSA/FDA/JECFA positions are not aligned with a high-risk designation at authorized use conditions [S2, S3, S4, S7].

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence does not support `risk_free` because EFSA’s ADI remains temporary and modeled exposures can exceed it substantially in some scenarios, while post-2018 literature continues to generate biologically plausible concern signals. It also does not support `high` because native E407 remains authorized, is IARC Group 3, and lacks corroborating major-jurisdiction regulatory withdrawal. The strongest defensible fit is `moderate`.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
