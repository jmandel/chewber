# E516 (Calcium sulfate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E516
- **Chemical name:** Calcium sulfate
- **CAS numbers:** 7778-18-9 (anhydrous calcium sulfate), 10101-41-4 (calcium sulfate dihydrate)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfate salt (calcium salt of sulfuric acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Gypsum, anhydrite, plaster of Paris, INS 516
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Natural mineral origin with industrial processing (e.g., dehydration of gypsum) for food-grade forms

Evidence: [S4][S5][S6]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Calcium sulfate dissociates into calcium and sulfate ions; calcium supports protein/polysaccharide network formation and texture setting (firming/coagulation), while sulfate contributes ionic strength.
- **Regulatory functional roles (U.S. CFR/JECFA):** Anticaking agent, firming agent, flour treatment agent, sequestrant, yeast food, and additional GMP-based functions listed in CFR.
- **Common food categories (U.S. direct-food CFR limits under GMP):**
  - Baked goods: up to 1.3%
  - Confections/frostings: up to 3.0%
  - Frozen dairy desserts/mixes: up to 0.5%
  - Gelatins/puddings: up to 0.4%
  - Grain products/pastas: up to 0.5%
  - Processed vegetables: up to 0.35%
  - Other food categories: 0.07% or less

Evidence: [S4][S5][S6]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation found:** 2019, EFSA FAF Panel opinion covering sulfuric acid and sulfate salts including E516.
- **Approval status:** Authorised in the EU (as reported in the EFSA opinion summary).
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI set; EFSA concluded there is "no need for a numerical ADI" for this group.
- **Key EFSA conclusions for E513/E514/E515/E516/E517:**
  - Low acute oral toxicity
  - No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity in the available database
  - Refined non-brand-loyal exposure (as sulfate): mean 0.4-35 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile 3-68 mg/kg bw/day
  - Human laxative effect reference point: sodium sulfate at 300 mg/kg bw/day (equivalent to 207 mg sulfate/kg bw/day), well above estimated food-additive exposure
  - Overall: no safety concern at reported uses and use levels
- **Recommendation noted by EFSA:** Consider lowering arsenic/lead/mercury limits in specifications to reduce impurity contribution.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA reports low acute oral toxicity for sulfate salts overall and no genotoxicity signal from available data across the evaluated sulfate group.
- EFSA reviewed chronic/carcinogenicity data where acidic-gavage forestomach findings were judged not relevant to food-additive use because of food buffering context.

Evidence: [S2]

### Human data
- EFSA cites human data indicating laxative effects at high sulfate doses (sodium sulfate 300 mg/kg bw/day; 207 mg sulfate/kg bw/day equivalent), substantially above modeled food-additive exposure.
- PubMed human oral sulfate studies (non-E516 therapeutic bowel-prep context) continue to report generally favorable short-term safety/tolerability with mostly mild adverse events, supporting that GI effects are dose-context dependent.

Evidence: [S2][S13][S14][S15]

### Epidemiological data
- No recent epidemiologic studies directly linking food-additive E516 intake to chronic disease outcomes were identified in the PubMed searches run for this task.

Evidence: [S10]

### Mechanistic concerns
- **Plausible concern at high doses:** Osmotic/laxative gastrointestinal effects from sulfate load.
- **Specification concern (regulatory):** EFSA recommendation to tighten toxic-element impurity limits (As/Pb/Hg).

Evidence: [S2]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal exposure estimate (sulfate equivalent):**
  - Mean: 0.4 mg/kg bw/day (infants) to 35 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers)
  - 95th percentile: 3 mg/kg bw/day (adolescents) to 68 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers)
- **Comparison with GI-effect threshold used by EFSA:** 207 mg sulfate/kg bw/day equivalent (from 300 mg sodium sulfate/kg bw/day) -> modeled exposures are far below this level.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not applicable to a numerical ADI (EFSA and JECFA did not set a numerical ADI for this group).
- **Potentially higher-exposure populations:** Toddlers had the highest modeled sulfate exposure in EFSA scenarios.
- **Other vulnerable populations (inference):** Individuals with high supplemental/therapeutic sulfate exposure or impaired electrolyte/renal handling may have lower tolerance to high sulfate loads than general dietary exposures.

Evidence: [S2][S6][S13][S14]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported uses and no need for numerical ADI.
  - JECFA ADI status is NOT LIMITED.
  - Sulfate is a normal constituent of biological systems and food.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - High-dose sulfate can produce laxative effects.
  - EFSA flagged toxic-element specification limits as an area for tightening.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - FDA GRAS affirmation with explicit conditions of use in CFR.
  - Mild GI adverse effects are plausible at higher sulfate intakes.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No evidence of safety concern at current reported additive exposure; no numerical ADI required by EFSA/JECFA.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Could be argued only if one prioritizes high-dose GI effects or impurity-management caveats.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - EFSA exposure estimates remain far below the laxative reference dose.
  - No regulatory downgrading, withdrawal, or ADI tightening event found for E516.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None from the reviewed regulatory databases.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No major bans identified.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found for calcium sulfate.
  - EFSA/FDA/JECFA positions remain permissive under regulated use.

### 2. Rationale
The core regulatory dataset is consistent: E516 remains authorized/GRAS, EFSA found no safety concern at reported uses, and JECFA keeps a non-numerical permissive ADI status (NOT LIMITED). Safety caveats are mainly dose-related GI effects and impurity-specification management, not demonstrated harm at current modeled dietary exposure.

### 3. Recommended tier
**risk_free**
