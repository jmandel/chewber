# E210 (Benzoic acid) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E210
- **Name:** Benzoic acid
- **CAS number(s):** 65-85-0
- **Chemical class:** Aromatic monocarboxylic acid (benzenecarboxylic acid); weak-acid preservative
- **Common synonyms:** Benzenecarboxylic acid, phenylformic acid, dracylic acid
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Benzoic acid occurs naturally in foods (e.g., cranberries, prunes, plums, cinnamon, cloves, berries), but food-additive grade is also manufactured industrially (e.g., oxidation of toluene).

**Evidence used:**
- EFSA archived publication page metadata/abstract (2016) and eCFR §184.1021 identity/manufacture text.
- PubChem synonym/property records for CAS/synonyms.

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative.
- **Mechanism of action:** Benzoic acid is a weak organic acid preservative; antimicrobial effect is pH-dependent and used to inhibit growth of spoilage microorganisms in foods/beverages.
- **Regulatory functional roles (US):** Antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent/adjuvant under 21 CFR 184.1021.
- **Common food categories:** Acidic/flavoured drinks and other foods where microbial inhibition is needed; EFSA exposure modeling identified flavoured drinks (and, with carry-over assumptions, some fruit/vegetable categories) as key contributors to high intake scenarios.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation located:** 2016 Scientific Opinion (EFSA Journal, DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4433).
- **Approval status:** Authorised in EU food categories, but with a **temporary group ADI** and exposure concerns in children in EFSA scenarios.
- **ADI:** **5 mg/kg bw/day** (group ADI, expressed as benzoic acid).
- **Evaluation year:** **2016**.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern for benzoic acid/benzoates based on reviewed data.
  - Available data did not indicate carcinogenic potential.
  - EFSA intake modeling found ADI exceedance for toddlers/children in brand-loyal and some carry-over scenarios.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS (affirmed direct human food substance).
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 184.1021 (Benzoic acid)**, within **21 CFR Part 184** (direct food substances affirmed as GRAS).
- **Conditions of use (from §184.1021):**
  - Used as antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent/adjuvant.
  - Used at levels not to exceed GMP; current usage states a maximum of **0.1% in food**.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Relevant JECFA entry used:** WHO/JECFA database page for sodium benzoate (INS 211), which explicitly states the group ADI applies to benzoic acid and benzoate salts.
- **ADI:** **0-20 mg/kg bw** (group ADI, expressed as benzoic acid equivalents).
- **Last evaluation year found:** **2021** (JECFA meeting 92).
- **Historical note:** The prior group ADI 0-5 mg/kg bw was withdrawn.

### IARC
- **IARC result:** No exact listing for **Benzoic acid** found in the IARC list-of-classifications dataset used by the official table page.
- **Interpretation:** No direct IARC group classification identified for benzoic acid in the checked dataset snapshot.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and regulatory toxicology baseline
- EFSA (2016) identified a rat four-generation reproductive study NOAEL of 500 mg/kg bw/day and derived a temporary group ADI of 5 mg/kg bw/day.
- EFSA concluded no genotoxicity concern and no indication of carcinogenic potential in the reviewed evidence base.

### Epidemiological / human-linked evidence
- A 2025 childhood asthma case-control/metabolomics study reported benzoic-acid-associated signals in relation to asthma endpoints (mixture context; not isolated-causality proof for E210 alone).
- A 2026 beverage surveillance risk-assessment study reported high sodium benzoate concentrations in some products and flagged higher chronic exposure signals in children vs adults.

### Mechanistic and experimental concerns
- 2025 systematic review (mammalian cells) reported frequent cytogenotoxic signals for preservatives including sodium benzoate.
- 2025-2026 experimental studies reported possible reproductive toxicity and nephrotoxicity signals for sodium benzoate/benzoic acid, including combination-exposure effects.
- A 2025 review discussed a theoretical/experimental concern for benzene formation in settings involving benzoate and vitamin C, with potential relevance to susceptible populations (e.g., impaired renal clearance contexts).

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure finding (2016):** The group ADI (5 mg/kg bw/day) was exceeded in certain toddler/child scenarios, especially with regular flavoured-drink consumption and additional carry-over assumptions.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - **At risk:** Yes, in EFSA-modeled high-use child scenarios.
  - **Most vulnerable populations:** Toddlers and children with frequent intake of preserved flavoured beverages.
- **Cross-framework caveat:** JECFA later set a higher group ADI (0-20 mg/kg bw, 2021), so exceedance conclusions depend on which regulatory benchmark is applied.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Longstanding authorization across major regulators.
  - No direct IARC listing for benzoic acid found.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA uses a numeric temporary ADI (not “ADI not specified”).
  - EFSA identified child intake scenarios exceeding ADI.
  - Recent mechanistic/animal studies report non-trivial toxicity signals.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - FDA GRAS status with explicit GMP conditions.
  - EFSA concluded no genotoxicity concern/carcinogenic indication in its reviewed dataset.
  - JECFA retained a permitted group-ADI framework and updated it in 2021.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - EFSA child-scenario ADI exceedance and temporary ADI framing indicate unresolved exposure-management concerns.
  - Recent literature includes multiple adverse mechanistic/animal findings.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved but with caveats: explicit ADI-based control, child-exceedance signals (EFSA), and a mixed but concerning newer study signal set.
  - Credible experimental evidence for reproductive/renal/genotoxic endpoints exists, though often at high dose or mixed-exposure conditions.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major regulatory body has withdrawn authorization for food use.
  - Core authorities continue to permit use under ADI/GMP constraints.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Multiple recent mechanistic/animal studies report potentially serious endpoints.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major bans identified.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 listing located for benzoic acid itself.
  - Regulatory status remains authorized with defined limits.

### 2. Rationale
The evidence profile is not compatible with “risk_free” because EFSA used a temporary numeric ADI and identified exceedance scenarios in children. It is also not “high” because there is no major ban pattern or direct IARC carcinogen classification for benzoic acid, and major regulators still authorize use with controls. Overall, benzoic acid (E210) is best characterized as approved with meaningful caveats, especially for high-exposure child scenarios and for mixture/high-dose uncertainty in newer studies.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
