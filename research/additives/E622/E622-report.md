# E622 (Monopotassium glutamate) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E622 (INS 622).
- **Primary name:** Monopotassium L-glutamate (also listed as monopotassium glutamate / potassium glutamate).
- **CAS number:** 19473-49-5 (JECFA chemical record).
- **Chemical class:** Potassium salt of L-glutamic acid (glutamate salt; amino-acid-derived flavour enhancer).
- **Synonyms found in regulatory databases:** Monopotassium L-glutamate; Potassium glutamate; L-glutamic acid, monopotassium salt (1:1), monohydrate.
- **Natural vs synthetic origin:** Free glutamate is naturally present in foods/proteins, but food-additive E622 is generally produced industrially via fermentation-derived glutamic acid followed by neutralization to a potassium salt (EFSA C. glutamicum production-strain opinion; EFSA/JECFA records).

## Function in Food
- **Main function:** Flavour enhancer (umami/savoury enhancement).
- **Mechanism:** Provides free glutamate ions that intensify umami taste perception.
- **Common food categories:** Broad use in savoury processed foods (seasonings, soups, sauces, snacks, processed foods), consistent with EFSA’s description of glutamates as flavour enhancers used across a wide range of foods and with CFR flavour-enhancer designation.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved in the EU as part of the glutamic acid/glutamates group (E620-625), with a group re-evaluation completed in 2017.
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation located:** *Re-evaluation of glutamic acid (E620), sodium glutamate (E621), potassium glutamate (E622), calcium glutamate (E623), ammonium glutamate (E624) and magnesium glutamate (E625) as food additives* (EFSA Journal, 2017; article 4910).
- **ADI:** Group ADI **30 mg/kg bw/day** (expressed as glutamic acid).
- **Key conclusions extracted:** EFSA reported no genotoxicity concern, used a developmental neurotoxicity NOAEL of 3,200 mg/kg bw/day to derive the ADI, and stated that estimated dietary exposure could exceed the ADI in high consumers across age groups (and for toddlers/children at mean exposure in some scenarios).
- **Access note:** The EFSA record page was reachable, but direct journal full-text endpoints (Wiley/DOI links) returned HTTP 403 in this session; conclusions were cross-checked against EFSA’s own press release summarizing the same opinion.

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Status in 21 CFR:** **GRAS**.
- **Citation:** **21 CFR §182.1516** (“Potassium glutamate and monopotassium glutamate”).
- **Conditions of use:** Produced by neutralization of glutamic acid with potassium hydroxide; permitted as flavour enhancers (21 CFR 170.3(o)(11)) under good manufacturing practice.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Chemical record:** Monopotassium L-glutamate (JECFA Chemical No. 4073).
- **Functional class:** Flavour enhancer.
- **ADI:** **Not specified** (group ADI context for L-glutamic acid and its ammonium, calcium, monosodium and potassium salts).
- **Last evaluation year:** **1987** (as shown in the JECFA record).

### IARC
- IARC monograph classification resources were checked (interactive list page and underlying dataset bundle used by the page).
- No entry was found for monopotassium glutamate/potassium glutamate/glutamate salts as an IARC-classified carcinogenic agent.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban specific to E622 was identified in the sources reviewed for this report.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory baseline (EFSA/JECFA):**
  - EFSA (2017) set a numerical group ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day), found no genotoxicity concern, and highlighted exposure exceedance in some populations.
  - JECFA lists ADI “not specified” for this glutamate-salt group.
- **Recent mechanistic/toxicology evidence (PubMed):**
  - 2020 in vitro study on monopotassium glutamate (human lymphocytes) reported chromosomal/micronucleus/comet assay changes mainly at higher concentrations, concluding genotoxic/cytotoxic effects under those test conditions.
  - 2022 rat study reported high-dose MSG exposure altered gut microbiome profile (reduced *Akkermansia muciniphila*), increased serum TMAO, and suggested reversible kidney-injury biomarker changes.
  - 2025 rat overconsumption study (0.8-3 g/kg/day MSG) reported dose-related hepatic/renal/cardiac toxicity markers with oxidative-stress/inflammation patterns.
  - 2025 GLP-style 28-day and 90-day dietary rat studies reported no toxicologically significant findings, with NOAELs around 3,170-3,620 mg/kg bw/day (higher in one short-term arm); publication disclosed industry affiliations/funding context.
  - 2024 in vivo/in vitro study reported no toxicity/genotoxicity at ADI-equivalent concentration in a Drosophila model, with mixed additional cellular findings.
- **Human/clinical interpretation context:**
  - A review article indexed in PubMed reported that usual dietary MSG intake does not raise brain glutamate concentrations in a way that disrupts brain function; experimental effects generally required non-physiologic high exposures.
- **Evidence-gap note:**
  - Direct long-term human epidemiology specifically on **E622** (not other glutamate salts) remains limited.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory exposure benchmark:** EFSA group ADI = 30 mg/kg bw/day (as glutamic acid equivalents for E620-625).
- **Exceedance risk:** EFSA concluded ADI exceedance is possible in high consumers across age groups; toddlers and children could exceed in mean scenarios under some assumptions.
- **Published intake context (EU review):** A Karger review (linked from EFSA-related literature) reported estimated mean glutamate exposure from additive use in the EU around 11-74 mg/kg bw/day and highly exposed groups above 110 mg/kg bw/day, with major uncertainty due to sparse use-level data.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Toddlers/children, and generally high consumers of savoury processed foods containing glutamate-based flavour enhancers.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - Glutamate is an endogenous amino-acid-related metabolite.
  - JECFA ADI status is “not specified” for the glutamate-salt group.
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - EFSA set a finite ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day) and identified plausible exceedance in population subgroups.
  - Recent high-dose animal and in vitro studies report potential adverse signals under certain conditions.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - EFSA/FDA/JECFA maintain regulatory acceptance (EU approved with ADI; U.S. GRAS CFR listing; JECFA ADI not specified).
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - EFSA’s own exposure modeling indicates exceedance risk in children/high consumers.
  - Some newer experimental studies raise mechanistic concern at high or non-typical doses.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - Clear EFSA caveat on potential ADI exceedance in real dietary scenarios.
  - Credible but mixed experimental literature showing possible renal/cardiometabolic/genotoxic signals, mostly at higher exposures.
  - Heterogeneous study outcomes increase uncertainty and justify caution for high consumers.
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - Regulatory bodies still permit use; core toxicology benchmarks include high NOAEL values.
  - Several studies at ADI-relevant conditions do not show strong harm signals.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Some high-dose animal/in vitro papers report adverse effects.
- **Evidence against high:**
  - No major-jurisdiction bans identified.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification identified.
  - EFSA/JECFA/FDA regulatory positions do not support high-risk categorization at permitted uses.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest drivers are: (1) established regulatory approval status, (2) EFSA’s explicit ADI and exceedance warnings for some populations, and (3) mixed but non-negligible high-dose experimental toxicity literature. This pattern is more cautious than `limited` but not consistent with `high`.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
