# E1410 (Monostarch phosphate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1410
- **Name:** Monostarch phosphate
- **INS number:** 1410
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (phosphate-modified starch; starch ester)
- **Common synonyms:** Monostarch phosphate, starch phosphate, phosphated starch, INS 1410, food starch-modified (U.S. labeling term)
- **CAS number(s):** Not listed on the WHO/JECFA monostarch phosphate record (CAS field is blank) and not provided in the EFSA re-evaluation source consulted; monostarch phosphate is a polymeric/variable-composition modified starch rather than a single low-molecular-weight substance.
- **Origin:** **Semi-synthetic** (native edible starch chemically modified by phosphorylation).

Key source evidence:
- WHO JECFA chemical page for MONOSTARCH PHOSPHATE (INS 1410) with overview/functional class and no CAS displayed: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/2816
- EFSA re-evaluation (2017): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009865/

## Function in Food
- **Primary technological role:** Thickener/stabilizer (WHO JECFA functional class lists “STABILIZER” and “THICKENER”).
- **Mechanism of action:** Phosphate modification alters native starch physical behavior (e.g., process stability/texture behavior). EFSA notes modified starches are not absorbed intact; they are largely hydrolyzed by intestinal enzymes and then fermented by gut microbiota to short-chain fatty acids.
- **Common food categories:** Broad use across food categories as modified starches (EU exposure assessment handled as a group of modified starches; U.S. permits “food starch-modified” for food use under specified treatment/residual limits).

Key source evidence:

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (re-evaluated in 2017 as part of modified starches E1404–E1452).
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** (equivalent to ADI not specified / non-numeric outcome).
- **Evaluation year:** 2017.
- **Key conclusion:** “No safety concern” for reported uses and use levels in the general population.
- **Important caveat:** EFSA stated available data did **not** allow an adequate safety assessment for certain uses in infants/young children consuming foods for special medical purposes and special formulae at currently authorized maximum levels.

### FDA / eCFR (U.S.)
- **Status:** Regulated as an **approved direct food additive** as “food starch-modified” under **21 CFR §172.892** (not identified as a specific GRAS-affirmed monostarch phosphate entry in parts 184/186 in the retrieved Title 21 text).
- **CFR citation:** 21 CFR §172.892 (Food starch-modified).
- **Conditions of use (examples from §172.892):**
  - Must not exceed amount reasonably required for intended technical effect.
  - Label of additive container must bear name “food starch-modified”.
  - For phosphate-related modification routes, residual phosphate limits are specified (e.g., 0.4% as phosphorus for certain treatments; 0.04% for sodium trimetaphosphate route).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA (2017) used read-across across modified starches and reported no treatment-related effects relevant for human risk assessment at very high doses in rats (up to 31,000 mg/kg bw/day for modified starches as a group).
- EFSA notes kidney mineralization findings in some studies occurred with modified and unmodified starches and were not considered toxicologically relevant to humans.
- EFSA states short-term/subchronic studies were not available for monostarch phosphate specifically, but considered the group data sufficient.

### Epidemiological data
- No robust human epidemiology specifically linking E1410 to adverse outcomes was identified in the searched regulatory set.
- PubMed searching for monostarch phosphate primarily retrieved the EFSA re-evaluation and physicochemical starch-modification papers rather than new clinical safety-signal studies specific to E1410.

### Mechanistic/genotoxic/carcinogenic considerations
- EFSA reported modified starches are extensively hydrolyzed/fermented rather than absorbed intact.
- EFSA in silico review did not identify genotoxic alerts for modified starch moieties.
- EFSA cited no carcinogenicity evidence in available group-relevant data (e.g., E1442 mouse study in the group dataset).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reported combined exposure for modified starches E1404–E1451 at the 95th percentile (refined brand-loyal scenario) up to **3,053 mg/kg bw/day** for the general population.
- For E1452 supplement consumers, EFSA reported up to **22.1 mg/kg bw/day** (95th percentile).
- For E1410 specifically, publicly presented EFSA exposure is grouped with other modified starches rather than isolated as a single-additive intake estimate.
- Because EFSA/JECFA ADI outcome is non-numeric (“not specified” / no numerical ADI), classic ADI exceedance calculations are not directly applicable.
- Vulnerable subgroup caveat: EFSA identified insufficient data for adequate assessment in infants/young children consuming certain special medical formula categories at current authorized maximum levels.

Source:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009865/

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA and JECFA reached non-numeric ADI outcomes (no numerical ADI / ADI not specified) and no general-population safety concern at reported uses.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - E1410 is chemically modified (semi-synthetic), not a simple endogenous nutrient.
  - EFSA reported data limitations for specific infant medical-formula uses.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Regulatory acceptance in EU/U.S./JECFA framework.
  - No strong genotoxic/carcinogenic signal in core regulatory evaluation.
  - FDA conditions of use and residual limits constrain manufacturing/use.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - “Limited” can understate subgroup uncertainty where EFSA explicitly said data were insufficient for adequate assessment in certain infant uses.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved additive, but with explicit EFSA caveat for vulnerable infant/young-child medical-formula contexts.
  - Monostarch phosphate-specific toxicology is limited; conclusions depend on modified-starch group read-across.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No major evidence of harm at authorized general-population uses.
  - No IARC listing/classification for monostarch phosphate.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - None found in consulted EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC/PubMed sources.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions.
  - Regulatory evaluations do not indicate high-level hazard characterization.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight of evidence supports low concern for the general population at authorized use levels, but not a fully “risk-free” profile because EFSA identified a meaningful data gap in specific infant medical-formula uses and because E1410-specific toxicology is partly inferred via modified-starch read-across.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
