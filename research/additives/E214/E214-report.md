# E214 Research Report — Ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate (Ethylparaben)

## Identity
- **E-number:** E214 (INS 214) [S7]
- **Primary name:** Ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate [S7]
- **CAS number:** 120-47-8 [S7]
- **Chemical class:** Paraben (p-hydroxybenzoic acid ester preservative) [S1][S7]
- **Common synonyms:** Ethylparaben; Ethyl p-oxybenzoate; Ethyl ester of p-hydroxybenzoic acid [S7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** In food regulation, E214 is treated as a manufactured additive/preservative (synthetic commercial use context). (Inference from regulatory/additive classification data) [S1][S7]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Antimicrobial preservative used to inhibit microbial growth and extend shelf life [S1][S7].
- **Mechanism of action:** Parabens act as antimicrobial preservatives; mechanistic studies describe membrane-related antimicrobial effects for paraben compounds (including p-hydroxybenzoates). (Mechanism wording is an inference from paraben literature, not an EFSA mechanistic statement) [S16].
- **Common food categories:**
  - EU regulatory categories include certain processed meat products (categories 8.3.1 and 8.3.2), with reduced maximum levels for E214/E215 [S3].
  - Non-EU dietary monitoring literature reports paraben occurrence in multiple categories (e.g., vegetables, condiments, bakery/cookies), indicating broad potential exposure pathways [S11].

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** **Restricted/approved**.
- EFSA (2004) established a **full group ADI of 0-10 mg/kg bw/day** for the sum of methyl and ethyl p-hydroxybenzoic acid esters and their sodium salts [S1][S2].
- EU legal text cites a later EFSA opinion adopted in **2005** that maintained the full ADI for methyl/ethyl parabens while addressing propyl separately [S3].
- Subsequent EU action reduced maximum levels for E214/E215 to 300 mg/kg in specified meat categories and removed E216/E217 authorisations [S3].
- **Key conclusion for E214:** No EFSA withdrawal found for E214; current EU framing is continued use under tighter conditions and group ADI context [S1][S3].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** No direct GRAS listing found for ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate in the queried eCFR records; current explicit listing found as an **approved indirect food-contact substance** in adhesives [S4][S5].
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 175.105 (Adhesives)** [S5].
- **Conditions of use:** Adhesive use under prescribed food-contact conditions (functional barrier/trace transfer GMP framework); table entry lists **“Ethyl-p-hydroxybenzoate — For use as preservative only.”** [S5].

### JECFA / WHO
- JECFA database entry for ETHYL p-HYDROXYBENZOATE (INS 214) reports **ADI 0-10 mg/kg bw** [S7][S8].
- **Evaluation year:** **1973** (meeting 17), with comment that propyl paraben was excluded later from the group ADI context (2006 note) [S7].

### IARC
- No IARC Monographs classification entry found for ethyl p-hydroxybenzoate / ethylparaben in the IARC classification dataset (last-update field in the app data: 2026-02-25 CET) [S9][S10].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban found specifically for E214 from the reviewed sources.
- Related parabens E216/E217 were removed from EU authorisation, but this is not an E214 ban [S3].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology basis (supportive):** EFSA reported no hormonal or reproductive effects for methyl/ethyl parabens at tested levels in juvenile rats and set group ADI 0-10 mg/kg bw/day [S1].
- **Animal/mechanistic concern signals (high-dose, non-regulatory studies):**
  - Mouse implantation study (2021): oral ethylparaben 400-1600 mg/kg affected implantation/decidualization endpoints [S13].
  - Mouse embryo study (2025): ethylparaben exposure associated with developmental arrest/oxidative stress markers in early embryos [S14].
- **Human exposure/epidemiology context:**
  - Taiwan biomonitoring/risk study (2025): paraben hazard indices remained <1 in that population model [S12].
  - Global food scoping review (2025): EU values mostly within limits, but high concentrations in some non-EU datasets and possibility of cumulative ADI exceedance [S11].
- **Interpretation:** strongest direct regulatory safety support comes from EFSA/JECFA ADI frameworks; newer concern signals are mostly mechanistic or high-dose and require cautious extrapolation to real dietary intake [S1][S7][S11][S13][S14].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** Variable by geography and food mix; modern literature shows widespread low-level presence and occasional high concentrations in some regions/food categories [S11].
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - **EU context:** likely mitigated by restricted uses and reduced MLs in relevant categories [S3].
  - **Global/cumulative context:** possible exceedance in high-exposure scenarios when multiple contaminated foods are combined [S11].
- **Vulnerable populations:** children/high consumers of processed foods, and potentially pregnancy-related windows due reproductive/developmental signals in animal models (uncertain human translation) [S11][S13][S14].

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: established ADI by EFSA and JECFA; no IARC listing found [S1][S7][S9][S10].
  - Evidence against: finite ADI (not “ADI not specified”), restricted-use management actions in EU, and non-clinical endocrine/reproductive concern signals at high doses [S3][S13][S14].

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved/restricted in EU and approved for specific US indirect use; established ADI 0-10 mg/kg bw/day; population risk assessments can show HI <1 [S1][S3][S5][S7][S12].
  - Evidence against: newer literature reports mechanistic reproductive/developmental effects in animals and potential cumulative exposure concerns [S11][S13][S14].

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: credible animal findings and cumulative exposure concerns in some datasets could justify caution beyond baseline approval [S11][S13][S14].
  - Evidence against: no IARC carcinogenic classification, no specific E214 ban identified, and core regulatory ADI framework remains in place [S1][S3][S7][S9][S10].

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: limited; some mechanistic toxicity findings exist [S13][S14].
  - Evidence against: lacks key “high-tier” triggers (no major-jurisdiction ban for E214, no IARC 2A/2B/1 classification, no EFSA withdrawal of E214) [S1][S3][S9][S10].

### 2. Rationale
E214 has established EFSA/JECFA ADI values and remains regulated rather than banned. Safety concerns exist mainly from newer mechanistic/high-dose and cumulative-exposure studies, but these have not translated into a clear regulatory reclassification of E214 itself.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
