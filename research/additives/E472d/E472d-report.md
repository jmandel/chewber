# E472d (Tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E472d (INS 472d) [S1, S5].
- **Chemical class**: Tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids (glycerol ester emulsifier mixture) [S1, S5].
- **Common synonyms**: Tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides; INS 472d [S1, S5].
- **CAS number(s)**: No single CAS number was provided in the primary EFSA/JECFA/eCFR records consulted for E472d; JECFA API returns `CAS_NO: null` for this entry [S5, S6].
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (manufactured ester mixture from edible glycerides/fatty acids and tartaric acid) [S1, S9].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**: Amphiphilic glyceride esters act as emulsifiers by lowering oil-water interfacial tension and stabilizing dispersed systems.
- **Common food categories**: Emulsifier uses in processed foods, including bakery-related contexts for E472 esters in EFSA exposure/use datasets [S1, S2].
- **Regulatory function class**: JECFA lists the entry in food additive/emulsifier functional class context [S5].

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU)**:
  - **Latest scientific opinion/re-evaluation used**: 2025 EFSA scientific opinion on amended specifications for E472a,b,d,e,f, following the 2020 E472a-f re-evaluation [S2, S1].
  - **Approval status**: Approved/authorized in EU framework; EFSA did not identify a safety concern at reported uses in 2020 and concluded no safety concern for the proposed revised specs in 2025 [S1, S2].
  - **ADI**:
    - 2020 re-evaluation: group ADI of **240 mg/kg bw/day expressed as tartaric acid** for E472d/e/f (linked to the l(+)-tartaric acid-tartrates group ADI) [S1].
    - 2025 opinion: EFSA notes this corresponds to an ADI of **480 mg/kg bw/day for E472d on an additive basis** [S2].
  - **Key conclusions**: No adverse effects relevant for humans identified in the available E472a-f toxicological database; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels; 2025 specification changes not considered a safety concern under proposed limits [S1, S2].
- **FDA / eCFR (US)**:
  - **Exact E472d name search result in current Title 21**: No explicit listing for "tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides" was found in current Title 21 XML [S3, S4].
  - **Closest related CFR listing**: **21 CFR 184.1101** covers **diacetyl tartaric acid esters of mono- and diglycerides (DATEM; E472e)** as GRAS, with GMP-based conditions of use as an emulsifier/flavoring in specified food categories [S4].
  - **Status for E472d in this review**: No direct E472d GRAS affirmation/citation identified in current eCFR Title 21 records; treated as **not explicitly listed** [S3, S4].
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **JECFA entry**: Dedicated entry exists for **TARTARIC ACID ESTERS OF MONO- AND DIGLYCERIDES** (Chemical ID 3715) [S5].
  - **ADI**: **WITHDRAWN** [S5].
  - **Last evaluation year shown**: **2001** [S5].
  - **Specification note**: Listed as **WITHDRAWN (1998)** on the JECFA entry page [S5].
- **IARC**:
  - No IARC monograph classification entry was found for E472d/additive name in IARC search/list pages checked [S7, S8].
- **Notable bans**:
  - No major-jurisdiction outright ban found in the consulted EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (directly relevant)**:
  - EFSA (2020) reported no adverse effects relevant for humans for E472a-f and set E472d/e/f ADIs on a tartaric-acid basis, with no safety concern at reported uses/use levels [S1].
  - EFSA (2025) found no safety concern for proposed revised impurity specifications and clarified additive-basis ADI expression for E472d [S2].
- **Animal/mechanistic evidence (indirect to E472d)**:
  - A 2024 mouse study on common dietary emulsifiers reported that mono-/diglyceride emulsifier exposure was associated with gut microbiota changes and metabolic disturbances; this was not E472d-specific testing [S10].
- **Epidemiological evidence (indirect to E472d)**:
  - 2023 BMJ NutriNet-Sante analyses reported associations between higher intake of E471/E472 groups and cardiovascular outcomes (observational design) [S11].
  - 2024 PLoS Med NutriNet-Sante analyses reported associations between higher E471 intake and selected cancer outcomes (observational design; not E472d-specific) [S12].
- **Evidence gap**:
  - Recent human studies specifically isolating E472d exposure and outcomes were not identified in the queried PubMed set [S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure values for E472d**:
  - EFSA reported highest mean and high-percentile exposure estimates for E472d up to **30 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **89 mg/kg bw/day (95th percentile) in toddlers** in its re-evaluation context [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - Compared with EFSA's tartaric-acid-based group ADI (240 mg/kg bw/day) and later additive-basis expression for E472d (480 mg/kg bw/day), reported E472d exposure estimates are below ADI thresholds in EFSA conclusions [S1, S2].
- **Vulnerable populations**:
  - Toddlers/children are the highest per-kg consumers in EFSA exposure scenarios [S1].

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA found no safety concern at reported uses and exposure estimates below ADI framework; no IARC classification found [S1, S2, S7].
  - Evidence against: E472d has a numerical ADI (not "ADI not specified"), JECFA entry is withdrawn, and direct modern E472d-specific human evidence is limited [S5, S13].
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA approval context with explicit ADI framework and no current EFSA safety concern; no major ban/IARC signal; exposure estimates below EFSA ADI context [S1, S2, S7].
  - Evidence against: U.S. Title 21 did not show an explicit E472d listing in current records; indirect emulsifier-class epidemiology/mechanistic concerns exist [S3, S4, S10, S11, S12].
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Credible recent class-level studies (mouse + large cohorts) raise concern signals for some emulsifiers/E471-E472 groups, and JECFA withdrew the dedicated E472d ADI/spec entry [S5, S10, S11, S12].
  - Evidence against: Signals are not E472d-specific causal proof, and EFSA's latest evaluations remain reassuring within approved-use conditions [S1, S2].
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: limited; no direct evidence of major-jurisdiction ban or IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification in checked sources.
  - Evidence against: EFSA conclusions remain permissive for approved uses/specifications; no corroborating high-hazard regulatory actions identified [S1, S2, S7].

2. **Rationale**
- The strongest additive-specific evidence is EFSA's re-evaluation and specifications work, which supports safety at reported uses under current specifications/ADI framing.
- Uncertainty remains because JECFA's dedicated entry is withdrawn and modern human evidence specific to E472d is sparse; recent epidemiology/mechanistic studies are mostly class-level and indirect.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **limited**.
