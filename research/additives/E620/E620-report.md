# E620 (Glutamic Acid) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E620.
- **Name:** L-glutamic acid (food additive E620).
- **CAS number(s):** 56-86-0 (WHO JECFA chemical record for L-glutamic acid, ID 668). [9]
- **Chemical class:** Amino acid (acidic amino acid); glutamate-family flavour enhancer/salt-substitute additive. [9]
- **Common synonyms:** Glutamic acid; L-(+)-glutamic acid; L-alpha-aminoglutaric acid; L-2-amino-pentanedioic acid. [9]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Naturally occurring and endogenous amino acid; EFSA notes glutamates are naturally present in many foods and produced by the human body. [1]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Flavour enhancer (umami/savoury intensity). [1]
- **Mechanism of action (practical food function):** Adds free glutamate to intensify savoury/umami taste perception; also used in some formulations as a salt substitute. [1][6][9]
- **Common food categories:** Flavoured foods across population groups in the EU; JECFA functional classes list flavour enhancer and salt substitute uses. [1][9]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** EFSA states glutamates (E620-E625) are currently authorised in the EU. [1]
- **Latest re-evaluation found:** 2017 EFSA ANS Panel re-evaluation of E620-E625. [1][2]
- **ADI:** Group ADI **30 mg/kg bw/day** (expressed as glutamic acid), derived from a NOAEL of 3,200 mg monosodium glutamate/kg bw/day with UF 100. [2]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern identified. [2]
  - No adverse effects were seen across many toxicology domains except increased kidney/spleen weights without adverse histopathology (not considered adverse). [2]
  - EFSA noted exposure could exceed the ADI in some population groups (high consumers across groups; children/adolescents also at mean exposure) and recommended review of maximum permitted levels. [1][2]

### FDA / CFR (U.S.)
- **GRAS status in CFR:** In Title 21, **21 CFR 182.1045 (Glutamic acid)** states this substance is generally recognized as safe.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 182.1045**.
- **Conditions of use:** GRAS **when used as a salt substitute in accordance with good manufacturing practice**. [6]
- **Access note:** Human-facing eCFR pages returned CAPTCHA from this VM; section text was extracted from the official eCFR API XML snapshot. [4][6]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** WHO JECFA database entry for **L-GLUTAMIC ACID (INS 620)** lists ADI as **NOT SPECIFIED (1987)** and indicates the group ADI for glutamic acid and selected glutamate salts was maintained at the 63rd meeting. [9]
- **Last evaluation year in the record:** **2004** (with ADI notation referencing 1987 group ADI origin). [9]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No glutamic acid/glutamate entry found in the current IARC list-of-classifications dataset checked (dataset last-update field: 2026-02-25 CET). [11][12]

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA-CFR/JECFA/IARC sources. [1][6][9][11]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology baseline (EFSA 2017):** No genotoxicity concern; broad nonclinical dataset did not show adverse effects at typical test endpoints apart from non-adverse organ-weight findings; ADI established due neurodevelopmental NOAEL framework and uncertainty factors. [2]
- **Recent dietary toxicity study (PubMed 2025, rats):** 28-day and 90-day dietary studies reported no toxicologically significant findings; reported NOAEL at highest tested dose in 90-day design (3170 mg/kg bw/day males; 3620 mg/kg bw/day females). Funding/conflict statement indicates industry support (IGTC). [13]
- **Recent mechanistic concern signal (PubMed 2026, high-dose in utero rat exposure):** Dose-dependent adverse effects on testicular morphology in offspring at 1-4 g/kg maternal dosing. High-dose animal model limits direct extrapolation to normal dietary intake. [15]
- **Recent gut/microbiome study (PubMed 2025, in vitro):** Short-term exposure to MSG in human fecal batch cultures showed no significant gut-community shifts and no cytotoxicity in HT29 monolayers under tested conditions; authors call for chronic-exposure follow-up. [14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **ADI exceedance signal (regulatory):** EFSA concluded estimated dietary exposure to glutamates (E620-E625) could exceed the ADI in some groups, especially high consumers and younger groups. [1][2]
- **Recent dietary intake dataset (France infant feeding, 2023 paper):** Mean glutamic-acid intake from infant formulas/complementary foods was reported at 171.5 mg/kg bw/day and high intake at 249.0 mg/kg bw/day, with estimated exceedance versus EFSA ADI; this reflects total glutamic-acid dietary exposure in that cohort context, not only E620 additive-only exposure. [16]
- **Vulnerable populations:** Infants/young children and other high consumers of glutamate-containing processed foods are the key ADI-exceedance concern groups in available evidence. [1][2][16]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Endogenous/naturally occurring amino acid; long regulatory history; JECFA ADI "not specified" for glutamic acid/salts group. [1][9]
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA assigned a finite group ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day) and identified exceedance in some populations; this is inconsistent with a fully risk-free profile under real-world exposure. [1][2]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Approved in EU and listed GRAS in U.S. CFR with GMP conditions; recent short-term models did not identify consistent toxicity at tested conditions relevant to dietary use. [1][6][13][14]
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA exceedance findings and high-dose developmental/reproductive animal signals suggest more than minor theoretical concern. [1][2][15]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Additive remains approved, but EFSA set a conservative ADI and documented exceedance risk in specific populations; credible animal evidence shows adverse effects at elevated doses; exposure concerns are strongest for younger/high-intake groups. [1][2][15][16]
- **Evidence against this tier:** JECFA historical position is ADI not specified, and some newer toxicology/microbiome studies are reassuring under tested conditions. [9][13][14]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Some high-dose animal studies report adverse reproductive/developmental outcomes. [15]
- **Evidence against this tier:** No major-jurisdiction bans identified, no IARC carcinogenic group listing, and regulators continue to authorize use with risk-management framing rather than prohibition. [1][6][11][12]

### 2. Rationale
The evidence supports a **regulated-with-caveats** profile: E620 is authorized and GRAS-listed in CFR for specified use, but EFSA’s 2017 re-evaluation established a finite ADI and identified exceedance risks in some populations. Recent literature continues to show mixed signals, with high-dose animal effects versus reassuring short-term dietary/in vitro studies.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
