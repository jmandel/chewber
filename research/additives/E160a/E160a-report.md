# E160a (Beta-carotene) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E160a (carotenes; includes beta-carotene forms such as E160a(i) synthetic and E160a(ii) from *Blakeslea trispora*).
- **Primary substance reviewed:** beta-carotene.
- **CAS number(s):** 7235-40-7 (beta-carotene).
- **Chemical class:** Carotenoid (tetraterpene hydrocarbon pigment; molecular formula C40H56).
- **Common synonyms:** beta-carotene, β-carotene, beta,beta-carotene, all-trans-beta-carotene, Food Orange 5, E160a.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Both natural-source and synthetic forms are used/authorized in food systems (FDA color-additive text explicitly allows both).

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Food color (yellow-orange pigment).
- **Mechanism of action:** The conjugated polyene chain absorbs visible light (blue/violet range), producing yellow-orange coloration in foods; beta-carotene can also act as provitamin A.
- **Common food categories:**
  - Foods generally (color use under GMP).
  - Dairy product analogs, fats and oils, processed fruits and fruit juices, and infant formula use as vitamin A source under specified CFR conditions.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest additive re-evaluation identified:** EFSA Journal 2012;10(3):2593 (re-evaluation of beta-carotene E160a(i) and beta-carotene from *Blakeslea trispora* E160a(ii)).
- **Follow-up EFSA statement:** EFSA Journal 2012;10(7):2953 (clarification on use in foods for smokers/heavy smokers).
- **Approval status:** Effectively remains authorized under current use conditions; EFSA concluded no safety concern for food-additive uses considered.
- **ADI:** EFSA stated no ADI was established in the 2012 additive re-evaluation (historically a group ADI had existed).
- **Evaluation year:** 2012.
- **Key conclusion:** No concern at current uses; regular diet contributes ~5-10 mg/day beta-carotene and additive use was estimated to keep totals below ~15 mg/day; concern context is high supplemental intake in heavy smokers.

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Color additive status:** Listed at **21 CFR 73.95** (beta-carotene), exempt from certification, may be safely used for coloring foods generally under GMP and with standards-of-identity caveat.
- **GRAS status:** **21 CFR 184.1245** affirms beta-carotene as GRAS as a direct human food ingredient, with GMP-based conditions of use and listed food categories.
- **Conditions of use:** GMP limitations; specified categories include dairy analogs, fats/oils, processed fruits/juices; can be used in infant formula as vitamin A source per applicable law.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Record found:** JECFA chemical page for **beta-CAROTENE (synthetic)**.
- **ADI:** **WITHDRAWN** (2019); previously group ADI 0-5 mg/kg bw.
- **Last evaluation year:** 2019.
- **Key conclusion:** JECFA withdrew prior group ADIs and stated inability to establish a group ADI applicable to the general population because that includes heavy smokers.

### IARC
- Checked IARC Monographs classification resources (list page and current data bundle).
- **Finding:** No explicit beta-carotene listing/classification located in the current IARC “List of Classifications” dataset reviewed.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified for food-additive beta-carotene use at current authorized levels.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/mechanistic
- Recent mechanistic discussion (including smoke interaction) proposes that high-dose supplemental beta-carotene in smoke-exposed conditions may generate oxidative metabolites linked to pro-proliferative signaling.

### Human epidemiology / trials
- RCT meta-analysis (2023) found no significant effect on overall cancer incidence, but elevated **lung cancer risk** for supplemental beta-carotene (RR ~1.19) and higher risk signals in smokers.
- Historic ATBC RCT follow-up literature continues to anchor concern: intervention-phase high-dose beta-carotene in male smokers increased lung-cancer and mortality risk.
- Observational/screening-trial analyses show dietary antioxidant intake patterns can be protective, while supplement effects are not consistently protective.

### Regulatory synthesis of evidence
- EFSA (2012 additive re-evaluation + statement) and JECFA (2019) both converge on a key caveat: uncertainty/risk context is driven by high supplemental exposure in smokers, not typical food-additive exposure levels.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake context (EFSA):** ~5-10 mg/day beta-carotene from regular diet; EFSA estimated food-additive use would generally keep total intakes below ~15 mg/day.
- **ADI exceedance framing:** Current central issue is less about exceeding a numeric ADI (JECFA withdrew prior group ADI; EFSA additive re-evaluation did not set an ADI) and more about high supplemental intakes in high-risk groups.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Heavy smokers and people taking high-dose beta-carotene supplements (20-30 mg/day in historical risk-signaling trials).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting placement: naturally present in many foods; no signal at usual dietary intakes; EFSA found no concern for current additive-use exposure scenario.
- Evidence against placement: high-dose supplement data in smokers shows credible harm signal; JECFA withdrew prior group ADI.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting placement: approved/authorized by EFSA context and FDA (CFR 73.95 + GRAS 184.1245); no broad safety alarm at GMP-level food uses.
- Evidence against placement: presence of a subgroup harm signal (smokers with high supplemental intake) weakens a simple low-concern classification.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting placement: regulatory caveats are material (no current harmonized numeric ADI for general population context, JECFA withdrawal, smoker-specific risk evidence, consistent lung-cancer signal in supplemental meta-analyses).
- Evidence against placement: risk appears exposure-context dependent (supplements/smoking), not clearly attributable to normal additive dietary exposure.

**high**
- Evidence supporting placement: credible carcinogenic-risk concerns exist in a defined high-risk subgroup under high supplemental exposure.
- Evidence against placement: no major-jurisdiction ban for food-additive use, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing identified for beta-carotene itself in checked resources, and EFSA did not report concern for current food-additive uses.

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factors are: (a) continued authorization in EU/US food additive frameworks, (b) strong caveat around high supplemental doses in smokers, and (c) ADI uncertainty/withdrawal at international level for general-population applicability. This supports a middle classification rather than either risk-free or high.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
