# E311 (Octyl gallate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E311
- **Name:** Octyl gallate
- **CAS number(s):** 1034-01-1
- **Chemical class:** Alkyl gallate (octyl ester of gallic acid); phenolic antioxidant
- **Common synonyms:** Octyl ester of gallic acid; n-octyl ester of 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoic acid; octyl 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily **synthetic** food additive (esterified gallic acid derivative)

Evidence: WHO/JECFA chemical page for Octyl gallate (INS 311, CAS 1034-01-1) [Source 7].

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Antioxidant.
- **Mechanism of action:** Gallate esters are chain-breaking phenolic antioxidants that inhibit lipid oxidation/rancidity; in regulatory classifications, octyl gallate is listed as a food additive antioxidant [Source 7].
- **Common food categories (from current regulatory texts reviewed):**
  - EU: retained only for food supplements category 17.1 after the 2018 amendment [Source 3].
  - US: listed within margarine standard preservatives (in combination limits) [Source 5].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA scientific opinion/re-evaluation identified:** 2015, EFSA Journal opinion on re-evaluation of octyl gallate (E311) [Source 1].
- **ADI:** EFSA Panel was **unable to establish an ADI** for general uses due data limitations; however, it concluded no safety concern for the then-current use in solid food supplements at the maximum permitted level [Source 3].
- **Approval status (current practical status from EU legal follow-up):** **Restricted**. Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/1462 states that because requested additional data were not submitted, uses were removed except food category 17.1 (food supplements in solid form) [Source 3].
- **Key EFSA-derived conclusion (as quoted in EU legal text):** no safety concern at current levels for the specific solid food supplement use, but insufficient basis to establish ADI for broader uses [Source 3].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Search target:** Title 21 (Food and Drugs) on eCFR.
- **CFR citation located:** **21 CFR 166.110(b)(5)** (margarine), which allows "propyl, octyl, and dodecyl gallates ... all individually or in combination, 0.02 percent" as preservative limit in finished food [Source 5].
- **GRAS status:** No dedicated octyl gallate GRAS affirmation section was found in Title 21 during full-text/API search; the identified allowance is a use-specific authorization in a standard of identity. Practical status for this dataset: **approved (use-specific), not explicitly GRAS-affirmed in the located octyl entry** [Sources 4, 5].
- **Conditions of use from located CFR text:** Combination preservative cap of 0.02% by weight in margarine/oleomargarine context [Source 5].

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA entry located:** Octyl gallate (INS 311, chemID 4118) [Source 7].
- **ADI:** **NO ADI ALLOCATED** [Source 7].
- **Last evaluation year shown:** **1996** (meeting 46); page notes the temporary ADI from 1993 was not extended [Source 7].

### IARC
- Checked IARC Monographs classification pages and the current list-of-classifications dataset feed (last update shown in script: 2026-02-25).

### Notable bans/restrictions
- No major global "ban" record was confirmed from the checked EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.
- A notable **restriction** was confirmed in the EU: uses removed except category 17.1 via Regulation (EU) 2018/1462 [Source 3].

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory safety signal:** EFSA could not set an ADI for broad uses but judged no safety concern for current solid supplement use at MPL in 2015 context [Source 3].
- **Genotoxicity-focused study (in vitro):** 2023 Mutagenesis paper in human lymphocytes reported no major genotoxic effect across several endpoints, but did report increased sister chromatid exchange at higher concentrations and reduced mitotic index at higher exposure levels [Source 12].
- **Microbiome/biological activity signal:** 2024 Antibiotics (Basel) mouse work reported altered fecal microbiome composition and reduced diversity after octyl gallate exposure, alongside low cytotoxicity/hemolytic activity in the study system [Source 13].
- **Other mechanistic/therapeutic models:** 2024-2025 studies show strong biological activity (beta-glucuronidase inhibition, anti-infective effects), but these are not direct chronic dietary safety studies for normal food-additive intake [Sources 14, 15].
- **Epidemiological data:** No robust population epidemiology directly linking routine E311 dietary exposure to adverse outcomes was identified in the collected sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake (quantitative):** No robust current population-wide intake estimate (mg/kg bw/day) was identified in the accessible sources used here.
- **Regulatory exposure interpretation available:**
  - EFSA/EU legal recital indicates "no safety concern" for the specific retained solid supplement use at maximum permitted level [Source 3].
  - EU legal follow-up removed other uses, which likely lowers broad dietary exposure opportunities relative to historical wider uses [Source 3].
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - EFSA: no ADI established for broad uses in that re-evaluation context [Source 3].
  - JECFA: no ADI allocated [Source 7].
  - Therefore, classical ADI-exceedance framing is limited; risk management is mainly use-restriction based.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:** High consumers of food supplements in the retained category, and potentially children if consuming supplement products outside intended patterns.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: No IARC carcinogen classification found; EFSA did not identify safety concern for the specific retained supplement use at MPL.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: EFSA could not establish a general ADI and EU later removed most uses; recent mechanistic studies show biologic effects (e.g., microbiome shifts), so "risk_free" is too strong.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Still authorized in restricted EU use and use-specific US CFR context; no major acute red-flag consensus from core regulators; JECFA shows no ADI allocated rather than an explicit hazard classification.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: EFSA inability to establish ADI for broad uses and subsequent EU restriction indicate meaningful uncertainty beyond a simple low-concern profile.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Approved but with clear caveats, including EFSA ADI non-establishment for broad uses, explicit EU use-removal to a narrow category, and some credible mechanistic/in vitro signals at higher concentrations.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: No IARC listing, no confirmed major-jurisdiction ban, and EFSA found no concern for the retained use at MPL.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Limited.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier: No IARC 2A/2B/1 classification identified; not broadly banned across major jurisdictions; retained authorized uses remain.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest drivers are regulatory caveats: EFSA could not establish a broad ADI and EU risk management subsequently narrowed authorized uses to a specific supplement category. This is stronger than a simple "limited" profile, but current evidence does not reach "high" (no IARC carcinogen classification and no major global ban pattern).

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
