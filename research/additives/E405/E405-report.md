# E405 Research Report: Propane-1,2-diol alginate (Propylene glycol alginate)

## Identity
- **E-number**: E405 (INS 405)
- **Name**: Propane-1,2-diol alginate (propylene glycol alginate)
- **CAS number**: 9005-37-2
- **Chemical class**: Partially esterified alginic acid derivative (propylene glycol ester of alginic acid)
- **Common synonyms**: 1,2-Propane-diol ester of alginic acid; hydroxypropyl alginate; propane-1,2-diol alginate; propylene glycol ester of alginic acid
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (derived from alginic acid; alginic acid itself is obtained from brown algae, then chemically esterified)

Data points above come from WHO/JECFA chemical record and 21 CFR text ([WHO/JECFA chemical record, accessed 2026](https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/585); [21 CFR 172.858](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-172.858); [21 CFR 184.1011](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml)).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action (functional)**: Amphiphilic polysaccharide thickener/emulsifier; esterification of alginate supports interfacial stabilization and viscosity control in complex foods.
- **Regulatory functional roles** (US CFR): emulsifier, flavoring adjuvant, formulation aid, stabilizer, surfactant, thickener.
- **Common food categories in regulation**:
  - Frozen dairy desserts
  - Fruit and water ices
  - Confections and frostings
  - Baked goods
  - Cheeses
  - Fats and oils
  - Gelatins and puddings
  - Gravies and sweet sauces
  - Jams and jellies
  - Condiments and relishes
  - Seasonings and flavors
  - Other foods (general category)

These categories and maximum levels are listed in **21 CFR 172.858(b)** ([eCFR section page](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-172.858); [full Title 21 XML used for extraction](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml)).

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Approved food additive in the EU, re-evaluated by EFSA ANS Panel.
- **ADI**: **55 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **Evaluation year**: **2018**.
- **Key conclusions**:
  - EFSA reaffirmed the ADI of 55 mg/kg bw/day (from earlier SCF basis).
  - EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
  - Exposure could exceed ADI in a conservative non-brand-loyal scenario for toddlers and in consumers of food supplements at maximum reported levels.

Source: EFSA Journal 2018;16(7):5235 ([EFSA page visited](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/5235)).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status**: Not GRAS-affirmed in Parts 182/184/186; instead regulated as an **approved food additive**.
- **Citation**: **21 CFR 172.858** (Propylene glycol alginate).
- **Conditions of use**: Specific maximum percentages by food category, e.g. 0.4%-1.7% depending on category, plus labeling requirements.

Primary source: [21 CFR 172.858](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-172.858). Parsing of current Title 21 XML used to confirm presence in Part 172 and no dedicated GRAS monograph entry in Parts 182/184/186 ([API URL visited](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-02-25/title-21.xml)).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA cites a key 2-year rat study with NOAEL 2,500 mg/kg bw/day (basis for reaffirmed ADI framework inherited from SCF rationale).
- EFSA overall conclusion: no safety concern at reported usage levels.

Source: [EFSA Journal 2018;16(7):5235](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/5235).

### Epidemiological / human evidence
- No strong modern human epidemiology directly linking dietary E405 exposure to major adverse outcomes was identified in this focused search.
- PubMed retrieval is dominated by formulation/materials papers rather than direct oral safety cohorts.

Sources: [PubMed query URL used](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%22propylene+glycol+alginate%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+%22E405%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D&sort=date), [example recent PGA-linked paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34443935/).

### Mechanistic concerns
- A 2023 PubMed-indexed paper reported allergic-side-effect mechanisms for **propylene glycol alginate sodium sulfate (PSS)**, including IgE/mast-cell signaling findings; this is a sulfated derivative used as a pharmaceutical context, not standard food-grade E405.
- This supports a **signal to monitor hypersensitivity biology in related compounds**, but direct extrapolation to normal dietary E405 is limited.

Source: [PMID 37119889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37119889/).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA reported exposure estimates generally below ADI in most scenarios.
- Potential ADI exceedance was noted in:
  - **Toddlers** in a conservative non-brand-loyal maximum-level scenario.
  - **Consumers of food supplements** containing E405 at highest reported use levels.
- Overall EFSA conclusion remained no safety concern for reported uses/use levels.

Source: [EFSA Journal 2018;16(7):5235](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/5235).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - EFSA and JECFA both establish numeric ADIs and do not report a current broad safety failure under normal use.
- **Evidence against**:
  - ADI is finite (not "ADI not specified").
  - EFSA identified scenarios/populations (toddlers, supplement users at maxima) where ADI exceedance may occur.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Approved in EU and US frameworks.
  - FDA regulation provides explicit concentration limits by category.
  - EFSA and JECFA provide established ADIs.
  - No strong direct carcinogenic classification signal found (IARC no listing found for this agent).
- **Evidence against**:
  - Exposure exceedance can occur in worst-case scenarios.
  - Some mechanistic/allergy findings exist for related sulfated derivatives (not direct food-grade E405 evidence).

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - EFSA highlighted potential ADI exceedance in specific groups/scenarios (especially toddlers in conservative modeling).
- **Evidence against**:
  - EFSA still concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels.
  - Evidence of serious harm at dietary exposure levels remains limited.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Limited direct support found.
- **Evidence against**:
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification located for this specific additive.
  - Regulatory bodies continue to permit use with limits.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest facts are: (a) continued authorization with explicit limits; (b) established ADIs by EFSA and JECFA; (c) EFSA concern flags mainly tied to conservative high-exposure scenarios rather than confirmed harm at typical intakes; (d) recent PubMed evidence directly on food-grade oral toxicity is relatively sparse.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
