# E465 (Ethyl methyl cellulose) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E465
- **Name**: Ethyl methyl cellulose (methyl ethyl cellulose)
- **INS number**: 465
- **CAS number**: 9004-69-7
- **Chemical class**: Modified cellulose ether (mixed alkyl cellulose ether)
- **Common synonyms**: Methyl ethyl cellulose; Ethyl methyl ether of cellulose; MEC
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Semi-synthetic** (cellulose from plant fibre is chemically etherified with methyl and ethyl groups)

**Data found**:
- FAO/JECFA additive detail record lists CAS 9004-69-7, synonym MEC, and INS 465.
- EFSA summary text describes E465 as a synthetically obtained modified cellulose from fibrous plant material.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action**:
  - Polymer cellulose backbone with methyl/ethyl substitutions hydrates and increases viscosity.
  - Supports emulsion and foam stability by changing interfacial and bulk rheology.
- **Functional roles reported by regulators/standards**:
  - JECFA monograph: emulsifier, stabilizer, thickening agent, foaming agent.
  - U.S. CFR: aerating, emulsifying, and foaming agent.
- **Common food use context**:
  - EU: authorized broadly as part of the cellulose group in many categories at quantum satis (group authorization context).
  - U.S.: direct food additive under GMP-style limit (amount not more than reasonably required).

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation found**: EFSA Journal 2018;16(1):5047 (DOI: 10.2903/j.efsa.2018.5047), adopted **27 September 2017**, amended **31 January 2018**, first published January 2018.
- **Approval status**: authorized food additive in the EU (Annex II/III context in EFSA summary).
- **ADI**: EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for unmodified/modified celluloses (includes E465).
- **Key conclusions**:
  - No genotoxic concern, no carcinogenic properties identified for the cellulose group.
  - Chronic NOAELs reported up to 9,000 mg/kg bw/day (group dataset including E465 studies).
  - No safety concern at reported uses/use levels for the cellulose group.
  - Indicative total exposure of ~660-900 mg/kg bw/day considered compatible with conclusions.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Absorption and disposition**:
  - JECFA monograph reports most administered dose recovered in feces; limited evidence of systemic uptake.
  - EFSA group assessment states modified celluloses (including E465) are largely not absorbed intact and are excreted mainly in feces.
- **Animal evidence (E465-specific historical studies)**:
  - Long-term rat and mouse dietary studies (0, 0.1%, 1% for 2 years) reported lower body weight at high dose, with no increase in tumor incidence and no major pathology signal (cited by JECFA/EFSA).
- **Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity**:
  - EFSA re-evaluation: no genotoxic concern and no carcinogenic properties for microcrystalline/modified celluloses as a group.
- **Human/epidemiology**:
  - No strong modern E465-specific epidemiologic signal found.
- **PubMed search outcomes for recent studies**:
  - Query `"methyl ethyl cellulose"[Title/Abstract]` with publication date filter 2016+ returned **0** records.
  - Query `"ethyl methyl cellulose"[Title/Abstract]` with publication date filter 2010+ returned **1** record (the 2018 EFSA re-evaluation paper).
- **Mechanistic concerns**:
  - High intake of cellulose-type additives can produce GI symptoms (e.g., bloating/flatulence/laxative effects) as non-specific bulk effects.
  - EFSA noted microbiome/inflammation concerns in literature mainly for **E466**, not as direct evidence of E465 harm.

## Exposure Assessment
- **E465-specific intake data**: not reported separately in the retrieved EFSA/JECFA material; exposure is generally assessed as a combined cellulose group.
- **Group exposure estimates from EFSA (celluloses E460-466, E468, E469)**:
  - Highest 95th percentile refined estimate for general population: up to **506 mg/kg bw/day**.
  - EFSA indicative total exposure consistent with no-safety-concern conclusion: around **660-900 mg/kg bw/day**.
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - For E465 specifically, no direct exceedance dataset found.
  - A high infant estimate (1,557 mg/kg bw/day) in EFSA pertained to FSMP scenarios tied to **E466** data limitations, not a direct E465 intake estimate.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups**:
  - High consumers of products using cellulose additives and people sensitive to GI bulking effects.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - EFSA/JECFA conclusions are highly permissive (no numerical ADI / ADI not specified in group context).
  - No genotoxic or carcinogenic signal identified in regulatory evaluations.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - E465 is semi-synthetic (not an endogenous nutrient).
  - GI intolerance effects can occur at high intake.
  - Dataset is largely historical and often grouped across multiple celluloses.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Clear approvals in EU and U.S. with specifications and GMP/quantum satis controls.
  - Regulatory evaluations found no safety concern at reported uses/levels.
  - No IARC classification and no major ban signal found.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Limited modern E465-specific human outcome data.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Group-level exposure and uncertainty issues exist for some cellulose scenarios (especially infant/FSMP discussions around E466).
  - High-dose bulk effects can occur.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - These caveats are not strong E465-specific toxicity signals.
  - Regulatory conclusions remain overall favorable.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - No strong evidence found.
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No major-jurisdiction bans found.
  - No IARC carcinogenic group classification found.
  - EFSA/JECFA safety conclusions are not consistent with high-risk categorization.

### 2. Rationale
The evidence base supports regulatory acceptance with low intrinsic toxicity and no carcinogenic/genotoxic signal, but does not support a "risk_free" label due to high-intake GI effects and limited recent E465-specific human data.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
