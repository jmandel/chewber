# E952 (Cyclamates / Cyclamic acid) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E952 (E952(i) cyclamic acid, E952(ii) sodium cyclamate, E952(iii) calcium cyclamate)
- **Primary name:** Cyclamic acid (cyclohexylsulfamic acid)
- **CAS number(s):** 100-88-9 (cyclamic acid), 139-05-9 (sodium cyclamate), 139-06-0 (calcium cyclamate)
- **Chemical class:** Sulfamic acid derivative, high-intensity non-nutritive sweetener
- **Common synonyms:** Cyclohexylsulfamic acid, cyclamates, sodium cyclamate, calcium cyclamate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic (CFR describes cyclamates as synthetic chemicals not found in natural products at detectable levels)

Evidence: WHO/JECFA chemical overview and synonyms; PubChem CAS/synonym records; 21 CFR 189.135 identity text [S3][S7][S8][S2].

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** High-intensity sweet taste (CFR: about 30-40x sucrose), used in very low concentrations.
- **Common food categories:** Soft drinks and other flavoured drinks, beer, and tabletop sweeteners were specifically discussed in JECFA exposure work; sweeteners are broadly used in low-calorie/sugar-reduced products.

Evidence: 21 CFR 189.135(a); JECFA 2009 comments on evaluated use-level scenarios; EFSA sweeteners topic context [S2][S3][S1].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Not GRAS/approved for direct food use; in practice treated as prohibited in human food.
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 189.135 (Cyclamate and its derivatives).**
- **Conditions of use:** Section 189.135(b) states food containing any added or detectable cyclamate is deemed adulterated.

Sources: eCFR API section text [S2].

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-11 mg/kg bw** (expressed as cyclamic acid).
- **Last evaluation year shown:** **2009** (with earlier toxicological evaluation year **1982** also shown).
- **Key JECFA conclusion:** At maximum use levels, only the lowest level evaluated (250 mg/kg) was not likely to exceed ADI for high consumers including children; 350 mg/kg was also reported below ADI for high consumers.

Sources: WHO/JECFA chemical entry for cyclamic acid [S3], WHO API search output [S4].

### IARC
- **Classification:** **Group 3** (Cyclamates, sodium cyclamate entry in IARC list dataset; eval year 1998 / publication year 1999).

Sources: IARC classifications app data (`loc.app.js`) and IARC list page [S5][S6].

### Notable bans/restrictions
- **United States:** Prohibited from direct addition/use in human food under 21 CFR 189.135 [S2].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/mechanistic evidence
- A 2022 mouse study using intraperitoneal sodium cyclamate (up to 6000 mg/kg/day for 5 days) reported dose/time-related liver injury and inflammatory markers, with heart/kidney injury signals at high doses.
- A 2025 rat ex vivo study reported that cyclohexylamine (a cyclamate metabolite) caused vas deferens contraction via adrenergic pathways, supporting biologically plausible reproductive-system pharmacology concerns at experimental conditions.

Sources: PubMed PMIDs 35264869 and 41080636 [S10][S11].

### Carcinogenicity and epidemiology context
- IARC classifies cyclamates (sodium cyclamate) as Group 3 (not classifiable as to carcinogenicity to humans).
- Recent reviews including cyclamate report no consistent epidemiologic or mechanistic evidence for human-relevant carcinogenicity, but notable conflict-of-interest disclosures are present in some 2025 reviews.

Sources: IARC dataset/list page; PubMed PMIDs 41197905 and 41038346 [S5][S6][S12][S13].

## Exposure Assessment
- **Reference benchmark:** JECFA ADI 0-11 mg/kg bw.
- **Potential exceedance:** JECFA's 2009 evaluation comments explicitly indicate high-consumer exceedance risk (including children) at several higher maximum-use scenarios.
- **Vulnerable populations:** Children/high consumers of products sweetened with cyclamates (e.g., frequent intake of diet beverages/tabletop sweeteners).
- **Practical interpretation:** Risk is exposure-dependent; lower use levels discussed by JECFA were less likely to exceed ADI, while higher-use scenarios increased exceedance risk.

Sources: WHO/JECFA chemical entry (2009 comments) [S3].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting:** IARC Group 3; JECFA maintains a numeric ADI rather than identifying current widespread harm at normal use levels [S3][S5].
- **Evidence against:** U.S. regulatory prohibition for human food use and JECFA-described ADI exceedance scenarios for high consumers/children argue against a "risk-free" classification [S2][S3].

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting:** EU remains authorised with ongoing re-evaluation; JECFA ADI is established and maintained [S1][S3].
- **Evidence against:** Clear exceedance caveats in JECFA high-intake scenarios, plus recent animal/mechanistic toxicity findings, weaken a pure "limited concern only at very high doses" interpretation [S3][S10][S11].

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting:** Approved/authorised in major frameworks but with meaningful caveats: ADI exceedance risk in high consumers (including children) and credible experimental toxicity signals at high doses; EFSA has not completed re-evaluation yet [S1][S3][S10][S11].
- **Evidence against:** IARC is Group 3 and recent reviews do not show consistent human carcinogenic signal [S5][S12][S13].

#### high
- **Evidence supporting:** U.S. prohibition is a strong adverse regulatory signal in a major jurisdiction [S2].
- **Evidence against:** Continued EU authorisation and maintained JECFA ADI, plus IARC Group 3 rather than Group 2A/2B/1, do not support a broadly "high" global hazard tier [S1][S3][S5].

### 2. Rationale
The evidence base is mixed: international bodies (JECFA, IARC, current EU authorisation status) do not indicate a clear high-tier carcinogenic hazard, but JECFA's own 2009 exposure analysis identifies realistic ADI exceedance scenarios for high consumers including children, and recent animal/mechanistic studies provide non-trivial biological concern signals. Combined with unresolved EFSA re-evaluation status, this best fits a **moderate** tier.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
