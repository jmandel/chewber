# E462 (Ethyl cellulose) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E462 (INS 462) [S5]
- **Primary name:** Ethyl cellulose [S3][S5]
- **CAS number(s):** 9004-57-3 [S3][S4][S5]
- **Chemical class:** Modified cellulose ether (cellulose polymer with ethoxy groups) [S4]
- **Common synonyms:** Ethyl ether of cellulose; Cellulose ethyl ether [S3]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (chemically modified cellulose derivative) [S1][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Largely not absorbed intact in the GI tract; acts primarily as a technological polymer (binder/coating/fixative/thickener), with some fermentation of cellulose derivatives by intestinal flora reported in EFSA re-evaluation [S1].
- **Common food roles/categories:**
  - Binder/filler in dry vitamin preparations [S4]
  - Protective coating component for vitamin/mineral tablets [S4]
  - Fixative in flavoring compounds [S4]
  - Additional FDA inventory functional classes include flavoring adjuvant, formulation aid, solvent/vehicle, and stabilizer/thickener [S5]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Authorized in the EU food additive framework; still listed in current EU legislation updates that include E462 [S2][S8].
- **Latest EFSA food-additive re-evaluation:** EFSA ANS Panel opinion published as EFSA Journal 2018;16(1):5047 (re-evaluation of celluloses including E462) [S1].
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI required (consistent with JECFA/SCF "ADI not specified" approach for this group) [S1].
- **Evaluation year (food additive):** 2018 [S1].
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - No genotoxic concern.
  - No carcinogenic signal in available datasets.
  - Chronic NOAELs for celluloses in the dataset up to 9,000 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Refined 95th percentile combined exposure estimate up to 506 mg/kg bw/day; panel still concluded no need for a numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses/levels [S1].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Direct food additive status:** Permitted/approved under **21 CFR 172.868** with specified conditions of use [S4].
- **GRAS context:** Ethyl cellulose is also listed in **21 CFR 182.90** for substances migrating to food from paper/paperboard products (GRAS for that indirect use context) [S4][S5].
- **Conditions of use in 21 CFR 172.868:**
  - Substance definition/specification as cellulose ether with ethoxy substitution limit.
  - Uses: binder/filler in dry vitamin preparations; protective coatings for vitamin/mineral tablets; fixative in flavoring compounds [S4].

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED** (group ADI for modified celluloses including ethyl cellulose) [S3].
- **Evaluation year:** 1989 entry in WHO/JECFA database for ETHYL CELLULOSE (with earlier references shown) [S3].

### IARC
- Ethyl cellulose was **not found as a classified agent** in the current IARC Monographs list-of-classifications dataset (updated 2026-02-25) [S6][S7].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC materials [S1][S3][S4][S6].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies and regulatory toxicology
- EFSA re-evaluation reports low acute toxicity for celluloses, no genotoxic concern, no carcinogenic properties detected in evaluated datasets, and no reproductive/developmental effects at doses above 1,000 mg/kg bw in relevant studies; chronic NOAELs reported up to 9,000 mg/kg bw/day [S1].

### Human epidemiological data
- No robust additive-specific epidemiological signal for ethyl cellulose in the reviewed regulatory sources [S1][S3].

### Recent PubMed safety-related literature
- **2024 EFSA FEEDAP update**: ethyl cellulose additive considered safe for all animal species, consumers, and environment; user safety could not be concluded due to missing data [S9].
- **2020 EFSA FEEDAP opinion**: similar conclusion pattern for feed use (safe for animals/consumer/environment; insufficient user-safety data) [S10].
- PubMed search results are dominated by materials/packaging/drug-delivery applications rather than new direct food-toxicology signals for E462 [S11].

### Mechanistic concerns
- No strong mechanistic hazard signal (genotoxic/carcinogenic) was identified in the core EFSA/JECFA evaluations for food use levels [S1][S3].

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA refined 95th percentile **combined** exposure estimate for celluloses (E460-466, E468, E469) reached up to **506 mg/kg bw/day**, with an indicative total exposure estimate around **660-900 mg/kg bw/day**; despite this, EFSA concluded no need for numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses [S1].
- For E462 specifically, exposure is typically part of the broader modified-cellulose exposure pool rather than assessed in isolation in the main EFSA opinion [S1].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not directly applicable because ADI is not specified; based on EFSA/JECFA conclusions, exceedance-driven concern was not identified for reported uses [S1][S3].
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** Evidence for special vulnerability in consumers is limited in reviewed food-additive evaluations; occupational/user-data gaps were noted in feed-additive opinions [S9][S10].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - EFSA and JECFA conclusions are highly permissive (no numerical ADI needed / ADI not specified).
  - No convincing genotoxic or carcinogenic signal in reviewed regulatory toxicology [S1][S3].
- Evidence arguing against placement:
  - Ethyl cellulose is a **semi-synthetic** modified polymer, not an endogenous nutrient.
  - Data are stronger for grouped celluloses than for highly granular E462-only modern datasets [S1].

**limited**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - Clear regulatory acceptance across EU, US, and JECFA.
  - Established permitted-use conditions (21 CFR 172.868) and no major safety alerts at current food use patterns [S1][S3][S4].
- Evidence arguing against placement:
  - Limited recent additive-specific epidemiology; some recent literature focuses on applications rather than food safety endpoints [S11].

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - Could be argued if one emphasizes data gaps and grouped read-across rather than additive-specific modern toxicology.
- Evidence arguing against placement:
  - Regulators did **not** reduce ADI or flag consumer intake concern for reported uses.
  - No major jurisdictional restrictions/bans from reviewed sources [S1][S3][S4].

**high**
- Evidence supporting placement:
  - No substantial evidence found.
- Evidence arguing against placement:
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B signal, no major bans, and consistent regulatory acceptance [S1][S3][S6].

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence is mainly from authoritative regulatory re-evaluations rather than new independent epidemiology, but those evaluations are consistent: low toxicological concern at permitted uses, no numerical ADI required, and no major carcinogenic/genotoxic warning flags.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
