# E319 (Tertiary butylhydroquinone, TBHQ) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E319
- **Primary name:** Tertiary butylhydroquinone (TBHQ)
- **CAS number(s):** 1948-33-0
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic phenolic antioxidant (hydroquinone derivative)
- **Common synonyms:** tert-Butylhydroquinone; Mono-tert-butylhydroquinone; t-Butylhydroquinone; 2-(1,1-Dimethylethyl)-1,4-benzenediol; TBHQ
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic food antioxidant

Evidence: JECFA chemical entry lists chemical names/synonyms/CAS/functional class; 21 CFR 172.185 defines TBHQ identity and CAS; toxicology review describes TBHQ among synthetic phenolic antioxidants. [S3][S6][S14]

## Function in Food
TBHQ functions as an **antioxidant** to slow oxidative deterioration/rancidity in fat-containing foods. Mechanistically, literature describes radical-scavenging/lipid-peroxidation inhibition and Nrf2-related redox signaling effects. [S3][S8][S14]

In U.S. regulation (21 CFR 172.185), TBHQ is used as an antioxidant alone or with BHA/BHT, with a total antioxidant cap of **0.02% of the oil/fat content** of the food (including volatile oils). [S3]

EFSA exposure work indicates use across fat/oil-related categories (e.g., fats/oils for cooking/frying, fat emulsions, dehydrated potato products, and snacks). [S1]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved with restrictions (ADI-based risk management and authorized-use limits)
- **ADI:** **0.7 mg/kg bw/day**
- **Evaluation year(s):** 2004 opinion (AFC Panel), 2016 refined exposure statement
- **Key conclusions:**
  - 2004: AFC accepted ADI 0–0.7 mg/kg bw/day; potential adult exposure could exceed ADI in some countries. [S2]
  - 2016: EFSA reported that non-brand-loyal modeled exposure could exceed ADI in toddlers/children, but refined exposure estimates were lower and EFSA concluded **no safety concern** for refined estimates. [S1]

### FDA / U.S. CFR
- **Status:** Not GRAS-listed in examined GRAS parts; regulated as an **approved direct food additive** under **21 CFR 172.185**. [S3][S4][S5]
- **CFR citation:** 21 CFR 172.185 (TBHQ)
- **Conditions of use:** identity/purity specifications (including >=99.0% TBHQ in additive), use as antioxidant alone or with BHA/BHT, and total antioxidant content limit 0.02% of food oil/fat content. [S3]

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **0–0.7 mg/kg bw**
- **Last evaluation year:** **1997** (Meeting 49 in JECFA database entry)
- **Notes:** JECFA intake summary (1998) reported some mean intakes exceeding ADI in certain submitted national datasets and potential high-consumer exceedance. [S6]

### IARC
- TBHQ was **not found** in IARC’s current “Agents classified by the IARC Monographs” dataset/page checked (last update shown 2026-02-25 in the loaded dataset). [S10][S11]
- Practical interpretation for this report: **no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing identified** for TBHQ in checked resources.

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban was identified in the retrieved EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and experimental evidence
- **Zebrafish dietary exposure study (2023):** long-duration dietary tBHQ exposure impaired survival/growth and altered development/gene expression (Nrf2a-dependent and independent patterns). [S12]
- **Rat studies (2024, 2025):** TBHQ exposure was associated with memory impairment and oxidative-stress signatures in brain/liver/kidney; co-treatment with chitosan mitigated effects in these models. [S9][S13]
- **Mechanistic/network toxicology study (2025):** predicted links to hepato/nephro/neurotoxicity pathways (computational + docking, not definitive causal human evidence). [S8]
- **Review-level hazard literature:** recent reviews discuss cytotoxic/genotoxic/carcinogenic concerns at high doses and mechanistic hypotheses (oxidative stress, metabolite reactivity). [S7][S14]

### Epidemiological/human data
- No robust prospective human epidemiological dataset establishing harm at approved dietary exposure levels was identified in the retrieved core sources; evidence base is dominated by animal/in vitro/mechanistic work. (Inference from source mix.) [S7][S8][S9][S12][S13][S14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmark:** ADI 0.7 mg/kg bw/day (EFSA/JECFA). [S1][S2][S6]
- **EFSA 2016 modeled exposure:**
  - Non-brand-loyal scenario: mean up to ~0.9 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers) and ~0.7 mg/kg bw/day (children); high-level up to ~1.8 (toddlers) and ~1.5 (children), i.e., potential exceedance.
  - Refined assessment: substantially lower means/highs (e.g., up to ~0.11 mean and ~0.56 high in key child groups), with EFSA concluding no safety concern for refined estimates. [S1]
- **JECFA intake note (1998):** some submitted national mean intakes exceeded ADI; possibility of high-consumer exceedance acknowledged with limited quantification. [S6]

### Vulnerable populations
- **Toddlers/children** and **high consumers** of fat/oil-rich processed foods are the most plausible groups for approaching/exceeding ADI in conservative scenarios. [S1][S6]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - EFSA/JECFA established ADI and retained authorization framework.
  - EFSA refined 2016 exposure conclusion reported no safety concern at refined exposure estimates. [S1][S6]
- **Evidence against:**
  - ADI exceedance can occur in non-refined/conservative scenarios (children/toddlers).
  - Experimental literature reports toxicity signals at higher exposures (animal/in vitro). [S1][S7][S9][S12][S13]

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Clear regulatory approvals with explicit use limits (EU/US/JECFA framework).
  - No IARC carcinogen group listing identified. [S1][S3][S6][S10][S11]
- **Evidence against:**
  - Recurrent mechanistic/animal concern signals and potential ADI exceedance in specific populations/scenarios. [S1][S6][S7][S9][S12][S13]

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Approved additive but with ADI and concentration caps.
  - Credible signals from animal/mechanistic studies.
  - Regulatory exposure analyses identify possible exceedance in children/toddlers under some assumptions. [S1][S3][S6][S7][S9][S12][S13][S14]
- **Evidence against:**
  - EFSA refined assessment concluded no safety concern at refined use/intake assumptions.
  - No major ban or IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification identified. [S1][S10][S11]

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting:**
  - Mechanistic literature includes cytotoxic/genotoxic/carcinogenic hypotheses, especially at high exposure ranges. [S7][S8][S14]
- **Evidence against:**
  - Not banned in major jurisdictions among checked sources.
  - Regulators maintain ADI-based approval; IARC listing not identified. [S1][S3][S6][S10][S11]

### 2. Rationale
TBHQ has a mature regulatory framework and remains authorized, but the evidence does not justify a “risk_free” label because (a) ADI exceedance appears possible in some child/high-consumer exposure scenarios and (b) animal/mechanistic toxicity signals persist. At the same time, absence of major bans/IARC high-risk classification and EFSA’s refined-exposure “no safety concern” conclusion argue against a “high” tier.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
