# E623 (Calcium diglutamate) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E623 (INS 623).
- **Name:** Calcium diglutamate (calcium di-L-glutamate; monocalcium di-L-glutamate).
- **CAS number(s):** 19238-49-4.
- **Chemical class:** Calcium salt of L-glutamic acid (glutamate flavour-enhancer salt).
- **Common synonyms:** Calcium glutamate; calcium di-L-glutamate; monocalcium di-L-glutamate.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** The glutamate moiety is naturally occurring, but food-additive E623 is generally manufactured by processing/neutralizing industrially produced L-glutamic acid (typically fermentation-derived), so practical food-additive origin is industrial (semi-synthetic).

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Flavour enhancer (umami enhancer) and sodium-reduction aid.
- **Mechanism of action:** Dissociates to provide free glutamate, which activates umami taste pathways and can increase savoury perception at lower sodium levels.
- **Common food categories:** Seasonings/condiments, soups and broths, sauces, savoury processed foods; EFSA exposure work on glutamate additives identified major contributors such as fine bakery wares, soups/broths, sauces, meat products, and seasonings/condiments.

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion/re-evaluation found:** EFSA Panel re-evaluation of E620-E625 (2017; EFSA Journal article 4910), explicitly including **E623 calcium glutamate**.
- **Approval status:** Approved in the EU as part of currently authorised glutamate additives (E620-E625), with re-evaluation completed.
- **ADI:** Group ADI **30 mg/kg bw/day** (expressed as glutamic acid).
- **Evaluation year:** 2017.
- **Key conclusions:**
  - No genotoxicity concern for E620-E625.
  - NOAEL of 3,200 mg monosodium glutamate/kg bw/day used to derive ADI.
  - EFSA stated that exposure could exceed the proposed ADI in some population groups.
  - EFSA's refined non-brand-loyal scenario (additive uses in Annex II food categories) reported mean exposure up to 51 mg/kg bw/day and 95th percentile up to 93 mg/kg bw/day; broader "all available data" scenario reported substantially higher exposure ranges (mean up to 198 mg/kg bw/day; 95th percentile up to 489 mg/kg bw/day).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- **EFSA (2017):** No adverse effects in the key short-term/subchronic/chronic/repro-developmental dataset sufficient to preclude approval at the derived ADI; no genotoxicity concern for the E620-E625 group.
- **Allium cepa assay (2013, includes calcium diglutamate):** reported cytogenetic abnormalities and reduced mitotic index in onion root meristem cells after exposure to glutamate flavour enhancers; relevance to human dietary risk is limited (non-mammalian in vitro model).

### Human and epidemiological/clinical context
- **EFSA human-data summary:** human challenge evidence suggested some effects at higher single doses but lacked a robust dose-response suitable for deriving the ADI.
- **Recent review on MSG-headache literature (2024):** evidence remains mixed/conflicting; many positive studies use doses above typical intake.
- **Recent systematic review on MSG and gut microbiota/metabolic dysbiosis (2024):** raises possible associations but largely based on heterogeneous and non-E623-specific evidence.
- **Recent systematic review (2025):** reported potential adverse outcomes at high/repeated MSG exposures; not E623-specific and largely non-clinical evidence.
- **Methodological appraisal paper (2025):** in a case-study set of MSG toxicity/amelioration papers, no study was rated high quality for human safety inference due to relevance/reliability limitations.

### Bottom line on evidence quality
- Direct long-term human safety studies specific to **calcium diglutamate (E623)** are sparse.
- Most modern concern signals are extrapolated from MSG-focused literature, often at high-dose or model-specific conditions.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Health benchmark used by EFSA:** Group ADI 30 mg/kg bw/day (as glutamic acid equivalent).
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal exposure (additive-use scenario):**
  - Mean: 3-51 mg/kg bw/day (highest in toddlers).
  - 95th percentile: 7-93 mg/kg bw/day (highest in children).
- **EFSA "all available data" scenario (including broader glutamate sources):**
  - Mean: 8-198 mg/kg bw/day (highest in infants).
  - 95th percentile: 17-489 mg/kg bw/day (highest in infants).
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Yes, especially for infants/toddlers/children and high consumers.
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants, toddlers, children, and frequent consumers of glutamate-contributing processed foods.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting risk_free:**
  - Glutamate is an endogenous amino acid-related nutrient.
  - JECFA lists a group ADI "not specified" for glutamate salts.
- **Evidence against risk_free:**
  - EFSA set a finite group ADI (30 mg/kg bw/day).
  - EFSA exposure modelling showed ADI exceedance in multiple populations.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting limited:**
  - EU approval maintained after re-evaluation.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - No major bans identified.
- **Evidence against limited:**
  - EFSA's own exposure analysis indicates exceedance risk at population level.
  - Some mechanistic/nonclinical literature reports adverse effects under high-dose conditions.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting moderate:**
  - Approved additive with defined ADI and explicit EFSA caveat that ADI can be exceeded in real diets, especially for younger groups/high consumers.
  - Mixed but credible concern signals in modern literature (mostly indirect via MSG), without strong direct long-term E623 human data.
- **Evidence against moderate:**
  - EFSA also noted exposure uncertainty tends to overestimate in some scenarios.
  - High-quality direct E623-specific harm evidence is limited.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting high:**
  - Some recent reviews and experimental studies report broad toxicity concerns at repeated/high doses of glutamate additives (mainly MSG-focused evidence).
- **Evidence against high:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for calcium diglutamate.
  - Authoritative regulatory bodies (EFSA/JECFA) maintain permitted-use status.

### 2. Rationale
E623 has a clear regulatory acceptance baseline, but EFSA's 2017 re-evaluation introduced a finite ADI and documented potential exceedance in important subgroups (notably younger populations and high consumers). Combined with mixed modern mechanistic literature and sparse E623-specific human long-term data, this supports a cautionary but not high-risk classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
