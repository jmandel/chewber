# E1442 Research Report — Hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1442 (INS 1442). [S5]
- **Primary name:** Hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate. [S5]
- **CAS number(s):** 53124-00-8 (PubChem entry for hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate). [S13]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (cross-linked and hydroxypropylated starch phosphate; polymeric carbohydrate derivative). [S2][S5][S13]
- **Common synonyms:** HPDSP, HDP, starch hydrogen phosphate 2-hydroxypropyl ether, INS 1442. [S5][S13]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from starch and chemically modified). [S2][S4]

## Function in Food
- **Core technological role:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener (JECFA functional classes). [S5]
- **Mechanism of action:** Modified starch structure increases hydration/viscosity and process stability in food matrices; it is susceptible to amylase-driven hydrolysis, which can reduce viscosity over storage. [S11][S12]
- **Common food categories (documented examples):** starch thickeners for dysphagia formulations, yogurt systems, and frozen dough improver systems. [S11][S12][S15]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved in the EU within the group re-evaluation of modified starches (including E1442). [S2]
- **Latest re-evaluation year:** 2017 (EFSA Journal 15(10):e04911; DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4911). [S1][S2]
- **ADI:** No numerical ADI required (effectively ADI “not specified” approach for this modified starch group). [S2]
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA concluded no safety concern at reported uses/use levels for the general population; no numerical ADI needed. [S2]
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA page access from this VM hit anti-bot protection, so conclusions were confirmed via the PubMed-indexed EFSA article record/abstract and DOI metadata. [S1][S2]

### FDA / CFR (US)

- **Conditions of use (examples from CFR text):**

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- **Chronic mouse feeding (89 weeks, PMID 2430873):** Very high dietary inclusion (55% diet) of hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate was associated with increased water intake, cecal/colonic enlargement, and mild renal/urinary findings; study reported no evidence of carcinogenicity. [S8]
- **Rat mineral studies (PMID 11160549; PMID 993856):** Hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate reduced iron/zinc absorption in rat models under specific diet/formulation contexts; effects depended on starch modification and preparation conditions. [S9][S10]

### Human/epidemiology
- **EFSA re-evaluation evidence synthesis:** EFSA panel considered available short-/long-term toxicity, carcinogenicity, and reproductive toxicity data via read-across and found no safety concern at reported uses. [S2]
- **Large cohort additive-exposure mapping (NutriNet-Santé):** documents population exposure mixtures but does not establish E1442-specific causal harm. [S14]

### Mechanistic concerns
- **Potential nutrient interaction signal:** animal data suggest possible mineral bioavailability effects under some high/experimental conditions. [S9][S10]
- **Digestive handling:** modified starches are hydrolyzed/fermented rather than absorbed intact, per EFSA abstract. [S2]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Best available quantitative benchmark from EFSA abstract:** combined exposure to modified starches E1404–E1451 at 95th percentile up to **3,053 mg/kg bw/day** in the refined brand-loyal scenario; this is a combined-group estimate, not E1442-only. [S2]
- **E1442-specific intake:** not separately quantified in accessible public extracts used here. [S2][S5]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** a numerical ADI is not set (EFSA/JECFA “no need for numerical ADI” / “NOT SPECIFIED”), so exceedance against a numeric threshold is not directly applicable. [S2][S5]
- **Potentially higher-exposure groups:** people consuming many starch-thickened processed foods and users of starch-based dysphagia thickeners may have relatively higher exposure than average consumers; high-quality E1442-specific exposure distributions remain limited in reviewed sources. [S11][S14]

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** EFSA and JECFA both indicate no numerical ADI requirement / ADI not specified; EFSA found no safety concern at reported use levels. [S2][S5]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** additive is chemically modified (not endogenous), and animal studies report biological effects (e.g., mineral absorption changes, GI/renal findings at high doses), even if not judged to indicate major toxicity in current-use contexts. [S8][S9][S10]

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** approved by EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks; no carcinogenic signal in chronic mouse study; no EFSA genotoxic concern in re-evaluation; no IARC classification found. [S2][S3][S5][S7][S8]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** direct modern human safety datasets specific to E1442 are limited; some animal findings suggest context-dependent physiological effects. [S8][S9][S10]

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** credible animal evidence of altered mineral absorption and some renal/urinary findings at high exposure scenarios could be interpreted as caveats requiring continued monitoring. [S8][S9][S10]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** regulators did not reduce to a numeric ADI or identify current-use safety concern; evidence does not show strong adverse outcomes at typical use levels. [S2][S5]

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** none identified (no major bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification, no EFSA finding of inability to confirm safety). [S2][S5][S7]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** current regulatory consensus from EFSA/JECFA/FDA context supports permitted use with controls rather than prohibition. [S2][S3][S5]

### 2) Rationale
The strongest weight of evidence is from EFSA/JECFA evaluations indicating no safety concern at reported use levels and no need for a numerical ADI. Counter-signals exist from older animal studies (mineral absorption and high-dose physiological effects), but these have not led to current major-jurisdiction withdrawal or high-hazard classification.

### 3) Recommended tier
**`limited`**
