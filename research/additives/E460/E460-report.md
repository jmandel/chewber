# E460 (Microcrystalline Cellulose) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E460 (E460(i) microcrystalline cellulose; E460(ii) powdered cellulose)
- **Primary name:** Microcrystalline cellulose
- **CAS number(s):** 9004-34-6 (JECFA chemical entry for INS 460)
- **Chemical class:** Purified cellulose polysaccharide (insoluble dietary fiber/excipient)
- **Common synonyms:** Cellulose, cellulose gel, MCC, INS 460
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic/processed natural polymer** (derived from natural cellulose and physically/chemically processed to defined particle characteristics)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Bulking/texturizing and physical structuring via water binding and particle network effects; can function as anticaking and stabilizing aid.
- **Common functions/classes:** Anticaking agent, emulsifier, stabilizer (JECFA INS 460 listing); also used as thickener/gelling/binder contexts in regulatory dossiers.
- **Common food categories:** Broadly used in processed foods where texture, suspension stability, moisture management, or calorie dilution is needed (e.g., bakery/confectionery, emulsified products, beverage suspensions, reduced-fat formulations).

- **Latest food-additive re-evaluation found:** 2018 EFSA Journal opinion on celluloses (E460(i), E460(ii), E461-466, E468, E469).

- **Key EFSA conclusion:** No safety concern at reported uses/use levels for unmodified and modified celluloses.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies and toxicology
- EFSA 2018 summary reports:
  - Low acute toxicity.
  - No genotoxic concern.
  - Subchronic/chronic studies up to high dietary levels without specific treatment-related adverse effects.
  - Reported chronic NOAELs up to ~9,000 mg/kg bw/day for cellulose group members.
  - No carcinogenic signal for microcrystalline/modified celluloses in the evaluated data package.

### Human and mechanistic data
- JECFA toxicological monograph (WHO Food Additives Series 40) concluded no evidence that ingestion causes toxic effects in humans under GMP use; ADI retained as not specified.
- JECFA acknowledged historical concern regarding very fine particle persorption and retained a precautionary specification approach for particle size distribution.
- Gastrointestinal-function changes have been reported in some human studies at high intakes, but not indicating systemic toxicity in the JECFA evaluation context.

### Recent PubMed-indexed safety-related records
- 2020 EFSA FEEDAP opinion: microcrystalline cellulose considered safe for all animal species, consumers, and environment when meeting food-additive specifications; user safety could not be concluded due to limited data.
- 2024 EFSA FEEDAP update: with additional characterization data, safety conclusions for animals/consumer/environment remained supportive; user safety data gap persisted.
- 2025 case report (non-dietary route): pulmonary talc/cellulose granulomatosis linked to intravenous misuse of crushed oral tablets; this does **not** represent normal dietary exposure.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate (cellulose group):** 95th percentile refined exposure up to **506 mg/kg bw/day**; indicative total exposure around **660–900 mg/kg bw/day** for microcrystalline/powdered/modified celluloses.
- **JECFA historical intake estimates (microcrystalline cellulose):**
  - US mean: ~2.7–5.1 g/person/day; heavy consumers (90th): ~5.4–10.2 g/person/day.
  - UK mean: ~0.65 g/person/day; heavy consumers (90th): ~1.13–1.83 g/person/day.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not applicable in the conventional numeric sense because ADI is “not specified”/no numerical ADI.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups:** High-consumption subgroups (especially children by body weight) for GI tolerance considerations; occupational/user handling remains a separate uncertainty domain in some feed-additive assessments.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting placement:**
  - EFSA and JECFA both converge on no need for a numerical ADI / ADI not specified.
  - No genotoxicity/carcinogenicity signal in core regulatory evaluations.
  - Material is cellulose-based and largely not absorbed systemically.
- **Evidence against placement:**
  - Historical fine-particle persorption concern prompted specification controls.
  - Some GI function changes at high intakes; not systemic toxicity, but not strictly “zero signal.”

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting placement:**
  - Strong regulatory support (EFSA/JECFA) with no major safety signal at current uses.
  - ADI not specified/no numerical ADI indicates low concern at GMP uses.
  - Recent FEEDAP assessments remain broadly supportive for safety (with defined quality/specification conditions).
- **Evidence against placement:**
  - Data gaps remain for user/handler safety in feed-additive context.
  - Not every endpoint has substance-specific modern datasets; read-across is used for cellulose group in EFSA food-additive re-evaluation.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting placement:**
  - Exposures can be substantial in high consumers.
  - Particle-size and user-safety uncertainties are not fully eliminated in all contexts.
- **Evidence against placement:**
  - Regulatory bodies did not reduce to a restrictive numeric ADI and did not report a food-use safety concern requiring tighter hazard-tier classification.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting placement:**
  - None found for dietary use (no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for this agent, no major-jurisdiction ban found).
- **Evidence against placement:**
  - No corroborating regulatory action consistent with high-hazard tier for food-use exposure.

### 2. Rationale
The weight of evidence is driven by concordant EFSA/JECFA conclusions of low toxicological concern at current uses, no numeric ADI requirement, and absence of carcinogenic/genotoxic signals in evaluated datasets. Residual uncertainty is mainly around non-dietary/user handling contexts and specification-quality controls, not a clear dietary hazard signal.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier:** `limited`
