# E1412 (Distarch phosphate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1412 (INS 1412). Source: WHO/JECFA chemical page (Evaluation database, accessed Feb 27, 2026): https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3281
- **Name:** Distarch phosphate. Source: WHO/JECFA chemical page: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3281
- **CAS number(s):** 55963-33-2; 63055-37-8 (modified amylopectin). Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19 (2016), Distarch phosphate specification section: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified, cross-linked starch (phosphate cross-linking). Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19 definition text: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf
- **Common synonyms:** Modified starch; Distarch phosphate; INS No. 1412. Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19 and WHO/JECFA chemical page: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf ; https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3281
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Semi-synthetic** (derived from food starch, then chemically esterified/cross-linked with sodium trimetaphosphate or phosphorus oxychloride). Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Cross-linking starch chains increases process stability (heat/shear/acid tolerance) and controls thickening/viscosity behavior in food systems. Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19 definition + regulatory treatment chemistry in 21 CFR 172.892: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf ; https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=172
- **Primary technological roles:** Thickener, stabilizer (also binder/emulsifier in JECFA functional uses). Source: FAO JECFA Monographs 19 and WHO/JECFA page: http://www.fao.org/3/a-i6413e.pdf ; https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/3281
- **Common food categories (regulatory + literature context):** Broadly permitted in foods as "food starch-modified" in the U.S.; commonly used in thickened/stabilized products (e.g., sauces/surimi/noodles/dairy and texture-modified products in recent literature). Sources: 21 CFR 172.892 text; PubMed records on modified starch applications: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2024-06-03/title-21.xml?part=172 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31286513/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39183986/

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology (strongest evidence):**
  - EFSA 2017 re-evaluation of modified starches (includes E1412) found no safety concern at reported uses and no need for numerical ADI; no genotoxic concern; no treatment-related adverse effects relevant for risk assessment in high-dose rat studies (up to 31,000 mg/kg bw/day in read-across set). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625282/

- **Class-level/related evidence with caveats:**
  - 1982 rat study with related modified starches (E1414 and E1422) reported caecal enlargement and renal mineralization findings under test diets, but authors also discussed diet mineral formulation as a confounder and effects were less than lactose for some endpoints. This is **not** a direct E1412 chronic toxicity study, but relevant as historical context for modified starch interpretation. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6890017/

- **Mechanistic/physiology signal (low certainty for harm):**
  - In vitro infant fecal fermentation study (2018) using maize hydroxypropylated distarch phosphate reported higher gas production, faster pH drop, and less diverse microbiota profile versus locust bean gum in that model. This is a mechanistic/in vitro signal, not direct clinical toxicity evidence for E1412. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29508870/

- **Recent PubMed landscape:**
  - Recent "distarch phosphate" literature is dominated by food technology/material property studies rather than new toxicology studies directly demonstrating harm in humans. Source query and IDs: https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=distarch%20phosphate&retmax=20&retmode=json

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA exposure estimate (grouped modified starches):** Combined exposure to E1404-E1451 at 95th percentile (refined brand-loyal scenario) up to **3,053 mg/kg bw/day** in general population; EFSA still concluded no safety concern and no need for numerical ADI for the modified starch group. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625282/
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Classical ADI exceedance cannot be calculated because EFSA/JECFA use "not specified"/"no numerical ADI" for this group.
- **Potentially vulnerable groups (uncertainty-based):** Infants/young children consuming thickened foods may merit monitoring for gastrointestinal tolerance in high-use contexts, based on in vitro fermentation signals and general modified-starch fermentability evidence. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29508870/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32625282/

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA and JECFA both maintained non-numerical ADI framing ("not specified" / "no need for numerical ADI") and reported no safety concern at current uses.
  - Evidence against this tier: Distarch phosphate is a chemically modified ingredient (not endogenous), and direct modern long-term E1412-specific human toxicity studies are limited.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Approved in EU and U.S. frameworks; no IARC classification found; major regulators report no serious safety signal at permitted uses.
  - Evidence against this tier: Some mechanistic/in vitro gut fermentation findings and older class-level rat findings suggest that high-dose or formulation-dependent effects are possible, even if not judged as current regulatory concern.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Non-clinical signals exist (microbiota fermentation differences; historical related modified starch findings), and exposure is high in some grouped scenarios.
  - Evidence against this tier: Regulators did not reduce ADI or identify population-level risk requiring restrictions specific to E1412; no major jurisdictional bans or IARC hazard signal.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: None identified from reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC/PubMed sources.
  - Evidence against this tier: Continued authorization in major jurisdictions, no identified IARC classification, and no regulatory conclusion of inability to establish safety.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence is current regulatory re-evaluation (EFSA 2017) and JECFA history showing no safety concern at authorized uses with non-numerical ADI framing. U.S. regulation allows use under defined conditions in 21 CFR §172.892. The uncertainty lies mainly in limited additive-specific modern toxicology studies and some low-level mechanistic signals, but these do not currently outweigh the regulatory consensus.

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
