# E341 (Calcium phosphates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E341 (E341(i) monocalcium phosphate, E341(ii) dicalcium phosphate, E341(iii) tricalcium phosphate). [S2][S6][S7][S8]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic calcium salts of phosphoric acid (orthophosphates). [S2][S6][S7][S8]
- **CAS numbers:**
  - **7758-23-8** (calcium dihydrogen phosphate / monobasic calcium phosphate; E341(i)). [S6]
  - **7757-93-9** (calcium hydrogen phosphate / dibasic calcium phosphate; E341(ii)). [S7]
  - **7758-87-4** (calcium phosphate, tribasic / tricalcium phosphate; E341(iii)). [S8]
- **Common synonyms:** monocalcium phosphate, monobasic calcium phosphate, dicalcium phosphate, dibasic calcium phosphate, tricalcium phosphate, tribasic calcium phosphate, precipitated calcium phosphate. [S6][S7][S8]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** In additive use, E341 is treated as **synthetic** (industrially produced calcium phosphate salts). EFSA describes production from phosphoric acid with calcium oxide/hydroxide followed by drying/milling/sieving. [S2]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Calcium phosphates act as pH/acidity regulators and buffering salts; depending on form, they also function as anticaking and raising/leavening agents and can support texture/firmness. [S6][S7][S8]
- **JECFA functional classes (variant-dependent):** acidity regulator, anticaking agent, raising/leavening agent, firming agent, flour treatment agent, nutrient supplement, sequestrant, texturizer, thickener, yeast food. [S6][S7][S8]
- **Common food categories (EU authorization context):** processed cheese, bread and rolls, fine bakery wares, infant formula/foods for infants and young children, plus other categories covered under the phosphate group authorization. [S2]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest scientific re-evaluation identified:** EFSA Journal publication **5674**, *Re-evaluation of phosphoric acid-phosphates ... (E 338-341, E 343, E 450-452)*; adopted **4 June 2019**, published **12 June 2019**. [S1]
- **Approval status:** phosphates (including **E341**) are authorized in the EU under Annex II/III to Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008. [S2]
- **ADI:** EFSA established a **group ADI of 40 mg/kg bw/day (as phosphorus)**. [S2]
- **Key conclusions relevant to E341 within the phosphate group:**
  - No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity; no developmental toxicity signal.
  - The ADI was considered protective for healthy adults.
  - Exposure estimates exceeded the ADI in infants/toddlers/children (mean) and infants/toddlers/children/adolescents (95th percentile).
  - ADI does not apply to people with moderate-to-severe renal impairment.
  - EFSA recommended revising the EU aluminium limit for calcium phosphate (E341). [S2]
- **Access note:** direct EFSA pages were queried, but this environment encountered anti-bot restrictions / redirect-to-Wiley challenge for full content; data extraction used EFSA metadata and the PubMed Central mirror of the EFSA opinion. [S1][S2][S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies / toxicology
- EFSA reports that phosphates are of low acute oral toxicity; key adverse effect at higher exposures in repeat-dose studies is renal calcification/tubular nephropathy (for phosphate group assessment). [S2]
- A recent mouse study (2025) found chronic high-phosphate intake induced anxiety-like behavior with neuroimmune changes; this is mechanistic animal evidence under high dietary phosphate conditions. [S14]

### Human and epidemiological evidence
- A randomized cross-over human study (2024) showed meals containing inorganic phosphate additives altered acute calcium/phosphate homeostasis and urinary/hormonal responses. [S12]
- A cohort study (2023) found urinary phosphate associated with higher cardiovascular disease incidence, with interpretation linked to highly processed-food patterns. [S13]
- A 2025 packaged-food analysis reported widespread phosphate-additive presence in U.S. packaged foods, supporting exposure plausibility. [S15]

### Mechanistic/review evidence
- A 2023 review proposes phosphate additives as one plausible mechanism linking ultra-processed food intake to cardiorenal risk, while emphasizing data gaps and the need for improved exposure characterization. [S16]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA whole-diet phosphorus exposure estimate (analytical data scenario):** mean about **251 to 1,625 mg P/person/day** and high (95th percentile) about **331 to 2,728 mg P/person/day** across age groups. [S2]
- **Refined additive exposure scenario (non-brand-loyal):** about **1 to 48 mg P/kg bw/day** at mean and **3 to 62 mg P/kg bw/day** at the 95th percentile across groups. [S2]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA reported exceedance of the 40 mg/kg bw/day ADI in multiple younger populations and high consumers, with supplements also noted as potentially exceeding ADI. [S2]
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants/toddlers/children, high consumers of additive-rich processed foods, supplement users, and persons with impaired renal function (ADI not applicable to moderate-severe renal impairment). [S2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** phosphate is an essential nutrient; EFSA/FDA/JECFA all maintain regulatory acceptance rather than prohibition. [S2][S4][S6][S7][S8]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** EFSA set a finite group ADI (not “ADI not specified”) and documented exceedance in several younger/high-exposure groups; renal caveat is explicit. [S2]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** FDA GRAS with GMP condition, EFSA ADI framework, and JECFA MTDI provide a controlled-use profile without carcinogenic classification by IARC. [S2][S4][S6][S7][S8][S10][S11]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** EFSA exceedance findings plus emerging human/mechanistic literature indicate non-trivial concern at higher exposure patterns. [S2][S12][S13][S16]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** additive remains approved **with caveats**, EFSA reports ADI exceedance in some populations (especially children/high consumers), and recent evidence supports biologically plausible cardio-renal/mineral-balance concerns at higher inorganic phosphate intake. [S2][S12][S13][S16]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** no major regulatory ban and no IARC carcinogenic classification for calcium phosphates. [S4][S10][S11]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** limited; there are mechanistic and epidemiologic signals at high intake, but not equivalent to the criteria for “high” in this framework. [S12][S13][S14][S16]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:** no major-jurisdiction bans, EFSA did establish an ADI, and regulators continue to authorize use under GMP/authorized conditions. [S1][S2][S4][S6][S7][S8]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest regulatory signal is EFSA’s 2019 group ADI with documented exceedance in children/high consumers, alongside continued legal authorization in EU/US and no IARC classification. This combination indicates meaningful caveats without reaching a ban-level or carcinogen-classified risk profile.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
