# E350 (Sodium malates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E350 (subtypes: E350(i), E350(ii)).
- **Primary names:** Sodium malates; malic acid sodium salts.
- **CAS numbers found in primary records:**
  - **E350(i) (INS 350(i))**: Monosodium DL-malate, CAS **3105-51-9**.
  - **E350(ii) (INS 350(ii))**: Disodium DL-malate, CAS **676-46-0**.
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salts of malic acid (hydroxy-dicarboxylic acid salts).
- **Synonyms encountered in authoritative databases:**
  - Monosodium dl-malate; malic acid monosodium salt.
  - Disodium dl-malate; hydroxybutanedioic acid disodium salt; malic acid sodium salt.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Malate is a natural metabolic intermediate, but commercial food-grade malates are generally manufactured ingredients (neutralization of malic acid; malic acid itself may be fermentation-derived or synthesized).

- WHO/JECFA chemical record for INS 350(ii), 1979 evaluation page: https://apps.who.int/food-additives-contaminants-jecfa-database/Home/Chemical/5577

## Function in Food
- **Mechanistic role:** Acidification and buffering (pH control), tartness/flavor enhancement via malate anion, and related flavor support in acidic foods.
- **Regulatory functional categories observed:** Flavor enhancer, flavoring agent/adjuvant, pH control agent (U.S. CFR text for malic acid).
- **Common food categories from CFR conditions text:**
  - Nonalcoholic beverages
  - Chewing gum
  - Gelatins/puddings/fillings
  - Hard candy
  - Jams/jellies
  - Processed fruits/fruit juices
  - Soft candy
  - Other foods (general GMP category)

**Source:**
- U.S. eCFR §184.1069(c)-(d): https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/2025-04-01/title-21?chapter=I&subchapter=B&part=184&section=184.1069

- **Relevant citation for malate chemistry in human foods:** **21 CFR §184.1069 (Malic acid)**.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- A detailed safety review (CIR, 2001) reports malic acid as relatively low acute toxicity in animals, no mutagenicity signal in tested assays, and chronic oral rat findings mainly involving feed consumption/weight effects.
- A 1998 mouse model study reported sodium malate reduced cisplatin-associated organ toxicity in that therapeutic context (not a food-additive exposure model).

**Sources:**
- PubMed PMID 11358110 (2001 review): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11358110/?format=pubmed
- PubMed PMID 9477166 (1998 mouse study): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9477166/?format=pubmed

### Human/clinical and modern reassessment signals
- A 2022 amended safety assessment (cosmetics context) concluded malic acid and sodium malate are safe under then-current cosmetic use concentrations.
- Direct modern human dietary epidemiology specifically isolating E350 sodium malates was not identified in this PubMed search run.

**Source:**
- PubMed PMID 35993419 (2022): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35993419/?format=pubmed

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis

**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - JECFA ADI status for E350(i)/(ii) is "NOT SPECIFIED".
  - Malate is a normal endogenous metabolite.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - JECFA group caveat for D(-)-malic acid salts in very young infants.
  - EFSA food-additive re-evaluation is still active (data call phase in retrieved EFSA source), so a contemporary EFSA conclusion is pending.

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - U.S. GRAS framework support via 21 CFR Part 184 and §184.1069 conditions.
  - JECFA ADI not specified for both E350 components in retrieved records.
  - No strong carcinogenicity signal from IARC classification list.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - Sparse recent dietary human safety studies specifically on E350.
  - Infant-specific caveat in JECFA group context for D(-) forms.

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Stereochemistry/infant caveat suggests non-uniform certainty for all populations.
  - Ongoing EFSA data collection indicates unresolved data needs.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No major regulator in retrieved sources has withdrawn the additive.
  - No IARC 2A/2B/1 classification identified.

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - None identified in retrieved primary sources.
- Evidence arguing against this tier:
  - No major-jurisdiction bans identified.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification identified.
  - JECFA ADI status is not restrictive in the usual numerical sense.

### 2) Rationale
The weight of evidence supports **limited** risk rather than risk_free because strong regulatory permissibility signals (JECFA ADI not specified; U.S. GRAS framework conditions) are tempered by two uncertainties: (1) infant-related stereoisomer caveat in JECFA group text and (2) pending EFSA re-evaluation output.

### 3) Recommended tier
**limited**
