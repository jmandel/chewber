# E553b (Talc) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E553b (INS 553iii).
- **Name:** Talc.
- **CAS number(s):** 14807-96-6.
- **Chemical class:** Naturally occurring hydrous magnesium silicate (layered silicate mineral/phyllosilicate).
- **Common synonyms:** Talcum, talcum powder, magnesium silicate, INS 553iii.
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Natural** (mined mineral, then processed/purified to additive specifications).

Evidence: EFSA re-evaluation text and summary metadata describe E553b as talc and define it as a naturally occurring hydrous magnesium silicate; JECFA lists CAS 14807-96-6 and INS 553iii.

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Anti-caking/flow aid via moisture adsorption and particle-separation effects in powders.
- **Common food categories/functions:** Anti-caking agent and related functions (bulking/glazing/filtering aid/chewing gum base compound) in additive frameworks; in U.S. food CFR text, magnesium silicate is GRAS for use in table salt.

Evidence: JECFA functional classes for talc include anticaking agent, bulking agent, glazing agent, filtering aid, chewing gum base compound, and thickener; 21 CFR 182.2437 covers magnesium silicate in table salt.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest EFSA re-evaluation:** 2018 (EFSA Journal DOI: 10.2903/j.efsa.2018.5375).
  - **ADI outcome:** No numerical ADI derived; EFSA states the old SCF group ADI "not specified" is obsolete.
  - **Key conclusion:** EFSA concluded safety of E552/E553a/E553b as food additives **cannot be assessed** because reliable subchronic/chronic/carcinogenicity/reproductive datasets were lacking.
  - **Approval status context:** E553b remains listed as an authorized EU additive in the consolidated Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 tables (E 553b = Talc).
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **Status:** GRAS context in food via magnesium silicate.
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 182.2437 (Magnesium silicate)**.
  - **Conditions of use:** Tolerance 2%; generally recognized as safe when used in **table salt** in accordance with GMP.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **ADI:** NOT SPECIFIED.
  - **Last evaluation year:** 1986 (Meeting 49 shown in WHO/JECFA database entry).
- **IARC:**
  - **Classification:** **Group 2A (probably carcinogenic to humans)** for talc in current IARC Monographs classification data (updated entry shows evaluation year 2024; volume 136 publication context 2025 online).
  - IARC press material states the 2A decision was based on limited human evidence (ovarian cancer), sufficient animal evidence, and strong mechanistic evidence.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major jurisdiction-wide ban on food-additive talc identified in the sources reviewed.

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA hazard dataset (oral food additive context):**
  - Very low absorption reported for silicates/talc.
  - No indication for genotoxicity or developmental toxicity in the available dataset.
  - But reliable subchronic/chronic/carcinogenicity/reproductive datasets were insufficient, leading EFSA to a non-confirmatory conclusion (safety cannot be assessed).
- **IARC carcinogenicity hazard classification (broad talc evidence base):**
  - Group 2A classification for talc.
  - Human evidence in IARC materials centers on ovarian cancer associations, with caveats (including historical contamination/bias concerns).
- **Recent PubMed evidence (2024-2026):**
  - 2024 Lancet Oncology summary article reports IARC Working Group conclusions for talc/acrylonitrile.
  - 2024 J Clin Oncol quantitative bias analysis reported that corrected estimates still supported a positive association between genital talc use and ovarian cancer.
  - 2024 reviews reached mixed conclusions, including distinction between occupational asbestos-free talc findings and perineal talc-use ovarian cancer signals.
  - 2025 commentary challenged parts of the animal-weighting logic used in recent carcinogenicity interpretation.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Dietary intake context from EFSA re-evaluation:**
  - EFSA notes that, in the food-supplement scenario (used as representative for risk characterization), estimated silicate exposure was below the 4 g/person/day magnesium trisilicate antacid benchmark.
  - EFSA also reported that submitted use-level data for the silicate group were predominantly for talc (E553b), so exposure calculations chiefly reflected talc.
- **ADI exceedance framing:**
  - A classic ADI exceedance calculation is not possible because no current numerical EFSA/JECFA ADI is set for talc as such (EFSA could not derive one; JECFA lists NOT SPECIFIED).
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - High users of relevant additive-containing products and users in non-dietary talc exposure settings (e.g., perineal cosmetic use), noting that the strongest carcinogenic signals in humans are not from routine dietary oral studies.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: Very low oral absorption; no clear genotoxic/developmental signal in EFSA-reviewed data; long-standing historical use.
  - Evidence against: EFSA explicitly could not confirm safety due missing core toxicology packages; IARC now classifies talc as Group 2A.
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: U.S. CFR includes GRAS use condition for magnesium silicate in table salt; JECFA ADI not specified; no broad food-use bans identified.
  - Evidence against: EFSA non-confirmatory conclusion and IARC Group 2A go beyond a "minor concerns only" profile.
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved/used with important caveats; hazard evidence is mixed and route-specific; dietary oral risk quantification remains uncertain.
  - Evidence against: EFSA inability to assess safety and IARC 2A classification together may justify a stricter tier than moderate under this framework.
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA concluded safety cannot be assessed (explicit high-tier trigger in this framework); IARC Group 2A classification adds material carcinogenicity concern.
  - Evidence against: No major jurisdiction-wide food ban and substantial uncertainty about direct translation of non-dietary exposure findings to ordinary dietary oral exposures.

2. **Rationale**
- The decision is driven mainly by two factors: (a) EFSA could not establish safety for E553b due data gaps, and (b) IARC upgraded talc to Group 2A. Countervailing evidence (low oral absorption, no clear genotoxicity/developmental toxicity signal, no broad ban) reduces certainty but does not fully offset those primary signals.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **high**
