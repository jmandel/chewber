# E150d (Sulphite ammonia caramel) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E150d
- **Name:** Sulphite ammonia caramel (Caramel colour, Class IV)
- **CAS number:** 8028-89-5 (JECFA Chemical 1668)
- **Chemical class:** Processed caramel colour (Class IV caramel colour; complex mixture from carbohydrate heat treatment with ammonium- and sulfite-containing compounds)
- **Common synonyms:** Sulfite ammonia caramel; ammonia sulfite process caramel; sulfite ammonia process caramel; beverage caramel; soft-drink caramel; acid-proof caramel
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic/process-derived** food additive (manufactured under controlled processing conditions)

**Evidence used:**
- WHO/JECFA database entry for Chemical 1668 (synonym, CAS, INS match 150d, class)
- JECFA monograph (WHO FAS 20) for Class IV manufacturing/synonyms

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Brown colourant for visual standardization and product identity.
- **Mechanism:** Produced via controlled caramelization/Maillard-type processing of carbohydrates with ammonium/sulfite reagents, generating high-molecular-weight coloured compounds that absorb visible light.
- **Common food categories:** Non-alcoholic flavoured beverages (including cola-type drinks), confectionery, soups/seasonings, beer, and other processed foods where brown colour is desired.

**Evidence used:**
- EFSA press summary of caramel colour uses in foods
- FDA dietary exposure work identifying major contributing categories for caramel-colour-associated 4-MEI exposure (e.g., cola beverages in earlier assessment; later shifts with reformulation)

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/re-evaluated.
- **Core re-evaluation:** EFSA Journal output **ON-2004** (publication date 8 March 2011) linked to question **EFSA-Q-2008-240** (E150d).
- **ADI:** EFSA ANS Panel established a **group ADI of 300 mg/kg bw/day** for caramel colours E150a,b,c,d (with a separate lower individual ADI for E150c).
- **Follow-up (latest EFSA item found for this group):** EFSA statement **ON-3030** (2012 refined exposure for E150a,c,d) reported lower exposure estimates than 2011 and that combined exposure to the four caramel colours did not exceed the group ADI.
- **Key conclusion:** EFSA maintained a broad approval posture with ADI-based management; main residual concern was not E150d-specific prohibition, but uncertainty and exposure management for specific caramel classes/constituents.

### FDA / U.S. CFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status:** `21 CFR 182.1235` lists **Caramel** as generally recognized as safe when used according to GMP.
- **Color-additive status/conditions of use:** `21 CFR 73.85` (Caramel) allows use for colouring foods generally under GMP, with identity provisions that include ammonium and sulfite process reagents; additive is exempt from batch certification.
- **Practical interpretation:** U.S. framework supports use under GMP via both GRAS listing for caramel and specific colour-additive provisions.

### JECFA / WHO
- **Entry:** Caramel Colour, Class IV (INS 150d).
- **ADI:** **0-200 mg/kg bw** (comment notes **0-150 mg/kg bw on solids basis**).
- **Evaluation year shown:** 1985 (with later listed updates/compendium references).

### IARC
- **E150d itself:** No direct IARC listing found for “sulphite ammonia caramel” / “E150d”.
- **Related constituent:** **4-Methylimidazole (4-MEI)** is IARC **Group 2B** (possibly carcinogenic to humans).
- **Related class entry:** “Sulfites” appears as Group 3.

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban on E150d identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC materials.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- Historical long-term animal studies of Caramel Colour IV reported no carcinogenic signal at very high tested doses (PMID: 1644385).
- More recent work on **4-MEI** (a process-related constituent associated with ammonia-process caramel colours) shows adverse reproductive/developmental findings in rats at high dietary doses (LOAEL around 50-60 mg/kg bw/day in that study context; PMID: 32229253).

### Genotoxicity/carcinogenicity evidence
- A 2020 weight-of-evidence assessment concluded no clear in vivo genotoxic signal for 4-MEI and did not support a genotoxic mode of action for mouse lung tumours (PMID: 32745573).
- IARC classifies 4-MEI as Group 2B; this is a cautionary carcinogenicity signal relevant to risk management of class III/IV caramel contaminants.

### Epidemiological data
- Additive-specific human epidemiology for E150d is limited.
- Recent cohort analyses often evaluate additive mixtures rather than isolated E150d effects, limiting causal attribution to this single additive.

### Mechanistic concerns
- Regulatory/toxicology focus is mostly on process contaminants/by-products (e.g., 4-MEI, THI) rather than the colour matrix alone.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2011 screening indicated potential ADI exceedance concerns for some caramel classes/populations under then-available use data.
- EFSA 2012 refined assessment (new use-level data) reported lower estimates; combined exposure to E150a,b,c,d did not exceed the group ADI in that review.
- FDA 2018 U.S. assessment identified added caramel-colour foods as a larger 4-MEI source than thermal formation-only foods for most groups.
- A 2026 U.S. update reported lower estimated added-source exposure than earlier FDA-era estimates, consistent with reformulation/specification tightening.

### ADI exceedance risk
- **Group caramel ADI (EFSA 300 mg/kg bw/day):** Not exceeded in the refined combined EFSA assessment.
- **Population caveat:** High-consuming subgroups (especially toddlers in older scenarios, and E150c-specific high consumers in refined EFSA analysis) are the key sensitivity groups in the regulatory narrative.

## Risk Assessment
### 1) Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Broad regulatory acceptance (EFSA/FDA/JECFA) and high numerical ADIs relative to typical intakes in modern exposure assessments.
- **Evidence against this tier:** IARC Group 2B classification for related constituent 4-MEI; historical/constituent-level toxicology signals mean this is not truly "no credible harm evidence." 

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Approved in major jurisdictions, ADIs established, refined exposure assessments generally below group ADI, and no major ban identified.
- **Evidence against this tier:** Evidence is not uniformly clean because concern centers on process contaminants (4-MEI/THI) and subgroup/high-end exposure scenarios.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Regulatory approval with caveats; historical EFSA concern about possible exceedance in some groups before refinement; IARC 2B for 4-MEI; high-dose animal findings for related constituent 4-MEI.
- **Evidence against this tier:** Most current regulatory exposure assessments do not indicate broad population exceedance of the group ADI for caramel colours.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** Presence of a carcinogenicity caution signal for a related process contaminant (4-MEI).
- **Evidence against this tier:** No major jurisdiction-wide ban of E150d, no direct IARC Group 1/2A classification for E150d itself, and regulators continue to permit use with ADI/GMP controls.

### 2) Rationale
The additive remains permitted and managed through ADI/GMP frameworks, but it is not best characterized as risk-free because the safety conversation is driven by process-related constituents (notably 4-MEI), historical high-end exposure scenarios, and uncertainty management rather than an unequivocal absence of concern.

### 3) Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
