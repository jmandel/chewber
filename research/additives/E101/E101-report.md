# E101 Research Report — Riboflavin (Vitamin B2)

## Identity
- **E-number:** E101
- **Name:** Riboflavin (E101(i)); Riboflavin-5'-phosphate sodium / riboflavin sodium phosphate (E101(ii)) [S1, S2]
- **CAS number(s):**
  - Riboflavin: **83-88-5** [S12]
  - Riboflavin-5'-phosphate sodium: **130-40-5** (also hydrate form listed as 6184-17-4) [S13]
- **Chemical class:** Flavin (isoalloxazine) vitamin; water-soluble B-vitamin derivative [S11, S12]
- **Common synonyms:** Vitamin B2, lactoflavin, riboflavin sodium phosphate, flavin mononucleotide sodium salt [S12, S13]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Riboflavin is naturally present in foods, while food-additive/fortificant material is industrially produced (typically synthetic/biotechnological production of the same molecule) [S11, S12, S13].

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Food colour (yellow; color additive) and vitamin fortification [S2, S4, S5, S11].
- **Mechanism of action:** Riboflavin is a yellow chromophore/fluorophore; nutritionally it is a precursor of FMN and FAD coenzymes in cellular redox/energy metabolism [S11].
- **Common food categories:**
  - EU: EFSA source indicates authorisation for broad food categories (quantum satis context in re-evaluation) [S2].
  - US: permitted in food under GRAS use conditions and as an exempt color additive in foods generally [S4, S5].
  - Major dietary contributors in US populations include milk/milk drinks, breads, and ready-to-eat cereals (reflecting both natural and fortified presence) [S11].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (EFSA food colours page shows authorisation validity and E101 entries) [S1].
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation year found:** **2013** [S2].
- **ADI:** EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for E101(i)/(ii) at reported uses/levels [S2].
- **Key conclusion:** “not of a safety concern at the currently reported uses and use levels” (from EFSA opinion abstract as mirrored in Europe PMC) [S2].

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** Riboflavin is affirmed as **generally recognized as safe (GRAS)** at 21 CFR **184.1695** [S4].
- **Conditions of use:** Must conform to specifications and be used in food as specified in 21 CFR 184.1(b)(1), i.e., consistent with current good manufacturing practice [S4].
- **Related color regulation:** 21 CFR **73.450** lists riboflavin as a color additive exempt from certification for use in foods generally, consistent with GMP [S5].
- **Access note:** Direct eCFR pages were requested but blocked in this environment; alternative CFR text mirrors were used (Cornell LII / GovInfo search results) [S3, S4, S5].

### JECFA / WHO-FAO
- **ADI:** **Not limited** [S6].
- **Last evaluation year found:** **1969** (13th meeting) on FAO/JECFA additive record; toxicological summary also available in WHO/INCHEM record [S6, S7].

### IARC
- **Classification:** No riboflavin entry found on IARC Monographs “Agents classified by the IARC” page; treated here as **no listed IARC group classification** [S8].

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdictional ban identified in EU/US/JECFA sources reviewed [S1, S4, S6].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- A recent rat study (PMID 41539788, 2026) reported metabolic and brain mitochondrial ultrastructure changes under **high-dose neonatal riboflavin (100 mg/kg)** in a maternal high-fat-diet model; findings were largely framed as metabolic improvement, but dose/context are pharmacological and not typical food-additive exposure [S10].

### Epidemiological / human evidence
- NIH ODS summary indicates no observable toxicity from intakes many times the RDA; no UL established due lack of reported adverse effects at high supplemental intakes in available data (with caution that limited evidence is not proof of zero risk) [S11].
- NIH ODS also summarizes large observational cancer analyses as mixed/inconclusive rather than showing a consistent harmful signal [S11].

### Recent clinical safety signal (PubMed)
- A 2024 pediatric migraine network meta-analysis (PMID 39388181) reported no significant differences in adverse events across included preventive interventions; riboflavin was among interventions in the network [S9].

### Mechanistic concerns
- Riboflavin is light-sensitive and photoreactive; this is mainly a stability/handling issue and can affect vitamin availability, not a strong signal of additive toxicity at normal dietary exposure [S11].

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA additive exposure estimate (re-evaluation abstract):** refined estimates up to **0.4 mg/kg bw/day (mean)** and **1.3 mg/kg bw/day (high level)** in toddlers/children for E101(i)/(ii) [S2].
- **General population intake context (US, total diet/supplements):** adults reported average riboflavin intake around 2.5 mg/day (men) and 1.8 mg/day (women) from foods; higher when supplements are included [S11].
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Numerical ADI not set by EFSA and ADI “not limited” by JECFA; current evidence does not indicate routine exceedance concern for normal dietary exposure [S2, S6].
- **Potentially relevant populations:** toddlers/children are highest estimated consumers in EFSA exposure modelling; deficiency-risk populations (not toxicity-risk groups) include people with low intake or specific clinical conditions [S2, S11].

## Risk Assessment
- **Recommended Chewber tier:** **risk_free**
- **Rationale:**
  - Naturally occurring vitamin used as additive/fortificant [S11]
  - EFSA 2013: no numerical ADI needed and no safety concern at reported uses [S2]
  - JECFA: ADI not limited [S6]
  - US CFR: GRAS affirmation and color-additive permission under GMP conditions [S4, S5]
  - No IARC listing found [S8]
- **Residual uncertainty:** very high-dose pharmacological contexts (e.g., animal neonatal high-dose models) are not fully representative of food-additive exposure [S10].
