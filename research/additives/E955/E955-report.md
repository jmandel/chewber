# E955 (Sucralose) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E955 [S1]
- **Name:** Sucralose [S1][S13]
- **CAS number(s):** 56038-13-2 [S4][S13]
- **Chemical class:** Chlorinated disaccharide, high-intensity non-nutritive sweetener [S4][S5]
- **Common synonyms:** Trichlorogalactosucrose, Trichlorosucrose, INS 955, Splenda (brand-associated common name) [S13]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic (produced by chlorination of sucrose) [S5]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Provides high sweetness intensity with negligible calories; FDA notes sucralose is made by replacing three hydroxyl groups on sucrose with chlorine atoms, which prevents caloric metabolism like sugar. [S5]
- **Common food categories:** General sweetener use in foods under current good manufacturing practice (GMP), including beverages, dairy products, baked goods, desserts, confectionery, and tabletop applications. [S4][S5]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest scientific re-evaluation found:** EFSA FAF Panel re-evaluation published in **2026** (EFSA Journal `e9854`). [S1][S2]
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorised food additive in the EU; EFSA concluded sucralose remains safe at assessed exposure levels. [S1][S2]
- **ADI:** **15 mg/kg bw/day** (retained). [S2]
- **Key conclusions (2026):**
  - No concern regarding genotoxicity.
  - No concern regarding carcinogenic potential.
  - No indication of concern regarding neurodevelopmental, glucose-homeostasis, or gut-microbiome effects at assessed exposure levels.
  - Existing ADI of 15 mg/kg bw/day was retained. [S2]

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **GRAS status:** **Not GRAS**; FDA identifies sucralose among high-intensity sweeteners approved as food additives, while only stevia extracts and monk fruit extracts are described as GRAS. [S5]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 172.831** (`§ 172.831 Sucralose`). [S4]
- **Conditions of use (from CFR text):**
  - May be used as a sweetener in foods generally under GMP in amounts not exceeding what is reasonably required.
  - Identity is defined chemically (with CAS 56038-13-2).
  - Foods represented for special dietary use must comply with labeling requirements in 21 CFR part 105. [S4]
- **FDA ADI context:** FDA lists ADI as **5 mg/kg bw/day** and reports estimated intake among consumers aged 2+ at about 1.6 mg/kg bw/day. [S5]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI:** **0-15 mg/kg bw/day**. [S6]
- **Last evaluation year:** **1990** (JECFA database entry year; Meeting 35). [S6]

### IARC
- **Classification:** No sucralose-specific IARC Monographs group classification identified in current IARC classification listings reviewed (no listing found for “sucralose”). [S7][S8]

### Notable bans
- No major current jurisdiction-wide bans were identified in the reviewed EFSA/FDA/JECFA/IARC sources. [S1][S4][S5][S6][S7]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology conclusions
- EFSA 2026 concluded no concern for genotoxicity or carcinogenicity and retained the ADI at 15 mg/kg bw/day. [S2]

### Human studies (PubMed)
- **2022 randomized trial (healthy adults):** Ten-week sucralose consumption in healthy young adults reported gut dysbiosis and altered glucose/insulin-related parameters. [S9]
- **2022 (Cell):** Personalized, microbiome-mediated glycemic-response changes were reported after short-term exposure to some non-nutritive sweeteners, including sucralose, in subsets of participants. [S10]
- **2025 triple-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial:** Reported that sucralose consumption modified glucose homeostasis and gut microbiota-related outcomes in healthy individuals. [S11]

### Mechanistic studies
- **2023 in vitro study:** Reported potential genotoxicity concerns for sucralose-6-acetate (an impurity/metabolite associated with sucralose), supporting continued scrutiny of impurity profiles and exposure relevance. [S12]
- **2023 mouse immunology study:** Reported that sucralose can modulate T-cell-mediated responses at sufficiently high exposure levels in animal models. [S14]

### Interpretation
- The evidence base includes reassuring regulatory evaluations and mixed mechanistic findings from newer studies. Current major regulators have not reduced sucralose ADI based on the newer literature reviewed here. [S2][S5][S6][S9][S10][S11][S12]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (non-brand-loyal + brand-loyal scenarios):**
  - Mean chronic exposure range: **0.1 to 5.0 mg/kg bw/day**.
  - 95th percentile range: **0.3 to 10.7 mg/kg bw/day**.
  - Highest exposures were in toddlers. [S2]
- **EFSA ADI exceedance statement:** In refined scenarios, ADI exceedance was limited and observed in toddlers in only some countries under brand-loyal assumptions; in a conservative maximum-permitted-level scenario, P95 exposure could exceed ADI substantially (reported up to 50.3 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers and 44.5 mg/kg bw/day in adolescents). [S2]
- **FDA context:** Estimated intake in US consumers (2+ years) is below FDA ADI on FDA’s summary page. [S5]
- **Vulnerable populations:** High-consuming toddlers/children in brand-loyal or high-concentration consumption patterns are the most relevant subgroup for potential ADI exceedance. [S2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting placement:** EU/US/JECFA approvals remain in place; EFSA found no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern and retained ADI. [S2][S4][S5][S6]
- **Evidence arguing against placement:** A numeric ADI is required (not “ADI not specified”); EFSA identified exceedance potential in some high-end toddler scenarios; newer mechanistic studies raise unresolved questions. [S2][S10][S11][S12][S14]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting placement:** Sucralose is broadly approved with defined ADIs and conditions of use; no major bans identified; core refined exposure estimates are generally below ADI. [S2][S4][S5][S6]
- **Evidence arguing against placement:** EFSA reported ADI exceedance in specific high-end child scenarios; experimental and clinical literature includes biologically plausible metabolic/microbiome and impurity-related signals requiring monitoring. [S2][S9][S10][S11][S12]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting placement:** The additive is approved but with caveat-relevant signals: documented ADI exceedance risk in some child scenarios and credible mechanistic evidence from recent studies. This matches the “approved but with caveats” definition. [S2][S9][S10][S11][S12][S14]
- **Evidence arguing against placement:** Major regulators have not reduced ADI or restricted sucralose based on current evidence; EFSA’s latest full re-evaluation remains overall reassuring. [S1][S2][S5]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting placement:** No strong evidence meeting “high” criteria (no IARC 2A/2B/1 listing for sucralose, no major-jurisdiction ban, no EFSA conclusion of inability to confirm safety). [S2][S7][S8]
- **Evidence arguing against placement:** Regulatory approvals and ADI-based frameworks remain active across EFSA/FDA/JECFA. [S2][S4][S5][S6]

### 2. Rationale
Sucralose remains authorized by major regulators with maintained ADIs, and EFSA’s 2026 re-evaluation was broadly reassuring. However, EFSA identified high-end ADI exceedance potential in toddlers under certain scenarios, and recent mechanistic/human studies provide credible but not definitive concern signals. This supports a caveated middle-tier position rather than a no-concern profile.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
