# E410 (Locust bean gum) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E410 (INS 410) [S4]
- **Primary name:** Locust bean gum [S4]
- **CAS number(s):** 9000-40-2 [S4]
- **Chemical class:** Plant-derived **galactomannan polysaccharide** (carob seed endosperm gum); EFSA describes it as derived from the ground endosperm of *Ceratonia siliqua* seeds [S1][S2]
- **Common synonyms:** Carob gum; Carob bean gum (clarified) [S4]
- **Origin:** Natural (from carob tree seeds) [S1][S2]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Hydrocolloid that increases viscosity and stabilizes dispersed systems; practically undigested and fermented by enteric bacteria [S1][S2].
- **Regulatory functional classes:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener [S4][S5].
- **Common food categories:** Broad EU use; major exposure contributors in EFSA assessments include infant/young-child foods and bread/rolls [S2]. It is also used in anti-regurgitation infant formula/FSMP products [S2][S4].

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Authorized food additive in the EU [S1][S2].
- **Latest EFSA scientific work:** 2023 follow-up re-evaluation for infants below 16 weeks and FSMP uses [S2].
- **ADI:**
  - 2017 EFSA re-evaluation: **no need for a numerical ADI** for the general population; no safety concern at refined exposure for reported uses [S1][S2].
  - 2023 follow-up: EFSA used a **reference point (RP) of 1,400 mg/kg bw/day** (piglet zinc endpoint) and an MoE approach rather than setting a numeric ADI [S2].
- **Key conclusions:**
  - General population: no numerical ADI needed, no safety concern at refined exposure [S1][S2].
  - Infant FSMP scenarios: MoE > 1 for some scenarios, but MoE < 1 under maximum-level/high-consumption scenarios, indicating scenario-dependent concern [S2].

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Status:** Listed in **21 CFR 184.1343** within Part 184 (direct food substances affirmed as GRAS), supporting GRAS status [S5].
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 184.1343):**
  - Direct human food ingredient.
  - May be used in food with no limitation other than current good manufacturing practice.
  - Used as emulsifier, stabilizer, and thickener.
  - Use level shall not exceed amount reasonably required for intended effect; ingredient must be suitable purity/quality [S5].

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** **Not specified** [S4].
- **Latest evaluation year in database:** **2025** [S4].
- **2025 infant-formula note:** JECFA reports low risk at 1,000 and 6,000 mg/L in infant formula but potential risk at 10,000 mg/L (MoE < 1); also states no concern for intestinal microbiota disruption based on available data [S4].

### IARC
- No IARC Group 1/2A/2B/3 classification entry for locust bean gum/carob bean gum was identified in IARC Monographs classification pages checked [S6][S7].

### Notable bans
- No major-jurisdiction ban identified from the reviewed EU/US/JECFA/IARC sources [S1][S2][S4][S5][S6][S7].

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- EFSA 2017 reports no adverse effects in 90-day and carcinogenicity rodent studies at highest tested doses, and no genotoxicity or reproductive/developmental concern [S1][S2].
- EFSA 2023 derived RP 1,400 mg/kg bw/day from reduced blood zinc in neonatal piglets; this drove infant FSMP MoE-based risk conclusions [S2].

### Human/clinical and observational evidence
- 2024 multicenter RCT in infants with regurgitation reported LBG-containing AR formula was well tolerated/safe and reduced regurgitation frequency [S8].
- 2023 real-world infant study reported no new safety concerns over 3 months, with stool consistency/frequency remaining physiologic [S9].
- 2025 case report suggests potential interaction with levothyroxine absorption in congenital hypothyroidism after switching to carob-bean-gum-thickened formula (single-patient evidence) [S10].

### Mechanistic concerns highlighted by regulators
- Potential GI intolerance in medically vulnerable infants/young children and uncertainty for special medical-purpose categories were emphasized by EFSA [S1][S2].
- JECFA 2025 similarly flags potential risk at highest infant-formula concentration (10,000 mg/L) [S4].

## Exposure Assessment
- **General population (EFSA 2017 refined scenarios):**
  - Brand-loyal mean: 35.7-368.9 mg/kg bw/day; p95: 74-765.2 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Non-brand-loyal mean: 20.7-204.3 mg/kg bw/day; p95: 38.8-415.4 mg/kg bw/day [S2].
- **Infants/toddlers consuming FSMP (EFSA 2017):**
  - Infants mean 179-553; p95 435-1,555 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Toddlers mean 135-245; p95 262-578 mg/kg bw/day [S2].
- **Infants <16 weeks (EFSA 2023):**
  - Exposure estimates up to 2,000 (mean) and 2,600 mg/kg bw/day (high) under maximum-level scenario; lower under mean reported use levels [S2].
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - No numerical ADI exists (EFSA/JECFA), so classic ADI exceedance is not directly applicable.
  - Under EFSA/JECFA infant high-use scenarios, MoE can drop below 1, indicating risk in vulnerable infant subgroups at highest concentrations [S2][S4].
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants below 16 weeks consuming FSMP formulae, infants/young children on special medical-purpose diets, and infants where nutrient/drug absorption issues matter clinically [S2][S4][S10].

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
**risk_free**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Natural food gum, not systemically absorbed intact.
  - EFSA (general population) and JECFA report no need for numerical ADI / ADI not specified [S1][S2][S4].
- Evidence against this tier:
  - EFSA/JECFA identified infant high-exposure scenarios where MoE can be <1.
  - EFSA 2017 said available data were inadequate for some infant special-medical-purpose uses [S1][S2][S4].

**limited**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Broad regulatory acceptance (EU authorized, US GRAS, JECFA ADI not specified).
  - Recent infant studies generally report good tolerance and no major new safety signals [S4][S5][S8][S9].
- Evidence against this tier:
  - Regulator-identified caveats are stronger than “minor high-dose concerns,” especially in medically vulnerable infants and maximum-use scenarios [S2][S4].

**moderate**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Approved additive but with clear caveats in specific populations.
  - EFSA/JECFA use MoE-based caution for infant FSMP/high-level use; potential risk at top concentrations.
  - Mechanistic concern (zinc reduction at high exposure in piglets) and specific clinical vulnerabilities (GI effects; possible drug absorption interaction case report) [S2][S4][S10].
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No broad population signal of serious toxicity/carcinogenicity/genotoxicity [S1][S2].

**high**
- Evidence supporting this tier:
  - Presence of scenario-specific infant concern at high concentrations [S2][S4].
- Evidence against this tier:
  - No major-jurisdiction bans identified.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found.
  - General-population regulatory evaluations remain permissive [S1][S2][S4][S5][S6][S7].

### 2. Rationale
The additive has strong general-population regulatory acceptance and no convincing broad toxicology signal, but multiple authoritative assessments now identify **non-negligible risk boundaries in infant special-medical-purpose contexts at higher exposure levels**. This keeps it above “limited” but below “high.”

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
