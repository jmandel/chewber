# E515 (Potassium sulfates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E515 (E515(i) and E515(ii))
- **Primary names:**
  - **E515(i):** Potassium sulfate (K2SO4)
  - **E515(ii):** Potassium hydrogen sulfate / potassium bisulfate (KHSO4)
- **CAS numbers:**
  - **7778-80-5** (potassium sulfate)
  - **7646-93-7** (potassium bisulfate)
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sulfate salts (potassium salts of sulfuric acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Potassium sulfate, dipotassium sulfate, sulfate of potash, potassium bisulfate, potassium hydrogen sulfate
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Occurs naturally (e.g., sulfate minerals), but food additive-grade potassium sulfate is commonly manufactured (e.g., neutralization of sulfuric acid with potassium hydroxide or potassium carbonate in 21 CFR text).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Dissociates to potassium and sulfate ions (and hydrogen ion for E515(ii)), contributing to ionic strength, taste profile (saline/bitter), and acidification where relevant.
- **Regulatory functional classes identified:**
  - **FDA (21 CFR 184.1643):** flavoring agent and adjuvant
  - **JECFA (INS 515):** flavour enhancer; salt substitute
- **Common food categories:** EFSA exposure modeling for the sulfate group identified major contributions from bread and rolls, fine bakery wares, and (in conservative scenarios) meat products; FDA section 184.1643 also states current GMP gives a maximum as-served level of 0.015% in nonalcoholic beverages.

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - **Latest re-evaluation:** EFSA Journal opinion 2019 (DOI: 10.2903/j.efsa.2019.5868)
  - **Status:** Authorized food additive in EU framework (Annex II/III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008, as stated in EFSA abstract/full text)
  - **ADI:** **No numerical ADI** (effectively ADI not specified)
  - **Key conclusion:** Exposure to sulfuric acid and sodium/potassium/calcium/ammonium sulfates, including E515, does not raise a safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - **GRAS status:** Yes, under Part 184 framework for direct food substances affirmed as GRAS
  - **Citation:** **21 CFR 184.1643 (Potassium sulfate)**
  - **Conditions of use:** Used as flavoring agent and adjuvant; used at levels not exceeding GMP; current GMP yields maximum as served of 0.015% for nonalcoholic beverages.
- **JECFA/WHO:**
  - **Substance record:** POTASSIUM SULFATE (INS 515)
  - **ADI:** **NOT SPECIFIED**
  - **Last evaluation year shown in WHO JECFA database:** **1985** (Meeting 29)
- **IARC:**
  - No specific IARC Group classification identified for potassium sulfate in IARC Monographs list-of-classifications resources checked.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No notable bans identified in major jurisdictions reviewed (EU/US/JECFA contexts).

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA toxicology synthesis (group-level, includes E515):**
  - Sulfates considered of **low acute oral toxicity**.
  - EFSA reported **no concern for genotoxicity and carcinogenicity**.
  - Subchronic NOAEL: **2,025 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** (highest dose tested; diarrhea observed at this dose but not considered adverse by EFSA).
  - Chronic/carcinogenicity NOAEL (ammonium sulfate dataset used in group assessment): **1,090 mg sulfate/kg bw/day**, with no carcinogenicity conclusion.
  - Data gap noted: no reproductive/developmental studies according to current OECD guideline expectations.
- **Human-effect threshold context:**
  - EFSA cites human data where sodium sulfate at **300 mg/kg bw** (about **207 mg sulfate/kg bw**) induced laxative effect; EFSA judged additive exposure estimates to be below this under refined scenarios.
- **Recent PubMed evidence directly relevant to potassium sulfate exposure:**
  - 2025 RCT on oral sulfate solution (including potassium sulfate, magnesium sulfate, sodium sulfate) for colonoscopy prep reported improved cleansing and **no severe adverse events**; this reflects high short-term medicinal dosing rather than routine food additive exposure.
- **Epidemiological evidence specific to dietary E515:**
  - No robust human epidemiological studies directly linking routine E515 dietary intake to adverse outcomes were identified in targeted PubMed searches.

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined non-brand-loyal scenario (most realistic long-term scenario):**
  - Mean: **0.4 to 35 mg sulfate/kg bw/day**
  - 95th percentile: **3 to 68 mg sulfate/kg bw/day**
- **EFSA refined brand-loyal scenario:**
  - 95th percentile up to **129 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** (children)
- **EFSA maximum-level conservative scenario:**
  - 95th percentile up to **217 mg sulfate/kg bw/day** (children), characterized by EFSA as conservative/likely overestimating typical long-term exposure.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - No numerical ADI exists (EFSA/JECFA), so classical ADI exceedance is not calculated.
  - Refined long-term exposure estimates are below the human laxative-effect threshold cited by EFSA.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations:**
  - Toddlers/children (highest modeled intake)
  - Individuals sensitive to osmotic GI effects at high sulfate bolus intakes

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - **Supports placement:** EFSA and JECFA do not set a numerical ADI; EFSA concludes no safety concern at reported uses; sulfate is a normal biological constituent.
  - **Argues against placement:** Clear high-dose laxative effect exists; EFSA noted gaps in modern reproductive/developmental datasets; impurity-limit tightening was recommended.
- **limited**
  - **Supports placement:** EFSA-approved status with no numerical ADI, JECFA ADI not specified, FDA GRAS codification in 21 CFR 184.1643, and refined exposures below effect thresholds.
  - **Argues against placement:** Evidence base for additive-specific modern human studies is limited; some uncertainty remains around conservative high-end exposure assumptions.
- **moderate**
  - **Supports placement:** Conservative modeled high-percentile exposure can approach/overlap laxative-threshold range; high-dose sulfate exposures are biologically active (osmotic diarrhea mechanism).
  - **Argues against placement:** Those higher estimates are conservative; EFSA explicitly considered refined estimates more realistic and still concluded no safety concern.
- **high**
  - **Supports placement:** Minimal direct support found.
  - **Argues against placement:** No major-jurisdiction ban identified, no IARC carcinogenic classification found for potassium sulfate, and EFSA did not report inability to establish safety at reported uses.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence comes from EFSA’s 2019 re-evaluation, which specifically includes E515(i)/(ii), reports low toxicological concern at reported uses, and concludes no need for a numerical ADI. JECFA’s ADI-not-specified conclusion and FDA’s GRAS codification with GMP limits are directionally consistent. Residual uncertainty is mainly about high-dose GI effects and standard evidence gaps rather than a clear hazard signal at typical additive exposure.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `limited`**
