# E124 (Ponceau 4R) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number / INS**: E124 / INS 124. [S8]
- **Primary name**: Ponceau 4R. [S8]
- **CAS number(s)**: 2611-82-7. [S8, S18]
- **Chemical class**: Synthetic **azo dye** (sulfonated azo colorant; JECFA chemical name includes an azo linkage). [S8]
- **Common synonyms**: Cochineal Red A, New Coccine, C.I. Food Red 7, Acid Red 18, Food Red No. 102. [S8, S18]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Synthetic**. [S8, S18]

## Function in Food
Ponceau 4R is used as a **color additive** to provide red coloration in foods. Its conjugated azo chromophore absorbs visible light to generate red hue in aqueous food systems. [S8]

Common categories include confectionery, beverages, desserts, and other colored processed foods where regulations permit use. [S1, S2, S8]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status**: Authorized in the EU with ADI-based restrictions; EFSA re-evaluated and retained use under limits rather than withdrawal. [S1, S2]
- **ADI**: **0.7 mg/kg bw/day** (EFSA 2009 re-evaluation). [S1]
- **Evaluation year**: Re-evaluation in **2009**; revised exposure statement in **2015** (EFSA Journal 13(12):4436). [S1, S2]
- **Key conclusions**:
  - EFSA established ADI 0.7 mg/kg bw/day and identified concerns related to possible unidentified constituents and behavioural findings context (McCann et al.). [S1]
  - In 2015 revised exposure calculations using updated use levels, exposure estimates were revised downward and no longer exceeded ADI in assessed populations. [S2]

### FDA / eCFR (United States)
- **eCFR name search result**: Title 21 search for "Ponceau 4R" returned **0 results**. [S3]
- **GRAS status**: No GRAS entry identified for Ponceau 4R/New Coccine/Cochineal Red A in queried Title 21 content; a non-GRAS ingredient requires a food additive regulation before use in food. [S4, S6]
- **CFR citations**:
  - **21 CFR 170.30(g)** (non-GRAS ingredient requires food additive regulation). [S6]
  - **21 CFR 70.5** (general restrictions/use framework for listed color additives). [S5]

### JECFA / WHO
- **ADI**: **0-4 mg/kg bw**. [S8, S9]
- **Last evaluation year**: **2011** (JECFA page lists evaluation year 2011, retaining existing ADI and concluding dietary exposure does not present a health concern). [S8]

### IARC
- Checked IARC classified-agents page and IARC search endpoints for "Ponceau 4R", "New Coccine", and "Cochineal Red A"; no additive-specific monograph classification was identified from these queries. [S10, S11]

- No explicit IARC-driven carcinogen classification or broad EFSA/JECFA withdrawal was identified in consulted sources.
- In U.S. eCFR consultation, Ponceau 4R was not found as a listed food color additive entry in Title 21 queries, indicating non-approval via absence of listing rather than a named section-specific ban. [S3, S4]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- **Long-term rat study (1987, PMID 3692403)**: No treatment-related increase in tumour incidence; NOAEL reported at 500 mg/kg bw/day in that study design. [S17]
- **Three-generation rat reproduction study (1987, PMID 3692404)**: Reported no adverse reproductive outcome at tested doses, with NOAEL reported up to 1250 mg/kg bw/day. [S19]
- **Mouse reproduction/neurobehaviour study (2006, PMID 16782257)**: No adverse reproductive effects, but some neurobehavioural effects (maze performance) at higher dietary levels; authors note tested doses exceeded ADI. [S16]
- **Recent model-organism evidence (2023, PMID 37254351)**: C. elegans study reported oxidative-stress and behavioural/lifespan effects after exposure to several synthetic dyes including Ponceau 4R. [S14]
- **Prenatal AFCA mixture study (2021, PMID 31679476)**: Rat offspring showed neurobehavioral and receptor-level changes after prenatal exposure to a dye mixture including Ponceau 4R (mixture study, not Ponceau-only). [S15]

### Reviews and synthesis
- **2024 review (PMID 39058118)**: Concludes current ADIs are mostly considered safe by authorities, but emphasizes aging regulatory toxicology datasets and need for updated evaluation using newer evidence. [S13]

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2015 statement indicates revised exposure estimates were reduced versus earlier scenarios and no longer exceeded ADI for assessed populations when updated use levels were applied. [S2]
- JECFA 2011 notes that conservative EFSA child high-percentile assumptions could exceed ADI, while a more realistic FSANZ estimate was far below ADI; JECFA concluded no health concern for children at estimated exposures. [S8]
- **ADI exceedance risk** appears **scenario-dependent** (highly conservative assumptions vs refined use data). [S2, S8]

### Vulnerable populations
- Children/high consumers of brightly colored foods and beverages remain the most plausible subgroup for higher relative intake under conservative assumptions. [S2, S8]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - EFSA and JECFA both retain ADI-based authorization frameworks rather than withdrawal. [S1, S2, S8]
  - Long-term rat evidence did not show treatment-related tumor increase in key historical study. [S17]
- **Evidence against**:
  - EFSA reduced ADI to 0.7 mg/kg bw/day and highlighted unresolved data issues in re-evaluation context. [S1]
  - Additional nonclinical signals exist (neurobehavioral/oxidative stress findings in newer models). [S14, S15, S16]

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Clear ADIs exist (EFSA 0.7; JECFA 0-4), and EFSA 2015 refined exposure no longer exceeded ADI. [S1, S2, S8]
  - No IARC classification identified in consulted IARC resources. [S10, S11]
- **Evidence against**:
  - Historical conservative child exposure scenarios can exceed ADI. [S8]
  - EFSA 2009 concerns plus newer mechanistic/animal signals support caution beyond a minimal-concern tier. [S1, S14, S15, S16]

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - Fits "approved with caveats": ADI reduced by EFSA vs earlier higher values, exposure concerns historically present in children under conservative assumptions, and emerging nonclinical neuro/oxidative signals. [S1, S8, S14, S15, S16]
- **Evidence against**:
  - Refined EFSA exposure update reduced concern (no ADI exceedance in revised estimates). [S2]
  - JECFA 2011 concluded no health concern at estimated dietary exposure. [S8]

#### high
- **Evidence supporting**:
  - U.S. Title 21 search did not identify a Ponceau 4R listing, indicating non-approval in consulted CFR pathway. [S3, S4]
- **Evidence against**:
  - No identified IARC Group 1/2A/2B classification in checked IARC resources; EFSA/JECFA still operate ADI-based control, not withdrawal. [S1, S2, S8, S10, S11]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest factors are: (1) continued EFSA/JECFA authorization under ADI limits, (2) EFSA’s historically lowered ADI and caveat-rich 2009 re-evaluation, and (3) persistent but mainly nonclinical evidence of neuro/oxidative effects. This profile is more cautious than `limited` but does not meet `high` criteria.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `moderate`**
