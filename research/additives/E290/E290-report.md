# E290 (Carbon dioxide) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E290 (INS 290).
- **Name:** Carbon dioxide.
- **CAS number:** 124-38-9.
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic oxide gas (CO2).
- **Common synonyms:** Carbon dioxide, CO2, carbonic acid gas, dry ice (solid CO2).
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Naturally occurring and endogenous in humans; industrial food-grade CO2 is manufactured/captured but chemically identical to naturally occurring CO2. (Source: 21 CFR 184.1240 description in eCFR API XML, URL below.)

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Dissolved CO2 creates carbonation and mild acidification.
  - Gaseous CO2 displaces oxygen in package headspace, helping preservation of oxygen-sensitive foods.
  - Solid CO2 (dry ice) is used for freezing/cooling through sublimation.
- **Common food categories/uses:**
  - Carbonated beverages.
  - Modified-atmosphere packaged foods.
  - Foamed/sprayed foods (as propellant/aerating gas).
  - Leavening/processing contexts in certain foods.
- **Regulatory functional classes recorded:** extraction solvent, food additive, freezing agent, preservative, propellant (JECFA entry).

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU): Approved / no safety concern at reported uses**
  - EFSA re-evaluation publication: 2019 (first published 17 January 2019; adopted 27 November 2018).
  - EFSA conclusion: no need for a numerical ADI; carbon dioxide (E290) not of safety concern at reported uses and use levels.
  - EFSA also states E290 was already authorized in Annex II and III to Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008.
  - Source: *Re-evaluation of carbon dioxide (E 290) as a food additive* (EFSA Journal, 2019), https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/5592 and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2019.5592

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology perspective (strongest line of evidence):**
  - EFSA concluded no need for a numerical ADI and no safety concern at reported uses; also noted substantial endogenous human CO2 production and pulmonary elimination.
  - JECFA assigned ADI “not specified.”

- **Human/clinical literature (PubMed, recent/relevant):**
  - 2024 systematic review on sparkling water and blood pressure found uncertainty regarding whether hypertensive patients should avoid sparkling water; no definitive harm conclusion.
  - 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis found carbonation had favorable effects on swallowing function and reduced aspiration risk in included studies.
  - 2017 controlled study reported no meaningful effect of carbonation on glycemic response, gastric emptying, or satiety (except thirst differences).
  - 2022 dental pH study found some carbonated waters had pH below enamel-relevant thresholds, indicating potential erosive risk in specific contexts (indirect concern; beverage matrix-dependent, not a carcinogenic/toxic signal for CO2 itself).

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical intake context:**
  - Numeric dietary ADI-based exposure management is not used here because EFSA and JECFA did not set numerical ADIs.
  - EFSA indicates endogenous CO2 production in humans is high relative to dietary additive contribution and is physiologically eliminated via lungs.
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Not applicable in standard numeric form (no numerical ADI).
- **Potentially vulnerable populations (contextual, non-ADI):**
  - People with dental erosion susceptibility may need to limit frequent exposure to low-pH carbonated beverages.
  - Hypertensive individuals: evidence is currently inconclusive for sparkling-water-specific BP harm.

## Risk Assessment
1. **Tier-by-tier analysis**
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at reported uses.
    - JECFA: ADI not specified.
    - FDA: GRAS under cGMP (21 CFR 184.1240).
    - Substance is endogenous/naturally occurring and physiologically regulated.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - Some indirect beverage-context concerns exist (e.g., low-pH beverage dental erosion context; uncertain BP effects in specific populations), though not strong evidence of intrinsic toxic hazard from E290 itself.

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - Approved/GRAS with cGMP conditions and minor context-dependent concerns.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - Core regulatory evaluations are stronger than a typical “limited” profile because they converge on no numerical ADI/no safety concern.

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - Could be argued only via indirect, formulation-dependent issues (acidity of certain beverages) rather than additive-intrinsic toxicity.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - No EFSA ADI reduction, no demonstrated ADI exceedance population, no consistent credible harm signal attributable specifically to E290.

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier:
    - None found in reviewed authoritative sources.
  - Evidence arguing against this tier:
    - Not banned in major reviewed jurisdictions.
    - No IARC classification identified for carbon dioxide in current IARC classification dataset.
    - EFSA and JECFA conclusions are not compatible with high-risk categorization.

2. **Rationale**
- The decisive factors are concordant regulatory conclusions (EFSA no numerical ADI/no safety concern; JECFA ADI not specified; FDA GRAS with cGMP), endogenous nature of CO2 metabolism, and lack of convincing additive-specific harm signals in current evidence.

3. **Recommended tier**
- **risk_free**
