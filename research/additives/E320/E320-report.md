# E320 (Butylated hydroxyanisole, BHA) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E320
- **Primary name:** Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA)
- **CAS number(s):** 25013-16-5
- **Chemical class:** Synthetic phenolic antioxidant
- **Common synonyms:** BHA; mixture of 3-tert-butyl-4-hydroxyanisole and 2-tert-butyl-4-hydroxyanisole
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Synthetic

BHA identity, CAS, INS 320, and functional class are listed in the WHO/JECFA chemical record. [S5]

## Function in Food
BHA is used as an **antioxidant** to reduce oxidation/rancidity in fats and oils. In U.S. CFR text, BHA is permitted in specific foods with ppm limits (e.g., dehydrated potato shreds, dry cereals, dry mixes) and with labeling directions for beverage/dessert dry mixes so prepared foods do not exceed 2 ppm BHA. [S3]

Common categories from CFR/JECFA evidence:
- Fat/oil-containing processed foods
- Dry mixes for beverages/desserts
- Dehydrated potato products
- Dry breakfast cereals
- Flavoring substances (with specific limits)

[S3][S5]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved (ADI-based risk management)
- **ADI:** 1.0 mg/kg bw/day
- **Evaluation year(s):** 2011 re-evaluation (scientific opinion), 2012 refined exposure statement
- **Key conclusions from EFSA records/snippets:**
  - No concern for genotoxicity.
  - Developmental/neurobehavior-related effects in rat offspring were considered in deriving a NOAEL of 100 mg/kg bw/day (high dose reported at 500 mg/kg bw/day with adverse findings).
  - 2012 exposure statement indicated food-only and food+cosmetic scenarios generally below ADI, while combined assumptions including food-contact materials could exceed ADI in toddlers/children high consumers.

[S1][S2]

### FDA / U.S. CFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status:** Listed as GRAS in **21 CFR 182.3169** with tolerance condition.
- **CFR citation:** 21 CFR 182.3169; also used as a direct food additive under 21 CFR 172.110.
- **Conditions of use (examples):**
  - 21 CFR 182.3169: total antioxidant content not over 0.02% of fat/oil content (including essential volatile oil), under GMP.
  - 21 CFR 172.110: specified foods and ppm limits; labeling directions for dry mixes to ensure prepared products contain no more than 2 ppm BHA.

[S3][S4]

### JECFA/WHO
- **ADI:** 0-0.5 mg/kg bw
- **Last evaluation year (ADI entry):** 1988 (JECFA meeting 33)
- **Additional intake note:** JECFA intake assessment text (1998) in the WHO database notes potential ADI exceedance for high consumers in some submitted datasets.

[S5][S6]

### IARC
- **Classification:** Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic to humans)
- **Evaluation year:** 1987 (IARC Volume 40/Supplement 7 listing entry)

[S7][S8]

### Notable bans
- No completed major-jurisdiction ban was identified in the retrieved EFSA/eCFR/JECFA/IARC primary records.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal and mechanistic evidence
- JECFA monograph discusses forestomach hyperplasia/tumor findings in rat models at high dietary concentrations and notes that humans do not have a forestomach; JECFA nonetheless retained an ADI-based approach. [S6]
- EFSA re-evaluation reports no genotoxicity concern and uses toxicity dataset elements (including neurobehavior-related endpoints in offspring) for ADI support. [S1]
- Recent PubMed studies report potential developmental/neurobehavioral effects in zebrafish embryos/larvae exposed to low-ppb BHA and developmental hepatotoxicity signals for 3-BHA (an isomer) in hESC-derived hepatocyte models. [S10][S11]
- A co-exposure mouse study reports kidney injury aggravation with nanoplastics + 3-BHA (mixed-exposure context, not BHA alone). [S12]

### Human/epidemiological evidence
- No strong prospective human epidemiology establishing harm at current regulated intake levels was identified in the retrieved regulatory and PubMed set. (Inference from source mix.) [S1][S2][S5][S9][S10][S11][S12]

### Countervailing evidence
- A 2025 ex vivo human gut microbiome study found BHA **minimally affected** microbiome structure/function at tested exposure conditions. [S9]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Regulatory benchmarks:** EFSA ADI 1.0 mg/kg bw/day; JECFA ADI 0-0.5 mg/kg bw. [S1][S5][S6]
- **EFSA refined exposure statement (2012):** adults remained below ADI in modeled food-only scenarios; toddlers/children had the highest modeled exposures; combined assumptions including food-contact materials produced potential ADI exceedance in high consumers. [S2]
- **JECFA intake note (1998):** potential high-consumer ADI exceedance acknowledged, with limited ability to quantify affected consumers/duration. [S5]

### Vulnerable populations
- Toddlers and children (especially high consumers of fat/oil-containing processed foods)
- High-end consumers under conservative combined-exposure assumptions

[S2][S5]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Established ADIs by EFSA/JECFA and continued authorization frameworks.
  - EFSA reported no genotoxicity concern.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - IARC Group 2B classification exists.
  - EFSA/JECFA exposure notes indicate potential ADI exceedance for some high-consuming child groups under some assumptions.
  - New experimental studies continue to report toxicity signals.

[S1][S2][S5][S8][S10][S11][S12]

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Clear regulatory controls (CFR use limits, ADI framework).
  - GRAS listing with quantitative tolerance condition in 21 CFR 182.3169.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Carcinogenicity concern signal (IARC 2B).
  - Persistent animal/mechanistic concerns and potential exceedance scenarios in children.

[S3][S4][S8][S10][S11][S12]

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved additive, but with clear caveats: ADI-based control, modeled exceedance risk in some populations, and animal/mechanistic concern signals.
  - IARC Group 2B supports non-trivial hazard concern (without by itself proving high real-world risk at current intakes).
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major completed bans identified in the reviewed core regulatory sources.
  - Some data (e.g., ex vivo microbiome) show limited observed effect at tested conditions.

[S2][S5][S8][S9][S10][S11][S12]

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - IARC Group 2B and experimental toxicity findings.
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Continued authorization in EFSA/FDA/JECFA frameworks with explicit limits.
  - Evidence base does not show broad human population-level harm at regulated use levels from the retrieved records.

[S1][S3][S4][S5][S8]

### 2. Rationale
BHA is not well placed in `risk_free` because both hazard signals (IARC 2B, animal/mechanistic findings) and exposure caveats (potential child high-consumer exceedance in conservative scenarios) are present. It is also not clearly `high` because major regulatory systems still permit use with ADI/limit controls and reviewed evidence is mixed in real-world relevance.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`moderate`**
