# E1451 Research Report: Acetylated oxidized starch

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1451 (INS 1451) [7]
- **Name:** Acetylated oxidized starch (also written as acetylated oxidised starch) [3][7][9]
- **CAS number(s):** Not clearly provided in the current WHO JECFA chemical record for E1451. A legacy WHO endpoint is now redirected and inaccessible; older indexed snippets showed CAS `65996-63-6`, but that CAS is used for acid-modified starch in U.S. FDA inventory records, so CAS assignment for E1451 remains uncertain in this run. [15]
- **Chemical class:** Chemically modified starch (oxidized and acetylated starch derivative) [3][9]
- **Common synonyms:** Acetylated oxidized starch; acetylated oxidised starch; INS 1451; E1451 [3][7][9]
- **Origin (natural vs synthetic):** **Semi-synthetic** (root/grain starch chemically oxidized and esterified) [9]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Chemical oxidation plus acetylation changes starch functionality (clarity/gel behavior), enabling stabilizing/thickening/binding effects in formulations. [9]
- **JECFA functional classes:** Emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener. [7]
- **Common food categories:**
  - Jelly/soft confectionery (JECFA monograph use case; binding/texture role). [9]
  - General processed foods using modified starches for texture and stability. [3][7]

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Approved/authorized in the EU food additive framework; included in EFSA’s 2017 re-evaluation of modified starches. [3]
- **Latest EFSA re-evaluation found:** EFSA Journal 2017, `15(10):4911` (includes E1451 in the modified starch group). [3]
- **ADI:** Historical ADI “not specified” from JECFA/SCF context; EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for the modified starch group under reported uses/use levels. [3]
- **Key EFSA conclusions:**
  - No genotoxic concern (based on in silico and read-across approach). [3]
  - No treatment-related effects relevant for human risk assessment at high tested levels in animals; no safety concern at reported uses/use levels for modified starches. [3]
- **Access note:** Direct EFSA and Wiley-hosted journal pages were visited, but full content access was blocked by robots/anti-bot controls in this VM; regulatory conclusions were extracted from the PubMed indexed EFSA abstract. [1][2][3][16]

### FDA / eCFR (US)
- **Regulatory status:** **Approved food additive pathway** (not identified as a standalone GRAS affirmation for this specific substance in reviewed CFR exports). [4][5][6]
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR § 172.892 (Food starch-modified)**. [4]
- **Conditions of use relevant to acetylated oxidized starch pathway:**
  - Food starch-modified “may be safely used in food,” with modification reagents limited to amounts needed for technical effect and specific limits. [4]
  - Oxidation condition includes sodium hypochlorite limit (`0.055 lb chlorine/lb dry starch`). [4]
  - Esterification condition includes acetic anhydride with acetyl groups in final starch not exceeding `2.5%`. [4]
  - Combination of treatment pathways is allowed subject to named paragraph limits. [4]
- **GRAS check in Title 21 exports:** Part 184/Part 186 searches did not identify an explicit entry naming acetylated oxidized starch; Part 182 includes other starch entries (e.g., unmodified/acid-modified) but not this specific additive name. [5][6]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA 2017 group re-evaluation (regulatory synthesis):**
  - Modified starches (including E1451) considered not genotoxic via read-across/in silico approach. [3]
  - No safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no numerical ADI required. [3]
  - Reported high-dose animal data and tolerance context summarized in EFSA abstract (e.g., no treatment-related effects up to very high doses in rats; high single-dose human tolerance examples for modified starches). [3]
- **JECFA monograph animal evidence specific to acetylated oxidized starch:**
  - 14-day rat study: soft feces and increased caecal weights at high dietary levels; NOEL `5000 mg/kg bw/day` (10% diet). [9]
  - 90-day rat study: caecal enlargement at high dose; urinary bladder/kidney microscopic findings at highest concentration; NOEL `5900 mg/kg bw/day` (10% diet). [9]
  - JECFA interpreted findings in context of high dietary slowly digested carbohydrates and established ADI “not specified.” [9]
- **Epidemiological/human outcome data:**
  - No robust E1451-specific epidemiological safety dataset was identified in this run. [12][13]
- **Recent PubMed landscape:**
  - Exact title/abstract phrase query for “acetylated oxidized starch” returned no direct hits. [12]
  - Broader “acetylated starch + toxicity” search returns mixed/indirect literature (including EFSA regulatory article and non-food-application papers), indicating limited recent direct toxicology studies specific to E1451. [13][14]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA 2017 exposure context (grouped modified starches):**
  - Combined exposure estimate for E1404–E1451 at 95th percentile refined brand-loyal scenario reported up to `3053 mg/kg bw/day` (general population scenario in abstract). [3]
- **JECFA confectionery scenario (historical intake estimate for acetylated oxidized starch):**
  - At 30% use in jelly confectionery and 200 g/day confectionery consumption, maximum intake estimated at `60 g/day` (about `1 g/kg bw/day` for 60 kg adult). [9]
  - Australian dietary modeling cited median/95th-percentile consumer intake estimates (95th percentile ~40 g/day). [9]
- **ADI exceedance framing:**
  - EFSA/JECFA do not provide a numerical ADI for this additive context, so classical numeric ADI exceedance cannot be computed from these sources. [3][7][9]
- **Potentially higher-intake groups:**
  - High consumers of confectionery, including adolescents in intake modeling scenarios. [9]

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA/JECFA conclusions are permissive and non-numerical ADI framing indicates low concern at intended use. [3][7]
  - Evidence against: Additive is chemically modified (semi-synthetic), and animal studies reported dose-related gastrointestinal/urinary tract findings at high doses. [9]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Regulatory status remains approved in EU/US context; EFSA and JECFA did not identify a safety concern at reported uses/use levels; no IARC classification signal found. [3][4][7][10][11]
  - Evidence against: Exposure data are often grouped with other modified starches and recent direct E1451-specific toxicology literature is limited. [3][12][13]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: High-dose rat findings (caecal and urinary tract histopathology at top dose) plus limited modern direct epidemiology could support a cautionary interpretation. [9][12][13]
  - Evidence against: Effects were observed at high dietary concentrations and were interpreted by JECFA/EFSA within a no-safety-concern regulatory conclusion for intended uses. [3][9]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: No strong supporting evidence identified (no IARC Group 2A/2B/1 assignment for this substance; no major-jurisdiction bans found). [10][11]
  - Evidence against: Current regulatory consensus remains permissive with ADI not specified/no numerical ADI needed. [3][7]

### 2. Rationale
The overall evidence points to low concern under current authorized uses, with caveats mainly around limited recent E1451-specific human epidemiology and reliance on grouped modified-starch assessments. High-dose animal findings exist, but were not judged by regulators to imply unsafe use at intended food-use levels.

### 3. Recommended tier
**Recommended tier: `limited`.**
