# E203 (Calcium sorbate) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E203.
- **Name**: Calcium sorbate.
- **CAS number(s)**: 7492-55-9 (from PubChem synonym registry output for calcium sorbate). [S11]
- **Chemical class**: Sorbic acid salt preservative; calcium salt of trans,trans-2,4-hexadienoic acid (EU specification text). [S4]
- **Common synonyms**: Calcium salts of trans,trans-2,4-hexadienoic acid; calcium bis((2E,4E)-hexa-2,4-dienoate). [S4][S11]
- **Natural vs synthetic**: **Synthetic (commercial additive form)**. This is an industrially used preservative salt form rather than a naturally extracted food constituent. [S12] (inference)

## Function in Food
- **Primary function**: Preservative (sorbate class, antifungal/anti-yeast use in acidic foods). [S1][S12]
- **Mechanism of action**: Sorbate preservatives are effective in weakly acidic pH systems; the preservative action is primarily associated with sorbic acid/sorbate activity against spoilage organisms. [S12]
- **Common food categories**: EFSA exposure contributors for sorbate uses included bread and rolls, fine bakery wares, flavoured drinks, sauces, and processed cheese. [S1]
- **Other uses noted in literature**: Calcium sorbate has been used in fungistatic wrappers, but this use was described as limited. [S12]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA opinion (latest for E203)**:
  - **Evaluation year**: 2015 (EFSA ANS Panel re-evaluation of E200/E202/E203). [S1]
  - **ADI**: EFSA set a **temporary group ADI of 3 mg/kg bw/day for E200 + E202 only** and stated calcium sorbate (E203) should be excluded pending genotoxicity data. [S1]
  - **Key EFSA conclusion**: EFSA could not confirm safety of calcium sorbate due to missing genotoxicity data. [S1]
- **Post-EFSA EU regulatory outcome**:
  - Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/98 states calcium sorbate (E203) was removed from the Union list of approved additives; application date was **12 August 2018**. [S3]
  - The later EFSA follow-up (2019) set a new group ADI of 11 mg/kg bw/day for E200/E202 and notes E203 had been removed from the Union list. [S2]
- **FDA / eCFR (Title 21)**:
  - **Status**: GRAS.
  - **CFR citation**: **21 CFR 182.3225** (food) and **21 CFR 582.3225** (animal feed).
  - **Conditions of use**: "generally recognized as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice" (or good manufacturing/feeding practice in part 582). [S5][S6]
- **JECFA/WHO**:
  - **ADI**: Group ADI **0-25 mg/kg bw** (expressed as sorbic acid, group including sorbic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium sorbates).
  - **Last evaluation year shown for calcium sorbate entry**: **1973**. [S7][S8]
- **IARC**:
  - Calcium sorbate is **not listed** in IARC's current agent classification dataset accessed from the Monographs site backend bundle. [S9][S10]
- **Notable bans/restrictions**:
  - EU: withdrawn from authorized food additive lists (from 12 August 2018). [S3]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology signal (highest weight for E203)**:
  - EFSA could not confirm E203 safety because of missing genotoxicity data; this directly triggered EU removal. [S1][S3]
- **Direct E203-related experimental literature**:
  - Older reaction-product studies (2000) involving sorbic acid and salts including E203 reported no mutagenic/genotoxic activity for tested sorbic acid-amine reaction products under their experimental conditions. [S13][S14]
  - 2021 thermal-behavior work included calcium sorbate and described decomposition behavior; this is physicochemical evidence, not direct chronic toxicity evidence. [S15]
- **Broader sorbate-salt evidence (indirect for E203)**:
  - 2018 cell study on potassium sorbate reported low-concentration safety in the specific model used. [S16]
  - 2018 rat study using five additives at 10x ADI (including potassium sorbate) observed hepato-renal/genotoxic effects; attribution to E203 specifically is not possible. [S17]
  - 2025 systematic review found frequent cytogenotoxicity signals for preservatives in mammalian systems (including potassium sorbate-focused literature), indicating uncertainty remains for the sorbate class. [S18]
- **Epidemiological data**:
  - No robust calcium sorbate-specific human epidemiology was identified in the searched sources.

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2015 estimated exposure to sorbic acid/sorbates (grouped use scenario) at mean **0.3-30.4 mg/kg bw/day** and high (95th percentile) **0.6-72.6 mg/kg bw/day** depending on scenario/population. [S1]
- Under EFSA's temporary group ADI (3 mg/kg bw/day for E200/E202), high-level toddlers/children exceeded that value in some scenarios/member states. [S1]
- For E203 specifically in the EU, current legal dietary exposure should be greatly reduced because E203 was removed from the authorized list effective 12 August 2018. [S3]
- **Vulnerable populations**: Historically high-consuming toddlers/children were the group most likely to exceed temporary sorbate ADI estimates in EFSA scenarios. [S1]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: JECFA group ADI exists; FDA GRAS listing remains in 21 CFR. [S6][S7]
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA could not confirm safety for E203 due to missing genotoxicity data, and EU removed E203 from approved lists. [S1][S3]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: Some jurisdictions still allow calcium sorbate under GMP/GMFP frameworks; no IARC classification found for calcium sorbate itself. [S6][S9][S10]
  - Evidence against this tier: Regulatory withdrawal in the EU after EFSA non-confirmation is stronger than a routine "minor concern" pattern. [S1][S3]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: There is not strong direct evidence of severe human harm at ordinary exposure, and some historical experimental data were negative for tested reaction products. [S13][S14]
  - Evidence against this tier: EFSA inability to confirm safety plus subsequent removal from EU positive list is a higher-severity regulatory signal than typical moderate caveats. [S1][S3]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting this tier: EFSA explicitly unable to confirm safety of E203; major jurisdiction (EU) removed authorization; no sponsor supplied requested genotoxicity data. [S1][S3]
  - Evidence against this tier: FDA GRAS/JECFA group ADI status still exist, and direct high-quality calcium sorbate-specific human harm data are limited. [S6][S7]

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factors are regulatory: EFSA could not establish safety for calcium sorbate (data gap on genotoxicity), and the EU then removed E203 from authorized food additive lists. That combination meets the "high"-tier trigger in the provided framework despite continued allowance in some non-EU systems.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `high`**
