# E524 (Sodium hydroxide) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E524
- **Name:** Sodium hydroxide
- **CAS number(s):** 1310-73-2
- **Chemical class:** Strong inorganic base (alkali metal hydroxide)
- **Common synonyms:** Lye, caustic soda, sodium hydrate, soda lye, white caustic
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Food-grade sodium hydroxide is industrially manufactured (synthetic), including chlor-alkali electrolysis routes.

Evidence: [S1][S4][S5]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Dissociates to hydroxide ions (OH-) and rapidly raises pH; used to neutralize acidity and control processing pH.
- **Primary technological roles:** pH control agent and processing aid under U.S. food GRAS regulation.
- **Common food-use context:** Broad GMP-based use in foods as a pH control/processing aid rather than a nutrient additive.

Evidence: [S4]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal/regulatory toxicology context
- JECFA's ADI of "NOT LIMITED" indicates no need for a numerical ADI at evaluated food-additive uses in its assessment framework.
- EFSA's dedicated E524 re-evaluation remains pending, so no new EFSA final toxicological conclusion specific to E524 was available in the retrieved EFSA records.

Evidence: [S3][S5]

### Epidemiological/clinical human evidence (recent PubMed)
- Recent clinical literature continues to show that **corrosive alkali exposures** can cause severe gastrointestinal injury, including strictures and need for intervention, particularly in accidental pediatric ingestion scenarios.
- These studies largely involve accidental or high-concentration corrosive exposure contexts (household/caustic products), not normal additive-level food exposure.

Evidence: [S8][S9][S10]

### Mechanistic concerns
- Sodium hydroxide is strongly alkaline and can cause caustic tissue injury at high pH/concentrated exposure.
- This mechanistic hazard is clear for misuse/accidental poisoning; it does not by itself imply equivalent risk at regulated food-processing GMP use levels.

Evidence: [S4][S8][S9][S10]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** No dedicated EFSA quantitative intake estimate specific to E524 was identified in the retrieved EFSA records because re-evaluation is still pending.
- **ADI exceedance risk:** Not assessed against a numerical EFSA ADI (none identified). JECFA uses "NOT LIMITED," so a numeric ADI exceedance calculation is not applicable.
- **Most relevant vulnerable populations:**
  - Young children in accidental corrosive ingestion scenarios (non-additive exposure context).
  - Workers/handlers exposed to concentrated alkali products.
- **Dietary-use interpretation:** At regulated GMP food use, sodium hydroxide is generally used for process pH control and is not typically consumed as concentrated free alkali.

Evidence: [S3][S4][S5][S8][S9][S10]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI status is "NOT LIMITED." [S5]
  - FDA GRAS framework permits use under GMP. [S4]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Strong caustic hazard exists at high concentrations; severe human injuries are documented in recent studies. [S8][S9][S10]
  - EFSA's dedicated re-evaluation for E524 is still pending. [S3]

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/authorised in major regulatory systems reviewed (EU authorised with EFSA re-evaluation pending; U.S. GRAS; JECFA ADI not limited). [S3][S4][S5]
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification entry found for sodium hydroxide in checked resources. [S6][S7]
  - Harm signal is primarily linked to concentrated/non-dietary exposures rather than normal additive uses. [S8][S9][S10]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - Lack of a completed modern EFSA re-evaluation leaves some uncertainty for updated EU risk characterization. [S3]

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Credible severe-harm evidence exists for corrosive exposure, especially in children with accidental ingestion. [S8][S10]
  - EFSA re-evaluation delay reduces certainty about updated exposure/tox endpoints. [S3]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No major bans identified and core regulatory status remains permissive under GMP.
  - JECFA ADI remains "NOT LIMITED." [S5]

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - High-dose caustic toxicity can be serious/fatal in poisoning contexts. [S8][S9][S10]
- **Evidence arguing against this tier:**
  - No evidence of major-jurisdiction food-additive bans in retrieved sources.
  - No IARC Group 1/2A/2B trigger found for sodium hydroxide.
  - Regulatory frameworks (FDA GRAS/JECFA) do not indicate high-risk classification at intended food use. [S4][S5][S6][S7]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight is the regulatory baseline: FDA GRAS use under GMP and JECFA ADI "NOT LIMITED," plus EFSA records confirming continued EU authorisation while re-evaluation is pending. The main hazard signal is clear but exposure-dependent: concentrated alkali causes corrosive injury, whereas food-additive use is process/GMP controlled and not equivalent to poisoning exposures.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
