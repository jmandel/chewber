# E307 (Alpha-tocopherol) - Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number**: E307
- **Primary name**: Alpha-tocopherol
- **CAS number(s)**:
  - 59-02-9 (d-alpha-tocopherol)
  - 10191-41-0 (dl-alpha-tocopherol)
  - Source: 21 CFR 184.1890 text (eCFR API)
- **Chemical class**: Tocopherol (fat-soluble vitamin E compound; phenolic chromanol antioxidant)
- **Common synonyms**: Vitamin E, d-alpha-tocopherol, dl-alpha-tocopherol, all-rac-alpha-tocopherol
- **Natural vs synthetic**:
  - Natural form exists (d-alpha-tocopherol from vegetable oil distillation)
  - Synthetic form exists (dl-alpha-tocopherol made by chemical synthesis)
  - Source: 21 CFR 184.1890

## Function in Food
- **Functional role**: Antioxidant (JECFA functional class).
- **Mechanism of action**: Lipid-phase radical scavenger that interrupts oxidation chains and protects fats/oils from rancidity.
- **Common food categories**:
  - Broadly used in many foods in the EU as part of the tocopherols group (EFSA news summary of E306-E309 re-evaluation).
  - In U.S. GRAS affirmation for alpha-tocopherols, use is specifically limited to inhibition of nitrosamine formation in pump-cured bacon at GMP (21 CFR 184.1890).

## Regulatory Status

### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest opinion located**: EFSA Journal re-evaluation for E306-E309 (2015), publication ID 4247.
- **Approval status**: Approved/authorized in EU use framework (EFSA states tocopherol additives are already approved in many foods).
- **ADI**: EFSA reported it **could not establish an ADI** because available toxicity data were limited.
- **Evaluation year**: 2015.
- **Key conclusions**:
  - No safety concern at reported uses/use levels for E306-E309.
  - Estimated exposures did not exceed upper intake levels in adults; one children survey exceeded the UL in one country.
- **Access note**: The direct Wiley-hosted full text was blocked by anti-bot protection in this environment; EFSA journal and EFSA news pages were accessible.

### FDA / eCFR (Title 21)
- **GRAS status**:
  - 21 CFR 182.3890: Tocopherols are GRAS when used in accordance with GMP.
  - 21 CFR 184.1890: Alpha-tocopherols are affirmed as GRAS with specific use limitations.
- **Conditions of use (21 CFR 184.1890)**:
  - Use as inhibitors of nitrosamine formation.
  - Use in pump-cured bacon at levels not to exceed current GMP.
- **Specific citation**: 21 CFR 182.3890; 21 CFR 184.1890.

### JECFA / WHO
- **JECFA ADI**: Group ADI **0.15-2 mg/kg bw/day** for dl-alpha-tocopherol and d-alpha-tocopherol concentrate.
- **Evaluation year**: 1986 (ADI record); WHO database also shows specification evaluations including year 2000 entries.
- **Toxicological notes from JECFA detail page**:
  - Short-term rat no-effect level: 117 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Long-term rat no-effect level: 31 mg/kg bw/day.
  - Human special studies: increased bleeding time and reduced platelet adhesiveness at >2 mg/kg bw/day (synthetic DL-alpha-tocopherol), and slight increase in bleeding time at 1-2 mg/kg bw/day.

### IARC
- **Classification check**:
  - No alpha-tocopherol entry identified in IARC "Agents Classified by the IARC" resources.
  - IARC site search endpoints checked for "tocopherol" returned no matching post/page results.
- **Classification result**: No IARC monograph classification identified for E307/alpha-tocopherol.

### Notable bans

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory baseline (food additive context)**:
  - EFSA (2015): no safety concern at reported uses/use levels for tocopherol additives, but ADI could not be established from available data.
- **Animal evidence (regulatory toxicology)**:
  - JECFA details report no-effect levels in rat studies (117 mg/kg short-term; 31 mg/kg long-term).
- **Human mechanistic concern (regulatory toxicology)**:
  - JECFA human studies found increased bleeding time at doses above the ADI range and slight effects near upper range.
- **Recent PubMed safety signal evidence (mostly supplement-dose context, not typical additive intake)**:
  - PMID 40499949 (2025): case report of reversible coagulopathy after vitamin E over-supplementation.
  - PMID 37698992 (2024): meta-analysis reported no stroke reduction from vitamin E alone; with antioxidants, ischemic stroke decreased but hemorrhagic stroke risk increased (RR 1.22).
  - PMID 21990298 (2011 SELECT): 400 IU/day all-rac-alpha-tocopheryl acetate increased prostate cancer risk (HR 1.17) in healthy men.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake**:
  - Precise numeric E307 intake estimates were not retrievable from the blocked EFSA full-text article in this environment.
  - EFSA summary indicates exposure assessment generally did not exceed adult upper intake levels.
- **ADI exceedance risk**:
  - EFSA: no numeric ADI established.
  - JECFA: ADI range exists (0.15-2 mg/kg bw/day); human bleeding-time findings align with concern above upper range.
  - EFSA reported one children survey exceeded UL in one country, indicating a potential subgroup risk signal.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations**:
  - Children with higher intake relative to body weight.
  - Individuals with vitamin K deficiency/malabsorption or on anticoagulant-related regimens (relevant to bleeding/coagulation concerns).
  - High-dose vitamin E supplement users.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Alpha-tocopherol is vitamin E and is naturally present in foods.
  - EFSA did not identify safety concern at reported additive uses/use levels.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA could not establish an ADI (data limitations).
  - JECFA has a finite ADI range and reports bleeding-time effects near/above the upper end.
  - EFSA noted UL exceedance in at least one children survey.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - FDA/eCFR GRAS status and EU authorization context are maintained.
  - No IARC classification and no major bans identified.
  - Core regulatory conclusions do not indicate additive-level harm at typical reported uses.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - EFSA data gaps prevented ADI derivation.
  - Credible human/supplement literature exists for bleeding and other adverse outcomes at higher doses.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - Approved status comes with caveats: EFSA no ADI due limited toxicity data.
  - EFSA identified a child subgroup UL exceedance signal.
  - JECFA and PubMed data support biologically plausible coagulation-related concerns at higher exposure.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - Regulatory bodies still consider current food-additive uses acceptable.
  - Strong direct harm evidence at ordinary additive exposure levels remains limited.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier**:
  - None from major regulator actions in reviewed sources.
- **Evidence against this tier**:
  - No major-jurisdiction bans.
  - No IARC Group 2A/2B/1 classification found.
  - EFSA and FDA frameworks still permit use.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest signal is "approved but with important caveats" rather than either "risk-free" or "high risk." EFSA did not derive an ADI due data limitations and identified a child UL exceedance signal in one survey, while JECFA and recent literature provide consistent high-dose coagulation concerns. At the same time, current regulatory frameworks continue to permit additive use.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
