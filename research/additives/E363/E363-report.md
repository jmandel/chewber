# E363 (Succinic acid) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E363
- **Name:** Succinic acid
- **CAS number:** 110-15-6
- **Chemical class:** Aliphatic dicarboxylic acid (butanedioic acid)
- **Common synonyms:** Amber acid, ethylenesuccinic acid, 1,4-butanedioic acid
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Endogenous/naturally occurring metabolite (TCA cycle) but commercial food-grade additive is typically produced by chemical synthesis (e.g., hydrogenation of maleic/fumaric acid) [21 CFR 184.1091; JECFA Chemical 6593]

## Function in Food
- **Primary roles:** Flavor enhancer and pH control agent (acidulant functionality) [21 CFR 184.1091(c)]
- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Lowers/adjusts pH, influencing taste profile and microbial stability.
  - Contributes sour/umami-modulating flavor enhancement.
- **Common food categories (documented in CFR use levels):**
  - Condiments and relishes (up to 0.084% as served)
  - Meat products (up to 0.0061% as served)
  - Otherwise GMP-limited use [21 CFR 184.1091(d)]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):**
  - EFSA states succinic acid (E363) is still in the re-evaluation pipeline; a 2024 public call requested technical/toxicological data and noted earlier calls did not receive data.
  - EFSA indicates no date can be set yet for final scientific output because of missing data.
  - **ADI (EFSA):** No EFSA numeric ADI identified in current accessible EFSA pages for E363.
  - **Latest EFSA re-evaluation activity year:** 2024 (data call publication).
  - Sources: EFSA call page and EFSA call-overview page.
- **FDA / eCFR (US):**
  - Listed in **21 CFR Part 184** (Direct food substances affirmed as GRAS).
  - Specific citation: **21 CFR §184.1091 (Succinic acid)**.
  - Conditions: use as flavor enhancer and pH control agent, at levels not exceeding GMP; CFR gives representative maximum as-served levels for condiments/relishes and meat products.
- **JECFA / WHO:**
  - WHO JECFA database entry for succinic acid (Chemical 6593): **Evaluation year 2023**, **ADI: “No ADI established”**, with comment that JECFA did not re-review because previous food additive evaluation concluded no hazard at likely use levels due normal metabolic role.
- **IARC:**
  - No succinic acid entry was found through IARC Monographs site search/API queries used here.
  - Therefore, no IARC Group classification identified for succinic acid in this research pass.
- **Notable bans:**
  - No major-jurisdiction ban identified in the sources reviewed here.

## Key Safety Evidence
### Regulatory toxicology baseline
- JECFA summary conclusion (as surfaced in WHO database): succinic acid was previously considered not hazardous at likely food-additive use levels because of its normal metabolic role.
- FDA framework places succinic acid in GRAS-affirmed direct food substances with GMP-based limits and specified use categories.

### Animal/mechanistic and recent literature signals (PubMed)
- **Mechanistic inflammation/metabolism context (review):** 2024 review describes succinate as an extracellular signaling molecule (SUCNR1 axis) linked to inflammatory pathways when elevated.
- **Metabolic disease context (review):** 2024 review on type 2 diabetes discusses dual role of succinate, including potential adverse signaling contexts and potential therapeutic targeting.
- **Recent experimental pathway evidence (not E363-specific exposure study):** 2025 mouse study (BPAF model) reports succinate-SUCNR1 pathway involvement in hepatic steatosis biology.
- **Dietary supplementation model (non-human food fish):** 2025 fish feeding study reports improved outcomes at certain dietary succinic acid levels in a high-starch stress model.

### Interpretation
- Direct modern human epidemiology specifically on **food additive E363 intake** was not identified in the searched PubMed set.
- Recent literature is mostly mechanistic/indirect (succinate biology broadly), rather than direct additive-exposure risk quantification in humans.

## Exposure Assessment
- **Typical dietary intake:** No robust, recent, additive-specific population intake estimate for E363 was identified in the sources accessed.
- **Regulatory use constraints indicating likely low exposure in practice:**
  - U.S. GMP framework with listed representative maximum as-served levels in certain food categories (21 CFR §184.1091).
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Not quantitatively assessable against an EFSA numeric ADI (none located in current EFSA materials reviewed).
  - JECFA current entry uses “No ADI established” with prior “no hazard at likely use levels” rationale.
- **Potentially vulnerable populations (theoretical):**
  - Individuals with metabolic/inflammatory disorders could be biologically relevant to succinate signaling hypotheses, but direct E363 dietary-risk evidence in humans is limited.

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Endogenous metabolite with normal role in intermediary metabolism.
  - JECFA statement indicates no hazard at likely additive-use levels and current entry shows no numeric ADI established for this use context.
  - FDA GRAS-affirmed status with GMP conditions.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EFSA re-evaluation remains incomplete and explicitly requests missing toxicological/technical data.
  - Modern direct human intake-risk datasets specific to E363 are sparse.

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/GRAS in major jurisdictions with condition-based use limits.
  - No strong direct signal of serious harm at food-use levels in regulatory summaries.
  - Mechanistic concerns generally arise under disease/toxicant contexts or altered succinate biology, not clearly from typical additive exposure.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - EFSA data gaps prevent a fully current EU risk characterization.

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - EFSA re-evaluation incompleteness and absence of a current EFSA numeric ADI create uncertainty.
  - Mechanistic literature on SUCNR1-linked inflammatory/metabolic effects suggests plausibility of context-dependent effects at elevated succinate.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No clear regulatory action reducing ADI for E363 or identifying current-population exceedance.
  - No clear additive-specific epidemiological harm pattern identified.

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Very limited support from current evidence set.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found for succinic acid.
  - No major-jurisdiction bans found.
  - No strong additive-specific evidence of severe toxicity at normal food-use exposure.

### 2. Rationale
The strongest weight comes from long-standing regulatory acceptance (FDA GRAS framework, JECFA no-hazard conclusion at likely use levels) and succinic acid’s endogenous metabolic role. The principal counterweight is uncertainty from EFSA’s still-pending re-evaluation and missing submitted data, plus indirect mechanistic literature that is not directly equivalent to dietary additive exposure.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
