# E322I Research Report: Lecithin (non-fractionated)

## Identity
- **E-number:** E322(i) (often written E322I)
- **Name:** Lecithin (non-fractionated)
- **CAS number(s):** 8002-43-5 (lecithins). EFSA also lists 85711-58-6 for **hydrolysed** lecithins (E322(ii)), which is distinct from non-fractionated E322(i). [1][2]
- **Chemical class:** Complex mixture of phospholipids/phosphatides (notably phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylinositol) with smaller amounts of other lipids. [1][4]
- **Common synonyms:** Phosphatides; phospholipids; soy lecithin. [4]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Primarily natural/extracted (e.g., from soy/safflower/corn oils; also egg sources in some contexts), though processed/refined for food use. [3][5]

## Function in Food
- **Primary role:** Emulsifier (amphiphilic phospholipids lower interfacial tension and stabilize oil-water systems). [1][4]
- **Other roles:** Antioxidant/processing aid in some formulations. [4]
- **Common food categories:** Broadly used across many food categories; EFSA highlights use in infant/young-child categories and foods for special medical purposes among others. [1]

## Regulatory Status
- **EFSA (EU):** Latest re-evaluation identified is EFSA Journal opinion 4742 (2017). EFSA concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** and **no safety concern** at refined exposure estimates for the general population (>1 year), infants (12 weeks to 11 months), and specified medical-purpose groups assessed. [1][2]
- **FDA / CFR (US):** Lecithin is in **21 CFR 184.1400** under Part 184 (direct food substances affirmed as GRAS). Conditions of use are GMP-based, with no specific quantitative limit beyond amount reasonably required for technical effect (via §184.1 and §184.1400(c)). [5][6]
- **JECFA/WHO:** WHO JECFA database entry for LECITHIN (INS 322(i)) shows ADI **NOT LIMITED** and evaluation year **1973** (meeting 17). [4]
- **IARC:** No lecithin entry found in IARC’s current list-of-classifications dataset (last-update string in dataset: 2026-02-25). [7][8]
- **Notable bans:** None identified in major jurisdictions reviewed (EU/US/JECFA context). [1][4][5]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory toxicology synthesis (EFSA):**
  - Low acute toxicity in tested species.
  - No genotoxicity concern.
  - Subchronic/chronic datasets did not show adverse effects at high tested doses; EFSA retained no numerical ADI. [1]
- **Gut microbiome/mechanistic signals (recent literature):**
  - In vitro human microbiota work reported strong microbiome composition/metabolite shifts with soy lecithin exposure under test conditions.
  - A mouse model reported intestinal morphology/microbiota perturbations with lecithin exposure, with effects less severe than CMC and partially mitigated by inulin. [9][10]
- **Human clinical signal (short-term):**
  - In adults with metabolic syndrome, a randomized crossover trial using a soy-lecithin comparator beverage reported no adverse endotoxemia/gut-barrier/cardiometabolic biomarker signal attributable to the lecithin comparator arm in that study context. [11]
- **Allergenicity:**
  - EFSA notes case reports of hypersensitivity linked to residual soy/egg proteins in lecithins.
  - A pediatric non-IgE soy-allergy crossover trial found mostly tolerance, with one slight reaction case. [1][12]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA refined exposure (food additive use):**
  - Brand-loyal scenario: mean up to 82 mg/kg bw/day (children); P95 up to 187 mg/kg bw/day (children).
  - Non-brand-loyal scenario: mean up to 22 mg/kg bw/day (toddlers); P95 up to 62 mg/kg bw/day (infants).
  - Additional subgroup scenarios (e.g., special medical purposes) reported higher P95 values in specific cases. [1]
- **Background diet contribution:**
  - EFSA indicates dietary lecithin intake from regular diet is also material (mean roughly 4–71 mg/kg bw/day across groups, per EFSA summary text). [1]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - Not applicable in the usual numeric sense because EFSA/JECFA did not set a numerical ADI for lecithin (E322(i)). Regulatory concern instead focuses on specific impurity/allergen-management and use-context considerations. [1][4]
- **Vulnerable populations:**
  - Infants/young children and medical-purpose-food consumers were specifically assessed by EFSA; no safety concern concluded for assessed scenarios, while EFSA still recommends prudent additive minimization in infant foods and control of residual allergens/impurities. [1]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: EFSA and JECFA both concluded no numerical ADI needed / ADI not limited; lecithins are natural phospholipid mixtures with endogenous metabolic handling.
  - Evidence against: Emerging microbiome perturbation findings in in vitro and animal models; documented rare hypersensitivity related to residual proteins. [1][4][9][10]
- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: Approved in EU/US, GRAS in FDA framework, no major regulatory safety signal, and no IARC listing; human short-term trial does not indicate clear adverse cardiometabolic/gut-barrier effect.
  - Evidence against: Some nonclinical microbiome and intestinal-barrier findings warrant continued monitoring. [1][3][5][7][9][10][11]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: Credible animal/in vitro signals (gut microbiome/intestinal effects) and known allergenicity edge cases could justify a cautionary interpretation.
  - Evidence against: These signals have not translated into a regulatory downgrade, ADI reduction, or broad human harm signal at permitted uses. [1][9][10][12]
- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: None identified (no major-jurisdiction bans, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing for lecithin itself).
  - Evidence against: Global regulatory status remains permissive with long-standing evaluations and no high-tier hazard classification trigger. [1][4][7]

### 2. Rationale
The strongest evidence base remains regulatory: EFSA (2017) and JECFA support non-numeric ADI conclusions, and FDA status is GRAS with GMP-limited use. Counter-evidence exists mainly as mechanistic/in vitro/animal gut-microbiome findings and residual-protein allergy edge cases, but these have not produced major regulatory restriction for E322(i).

### 3. Recommended tier
**limited**
