# E1105 (Lysozyme) Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E1105 (INS 1105) [S3]
- **Name:** Lysozyme (commonly used as lysozyme hydrochloride for food use) [S3]
- **CAS number(s):** 9066-59-5 (JECFA entry for lysozyme/lysozyme hydrochloride) [S3]
- **Chemical class:** Enzyme protein (peptidoglycan N-acetylmuramoylhydrolase; EC 3.2.1.17), preservative/antimicrobial [S2][S3]
- **Common synonyms:** Lysozyme, hen egg-white lysozyme (HEWL), muramidase, lysozyme hydrochloride [S2][S3][S10]
- **Origin:** Natural (extracted from hens' egg whites) [S2][S3]

## Function in Food
- Lysozyme hydrolyzes bacterial peptidoglycan and is mainly active against Gram-positive bacteria; this is used to control spoilage organisms and malolactic-related instability. [S2]
- Typical food uses include:
  - Wine stabilization against malolactic bacterial degradation [S5]
  - Cheese processing/preservation [S2][S3]
  - Brewing/beer process control [S2]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest EFSA scientific opinion located:** EFSA Journal 2025;23(9):9345 (published September 17, 2025) on lysozyme from hens' eggs. [S1]
- **Approval status:** Lysozyme is authorized in the EU and EFSA opinions describe it as used/authorized (including reference to food additive E1105 specifications under Regulation (EU) No 231/2012). [S2]
- **ADI:** No numeric EFSA ADI identified for E1105 in the EFSA material reviewed; EFSA food-enzyme safety conclusions are framed as "no safety concern under intended conditions" with allergen caveat, not as a numeric ADI. [S1][S2]
- **Key EFSA conclusion:** No general safety concern under intended conditions of use, except possible adverse allergic reactions in susceptible (egg-allergic) individuals. [S1][S2]
- **Evaluation year:** 2025 (latest opinion found), with detailed accessible EFSA assessment text from 2023. [S1][S2]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Regulatory safety conclusion (EFSA):** No general safety concern under intended uses; allergenic reactions remain possible in susceptible individuals. [S1][S2]
- **Exposure context (EFSA 2023):** Estimated dietary exposure to enzyme-TOS up to 4.9 mg/kg bw/day, below comparable intake from eggs across groups. [S2]
- **Allergenicity signal:** EFSA discusses known egg-allergen risk and residual lysozyme in treated foods potentially triggering reactions in sensitized persons. [S2]
- **Recent PubMed evidence (2024/2025):** Analytical studies continue to treat HEWL as a clinically relevant food allergen requiring sensitive detection in foods (wine/cheese/milk matrices). [S10]
- **Carcinogenicity:** No IARC carcinogenic group assignment identified. [S7][S8]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Quantified estimate (EFSA 2023, enzyme context):** up to **4.9 mg TOS/kg bw/day** at high-percentile exposure, with comparable source-material (egg) intake generally higher. [S2]
- **ADI exceedance risk:**
  - JECFA ADI is "not specified" (no numeric threshold), so numeric ADI exceedance assessment is not the central risk driver. [S3]
  - Practical risk driver is allergenicity in egg-allergic subpopulations at residual-use levels. [S1][S2][S10]
- **Vulnerable populations:** Individuals with egg allergy/sensitization. [S1][S2][S10]

## Risk Assessment
- **Recommended Chewber tier:** `limited`
- **Rationale:**
  - Regulatory bodies support use with no general toxicological concern under intended conditions. [S1][S2][S3]
  - JECFA ADI is not specified (historically low toxicological concern profile for this enzyme source). [S3]
  - There is a real, well-established allergenic caveat for sensitized individuals, so this is not `risk_free`. [S1][S2][S10]
