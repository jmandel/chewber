# E355 (Adipic acid) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E355.
- **Name:** Adipic acid.
- **CAS number:** 124-04-9 (shown in both U.S. CFR text and JECFA chemical entry). [7][8]
- **Chemical class:** Aliphatic dicarboxylic acid (hexanedioic acid).
- **Common synonyms:** Hexanedioic acid; 1,4-butanedicarboxylic acid. [7]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Predominantly **synthetic** for food use; U.S. CFR describes production by nitric-acid oxidation of cyclohexanol/cyclohexanone. [7]

## Function in Food
- **Functional roles:** Acidity regulator and flavouring agent (JECFA); also leavening agent and pH control agent in U.S. CFR use definitions. [8][7]
- **Mechanism of action (food technology):** Provides acidification/pH control and sourness contribution; in baking systems it can act as an acid component in leavening systems (consistent with CFR “leavening agent” designation). [7]
- **Common food categories:**
  - JECFA GSFA context includes dairy-based desserts, heat-treated fermented milks, and flavoured fluid milk drinks. [9]
  - U.S. CFR maximum-use examples include baked goods, nonalcoholic beverages, condiments/relishes, dairy analogs, fats/oils, frozen dairy desserts, gelatin/puddings, gravies, meat products, snack foods, and other categories at lower levels. [7]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **Animal toxicology basis used historically for ADI:** JECFA notes the ADI history is tied to long-term rat findings (reduced body-weight gain; earlier comments also mention diarrhoea-related effects at higher dietary concentrations). [8][9]
- **General toxicology profile:** PubMed review (2002) reports low acute oral toxicity in rats (LD50 > 5000 mg/kg), no genotoxic signal across assay systems, and no developmental toxicity in tested species; high-diet long-term feeding mainly produced weight effects. [12]
- **Recent literature pattern:** Recent PubMed-indexed work is more concentrated on packaging/NIAS and adipic-acid-related oligomer migration rather than direct new chronic oral E355 toxicology; some studies reported TTC-based concern signals in specific packaging contexts. [13][14]
- **Epidemiological data:** No strong modern human epidemiology specifically isolating dietary E355 was identified in the sources retrieved in this run.
- **Mechanistic concerns:** Primary concern remains **dose/exposure margin** relative to an old ADI rather than a new confirmed mechanistic hazard signal in humans. [9]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Most quantitative current source found:** JECFA 100th report (2025). [9]
- **Estimated exposure ranges (mg/kg bw/day):**
  - GSFA current maximum levels: high exposures up to **33** (children) and **4** (adults).
  - GSFA current + proposed maximum levels: high exposures up to **410** (children) and **304** (adults).
  - Use-level scenario: high exposures up to **155** (children) and **53** (adults). [9]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** JECFA concluded many conservative scenarios greatly exceed the current group ADI (0-5 mg/kg bw/day), with exceedances reported up to ~660% (children; current-max scenario), ~8000% (current+proposed scenario), and ~3000% (use-level scenario). [9]
- **Vulnerable populations:** Children/high consumers are explicitly at greater exceedance risk in JECFA calculations; frequent consumers of high-use categories are also relevant. [9]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence for: Longstanding authorization; no IARC listing; low acute toxicity and negative genotoxicity in older datasets. [7][10][12]
  - Evidence against: Numerical ADI exists (not “ADI not specified”), and recent JECFA exposure modeling shows frequent potential exceedance scenarios; JECFA calls for updated toxicology reassessment. [8][9]
- **limited**
  - Evidence for: FDA GRAS affirmation with explicit CFR conditions; JECFA retains a formal ADI framework; no major bans identified. [7][8]
  - Evidence against: Magnitude of modeled exceedances (especially in children) and need for toxicology re-evaluation are stronger caveats than “minor concerns only at high doses.” [9]
- **moderate**
  - Evidence for: Approved additive but with clear caveats: exposure scenarios exceeding ADI in children/adults, ADI based on older toxicology, and explicit JECFA recommendation for re-evaluation; EFSA re-evaluation record for E355 remains incomplete in latest state-of-play table. [4][9]
  - Evidence against: No major new human harm signal, no IARC carcinogenic classification, and no broad regulatory bans. [10][11][12]
- **high**
  - Evidence for: None of the high-tier triggers were met in retrieved sources (no major-jurisdiction ban, no IARC 2A/2B listing for adipic acid, no explicit regulatory withdrawal).
  - Evidence against: Continued authorization in U.S. CFR and EU legacy-permitted status; concern is primarily exposure-margin uncertainty and outdated toxicology, not confirmed high hazard. [4][7][9]

### 2. Rationale
The critical driver is not a newly proven severe hazard, but the **mismatch between old toxicology basis and high-end modeled intakes** (especially for children) relative to a low numerical ADI, plus explicit JECFA recommendation to update toxicology and ADI reassessment. EFSA re-evaluation status for E355 also appears unresolved in the latest official state-of-play table retrieved.

### 3. Recommended tier
- **Recommended tier: `moderate`**
