# E281 (Sodium propionate) Research Report

Research date: 2026-02-27

## Identity
- **E-number:** E281 ([R1], [R4])
- **Name:** Sodium propionate / sodium propanoate ([R4], [R11])
- **CAS number(s):** 137-40-6 ([R3], [R4], [R12])
- **Chemical class:** Sodium salt of propionic acid (short-chain fatty acid salt; preservative salt) ([R3], [R4])
- **Common synonyms:** Sodium propanoate, propionic acid sodium salt, propanoic acid sodium salt ([R4], [R12])
- **Natural vs synthetic:** Commercially prepared by neutralizing propionic acid with sodium hydroxide (synthetic industrial manufacture), while propionate is also endogenously produced (e.g., gut microbiota) ([R3], [R8])

## Function in Food
- **Primary function:** Preservative / antimicrobial (inhibits molds and spoilage organisms) ([R3], [R4], [R8])
- **Secondary function:** Flavoring agent (under FDA conditions of use) ([R3])
- **Mechanism of action (practical food context):** Weak-acid salt preservative effect that suppresses mold growth and extends shelf life in susceptible matrices (especially bakery-type products) ([R3], [R8])
- **Common food categories:** Baked goods, nonalcoholic beverages, cheeses, confections/frostings, gelatins/puddings/fillings, jams/jellies, meat products, soft candy (FDA GMP categories) ([R3])

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Status:** Approved/authorised food additive in the EU context; EFSA re-evaluation and later use-extension opinion did not identify safety concern at assessed uses/levels ([R1], [R2]).
- **ADI:** EFSA 2014 re-evaluation concluded **no need for a numerical ADI** for propionic acid and its salts (including E281) ([R1]).
- **Evaluation year(s):**
  - **2014:** Re-evaluation of E280/E281/E282/E283; no numerical ADI needed; no safety concern at reported uses/levels ([R1]).
  - **2016 (latest specific E281 opinion):** Extension of use/use levels for sodium propionate; no safety concern at proposed uses and use levels ([R2]).
- **Key EFSA conclusions extracted:**
  - No numerical ADI required for propionic acid and salts in the 2014 re-evaluation ([R1]).
  - No safety concern for proposed extension use of E281 in 2016 opinion ([R2]).

### FDA / CFR (US)
- **Status:** GRAS affirmation as a direct human food ingredient under specified conditions ([R3]).
- **CFR citation:** **21 CFR 184.1784 (Sodium propionate)** ([R3]).
- **Conditions of use:**
  - No limitation other than current good manufacturing practice (GMP).
  - Functional use as antimicrobial agent and flavoring agent.
  - Food categories include baked goods, beverages, cheeses, confections/frostings, gelatins/puddings/fillings, jams/jellies, meat products, soft candy ([R3]).

### JECFA / WHO
- **Entry:** Sodium propionate (INS 281), CAS 137-40-6 ([R4], [R5]).
- **ADI:** **NOT LIMITED** (group ADI for propionic acid and calcium/potassium/sodium salts) ([R4], [R5]).
- **Evaluation timing:** Listed evaluation year 1973; comment notes this group ADI was maintained at the 49th meeting (1997) ([R4]).

### IARC
- Sodium propionate was **not identified as a listed agent** in the IARC Monographs “List of Classifications” dataset checked on 2026-02-27 ([R6], [R7]).
- Therefore, no IARC Group 1/2A/2B/3 classification was found specifically for sodium propionate in the checked IARC classification list ([R6], [R7]).

### Notable bans
- No major jurisdiction-wide ban identified in the sources reviewed here (EU/EFSA, US/FDA-CFR, JECFA) ([R1], [R3], [R4]).

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal / mechanistic signals
- **Sodium propionate zebrafish embryo study (2022):** reported disrupted thigmotaxis behavior, altered circadian-related gene expression, and glucose-metabolism/insulin-resistance-related effects in zebrafish developmental model ([R10]).
- **Propionate metabolic signaling study (mice + human meal challenge, 2019):** reported propionate-triggered counter-regulatory hormonal responses (glucagon, FABP4, norepinephrine) and insulin resistance/hyperinsulinemia signals under study conditions ([R8]).
- **Human crossover acute challenge (2021, calcium propionate source of propionic acid):** reported inappropriate activation of insulin counter-regulatory hormonal network after propionic acid exposure under trial conditions ([R9]).

### Epidemiological / clinical evidence
- Human evidence exists mainly as acute intervention/challenge studies rather than long-term population-level epidemiology directly tied to routine E281 intake ([R8], [R9]).
- No clear high-quality evidence in reviewed sources demonstrating carcinogenicity classification or major chronic toxicity at normal authorized intake levels ([R1], [R2], [R6], [R7]).

### Mechanistic concerns to track
- Metabolic-endocrine signaling effects (counter-regulatory hormone activation, insulin resistance markers) in challenge settings ([R8], [R9]).
- Developmental neurobehavioral/metabolic effects in non-mammalian model (zebrafish) requiring cautious interpretation for human risk extrapolation ([R10]).

## Exposure Assessment
- EFSA 2014 reported estimated dietary exposure (as propionic acid equivalents from E280-283) with higher values in children/toddlers:
  - Mean up to ~41 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers and ~47 mg/kg bw/day in children.
  - High-level up to ~155 mg/kg bw/day in toddlers and ~111 mg/kg bw/day in children ([R1]).
- Because EFSA concluded no numerical ADI was needed and JECFA set ADI as “not limited,” a classic ADI exceedance calculation is not directly applicable ([R1], [R4]).
- **Potentially vulnerable groups for monitoring:** children/high consumers of preservative-rich foods; individuals with metabolic susceptibility (signal-generating, not regulatory-confirmatory) ([R1], [R8], [R9]).

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis

#### `risk_free`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - JECFA ADI: NOT LIMITED.
  - EFSA: no numerical ADI needed in re-evaluation.
  - Long-standing regulatory acceptance (EU/US/JECFA) ([R1], [R3], [R4]).
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Experimental literature shows metabolic/hormonal perturbation signals under challenge conditions (human and animal models).
  - Developmental model signals in zebrafish suggest that “no conceivable concern in all contexts” is too strong ([R8], [R9], [R10]).

#### `limited`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Approved/GRAS with established regulatory framework and GMP conditions.
  - EFSA and JECFA outcomes are non-restrictive (no numerical ADI / not limited), with no major regulatory withdrawal.
  - No IARC carcinogenic classification found for sodium propionate ([R1], [R2], [R3], [R4], [R6], [R7]).
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Mechanistic studies raise non-trivial hypotheses (metabolic-endocrine effects), so “minor concerns only” must be interpreted with caution ([R8], [R9], [R10]).

#### `moderate`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Credible experimental safety signals exist (metabolic/endocrine and developmental-behavioral findings).
  - Higher exposure estimates in toddlers/children may justify closer scrutiny even without numerical ADI ([R1], [R8], [R9], [R10]).
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - No EFSA/JECFA reduction to a restrictive ADI for E281.
  - No broad regulatory actions like ban/suspension.
  - Key adverse findings are not yet reflected as consensus regulatory hazard reclassification ([R1], [R2], [R4]).

#### `high`
- **Evidence supporting this tier:**
  - Limited. The reviewed data do not show major-jurisdiction bans or IARC 2A/2B/1 classification for sodium propionate.
- **Evidence against this tier:**
  - Strongly inconsistent with current EFSA/JECFA/FDA positions and absent major hazard reclassification signals ([R1], [R3], [R4], [R6], [R7]).

### 2. Rationale
The additive remains broadly authorised with permissive toxicological benchmarks (EFSA no numerical ADI; JECFA not limited) and GRAS status in US CFR. However, newer mechanistic and intervention studies indicate potential metabolic signaling effects under specific exposure conditions. Current evidence does not clearly justify a high-severity regulatory risk tier, but it also does not support a strict “risk-free” framing.

### 3. Recommended tier
**`limited`**
