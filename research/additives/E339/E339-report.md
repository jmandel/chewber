# E339 (Sodium phosphates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E339 (E339(i) monosodium phosphate, E339(ii) disodium phosphate, E339(iii) trisodium phosphate). [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic sodium salts of phosphoric acid (orthophosphates).
- **CAS numbers (by INS variant):**
  - **7558-80-7** (E339(i), sodium dihydrogen phosphate / monosodium phosphate). [JECFA-1]
  - **7558-79-4** (E339(ii), disodium phosphate / disodium hydrogen phosphate). [JECFA-2]
  - **7601-54-9** (E339(iii), trisodium phosphate). [JECFA-3]
- **Common synonyms:** sodium phosphate, monobasic sodium phosphate, dibasic/disodium phosphate, tribasic/trisodium phosphate. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3][FDA-1]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic** food additive (industrial phosphate salts used as ingredients).

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** phosphate salts regulate pH (acid-base buffering), complex metal ions (sequestration), and can support emulsification/stabilization depending on the salt form. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3]
- **Common functional classes:** acidity regulator, sequestrant, emulsifier, stabilizer, texturizer/thickener (variant-dependent). [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3]
- **Common food categories (EFSA exposure contributors):** bread/rolls, fine bakery wares, infant formula and baby foods (infants), processed cheese (toddlers), meat products (children/adults/elderly). [EFSA-2]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Latest re-evaluation:** EFSA Journal opinion on phosphates (E338-341, E343, E450-452), published **2019**. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3]
- **Approval status:** phosphates are authorised in the EU under Annex II/III of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 (includes E339). [EFSA-2]
- **ADI:** EFSA derived a **group ADI of 40 mg/kg bw/day (as phosphorus)**. [EFSA-2]
- **Key conclusions:**
  - ADI considered protective for the general healthy population.
  - Exposure estimates based on analytical total-phosphorus data exceeded the ADI for infants/toddlers/children at mean exposure, and for infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high exposure.
  - No genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern identified; no developmental toxicity concern identified.
  - ADI does not apply to people with moderate-to-severe renal impairment. [EFSA-2]
- **Access note:** direct EFSA Journal page on efsa.europa.eu redirected to Wiley and was inaccessible from this environment due bot protection; EFSA content was extracted via EFSA Open entry and the PubMed Central mirror of the same EFSA opinion. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2][EFSA-3]

## Key Safety Evidence
### Animal studies
- EFSA cites historical toxicology data and selected a NOAEL of 167 mg P/kg bw/day as the basis for ADI derivation; phosphates were considered of low acute oral toxicity. [EFSA-2]
- A 2025 mouse study reported anxiety-like behavior and neuroimmune changes under chronic high-phosphate diet conditions, supporting mechanistic concern at high intake levels (not a direct regulatory endpoint for normal additive use). [PUBMED-4]

### Epidemiological / human data
- A 2023 cohort study found higher urinary phosphate associated with higher cardiovascular disease incidence; authors note urinary phosphate may reflect high intake of highly processed foods/inorganic phosphate salts. [PUBMED-3]
- A 2024 randomized cross-over human study found meals high in inorganic phosphate additives altered acute calcium/phosphate homeostasis and urinary excretion dynamics. [PUBMED-2]

### Mechanistic / review evidence
- A 2023 review proposed phosphate food additives as one plausible mechanism linking ultra-processed food intake to cardiorenal risk, while noting exposure quantification gaps. [PUBMED-5]

### Regulatory toxicology conclusions
- EFSA: no concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity; no developmental toxicity signal in reviewed studies. [EFSA-2]
- JECFA: group MTDI maintained at 70 mg/kg bw (as P). [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3]

## Exposure Assessment
- **Whole-diet phosphorus exposure (EFSA analytical-data scenario):** mean 251 to 1,625 mg P/person/day (infants to adults); 95th percentile 331 to 2,728 mg P/person/day. [EFSA-2]
- **Refined non-brand-loyal additive scenario:** 1 to 48 mg P/kg bw/day (mean) and 3 to 62 mg P/kg bw/day (95th percentile), across population groups. [EFSA-2]
- **ADI exceedance risk:** EFSA reported exceedance of the 40 mg/kg bw/day ADI for infants/toddlers/children at mean exposure and for infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high exposure; supplement-related exposures could also exceed ADI. [EFSA-2]
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants and children (higher exposure per kg bw), frequent food-supplement users, and people with reduced renal function (EFSA explicitly notes ADI not applicable to moderate-severe renal impairment). [EFSA-2]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
#### risk_free
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** long-standing authorization (EU, US), EFSA no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern, and phosphate is an essential nutrient. [EFSA-2][FDA-1]
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA found ADI exceedance in multiple child populations and high consumers, plus renal-function caveat.

#### limited
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** FDA GRAS with GMP condition; EFSA and JECFA provide quantitative guidance values (EFSA ADI, JECFA MTDI) rather than outright hazard conclusions. [FDA-1][JECFA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Evidence against this tier:** EFSA explicitly reports population groups exceeding ADI; recent literature raises plausible cardio-renal and neurobehavioral concerns at higher exposures.

#### moderate
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** matches the provided definition because the additive is approved **with caveats**, and EFSA reports ADI exceedance in some populations (especially infants/children/adolescents at higher exposure). Additional human/epidemiologic literature supports concern around high inorganic phosphate exposure patterns. [EFSA-2][PUBMED-2][PUBMED-3][PUBMED-5]
- **Evidence against this tier:** no major agency has moved to ban E339, and core regulatory toxicology does not show genotoxic/carcinogenic hazard at standard use.

#### high
- **Evidence supporting this tier:** none of the required "high" triggers were found (no major bans, no IARC Group 2A/2B assignment for E339, EFSA did establish an ADI).
- **Evidence against this tier:** continued regulatory authorization in EU/US and absence of IARC classification for sodium phosphates.

### 2. Rationale
The decisive factor is EFSA's 2019 outcome: an established ADI but documented exceedance in key child groups and high consumers. That places E339 beyond "limited" and into a caveat-driven profile, while available evidence does not support "high" risk classification.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
