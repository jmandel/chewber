# E452 (Polyphosphates) — Research Report

## Identity
- **E-number:** E452 (polyphosphates), including **E452(i) sodium polyphosphates**, **E452(ii) potassium polyphosphates**, **E452(iii) sodium calcium polyphosphate**, and **E452(iv) calcium polyphosphate**. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3][JECFA-4]
- **Chemical class:** Inorganic condensed phosphates (linear/cyclic oligomers and polymers formed by condensation of orthophosphates). [EFSA-1]
- **CAS number(s) identified in visited sources:**
  - **50813-16-6** (E452(i), sodium polyphosphates, glassy). [JECFA-1]
  - **7790-53-6** (E452(ii), potassium polyphosphates). [JECFA-2]
  - **77226-95-6** (calcium polyphosphate listing in WHO/IPFS entry). [JECFA-5]
- **Common synonyms (from JECFA/WHO records):** sodium tetrapolyphosphate, sodium hexametaphosphate, Graham's salt, potassium metaphosphate, potassium polymetaphosphate, sodium calcium polyphosphate (glassy), calcium polyphosphate. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3][JECFA-4][JECFA-5]
- **Natural vs synthetic:** **Synthetic/industrial food additive form** (manufactured phosphate salts), although phosphorus itself is an essential nutrient. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]

## Function in Food
- **Mechanism of action:** Polyphosphates act as **sequestrants/chelators** and texture modifiers; they bind polyvalent ions, influence ionic balance, and support emulsification/water-binding/texture stability in processed foods. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-4][EFSA-1]
- **JECFA functional classes in visited E452 entries:**
  - E452(i): emulsifier, sequestrant, texturizer, thickener.
  - E452(ii): emulsifier, humectant, sequestrant, texturizer, thickener.
  - E452(iii): emulsifier, leavening/raising agent, nutrient supplement, stabilizer.
  - E452(iv): emulsifier, humectant, sequestrant, texturizer, thickener. [JECFA-1][JECFA-2][JECFA-3][JECFA-4]
- **Common food categories (from regulatory/scientific context):** broad processed food categories and supplements within phosphate-authorized uses in EU; high prevalence of phosphate additives in U.S. packaged foods is documented in recent surveillance literature. [EFSA-1][PUBMED-3]

## Regulatory Status
### EFSA (EU)
- **Approval status:** Phosphates including E452 are authorized in the EU and were re-evaluated by EFSA in 2019. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **ADI:** **Group ADI 40 mg/kg bw/day (as phosphorus)** for E338-341, E343, E450-452. [EFSA-1]
- **Evaluation year:** **2019**. [EFSA-1][EFSA-2]
- **Key conclusions (EFSA):**
  - No concern for genotoxicity/carcinogenicity in the phosphate group dataset.
  - No developmental-toxicity effects were reported in reviewed studies.
  - Exposure estimates exceeded the ADI for infants/toddlers/children at mean intake and for infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high intake.
  - Exposure from food supplements could exceed the ADI. [EFSA-1]

## Key Safety Evidence
- **EFSA re-evaluation (regulatory toxicology baseline):** low acute oral toxicity profile, no genotoxicity/carcinogenicity concern, no developmental toxicity signal in reviewed dataset; however, ADI exceedance was estimated in younger/high-intake groups. [EFSA-1]
- **Human intervention evidence (2024):** randomized cross-over studies found meals high in inorganic phosphate additives increased serum phosphate and urinary phosphate excretion, with measurable effects on calcium handling/homeostasis. [PUBMED-2]
- **Exposure prevalence evidence (2025):** cross-sectional U.S. packaged-food analysis reported phosphate additives in **56% of 39,937 products**, indicating widespread population exposure potential. [PUBMED-3]
- **Mechanistic review signal (2023):** review literature proposes plausible cardiorenal pathways (vascular/mineral-hormone mechanisms) from high inorganic phosphate additive exposure, while noting exposure quantification gaps. [PUBMED-1]
- **Recent animal signal (2025):** a mouse study reported anxiety-like behavior under high dietary phosphate intake (preclinical relevance; human translation uncertain). [PUBMED-4]

## Exposure Assessment
- **EFSA whole-diet phosphorus exposure estimates (as P):** mean exposure ranged from **251 mg P/person/day (infants)** to **1,625 mg P/person/day (adults)**; high (95th percentile) from **331** to **2,728 mg P/person/day**. [EFSA-1]
- **ADI exceedance:** EFSA reported exceedance of 40 mg/kg bw/day ADI in infants/toddlers/children at mean exposure and in infants/toddlers/children/adolescents at high exposure; supplement scenarios also exceeded ADI. [EFSA-1]
- **Vulnerable populations:** infants/children/high consumers and regular supplement users (per EFSA exposure modeling), plus populations with impaired phosphate handling (e.g., CKD-focused literature context). [EFSA-1][PUBMED-1][PUBMED-3]

## Risk Assessment
### 1. Tier-by-tier analysis
- **risk_free**
  - Evidence supporting: long-standing authorization in EU/U.S./JECFA frameworks; phosphorus is essential biologically.
  - Evidence against: EFSA set a finite ADI (not “ADI not specified”) and reported ADI exceedance in multiple younger/high-intake populations. [EFSA-1]

- **limited**
  - Evidence supporting: approved/GRAS status with explicit intake frameworks (EFSA ADI, JECFA MTDI) and no IARC classification signal found.
  - Evidence against: exceedance is documented in exposure modeling and recent human/mechanistic studies indicate potential biological effects at higher additive exposure. [EFSA-1][PUBMED-1][PUBMED-2][PUBMED-3]

- **moderate**
  - Evidence supporting: additive is approved but with caveats directly matching the rubric (EFSA ADI exceedance in some populations, plus credible human/mechanistic concerns with higher inorganic phosphate loads).
  - Evidence against: no major regulator has withdrawn authorization; evidence does not show a consistent severe hazard at typical general-population exposure. [EFSA-1][FDA-1][JECFA-1]

- **high**
  - Evidence supporting: cumulative exposure concerns and specific-risk-population concerns are credible.
  - Evidence against: no major-jurisdiction ban and no IARC Group 1/2A/2B listing found for E452-related names. [IARC-1][IARC-2]

### 2. Rationale
E452 is not in a “risk-free” profile because EFSA set a bounded group ADI and identified exceedance in younger/high-exposure groups. It is not “high” because major authorities still allow use and no IARC carcinogenic classification was found. The best fit is **moderate**: approved, but with clear exposure caveats and credible concern signals at higher inorganic phosphate intake.

### 3. Recommended tier
**moderate**
