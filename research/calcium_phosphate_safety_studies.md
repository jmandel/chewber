# Calcium Phosphate Food Additive Safety Studies (2015–2025)

Research conducted via PubMed and Google Scholar searches on calcium phosphate and phosphate food additive safety.

---

## 1. KEY REGULATORY REVIEW (EFSA Re-evaluation)

### EFSA Re-evaluation of Phosphates (E 338–341, E 343, E 450–452) as Food Additives
- **Authors:** EFSA Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings (FAF); Younes M, Aquilina G, Castle L, et al.
- **Journal:** EFSA Journal. 2019 Jun 12;17(6):e05674
- **PMID:** 32626329
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32626329/
- **Full text:** https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2903/j.efsa.2019.5674
- **Cited by:** 121 (per Google Scholar)
- **Key Findings:**
  - Established a **group ADI of 40 mg phosphorus/kg body weight/day** for all phosphate food additives (E 338–341, E 343, E 450–452)
  - Exposure estimates from the whole diet ranged from **251 mg P/person/day (infants) to 1,625 mg P/person/day (adults)** at mean level
  - High exposure (95th percentile): **331 mg P/person/day (infants) to 2,728 mg P/person/day (adults)**
  - Phosphates are of **low acute oral toxicity**; **no concern for genotoxicity or carcinogenicity**
  - No effects reported in developmental toxicity studies
  - **CRITICAL CONCERN:** Estimated exposure **exceeded the ADI for infants, toddlers, and children** at mean level, and for infants, toddlers, children, and adolescents at 95th percentile
  - Phosphates from food supplements also exceeded the ADI
  - Formula for infants below 16 weeks was considered safe
  - Phosphate is absorbed at 80–90% as free orthophosphate and excreted via the kidney

## 2. CALCIUM PHOSPHATE–SPECIFIC STUDIES

### 2a. On the Application of Calcium Phosphate Micro- and Nanoparticles as Food Additive
- **Authors:** Enax J, Meyer F, Schulze zur Wiesche E, Epple M
- **Journal:** Nanomaterials (Basel). 2022 Nov 19;12(22):4075
- **PMID:** 36432359
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36432359/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Comprehensive review of calcium phosphate as food additive
  - All calcium phosphates (hydroxyapatite, tricalcium phosphate) are **soluble in the acidic stomach environment regardless of particle size or phase**
  - After passing through the stomach, they are present as **dissolved calcium and phosphate ions indistinguishable from those ingested from natural sources** (e.g., cheese, milk)
  - Human breast milk naturally contains calcium phosphate as nanoscopic clusters inside casein micelles
  - **Concluded that calcium phosphates are generally safe as food additives, including in baby formula**

### 2b. The True Nature of Tricalcium Phosphate Used as Food Additive (E341(iii))
- **Authors:** El Moussaoui Y, Terrisse H, Quillard S, Ropers MH, et al.
- **Journal:** Nanomaterials (Basel). 2023 Jun 8;13(12):1823
- **PMID:** 37368253
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37368253/
- **Key Findings:**
  - TCP (E341(iii)) is used in powdered food preparations including baby formula
  - A commercial TCP food additive was identified as **actually being hydroxyapatite (HA)**, not TCP
  - Particles are of **nanometric dimension** — E341(iii) is thus a nanomaterial
  - In water, HA particles sediment rapidly at pH >6 and are **progressively dissolved in acidic media (pH <5), completely dissolved at pH 2** (stomach conditions)
  - Raises the question of potential persistency in the gastrointestinal tract before reaching stomach pH
  - **Concern:** Since TCP may be considered a nanomaterial on the European market, questions arise about safety characterization

## 3. PHOSPHATE ADDITIVES & CARDIOVASCULAR / CARDIORENAL RISK

### 3a. Industrial Use of Phosphate Food Additives: A Mechanism Linking Ultra-Processed Food Intake to Cardiorenal Disease Risk?
- **Authors:** (not captured — Nutrients review)
- **Journal:** Nutrients. 2023 Aug 9;15(16):3510
- **PMID:** 37630701
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37630701/
- **Type:** Review
- **Key Findings:**
  - Links rising UPF consumption to cardiovascular disease through **inorganic phosphate additives**
  - Inorganic phosphates are **rapidly and efficiently absorbed**
  - Elevated serum phosphate leads to negative cardiorenal effects:
    - **Directly** through tissue/vessel calcification
    - **Indirectly** through release of mineral-regulating hormones (PTH, FGF-23)
  - Association between serum phosphate and cardiovascular/bone disease in CKD patients is well-established
  - Epidemiological studies show association between **serum phosphate, dietary phosphate intake, and mortality even in the general American population**
  - The magnitude of the role of inorganic phosphate additives remains to be fully determined

### 3b. Phosphate and Kidney Healthy Aging
- **Authors:** (Kidney Blood Press Res)
- **Journal:** Kidney Blood Press Res. 2020;45(6):802-811
- **PMID:** 33049732
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33049732/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Higher serum phosphate levels associated with **higher risk of overall mortality and cardiovascular disease**
  - Higher phosphate levels **worsen CKD progression** and may contribute to renal dysfunction in healthy individuals
  - Acute high phosphate intake can cause **acute kidney injury**
  - Phosphate consumption estimates from dietary recalls are **largely underestimated**, especially in populations consuming high amounts of processed food
  - Recommends healthy diet with phosphate from natural food sources to promote healthy aging

## 4. PHOSPHATE ADDITIVES & KIDNEY/RENAL EFFECTS

### 4a. Use of Mechanistic Information to Derive Chemical-Specific Adjustment Factors — Refinement of Risk Assessment
- **Authors:** Gundert-Remy U, et al.
- **Journal:** Regul Toxicol Pharmacol. 2020 Nov;117:104776
- **PMID:** 32871170
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32871170/
- **Key Findings:**
  - When re-evaluating safety of phosphates as food additives, **nephrocalcinosis was identified as the critical endpoint**
  - Mechanism: precipitation of calcium phosphate in the kidney, depending on solubility
  - Used glomerular filtration rate (GFR) as proxy for primary urine volume
  - Derived a **phosphate-specific adjustment factor of 4.5** (median) for interspecies and intraspecies variability
  - Important for refining the risk assessment of phosphate food additives

### 4b. Dietary Phosphorus and Metabolic Health in CKD and ESKD
- **Journal:** Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2025 Sep 1;20(9):1289-1298
- **PMID:** 40111420
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111420/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Dietary phosphate contributes to CKD-associated mineral bone disease pathogenesis
  - Intestinal absorption: ~60–70% of dietary P content, with **lower rates for organic P from plant sources and higher rates for inorganic P** (additives)
  - Widespread use of phosphate additives increases cardiovascular risk and mortality
  - Managing P intake essential to slow disease progression

### 4c. Managing Phosphate Burden in Patients Receiving Dialysis: Beyond Phosphate Binders and Diet
- **Journal:** Kidney360. 2023 Nov 1;4(11):1650-1656
- **PMID:** 37870525
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37870525/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Hyperphosphatemia associated with **increased mortality** in dialysis patients
  - Dietary restriction is difficult because of **hidden phosphate additives in processed food**
  - Need for strategies beyond binders and diet alone

### 4d. Lack of Awareness of Dietary Sources of Phosphorus Is a Clinical Concern
- **Journal:** In Vivo. 2019 Jan-Feb;33(1):11-16
- **PMID:** 30587596
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30587596/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Hyperphosphatemia associated with more rapid CKD progression, **higher mortality, and higher cardiovascular disease rate**
  - CKD patients lack awareness of phosphorus sources including food additives

## 5. PHOSPHATE ADDITIVES & BONE HEALTH

### 5a. Inorganic Phosphate Additives in Meals and Adaptations to 5-Days of Dietary Inorganic Phosphate Loading Alter Acute Calcium Homeostasis
- **Journal:** JBMR Plus. 2024 Jun 6;8(8):ziae075
- **PMID:** 39238566
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39238566/
- **Type:** Two randomized cross-over studies in healthy adults
- **Key Findings:**
  - Diets with inorganic phosphate additives are **unbalanced with respect to calcium** and linked to altered bone metabolism
  - Meals with inorganic phosphate additives (lower Ca:P ratio) led to **higher serum phosphate and more urinary phosphate excretion**
  - Ingestion of inorganic phosphate **promotes calcium excretion** despite no calcium consumption
  - Homeostatic mechanisms may reduce calcium excretion with chronic high phosphate exposure
  - **Concern for bone health** from diets enriched with inorganic phosphate additives; further studies needed

### 5b. Sustained and Reversible Effects of a Dietary Phosphate Intake on Bone and Mineral Metabolism During Aging
- **Journal:** Geroscience. 2025 Jun 2 (online ahead of print)
- **PMID:** 40455097
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40455097/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Dietary phosphate choices made early in life could have **long-term consequences on bone health**
  - How duration of dietary Pi choices, stage of life, and gender influence impact on long-term bone health needs further study

## 6. PHOSPHATE FOOD ADDITIVE INTAKE ASSESSMENT

### 6a. Phosphate-Based Additives in Processed Foods: Is Excess Exposure a Cause for Concern?
- **Authors:** Dunford EK, Calvo MS
- **Journal:** Am J Clin Nutr. 2025 Apr;121(4):873-881
- **PMID:** 40180501
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40180501/
- **Type:** Cross-sectional study of U.S. packaged food supply
- **Key Findings:**
  - P-additives present in **56% of 39,937 products** from the top 25 U.S. food/beverage manufacturers
  - Represents **>$120 billion in consumer purchases** in 2020
  - Top P-additives: lecithin (32%), sodium phosphate (13%), **calcium phosphate (11%)**, modified starches (10%), sodium acid pyrophosphate (6%)
  - Products contained 0–8 P-additives; **21% of foods contained >1 P-additive**
  - P-additives have **more rapid/efficient absorption** than naturally occurring dietary phosphate
  - Not accounted for in nutrient databases despite greater contribution to total phosphorus intake
  - Calls for FDA to **mandate phosphorus labeling on nutrition facts labels**

### 6b. Phosphorus Additives and Their Impact on Phosphorus Content in Foods (USDA Database Analysis)
- **Journal:** J Ren Nutr. 2023 May;33(3):443-449
- **PMID:** 36731685
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36731685/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Analyzed frequency of phosphate additives in the USDA Branded Foods Product Database
  - Examined how additives impact actual phosphate content
  - Found relationship between phosphate and sodium content for products with/without additives

### 6c. Phosphorus-Containing Food Additives in the Food Supply — An Audit of Products on Supermarket Shelves
- **Authors:** Tuominen M, Karp HJ, Itkonen ST
- **Journal:** J Renal Nutrition, 2022
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1051227621002120
- **Key Findings:**
  - Investigated prevalence of P-containing additives in the Finnish food supply across different food categories
  - Phosphate additives pose a risk for CKD patients

### 6d. Phosphorus — A Scoping Review for Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023
- **Journal:** Food Nutr Res. 2023 Dec 20;67
- **PMID:** 38187803
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38187803/
- **Key Findings:**
  - Intake from food additives is unknown but may play a significant role due to common use
  - Bioavailability of phosphorus in **plant-based products is lower** than animal-based products
  - Bioavailability of phosphate from **inorganic additives is higher** than from natural food sources

## SUMMARY OF KEY THEMES

### Safety of Calcium Phosphate Specifically (E341)
- **EFSA (2019):** Established group ADI of 40 mg P/kg bw/day; considered phosphates of low acute toxicity, no genotoxicity or carcinogenicity concern
- **Enax et al. (2022):** Calcium phosphates dissolve in stomach acid regardless of particle size → present as ions indistinguishable from natural dietary sources → **generally safe**
- **El Moussaoui et al. (2023):** Commercial TCP (E341(iii)) is actually hydroxyapatite nanoparticles; dissolves completely at pH 2 (stomach); raises nanomaterial classification questions

### Concerns About Phosphate Additives Generally
1. **Exposure exceeds ADI** in children/adolescents (EFSA 2019)
2. **56% of U.S. packaged foods** contain phosphate additives; calcium phosphate is 3rd most common (11%)
3. **Inorganic phosphate additives are more bioavailable** (~80-90% absorbed) than organic/natural phosphate
4. **Cardiovascular risk:** Elevated serum phosphate → vascular calcification, elevated PTH and FGF-23
5. **Kidney effects:** Nephrocalcinosis identified as critical toxicity endpoint; worsens CKD progression
6. **Bone health:** Inorganic phosphate additives promote urinary calcium excretion, disrupt Ca:P balance
7. **Not labeled:** Phosphate content not required on U.S. food labels despite significant dietary contribution
8. **Emerging concerns:** Links to muscle atrophy, anxiety (animal), offspring mineral disorders, and possible autism associations
